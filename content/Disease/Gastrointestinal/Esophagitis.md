# Esophagitis

## Definition & Classification
- **Clinical Definition:** Esophagitis is defined as an acute or chronic inflammatory condition involving the esophageal mucosa and underlying tissue layers, resulting from noninfectious causes (such as acid reflux, chemical injury, or medication toxicity) or infectious etiologies (fungal, viral, bacterial, mycobacterial, or parasitic invasion).
- **Major Classifications:**
	- Infectious Esophagitis: (1) Fungal Esophagitis (Candida esophagitis); (2) Viral Esophagitis (Cytomegalovirus (CMV) esophagitis, Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV) esophagitis, Acute HIV infection esophagitis, Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV), Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV), Human Papillomavirus (HPV)); (3) Bacterial & Higher Bacterial Esophagitis (Actinomyces species, Treponema pallidum); (4) Mycobacterial Esophagitis (Mycobacterium tuberculosis, Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC)); (5) Parasitic Esophagitis (Cryptosporidium species, Pneumocystis jirovecii).
	- Noninfectious Esophagitis: (1) Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD; the single most common cause of esophagitis overall); (2) Eosinophilic Esophagitis (immune-mediated esophageal eosinophilia associated with food allergies); (3) Pill-Induced / Medication-Induced Esophagitis (local chemical mucositis caused by caustic tablets or capsules, e.g., doxycycline, tetracycline, clindamycin, penicillin, rifampin, zidovudine, zalcitabine/ddC, nelfinavir, NSAIDs, bisphosphonates/alendronate); (4) Radiation- and Chemotherapy-Induced Mucositis; (5) Corrosive Esophagitis (ingestion of lye or strong acids); (6) Idiopathic Aphthous Ulceration (in AIDS).
- **Risk Factors & Epidemiology:**
  - General & Population Epidemiology: GERD is the most common cause of esophagitis overall in the general population. Infectious esophagitis occurs overwhelmingly in immunocompromised hosts, particularly individuals with HIV/AIDS, cancer receiving antineoplastic chemotherapy, solid organ transplant (SOT) recipients, hematopoietic stem cell / bone marrow transplant (HSCT/BMT) recipients, or patients receiving systemic corticosteroids or immunosuppressive therapy, though healthy immunocompetent persons are occasionally affected.
  - HIV/AIDS Population: Before antiretroviral therapy (ART), esophageal symptoms occurred in 40% to 50% of AIDS patients. While ART has dramatically reduced incidence, opportunistic esophagitis remains common in treatment failure or noncompliance. Candida accounts for >=50% of cases in AIDS, followed by CMV (30% to 40%), idiopathic aphthous ulcers (27% to 32%), HSV (5% to 16%), and acute HIV infection.
  - Transplant & Oncology Populations: In bone marrow transplant recipients evaluated for esophageal symptoms, infectious etiologies are identified in ~45% (CMV and HSV are at least as common as Candida in BMT recipients, typically presenting >40 days post-transplant). In solid organ transplant recipients, Candida esophagitis occurs in ~2.2% of renal and organ transplant recipients.
  - Local & Medication-Related Risk Factors: Impeired esophageal motility (systemic sclerosis, achalasia, esophageal webs or rings, obstructing esophageal carcinoma); local mucosal injury (acid reflux, antecedent HSV esophagitis); H2-receptor blocker or proton pump inhibitor therapy (increases esophageal Candida colonization); and improper pill administration (ingesting medications without water or immediately before recumbency).

## Etiology (The Pathogens)
- **Most Common Pathogens (Overall):**
  - Candida species: Candida albicans is the predominant fungal pathogen across all groups, accounting for >=50% of infectious esophagitis cases. Non-albicans species (Candida tropicalis, Candida parapsilosis, Candida krusei, Candida glabrata) occur less frequently but are emerging due to selective pressure from prophylactic azole exposure.
  - Cytomegalovirus (CMV): Leading viral cause of ulcerative esophagitis in advanced AIDS (typically CD4 <50 cells/mm3), solid organ transplant, and BMT recipients.
  - Herpes Simplex Virus Type 1 (HSV-1): Major viral cause of acute ulcerative esophagitis in immunocompromised patients and immunocompetent hosts (HSV-2 is rarely implicated).
  - Human Immunodeficiency Virus Type 1 (HIV-1): Direct cause of severe primary ulcerative esophagitis during acute HIV seroconversion syndrome, and associated with idiopathic giant aphthous esophageal ulcers in advanced AIDS.
- **Pathogens by Specific Age Groups:**
  - Children & Adolescents: Eosinophilic esophagitis (associated with food allergy), Candida albicans, HSV-1, and viral/fungal causes in pediatric oncology or congenital immunodeficiency.
  - Adults & Elderly (>65 years): Gastroesophageal reflux disease, Candida albicans (common in elderly, debility, or H2-blocker/PPI use), Pill-induced esophagitis (doxycycline, NSAIDs, alendronate), CMV, and HSV-1.
- **Atypical or Opportunistic Pathogens:**
  - Mycobacteria: Mycobacterium tuberculosis (esophageal TB causing ulceration or esophagobronchial/esophagopleural fistulas) and Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC).
  - Fungi & Molds: Cryptococcus neoformans (in AIDS or Hyper-IgE / Job syndrome), Histoplasma capsulatum, Aspergillus species, Pneumocystis jirovecii, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
  - Bacteria & Higher Bacteria: Actinomyces species (Actinomyces israelii causing esophageal actinomycosis) and Treponema pallidum (erosive syphilitic gastritis/esophagitis).
  - Viruses: Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV; chickenpox or zoster esophagitis with hematemesis), Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV; associated with esophageal ulcers in AIDS), and Human Papillomavirus (HPV).
  - Parasites: Cryptosporidium species.

## Pathophysiology
- **Mechanism of Infection & Spread:**
  - Candida Esophagitis: C. albicans is part of normal oral microbiota (colonization present in up to 20% of healthy adults). Progression from superficial mucosal colonization to tissue-invasive infection occurs when systemic cell-mediated immunity (CD4+ T-cell depletion) or local mucosal barrier defenses are impaired. Adherent yeasts transform into tissue-invasive pseudohyphae and true hyphae, penetrating the esophageal mucosal epithelium.
  - Cytomegalovirus (CMV) Esophagitis: Reactivation of latent CMV infection or hematogenous dissemination in severely immunocompromised hosts. CMV exhibits specific vascular and stromal tropism, selectively infecting vascular endothelial cells and mucosal/submucosal fibroblasts in the esophageal wall. Endothelial swelling, necrosis, and microvascular thrombosis lead to localized tissue ischemia and deep ulceration.
  - Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV) Esophagitis: Reactivation of latent HSV-1 in trigeminal or vagal sensory ganglia, or direct spread from oropharyngeal herpetic lesions via retrograde axonal transport or secretions. HSV directly infects esophageal squamous epithelial cells, producing cell ballooning, acantholysis, intraepithelial vesicles, and focal epithelial necrosis.
  - Pill-Induced Esophagitis: Direct, localized, prolonged chemical contact of caustic or acidic medication tablets (e.g., doxycycline, tetracycline, bisphosphonates, NSAIDs, potassium chloride) with the esophageal mucosa due to transient esophageal stasis (swallowing without sufficient fluid, lying down immediately after ingestion, or anatomical compression at the aortic arch).
- **Host Tissue Response & Damage:**
  - Candida Pathology: Hyperemic, erythematous esophageal mucosa covered by discrete to confluent yellow-white exudative mucosal plaques that adhere firmly to the epithelium. Removal of plaques reveals an underlying rough, friable, eroded surface. Can progress to extensive necrosis, strictures, or rare transmural perforation.
  - CMV Pathology: Characterized by large (>10 cm2), solitary or multiple, shallow, "punched-out" or geographic ulcers located in the middle to distal esophagus, with well-demarcated margins and relatively normal intervening mucosa. Histology demonstrates cytomegaly (enlarged endothelial/stromal cells) with dense basophilic intranuclear ("owl's eye") and cytoplasmic inclusions.
  - HSV Pathology: Early stage shows multiple, small (1 to 3 mm), discrete, superficial, circumscribed "volcano" ulcers with raised, erythematous margins and central pale/yellow bases in the distal esophagus; ulcers may coalesce into diffuse erosive esophagitis with pseudomembranes. Histology demonstrates ballooning degeneration of squamous cells, multinucleated giant cells, and Cowdry A intranuclear inclusions.
  - Aphthous Ulcer Pathology: Giant, deep, painful, non-infectious mucosal ulcers in HIV/AIDS resulting from local immune dysregulation or direct HIV-mediated mucosal injury.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Classic Symptoms (Patient History):**
  - Dominant Symptoms: Odynophagia (severe pain on swallowing) and Dysphagia (difficulty swallowing or a sensation of food sticking/obstruction in the substernal, epigastric, or throat region). Liquids are typically better tolerated than solid foods.
  - Pain Quality & Distribution: Severe substernal or retrosternal burning or knife-like pain, exacerbated by ingestion of food or acidic liquids and by eructation; spontaneous retrosternal pain unrelated to swallowing may also occur.
  - Associated Symptoms: Anorexia, significant weight loss, dehydration (secondary to severe odynophagia limiting oral fluid intake), nausea, vomiting, epigastric distress, fever (frequently present in HSV, CMV, acute HIV, or necrotizing infection), and GI bleeding / hematemesis.
- **Physical Examination Findings (Signs):**
  - General & Nutritional Signs: Dehydration, fever, or weight loss/cachexia.
  - Oropharyngeal Findings: Oropharyngeal candidiasis (oral thrush—white curd-like plaques on buccal mucosa, palate, or tongue; present in >70% of HIV patients with Candida esophagitis); oral/labial herpetic vesicles or ulcers (present in only 19% to 38% of HSV esophagitis cases); and aphthous stomatitis.
  - Systemic Signs: Lymphadenopathy, cutaneous zoster lesions, Kaposi sarcoma, or signs of advanced HIV infection / immunosuppression.
- **Pathognomonic Features:**
  - Yellow-White Adherent Mucosal Plaques on Endoscopy: Pathognomonic gross visual appearance for Candida esophagitis.
  - Large Punched-Out Shallow Ulcers with Submucosal Intranuclear Inclusions: Endoscopic and histologic findings pathognomonic for Cytomegalovirus (CMV) esophagitis.
  - Multiple Small Volcanolike Ulcers with Cowdry A Inclusions: Endoscopic and histologic findings pathognomonic for Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV) esophagitis.

## Diagnostics & Workup
- **Initial Laboratory Tests (CBC, Inflammatory markers):**
  - Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Immune Status: Leukopenia, severe neutropenia (ANC <500/mm3 in post-chemotherapy/HSCT), lymphopenia, and CD4+ T-lymphocyte count in HIV/AIDS (CD4 <200 cells/mm3 predisposes to Candida; CD4 <50 cells/mm3 strongly predisposes to CMV, HSV, and aphthous ulcers).
  - Inflammatory Markers: Mildly elevated C-reactive protein (CRP) or Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate (ESR), non-specific.
- **Imaging Modalities:**
  - Barium Contrast Esophagography: Single or double-contrast esophagogram reveals shaggy, irregular mucosal contours with discrete or confluent filling defects/plaques (Candida esophagitis) or discrete ulcer craters. Limited diagnostic sensitivity/specificity; cannot differentiate viral etiologies or detect coinfections. Reserved for settings where endoscopy is unavailable.
  - Computed Tomography (CT) of Chest: Demonstrates focal or diffuse circumferential esophageal wall thickening (>5 mm), luminal narrowing, or periesophageal fat stranding; excludes periesophageal abscess, mediastinitis, or esophageal perforation.
- **Microbiological Diagnostics (Cultures, PCR, Stains):**
  - Upper Endoscopy (Esophagogastroduodenoscopy / EGD) with Directed Brushings & Biopsies (Primary Diagnostic Tool):
    - Candida Esophagitis: Cytology brushings of exudative lesions stained with Calcofluor White, silver (GMS), PAS, or Gram stain demonstrate pseudohyphae and budding yeast cells invading superficial epithelium. Biopsy shows mucosal pseudohyphae. Fungal culture isolates Candida species and determines azole susceptibility.
    - CMV Esophagitis: Endoscopic mucosal and SUBMUCOSAL biopsies MUST be taken specifically from the BASE and CENTER of the ulcer. Histology (H&E stain) demonstrates enlarged endothelial cells or fibroblasts containing large, dense basophilic intranuclear ("owl's eye") inclusions and cytoplasmic inclusions. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) with monoclonal antibodies is highly specific. Viral culture or quantitative PCR on biopsy tissue confirms diagnosis.
    - HSV Esophagitis: Biopsies MUST be taken specifically from the MARGINS and EDGES of the ulcer. Histology demonstrates ballooning degeneration of epithelial cells, acantholysis, multinucleated giant cells, and Cowdry A intranuclear inclusions. Quantitative real-time PCR for HSV-1 DNA on biopsy specimens exhibits 95% sensitivity and 73% specificity. Viral culture isolates HSV-1.
- **Gold Standard Diagnostic Test:** Upper Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (Esophagogastroduodenoscopy / EGD) with direct mucosal visualization, tissue brushings, and histopathologic/immunohistochemical examination (and PCR/culture) of esophageal biopsy specimens represents the gold standard diagnostic test.

## Management & Empiric Therapy
- **Initial Resuscitation & Supportive Care:**
  - Fluid, Nutritional & Analgesic Support: Intravenous fluid resuscitation for severe dehydration; enteral feeding via fine-bore tube or total parenteral nutrition (TPN) if severe odynophagia precludes oral intake. Pain control with topical viscous lidocaine or systemic analgesics.
  - Empiric Antifungal Trial Protocol in HIV/AIDS: In HIV-infected patients presenting with classic esophageal symptoms (odynophagia/dysphagia) AND concurrent oropharyngeal candidiasis (oral thrush), an empiric trial of oral Fluconazole (200-400 mg daily) WITHOUT immediate endoscopy is recommended (achieves >80% symptomatic response within 7 to 14 days). If symptoms fail to improve after 7 to 14 days of empiric azole therapy, EGD is mandatory to evaluate for azole-resistant Candida, CMV, HSV, or aphthous ulcers.
- **First-Line Empiric Antimicrobial Regimens:**
  - Candida Esophagitis:
    - First-Line Oral Regimen: Fluconazole 200 to 400 mg PO daily (loading dose 400 mg, then 200-400 mg daily) for 14 to 21 days.
    - First-Line Parenteral Regimen (unable to tolerate PO): Intravenous Fluconazole 400 mg IV daily OR an Echinocandin (Caspofungin 70 mg IV load then 50 mg IV daily; Micafungin 150 mg IV daily; Anidulafungin 200 mg IV load then 100 mg IV daily) for 14 to 21 days.
  - Cytomegalovirus (CMV) Esophagitis:
    - First-Line Regimen: Intravenous Ganciclovir 5 mg/kg IV q12h for 14 to 21 days. Step-down to Oral Valganciclovir 900 mg PO q12h x 14 to 21 days total once oral therapy is tolerated.
  - Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV) Esophagitis:
    - First-Line Regimen: Intravenous Acyclovir 5 mg/kg IV q8h for 7 to 14 days (in severe cases or unable to swallow) OR Oral Acyclovir 400 mg PO 5 times daily for 14 to 21 days OR Oral Valacyclovir 1 g PO tid for 14 to 21 days OR Oral Famciclovir 500 mg PO bid for 14 to 21 days.
- **Alternative Regimens:**
  - Azole-Refractory / Resistant Candida Esophagitis:
    - Itraconazole oral solution 200 mg PO daily x 14-21 days OR Voriconazole 200 mg PO bid x 14-21 days OR Posaconazole oral suspension 400 mg PO bid x 14-21 days OR Echinocandin (Caspofungin, Micafungin, Anidulafungin) IV x 14-21 days OR Amphotericin B deoxycholate 0.3 to 0.7 mg/kg/day IV (or lipid formulation Amphotericin B 3-5 mg/kg/day IV) x 7 to 14 days.
  - Ganciclovir-Resistant / Refractory CMV Esophagitis:
    - Intravenous Foscarnet 90 mg/kg IV q12h (or 60 mg/kg IV q8h) for 14 to 21 days.
  - Acyclovir-Resistant HSV Esophagitis:
    - Intravenous Foscarnet 90 mg/kg IV q12h (or 40-60 mg/kg IV q8h) for 7 to 14 days.
  - Idiopathic Aphthous Ulceration (in AIDS):
    - Oral Corticosteroids (Prednisone 40 to 60 mg PO daily tapered over 4 weeks) OR Oral Thalidomide 200 mg PO daily x 4 weeks.
  - Maintenance Suppressive Therapy (in AIDS with recurrent relapses):
    - Candida: Fluconazole 100 to 200 mg PO 3 times weekly.
    - CMV: Ganciclovir 5 mg/kg/day IV 5-7 days/week or Valganciclovir 900 mg PO daily.
    - HSV: Acyclovir 400 mg PO bid or Valacyclovir 500 mg PO daily.
- **Surgical or Procedural Interventions :**
  - Endoscopic Dilation: Controlled balloon dilation or wire-guided bougienage for persistent post-inflammatory esophageal strictures or stenosis.
  - Emergency Surgical Resection / Esophagectomy: Indicated for full-thickness esophageal perforation, transmural necrotizing esophagitis, uncontrollable massive GI hemorrhage, or esophagobronchial/esophagopleural fistulas.

## Complications & Sequelae
- **Acute Complications:**
  - Severe dehydration, malnutrition, and rapid weight loss due to intractable odynophagia.
  - Gastrointestinal Hemorrhage (hematemesis or melena, occurring in 5% to 10% of ulcerative esophagitis cases).
  - Full-Thickness Esophageal Perforation (rare, life-threatening complication of necrotizing candidal, HSV, or corrosive esophagitis, resulting in acute mediastinitis, subcutaneous emphysema, pneumomediastinum, and sepsis).
  - Esophagobronchial or Esophagopleural Fistula formation (complication of esophageal tuberculosis, histoplasmosis, MAC, or necrotizing malignancy).
  - Superinfections / Coinfections (e.g., Candida superinfection overlying CMV or HSV esophageal ulcers, occurring in up to 73% of CMV cases).
- **Long-term Sequelae:**
  - Esophageal Stricture & Stenosis (fibrotic luminal narrowing secondary to healing of severe ulcerative or necrotizing esophagitis, requiring repeat endoscopic dilations).
  - Esophageal Dysmotility & Secondary Achalasia-like syndromes.
  - Recurrent / Relapsing Esophagitis (common in advanced HIV/AIDS or persistent immunosuppression).

## Trivia & Edge Cases
- **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
  - Volcano Ulcers: Endoscopic appearance of small, discrete HSV ulcers with raised, erythematous margins surrounding a central crater.
  - Punched-Out Ulcers: Endoscopic appearance of large (>10 cm2), shallow, geographic CMV ulcers with distinct, non-elevated margins.
  - Shaggy Esophagus: Classic radiologic contrast esophagogram appearance in severe, confluent Candida esophagitis.
  - Owl's Eye Inclusions: Basophilic intranuclear inclusion bodies in enlarged endothelial/stromal cells pathognomonic for CMV.
  - Cowdry A Inclusion Bodies: Eosinophilic intranuclear inclusions in multinucleated epithelial giant cells characteristic of HSV.
  - Pill Esophagitis: Localized mucosal ulceration caused by lodged medication tablets (doxycycline, tetracycline, alendronate, NSAIDs) taken without adequate water or before recumbency.
  - Aphthous Ulcers: Giant, painful, non-infectious esophageal ulcers occurring in advanced HIV/AIDS, responding to steroids or thalidomide.
- **Exceptions to the Rule:**
  - Oral Thrush Predictability Exception: While oral thrush in an HIV patient with esophageal symptoms is >70% predictive of Candida esophagitis, up to 40% of AIDS patients with proven Candida esophagitis have NO oral thrush or esophageal symptoms!
  - Biopsy Location Rule (CMV vs. HSV): CMV infects endothelial cells and stromal fibroblasts in the SUBMUCOSA, so biopsies MUST be taken from the BASE/CENTER of the ulcer; HSV infects squamous EPITHELIAL cells, so biopsies MUST be taken from the MARGINS/EDGES of the ulcer!
  - Empiric Antiviral Avoidance: While empiric antifungal therapy (fluconazole) is standard for esophageal symptoms in HIV with thrush, empiric ANTIVIRAL therapy for CMV or HSV is STRICTLY DISCOURAGED; viral esophagitis MUST be confirmed by endoscopy and biopsy before starting toxic antivirals!
  - Pill Esophagitis Antibiotic Dominance: Antibiotics and antivirals (doxycycline, tetracycline, clindamycin, zidovudine) account for over 50% of all cases of medication-induced / pill esophagitis!
  - Immunocompetent HSV Esophagitis: HSV esophagitis can occasionally occur in young, healthy, immunocompetent individuals (predominantly males or patients with underlying eosinophilic esophagitis), presenting as acute self-limited fever, chest pain, and severe odynophagia!
