**CD314 (NKG2D)** CD314, universally referred to as **NKG2D**, is a potent activating receptor that plays a fundamental role in the immune system's ability to detect and destroy stressed, infected, or malignant cells.
### Structure and Classification
- **Molecular Weight and Family:** CD314 is a 42 kD protein.
- Unlike CD244, which belongs to the Ig superfamily, NKG2D belongs to the **C-type lectin** superfamily of proteins.
### Cellular Expression Profile
NKG2D is expressed on a variety of effector cells responsible for directly killing target cells. Its expression profile includes:
- [[NK Cell|Natural Killer (NK) cells]]
- Activated CD8+ [[T-Cell|T Cells]] (Cytotoxic T Lymphocytes)
- NKT (Natural Killer T) cells
- Some myeloid cells
### Ligand Specificity (Detecting Cellular Stress) 
NKG2D functions as a major sensor for cellular stress by recognizing specific ligands that are usually absent on healthy cells but heavily upregulated during viral infection or tumor transformation. It specifically binds to:
- **[[MHC-1|MHC Class I molecules]]**.
- **Nonclassical MHC Class I–like molecules:** Most notably, it binds to **MIC-A** and **MIC-B** (MHC class I chain-related proteins A and B), as well as **Rae1** and **ULBP4**.
### Intracellular Signaling Mechanism 
The biochemical mechanism by which NKG2D activates cells is unique compared to receptors with intrinsic signaling tails:
- **Lack of Intrinsic Motifs:** The recognition protein of the NKG2D activating receptor completely lacks signaling motifs (such as ITAMs) within its own cytoplasmic tail.
- **Adaptor Association:** To achieve cellular activation, NKG2D must noncovalently associate with separate, distinct signaling transmembrane proteins. It forms a complex with these **ITAM-containing signaling proteins**.
- **Kinase Activation:** When NKG2D binds to its ligands (like MIC-A or MIC-B) on a target cell, the ITAMs on the associated signaling chains become phosphorylated by nonreceptor tyrosine kinases, delivering a powerful activating signal into the NK cell or CD8+ [[T-Cell]], directing it to eliminate the target.
