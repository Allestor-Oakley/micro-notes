**CD94** CD94 is a 43 kD integral membrane protein that serves as a critical regulatory receptor, primarily functioning to control the activation and cytolytic activity of Natural Killer (NK) cells.

**1. Structure and Classification**
- **Family:** CD94 belongs to the **C-type lectin** superfamily of proteins.
- **Heterodimer Formation:** An essential biochemical feature of CD94 is that it does not typically function as an isolated monomer. Instead, on the surface of NK cells, CD94 covalently assembles with other specific C-type lectin molecules—most notably members of the **NKG2 family**—to form functional heterodimeric receptor complexes.

**2. Cellular Expression Profile**
- CD94 is prominently expressed on the surface of **[[NK Cell|Natural Killer (NK) cells]]**.
- It is also expressed on a specific subset of **CD8+ [[T-Cell|T cells]]**.

**3. Ligand Specificity**
- The primary ligand for the CD94/NKG2 receptor complex is **[[MHC-1|HLA-E]]**, which is a specialized, non-classical Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) class I molecule. By monitoring [[MHC-1|HLA-E]] expression on the surface of other cells, NK cells can survey the body for normal, healthy MHC class I expression (which is often downregulated by viral infections or malignant transformations).

**4. Dual Functionality: Inhibitory vs. Activating Complexes** Because CD94 can pair with different members of the NKG2 family, the resulting CD94/NKG2 complex can function as either an inhibitory or an activating receptor, depending entirely on its specific binding partner:
- **The Inhibitory Receptor (CD94/NKG2A):**
    - When CD94 pairs with **NKG2A (also known as CD159a)**, it forms an inhibitory receptor complex.
    - NKG2A is a 43 kD C-type lectin that contains an **Immunoreceptor Tyrosine-based Inhibition Motif (ITIM)** within its cytoplasmic tail.
    - When this CD94/NKG2A heterodimer recognizes and binds to HLA-E on a target cell, the ITIM transduces inhibitory signals that actively suppress NK cell activation and prevent the NK cell from killing the target.
- **The Activating Receptor (CD94/NKG2C):**
    - Alternatively, CD94 can pair with **NKG2C (also known as CD159c)**.
    - NKG2C is a 40 kD C-type lectin. Unlike NKG2A, it does not possess an inhibitory tail.
    - When the CD94/NKG2C heterodimer binds to the appropriate HLA class I molecules, it functions as an activating receptor, delivering signals that stimulate the NK cell to mount an immune response against the encountered cell.
