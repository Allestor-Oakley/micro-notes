ICAMs, VCAM , and PECAM all belong to the **Immunoglobulin (Ig) superfamily** of cellular adhesion molecules. The extracellular portions of these proteins are composed of repeating globular structures known as Ig domains.

While the selectins (CD62 family) mediate the initial, weak "rolling" of leukocytes, the Ig superfamily adhesion molecules serve as the critical endothelial ligands for leukocyte integrins, orchestrating the final, high-affinity "firm adhesion" and "transmigration" steps of leukocyte recruitment into tissues.

## The ICAM Family (Intercellular Adhesion Molecules)
The ICAM family proteins are the primary binding partners for the β2 integrin family (such as LFA-1 and MAC-1).
### ICAM-1 (CD54):
- **Structure and Expression:** ICAM-1 is a 75–114 kD integral membrane glycoprotein. It is widely expressed on endothelial cells, T cells, B cells, monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells (DCs), fibroblasts, and most epithelial cells. Crucially, on endothelial cells, it is present at low levels but is heavily upregulated (inducible) in response to inflammatory cytokines like Tumor Necrosis Factor (TNF) and Interleukin-1 (IL-1).
- **Ligands:** It is the primary ligand for the integrins **LFA-1** (CD11aCD18) and **MAC-1** (CD11bCD18).
- **Physiological Roles:** When rolling leukocytes are activated by chemokines, their LFA-1 and MAC-1 integrins shift to a high-affinity state and bind firmly to ICAM-1 on the activated endothelium. This causes stable integrin-mediated arrest, halting the rolling process so the cell can prepare to enter the tissue. Beyond leukocyte recruitment, ICAM-1 expressed on antigen-presenting cells (APCs) binds to LFA-1 on T cells, facilitating the stable physical contact (the immune synapse) required for T-cell activation. Interestingly, ICAM-1 also serves as the cellular entry receptor for the rhinovirus.
- **ICAM-2 (CD102):**
    - **Structure and Expression:** ICAM-2 is a 55–65 kD protein expressed on endothelial cells, lymphocytes, monocytes, and platelets.
    - **Ligands and Roles:** Like ICAM-1, it serves as a specific ligand for the LFA-1 and MAC-1 integrins to mediate cell-to-cell adhesion and leukocyte-endothelial interactions.
- **ICAM-3 (CD50):**
    - ICAM-3 is expressed on lymphocytes and serves as an additional ligand for the LFA-1 integrin.
### VCAM-1 (Vascular Cell Adhesion Molecule 1 / CD106)
- **Structure and Expression:** VCAM-1 is a 100–110 kD Ig superfamily protein. It is expressed on cytokine-activated endothelial cells, macrophages, follicular dendritic cells (FDCs), and bone marrow stromal cells. Similar to ICAM-1, its expression on vascular endothelium is significantly upregulated by inflammatory cytokines during an active immune response.
- **Ligands:** VCAM-1 is the specific ligand for the integrin **VLA-4** (Very Late Antigen 4, also known as α4β1 or CD49dCD29).
- **Physiological Roles:**
    - **Inflammatory Recruitment:** VCAM-1 mediates the firm adhesion of VLA-4-expressing leukocytes (which include monocytes, eosinophils, and naive/effector/memory T cells) to the endothelium at sites of infection or tissue injury, leading to their subsequent transmigration into the tissues. It plays a particularly prominent role in recruiting leukocytes to sites of chronic inflammation.
    - **Plasma Cell Homing:** VCAM-1 plays a vital role in long-term humoral immunity. After B cells activate and differentiate into IgG-secreting plasmablasts in the secondary lymphoid organs, these cells enter the blood and must migrate to the bone marrow to become long-lived plasma cells. The sinusoidal endothelial cells of the bone marrow express VCAM-1 (and the chemokine CXCL12), which tightly binds to the VLA-4 integrins (and CXCR4 receptors) on the plasma cells, drawing them out of the blood and into their permanent survival niches.
### PECAM-1 (Platelet/Endothelial Cell Adhesion Molecule 1 / CD31)
- **Structure and Expression:** PECAM-1, almost universally referred to by its cluster of differentiation marker **CD31**, is a 130–140 kD Ig superfamily molecule. It is uniquely expressed on both the surface of various circulating leukocytes (including platelets, monocytes, granulocytes, and B cells) and on endothelial cells.
- **Physiological Role (Transmigration):** While ICAMs and VCAM-1 are responsible for stopping the leukocyte on the vessel wall, CD31 orchestrates the final stage of recruitment: **paracellular transmigration or diapedesis**. After a leukocyte has firmly arrested, it must squeeze through the tight junctions between adjacent endothelial cells to reach the extravascular tissue. CD31 molecules expressed on the adhering leukocyte homophilically interact with CD31 molecules concentrated at the endothelial cell junctions. This specific interaction physically helps pull the leukocyte through the gap in the endothelial barrier and into the underlying tissue stroma.
