The integrin CD molecules, specifically the CD11 and CD18 families, are critical cell surface proteins that orchestrate leukocyte adhesion to the endothelium, cell-to-cell interactions, and the phagocytosis of opsonized microbes. These molecules constitute the **β2 integrin family**, which is indispensable for both innate and adaptive immune responses.

### Overview of β2 Integrins (CD11 and CD18)
Integrins are heterodimeric integral membrane proteins that integrate signals triggered by extracellular ligands with cytoskeleton-dependent motility, shape change, and phagocytic responses.
- **Structural Composition:** Every β2 integrin molecule consists of two noncovalently linked polypeptide chains: a common **β2 chain (CD18)** and one of three distinct **α chains (CD11a, CD11b, or CD11c)**.
- **CD18 (The β2 Chain):** CD18 is a 95 kD protein that acts as the universal binding partner for the CD11 α chains.
- **Domain Function:** The extracellular globular heads of both the α and β chains contribute to ligand binding, while their cytoplasmic domains interact with cytoskeletal components such as vinculin, talin, actin, and α-actinin.

### Mechanism of Action: Inside-Out Signaling
A defining physiological feature of these integrins is that they are normally maintained in a resting, low-affinity state on the surface of circulating leukocytes, which prevents them from randomly adhering to the walls of blood vessels.
- **Chemokine and Antigen Activation:** When a leukocyte rolls along the endothelium and encounters chemokines, or when a T cell recognizes its specific antigen, intracellular signaling cascades are triggered.
- **Conformational Shift:** These signals lead to the association of small GTPase proteins and cytoskeleton-interacting proteins with the cytoplasmic tails of the integrins. This interaction forces the extracellular stalks of the integrins to unbend and extend, thrusting the globular ligand-binding heads away from the plasma membrane and shifting the molecule into a high-affinity state.
- **Avidity Enhancement:** Chemokines also induce the integrins to cluster together on the cell surface, drastically increasing the overall strength (avidity) of the leukocyte's attachment to the endothelium.

### The Three Major β2 Integrin Complexes
The specific pairing of CD18 with a unique CD11 α chain generates three distinct integrins with specialized cellular distributions and functions.

## CD11a/CD18 (LFA-1 / αLβ2)
- **Structure:** Formed by the noncovalent linkage of CD18 with the 180 kD CD11a chain.
- **Expression:** Widely expressed on almost all leukocytes, including naive, effector, and memory T cells, naive B cells, monocytes, and neutrophils.
- **Ligands:** LFA-1 specifically binds to members of the immunoglobulin (Ig) superfamily, most notably **ICAM-1 (CD54)**, as well as ICAM-2 (CD102) and ICAM-3 (CD50). ICAM-1 is heavily upregulated on endothelial cells in response to inflammatory cytokines like TNF and IL-1.
- **Functions:** LFA-1 is primarily responsible for mediating the stable arrest and firm adhesion of leukocytes to cytokine-activated endothelium, halting selectin-mediated rolling so the cell can transmigrate into tissues. It is also essential for naive T-cell homing through High Endothelial Venules (HEVs) into lymph nodes, and it facilitates the critical physical interaction between T cells and antigen-presenting cells (APCs).

## CD11b/CD18 (MAC-1 / CR3 / αMβ2)
- **Structure:** Formed by the pairing of CD18 with the 165 kD CD11b chain.
- **Expression:** Highly expressed on cells of the innate immune system, including neutrophils, monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells (DCs), and Natural Killer (NK) cells.
- **Ligands:** MAC-1 functions dually as an adhesion molecule by binding to **ICAM-1 (CD54)** and as the **Type 3 Complement Receptor ([[Complement Receptors#Type 3 Complement Receptor (CR3 / MAC-1 / CD11bCD18)|CR3]])** by binding to **iC3b** (the inactive cleavage fragment of complement component C3b).
- **Functions:** By binding to ICAM-1, it promotes stable leukocyte attachment to the endothelium. By binding to iC3b-coated particles, it mediates the highly efficient phagocytosis of opsonized microbes. It may also directly recognize certain unknown microbial molecular patterns to mediate phagocytosis independent of complement.

## CD11c/CD18 (p150,95 / CR4 / αXβ2)
- **Structure:** Formed by the pairing of CD18 with the 145 kD CD11c chain.
- **Expression:** While present on monocytes, macrophages, granulocytes, and NK cells, CD11c is most famously recognized as the exceptionally abundant, defining surface marker for **Dendritic Cells (DCs)**.
- **Ligands & Functions:** Like MAC-1, it functions as the **Type 4 Complement Receptor ([[Complement Receptors#Type 4 Complement Receptor (CR4 / p150,95 / CD11cCD18)|CR4]])**, binding specifically to the iC3b complement fragment. It aids in the phagocytosis of opsonized microbes and specifically helps DCs ingest apoptotic cells.

### Clinical Significance: Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency Type 1 (LAD-1)
The critical nature of the CD11/CD18 integrin family is underscored by a rare autosomal recessive immunodeficiency known as Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency Type 1 (LAD-1).

- **Pathology:** LAD-1 is caused by a genetic mutation in the _CD18_ gene, resulting in the absent or severely reduced expression of all three β2 integrins (LFA-1, MAC-1, and p150,95) on the leukocyte surface.
- **Clinical Presentation:** Because their leukocytes lack LFA-1 and MAC-1, patients suffer from a profound failure of leukocyte (particularly neutrophil) recruitment to sites of infection and tissue injury. This manifests clinically as recurrent, life-threatening bacterial and fungal infections starting early in life, impaired wound healing, and a characteristic inability to form pus (since pus is largely composed of accumulated dead neutrophils). Furthermore, adherence-dependent lymphocyte functions, such as T-cell and NK-cell cytotoxicity, are severely defective.
