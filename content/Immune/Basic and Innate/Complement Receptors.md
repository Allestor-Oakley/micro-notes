## Type 1 Complement Receptor (CR1 / CD35)
CR1 is the master receptor for promoting the clearance of immune complexes and facilitating the phagocytosis of opsonized targets.
- **Structure:** CR1 is a 160–250 kD polymorphic protein (with four products of polymorphic alleles) that belongs to the regulators of complement activation (RCA) family. Its extracellular structure is characterized by multiple complement control protein repeats (CCPRs).
- **Ligand Specificity:** It functions as a high-affinity receptor that preferentially binds the complement fragments **C3b and C4b** (with an affinity ranking of C3b > C4b > iC3b).
- **Cellular Expression:** CR1 is broadly expressed on bone marrow–derived cells, including erythrocytes, mononuclear phagocytes, neutrophils, eosinophils, B and T lymphocytes, as well as on follicular dendritic cells (FDCs) in secondary lymphoid organs.
- **Physiology:**
    - _Phagocytosis:_ Phagocytes utilize CR1 to bind and internalize microbes or particles opsonized with C3b and C4b. However, CR1 alone is somewhat inefficient at inducing phagocytosis; its capacity to trigger engulfment and activate microbicidal mechanisms is drastically enhanced if the target is simultaneously coated with IgG (which engages Fcγ receptors) or if the macrophage has been activated by the cytokine IFN-γ.
    - _Immune Complex Clearance:_ On the surface of erythrocytes, CR1 binds to circulating immune complexes that have become coated with C3b and C4b. The erythrocytes then transport these complexes through the circulation to the liver and spleen. In these organs, tissue phagocytes strip the immune complexes from the erythrocyte surface and degrade them, allowing the undamaged erythrocytes to return to the systemic circulation.
    - _Complement Regulation:_ CR1 acts as a critical regulator of the complement cascade by promoting the dissociation of C3 convertases. Furthermore, when C3b or C4b is deposited on host cell surfaces, CR1 acts as a necessary membrane-bound cofactor for the plasma serine protease Factor I, which proteolytically cleaves C3b and C4b into inactive fragments (such as iC3b and C3f), thereby halting further complement activation on normal host cells.
- **Pathophysiology:** Deficiencies in the complement proteins that generate C3b and C4b (such as C1q, C2, and C4) lead to a failure in the CR1-mediated clearance of immune complexes. Consequently, circulating immune complexes deposit in blood vessel walls and tissues, where they aberrantly activate leukocytes via Fc receptors, leading to severe local inflammation and autoimmune tissue damage, which is a primary pathogenic mechanism in Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE).

## Type 2 Complement Receptor (CR2 / CD21)
CR2 is the fundamental link between the innate complement system and the adaptive humoral immune response.
- **Structure:** CR2 is a 145 kD protein that also belongs to the RCA family and features multiple CCPRs.
- **Ligand Specificity:** It specifically binds to the terminal proteolytic degradation products of C3b, namely **C3d, C3dg, and iC3b** (with an affinity ranking of C3d, C3dg > iC3b).
- **Cellular Expression:** It is expressed prominently on mature B lymphocytes, FDCs, and nasopharyngeal epithelial cells.
- **Physiology:**
    - _B-Cell Coreceptor Complex:_ On the surface of mature B cells, CR2 is noncovalently associated with two other membrane proteins, **CD19 and CD81 (TAPA-1)**, to form the B-cell coreceptor complex. When a microbial antigen activates the complement system, C3d becomes covalently attached to the antigen. The B cell simultaneously binds the antigen via its membrane immunoglobulin (BCR) and the attached C3d via CR2. This coligation brings the CD19 molecule into close proximity with BCR-associated SRC-family kinases (like LYN). The cytoplasmic tail of CD19 is rapidly tyrosine-phosphorylated, which recruits and activates PI3-kinase. PI3-kinase generates PIP3 in the membrane, which subsequently recruits and activates downstream signaling enzymes, including BTK and PLCγ2. This synergistic signaling lowers the threshold for B-cell activation, massively enhancing the cellular response compared to antigen alone.
    - _Antigen Trapping:_ Within the germinal centers of lymphoid organs, FDCs utilize CR2 to trap and retain iC3b-, C3d-, and C3dg-coated antigen-antibody immune complexes. This prolonged display of antigen is absolutely critical for the selection of high-affinity B cells during affinity maturation.
- **Pathophysiology:** CR2 serves as the primary cellular entry receptor for the **Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)**, allowing the virus to specifically infect B cells and nasopharyngeal epithelium. Experimentally, knockout mice engineered to lack the CR2 protein exhibit severe immunologic deficits, specifically manifesting as profound impairments in both antibody production and the formation of germinal centers.

## Type 3 Complement Receptor (CR3 / MAC-1 / CD11bCD18)
CR3 functions dually as a highly efficient phagocytic receptor and a critical leukocyte adhesion molecule.
- **Structure:** CR3 is a member of the **β2 integrin family**. It is a heterodimer consisting of a 165-kD α chain (CD11b) noncovalently linked to a shared 95-kD β2 chain (CD18).
- **Ligand Specificity:** It specifically binds the **iC3b** complement fragment. In its role as an adhesion molecule, it binds to **ICAM-1 (CD54)**. It can also directly bind to certain, as-yet uncharacterized, microbial molecules.
- **Cellular Expression:** It is heavily expressed on innate immune cells, including neutrophils, mononuclear phagocytes (monocytes/macrophages), dendritic cells, mast cells, and NK cells.
- **Physiology:**
    - _Phagocytosis:_ Monocytes and neutrophils use CR3 to facilitate the highly efficient phagocytosis of microbes that have been opsonized with the iC3b fragment.
    - _Leukocyte Adhesion:_ Independent of complement activation, CR3 binds to ICAM-1 expressed on cytokine-activated endothelial cells. This binding mediates the stable, firm attachment of circulating leukocytes to the endothelium, followed by paracellular transmigration into inflamed or infected extravascular tissues.

## Type 4 Complement Receptor (CR4 / p150,95 / CD11cCD18)
- **Structure:** CR4 is another member of the β2 integrin family, pairing a 145–150-kD α chain (CD11c) with the identical 95-kD β2 chain (CD18) used by CR3.
- **Ligand Specificity:** Like CR3, it is a specific receptor for the **iC3b** fragment.
- **Cellular Expression:** While expressed on monocytes, macrophages, neutrophils, and NK cells, CD11c is most notably and abundantly expressed on **dendritic cells (DCs)**, serving as a primary phenotypic surface marker used to identify DC populations.
- **Physiology:** The functions of CR4 are highly homologous to those of CR3, playing a role in both the phagocytosis of iC3b-opsonized particles and cellular adhesion.

## Complement Receptor of the Immunoglobulin Family (CRIg)
- **Structure:** Unlike the integrins or RCA family members, CRIg is an integral membrane protein characterized by an extracellular region composed of immunoglobulin (Ig) domains.
- **Ligand Specificity:** It specifically binds the complement fragments **C3b and iC3b**.
- **Cellular Expression:** Its expression is uniquely restricted to **Kupffer cells**, which are the specialized tissue-resident macrophages located within the liver sinusoids.
- **Physiology:** CRIg is specifically dedicated to the rapid capture and clearance of opsonized bacteria and other blood-borne pathogens as they filter through the hepatic circulation.

## Anaphylatoxin Receptors (C5a, C3a, and C4a Receptors)
The anaphylatoxin receptors are specialized cell-surface molecules that bind to the small, soluble proteolytic complement fragments C5a, C3a, and C4a, which are released during the activation of the complement cascade. They are fundamentally responsible for mediating the powerful proinflammatory effects of these complement by-products, triggering acute inflammation and reactions characteristic of anaphylaxis (hence the term "anaphylatoxins").
### Structure and Classification
- **Receptor Family:** Both the C5a receptor and the C3a receptor belong to the **G protein–coupled receptor (GPCR) superfamily**, characterized by a 7-membrane-spanning architecture. Like other chemoattractant receptors, they initiate intracellular signaling responses through associated trimeric G proteins.
- **CD88 (The C5a Receptor):** The C5a receptor, specifically designated as **C5aR1 or CD88**, is the most thoroughly characterized of these receptors. It is a 43 kD transmembrane protein.
### Ligand Specificity and Potency
- These receptors bind their respective complement cleavage fragments (C5a, C3a, and C4a).
- In terms of potency for initiating inflammatory responses (such as mast cell degranulation), **C5a is the most potent mediator**. C3a is approximately 20-fold less potent than C5a, and C4a is the weakest, being approximately 2500-fold less potent than C5a.
### Cellular Expression Profile
The expression of anaphylatoxin receptors, particularly C5aR1 (CD88), is extraordinarily broad, spanning both immune and non-immune tissue cells. CD88 is expressed on:
- Granulocytes (neutrophils, eosinophils, and basophils)
- Mononuclear phagocytes (monocytes and macrophages)
- Mast cells
- Dendritic cells (DCs)
- Endothelial cells and epithelial cells
- Smooth muscle cells
- Astrocytes (in the central nervous system)
### Physiological Roles and Effector Functions
By engaging these GPCRs, the anaphylatoxins act as potent chemoattractants and cell activators, establishing a severe inflammatory environment around microbes to help eliminate pathogens.
- **Mast Cell Degranulation:** C5a and C3a bind to their receptors on mast cells and directly trigger IgE-independent degranulation. This causes the rapid release of stored vasoactive mediators, such as histamine, resulting in increased vascular permeability and vasodilation. During acute bacterial infections, alternative pathway complement activation leads to C5a release, directly triggering mast cell degranulation as a critical early innate immune defense.
- **Phagocyte Recruitment and Activation:** The anaphylatoxin receptors collaborate directly with Toll-like receptors (TLRs) in the activation of sentinel cells and phagocytes. On neutrophils, the binding of C5a to CD88 stimulates cell motility (chemotaxis) and firm adhesion to endothelial cells. At high doses, C5a signaling stimulates the neutrophil respiratory burst, leading to the massive production of microbicidal reactive oxygen species (ROS).
- **Vascular Endothelial Effects:** C5a can act directly on vascular endothelial cells via its receptor to induce increased vascular permeability. It also upregulates the expression of P-selectin on the endothelial surface, which further promotes the binding and rolling of circulating neutrophils, recruiting them into the tissue.
### Regulation and Evasion
Because the inflammatory effects of anaphylatoxin receptors are so potent, they must be tightly regulated to prevent host tissue damage.
- **Host Regulation (Carboxypeptidases):** The inflammation induced by C3a and C5a is strictly regulated by plasma enzymes called carboxypeptidases. These enzymes rapidly cleave the C-terminal arginine residues from the C3a and C5a peptides. The resulting truncated fragments, known as **C3a des-Arg** and **C5a des-Arg**, retain only about 10% of the biologic activity of the native, intact proteins, effectively dampening receptor activation.
- **Microbial Evasion:** Certain pathogens have evolved mechanisms to block anaphylatoxin receptor signaling. For example, _Staphylococcus aureus_ synthesizes a specific protein known as "chemokine inhibitory protein of staphylococci," which acts as a direct antagonist of the C5a anaphylatoxin, preventing it from binding to its receptor and inhibiting complement-mediated inflammation.
### Clinical and Therapeutic Significance
- Because C5a drives powerful tissue inflammation, the C5aR1 (CD88) receptor is a major target for immunosuppressive therapy. A small molecule competitive inhibitor that binds specifically to the C5aR1 receptor has been developed to prevent C5a-driven inflammation. This inhibitor is clinically approved for the treatment of **ANCA (antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody)-associated vasculitis**, a severe autoimmune disease.
