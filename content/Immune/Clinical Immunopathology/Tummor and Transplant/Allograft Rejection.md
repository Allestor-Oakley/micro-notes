### Allograft Rejection (Hyperacute, Acute, and Chronic)

#### Pathogenesis & Molecular Defect
*   **Underlying Immune Defect / Mutation:**
    *   Allograft rejection is not caused by an intrinsic genetic defect or mutation in the recipient. Instead, it is an acquired, highly robust adaptive immune response directed against polymorphic non-self histocompatibility antigens, primarily human leukocyte antigens (HLA / MHC Class I and Class II) expressed on the donor tissue.
    *   The polymorphic nature of MHC molecules means that almost any mismatched transplant will display foreign peptide-binding grooves and helical regions that are recognized as non-self by host T cells.
    *   **Allorecognition Pathways**:
        *   **Direct Pathway of Allorecognition**: Host T cells directly recognize intact, unprocessed foreign MHC molecules displayed on the surface of donor antigen-presenting cells (specifically mature donor dendritic cells, historically termed "passenger leukocytes") that migrated from the graft to host secondary lymphoid organs. The host TCR binds to the allogeneic MHC molecule itself, mimicking the recognition of self-MHC plus foreign peptide. This pathway is exceptionally powerful, activating up to 1% to 10% of all host T cells, but it eventually fades as donor APCs die and are cleared.
        *   **Indirect Pathway of Allorecognition**: Host dendritic cells infiltrate the graft, internalize shed donor MHC molecules and other polymorphic proteins, and transport them to draining lymph nodes. The host APCs process these proteins into peptides and present them on host self-MHC Class II molecules to host CD4+ helper T cells. This is the classic pathway for processing foreign protein antigens and persists indefinitely, driving chronic cellular and antibody-mediated rejection.
*   **Inheritance Pattern:**
    *   N/A
*   **Cellular & Molecular Consequences:**
    *   **Sensitization and Activation Phase**:
        *   Following direct or indirect allorecognition in host lymph nodes, naive host T cells receive TCR-mediated signals (Signal 1) and CD28-B7 (CD80/CD86) costimulation (Signal 2).
        *   This triggers the Lck-ZAP70-PLCγ1 signaling cascade, activating the transcription factors NFAT, NF-κB, and AP-1 to drive transcription of the IL-2 gene and the high-affinity IL-2 receptor alpha chain (CD25).
        *   Autocrine IL-2 binding to CD25/CD122/CD132 activates the JAK3-STAT5 and mTOR pathways, driving clonal expansion and differentiation of T cells into CD8+ CTLs and CD4+ helper T cells (primarily Th1 and Th17).
        *   Host B cells recognize donor MHC molecules directly via their BCR, internalize them, and present donor peptides on self-MHC Class II to indirect-pathway-activated CD4+ helper T cells. This T-B cell interaction (mediated by CD40-CD40L costimulation and helper cytokines like IL-4 and IL-21) drives B-cell class-switching to IgG and somatic hypermutation, generating high-affinity donor-specific antibodies (DSAs).
    *   **Hyperacute Rejection (Minutes to Hours)**:
        *   Preformed circulating antibodies (IgG or IgM) in the recipient's blood, arising from prior blood transfusions, multiple pregnancies, or previous transplants (anti-HLA DSAs), or natural IgM antibodies against foreign ABO carbohydrate blood groups, bind immediately to donor antigens expressed on the vascular endothelium of the graft.
        *   Antibody-antigen binding initiates the classical complement pathway via C1q, generating the anaphylatoxins C3a and C5a, and assembling the membrane attack complex (C5b-9).
    *   **Acute Cellular Rejection (Days to Weeks)**:
        *   Activated host CD8+ CTLs and CD4+ helper T cells home to the graft. CD8+ T cells bind to donor MHC Class I on parenchymal and endothelial cells, executing direct cytolysis.
        *   CD4+ Th1 cells release IFN-γ and TNF, activating host macrophages and upregulating endothelial adhesion molecules (ICAM-1, VCAM-1, E-selectin).
    *   **Acute Antibody-Mediated Rejection (Days to Weeks)**:
        *   Host B cells differentiate into plasma cells that produce de novo IgG antibodies against donor HLA Class I or Class II molecules. These antibodies bind to the graft endothelium, activating complement and recruiting neutrophils.
    *   **Chronic Rejection (Months to Years)**:
        *   Indirect-pathway CD4+ T cells continuously recognize donor antigens presented by host APCs, driving chronic, low-grade Th1-mediated inflammation and antibody production.
        *   Activated macrophages and endothelial cells produce growth factors (such as TGF-β, Platelet-Derived Growth Factor (PDGF), and basic Fibroblast Growth Factor (bFGF)).
*   **Mechanism of Tissue Damage:**
    *   **Hyperacute Rejection**:
        *   Complement activation on endothelial cells leads to endothelial cell lysis, retraction, and exposure of the subendothelial basement membrane.
        *   This triggers rapid platelet adhesion and activation, leading to microvascular thrombosis, fibrin deposition, and complete occlusion of the graft's arterial lumina.
        *   The blood supply is severed, causing immediate, irreversible ischemic necrosis of the entire graft.
    *   **Acute Cellular Rejection**:
        *   **Parenchymal Lysis**: CD8+ CTLs destroy graft parenchymal cells (e.g., renal tubular epithelial cells in a kidney graft, hepatocytes in a liver graft, or myocytes in a heart graft) via perforin/granzyme-mediated and Fas/FasL-induced apoptosis, presenting as tubulitis or endotheliitis.
        *   **DTH-like Inflammation**: CD4+ Th1-derived IFN-γ classically activates macrophages, which release reactive oxygen species (ROS), nitric oxide, and lysosomal enzymes, destroying surrounding graft tissue and driving interstitial edema.
    *   **Acute Antibody-Mediated Rejection**:
        *   Complement classical pathway activation causes endothelial injury and capillary leak. Neutrophils and monocytes are recruited to the graft microvasculature via C5a, binding to deposited IgG via Fcγ receptors.
        *   The leukocytes release proteolytic enzymes and ROS, causing microvascular inflammation (glomerulitis, peritubular capillaritis), microvascular thrombosis, and acute graft dysfunction.
    *   **Chronic Rejection (Chronic Allograft Vasculopathy)**:
        *   Persistent, low-grade Th1 inflammatory cytokines and growth factors (PDGF, TGF-β) stimulate the migration of smooth muscle cells from the vessel media into the intima, where they proliferate and synthesize extracellular matrix.
        *   This leads to progressive, concentric intimal thickening and luminal narrowing of the graft's small and medium-sized arteries (arteriosclerosis).
        *   Luminal occlusion results in chronic graft ischemia, causing parenchymal cell atrophy, replacement by collagen fibers (fibrosis), and eventual complete loss of graft function (e.g., bronchiolitis obliterans in lung grafts, vanishing bile duct syndrome in liver grafts, or accelerated coronary artery disease in heart grafts).
*   **Environmental, Infectious, or Commensal Triggers:**
    *   **Viral Infections (especially Cytomegalovirus - CMV)**: CMV infection upregulates host inflammatory cytokines (TNF, IL-6), increases the expression of MHC Class I and Class II molecules on graft tissue, and induces endothelial cell adhesion molecules. This "danger signal" enhances host dendritic cell antigen presentation, cross-reacts with allogeneic peptides, and triggers or accelerates acute cellular rejection episodes.
    *   **Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury (IRI)**: The period of cold ischemia during organ storage and subsequent warm reperfusion in the recipient generates reactive oxygen species, activating the TLR2 and TLR4 pathways on donor graft cells. This innate danger response upregulates proinflammatory cytokines (IL-1, TNF) and chemokine gradients, facilitating host leukocyte homing and lowering the threshold required to initiate acute adaptive rejection.

#### Clinical Presentation
*   **Age of Onset:**
    *   Can occur in any transplant recipient at any age.
    *   **Hyperacute Rejection**: Manifests immediately within minutes to hours after vascular anastomosis during the transplant surgery.
    *   **Acute Rejection**: Most commonly occurs within the first 6 months post-transplant, but can develop at any time during the life of the graft, especially if immunosuppression is reduced, non-adhered, or discontinued.
    *   **Chronic Rejection**: Manifests slowly and insidiously, typically presenting months to several years after transplantation.
*   **Classic Phenotype & Susceptibilities:**
    *   **Hyperacute Rejection**: The surgeon observes the newly transplanted organ (such as a kidney) rapidly turn cyanotic, mottled, flaccid, and cold on the operating table, with a complete cessation of urine output (for kidney) or failure to contract (for heart).
    *   **Acute Kidney Graft Rejection**: Presents with rapid-onset oliguria (decreased urine output), rising serum creatinine and BUN, weight gain (fluid retention), peripheral edema, hypertension, and localized graft tenderness/pain.
    *   **Acute Heart Graft Rejection**: Manifests as acute congestive heart failure, presenting with dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea, fatigue, jugular venous distention, third heart sound (S3 gallop), and atrial or ventricular arrhythmias.
    *   **Acute Liver Graft Rejection**: Characterized by progressive jaundice, dark urine, pale stools, pruritus, hepatomegaly, rising serum bilirubin, and elevated transaminases (ALT/AST).
    *   **Chronic Kidney Graft Rejection (Chronic Allograft Nephropathy)**: Insidious, progressive decline in glomerular filtration rate (GFR), worsening proteinuria, and refractory hypertension.
    *   **Chronic Lung Graft Rejection**: Presents as progressive, irreversible exertional dyspnea, dry cough, and a steady decline in forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1) due to bronchiolitis obliterans.
    *   **Chronic Heart Graft Rejection**: Manifests as progressive exertional dyspnea, chest pain (angina may be absent due to autonomic denervation of the transplanted heart), and silent myocardial infarctions secondary to transplant coronary artery vasculopathy.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:**
    *   **Endotheliitis / Intimal Arteritis**: Under light microscopy of a graft biopsy, the presence of host lymphocytes lifting the endothelial cells away from the internal elastic lamina of arteriole walls is a diagnostic sign of active acute cellular rejection.
    *   **Tubulitis**: The presence of host CD8+ T cells infiltrating the basolateral membrane of renal tubular epithelial cells on a kidney biopsy, confirming acute cellular rejection.
    *   **C4d Capillary Staining**: Immunohistochemical detection of C4d (a stable, covalent cleavage product of the classical complement cascade) deposited along the peritubular capillaries (in kidney) or sinusoidal capillaries (in liver) on biopsy is a diagnostic hallmark of active antibody-mediated rejection.
    *   **Concentric Intimal Arteriosclerosis**: Histopathological finding of uniform, concentric fibroelastic thickening of the arterial intima with intact internal elastic lamina in chronic rejection, distinguishing it from eccentric, plaque-like classic atherosclerosis.
*   **Associated Syndromes or Triggers:**
    *   **Graft-Versus-Host Disease (GVHD)**: A distinct transplant pathology occurring primarily in hematopoietic stem cell transplants or vascularized small bowel transplants. Rather than the host rejecting the graft, immunocompetent donor T cells in the graft recognize polymorphic host HLA antigens as foreign. These donor T cells undergo activation and clonal expansion, directly attacking host epithelial cells in the skin (causing maculopapular rash, blistering), liver (causing hyperbilirubinemia, bile duct destruction), and gastrointestinal tract (causing profuse, watery, or bloody diarrhea).
    *   **Post-Transplant Lymphoproliferative Disorder (PTLD)**: A life-threatening lymphoma-like syndrome driven by the reactivation and uncontrolled proliferation of Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV)-infected B cells, complicating long-term pharmacologic T-cell immunosuppression.

#### Laboratory & Diagnostics
*   **CBC / Lymphocyte Subsets:**
    *   **Acute Rejection**: Usually shows normal circulating lymphocyte counts, though localized graft infiltrates are profound. Neutrophilic leukocytosis may be present during acute antibody-mediated rejection or systemic infection.
    *   **GVHD**: Often associated with severe cytopenias (anemia, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia) due to bone marrow suppression or direct immune-mediated marrow destruction.
*   **Immunoglobulin Levels:**
    *   Total serum IgG, IgA, and IgM levels are typically normal.
    *   **Donor-Specific Antibodies (DSAs)**: The presence of circulating host IgG antibodies directed specifically against donor HLA Class I or Class II alleles, quantified by single-antigen bead assays using Luminex technology, is a critical diagnostic marker for acute and chronic antibody-mediated rejection.
*   **Flow Cytometry Findings:**
    *   **Flow Cytometric Crossmatching (FCXM)**: Prior to transplantation, recipient serum is incubated with donor lymphocytes (T cells for MHC Class I, B cells for MHC Class I and II). If the recipient has preformed antibodies, they bind to the lymphocytes and are detected using fluorescently-labeled anti-human IgG antibodies via flow cytometry. A positive crossmatch indicates a high risk for hyperacute or severe acute antibody-mediated rejection and is a contraindication to proceeding with the transplant.
    *   Used to monitor B-cell depletion (absence of CD19+/CD20+ B cells) following Rituximab therapy for antibody-mediated rejection.
*   **Functional Assays & Genetic Testing:**
    *   **Mixed Lymphocyte Reaction (MLR)**: A classic historical in vitro functional assay to assess histocompatibility. Recipient T cells (responder cells) are co-cultured with irradiated or mitomycin C-treated donor lymphocytes (stimulator cells). If MHC differences exist, recipient T cells proliferate (measured by tritium-labeled thymidine incorporation) and differentiate into CTLs (measured by chromium release assays of lysed donor target cells). High MLR activity correlates with a high risk of acute cellular rejection.
    *   **HLA Genotyping**: Performed using PCR with sequence-specific primers (PCR-SSP) or next-generation sequencing to identify and compare HLA-A, HLA-B, HLA-C, HLA-DR, HLA-DQ, and HLA-DP alleles between the donor and recipient, aiming to minimize the number of HLA mismatches.
    *   **Organ Biopsy (with Banff Classification)**: The diagnostic gold standard for evaluating transplant rejection.
        *   *Acute Cellular Rejection (Banff Grade I-III)*: Shows interstitial lymphocytic infiltration, tubulitis (kidney), endotheliitis (intimal arteritis), and focal necrosis of parenchymal cells.
        *   *Acute Antibody-Mediated Rejection*: Shows peritubular capillaritis, glomerulitis, neutrophilic infiltration within capillaries, C4d deposition along endothelial membranes, and microvascular thrombi.
        *   *Chronic Allograft Nephropathy*: Shows interstitial fibrosis, tubular atrophy, and concentric intimal fibroelastic thickening of arteries.

#### Management & Therapeutics
*   **Acute Management:**
    *   **Corticosteroid Pulse Therapy**: First-line treatment for acute cellular rejection. High doses of intravenous methylprednisolone (typically 500 to 1000 mg daily for 3 consecutive days) rapidly suppress T-cell and macrophage cytokine transcription (IL-1, IL-2, TNF, IFN-γ) and decrease capillary permeability.
    *   **Anti-Thymocyte Globulin (ATG)**: A polyclonal antibody preparation derived from rabbits or horses immunized with human thymocytes. ATG is used to treat steroid-resistant acute cellular rejection, acting by binding to multiple T-cell surface markers (CD2, CD3, CD4, CD8, CD28, CD45), triggering rapid T-cell depletion via complement-mediated lysis, ADCC, and splenic clearance.
    *   **Plasmapheresis + IVIG**: Standard of care for acute antibody-mediated rejection. Plasmapheresis physically extracts circulating donor-specific antibodies (DSAs) and complement proteins from the recipient's plasma. High-dose IVIG (1 to 2 g/kg) is administered afterward to neutralize residual antibodies, downregulate B-cell activation via FcγRIIB, and accelerate the clearance of DSAs.
*   **Curative / Definitive Treatment:**
    *   There is no pharmacologic cure for chronic allograft rejection once severe fibrosis and luminal vascular occlusion have occurred.
    *   **Re-transplantation**: The only definitive curative treatment for chronic, irreversible graft failure.
*   **Contraindications (e.g., live vaccines):**
    *   **Live Attenuated Vaccines are Strictly Contraindicated**: Do NOT administer live viral or bacterial vaccines (such as MMR, varicella, oral polio, yellow fever, or BCG) to any transplant recipient on maintenance immunosuppressive therapy, due to the risk of uncontrolled vaccine-strain viral replication and life-threatening systemic infection.
*   **Immunosuppression or Desensitization Protocols:**
    *   **Triple Maintenance Immunosuppressive Regimen**: The clinical standard of care to prevent acute and chronic rejection, combining three drug classes with distinct molecular targets:
        1.  **Calcineurin Inhibitors (CNIs)**:
            *   *Tacrolimus (FK506)*: Binds to intracellular **FKBP-12**. This complex binds to and inhibits calcineurin, a calcium-dependent phosphatase, preventing the dephosphorylation and nuclear translocation of **NFAT**. This halts the transcription of IL-2 and CD25, blocking T-cell activation.
            *   *Cyclosporine A*: Binds to **cyclophilin**, forming a complex that similarly inhibits calcineurin.
        2.  **Antimetabolites**:
            *   *Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF)*: Reversibly inhibits **inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (IMPDH)**, the rate-limiting enzyme in the de novo purine synthesis pathway. Because T and B lymphocytes rely exclusively on de novo synthesis (lacking salvage pathways), MMF selectively blocks lymphocyte proliferation.
            *   *Azathioprine*: A prodrug converted to 6-mercaptopurine, which incorporates into replicating DNA to block cell division.
        3.  **Corticosteroids**:
            *   *Prednisone*: Mainstay anti-inflammatory that inhibits NF-κB and AP-1, suppressing cytokine production.
    *   **Adjunctive Maintenance Therapies**:
        *   **mTOR Inhibitors (Sirolimus / Rapamycin)**: Binds to FKBP-12. This complex inhibits the kinase **mTOR** (mammalian target of rapamycin), blocking downstream p70S6 kinase activation and cell cycle progression from G1 to S phase in response to IL-2, without blocking IL-2 transcription. Highly useful in chronic rejection because mTOR inhibitors also possess anti-proliferative actions on vascular smooth muscle cells.
        *   **Costimulation Blockers**:
            *   *Belatacept*: A soluble fusion protein consisting of the extracellular domain of **CTLA-4** joined to an IgG1 Fc region. It binds with high affinity to CD80 and CD86 on APCs, blocking their interaction with CD28 on T cells, selectively inhibiting Signal 2. Approved as an alternative to CNIs in renal transplant patients to avoid CNI-induced nephrotoxicity.
        *   **IL-2 Receptor Antagonists**:
            *   *Basiliximab / Daclizumab*: Monoclonal antibodies that bind with high affinity to **CD25 (IL-2Rα)** on activated T cells, blocking IL-2-mediated signaling and T-cell proliferation. Used as induction therapy at the time of transplant surgery.
        *   **B-Cell Depletion**:
            *   *Rituximab (Anti-CD20)*: Used to treat antibody-mediated rejection and in pre-transplant desensitization protocols to deplete B cells.
            *   *Bortezomib*: A proteasome inhibitor used to destroy DSA-producing plasma cells in refractory antibody-mediated rejection.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    *   **"Passenger Leukocytes"**: Donor-derived dendritic cells resident in the grafted organ that migrate to the recipient's draining lymph nodes, representing the primary antigen-presenting cells that initiate the direct pathway of allorecognition.
    *   **"Accommodation"**: An acquired state of resistance wherein graft endothelial cells survive and function normally despite the persistent presence of circulating donor-specific antibodies (DSAs) and low levels of complement activation. This is achieved by the upregulation of cytoprotective and anti-apoptotic genes (such as Bcl-2, Bcl-xl, and heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1)) and complement regulatory proteins (DAF/CD55, CD59) on the endothelial membrane.
    *   **"Vanishing Bile Duct Syndrome"**: The clinical phenotype of chronic liver allograft rejection, where chronic immunological damage selectively destroys the interlobular bile ducts, leading to ductopenia and biliary cirrhosis.
    *   **"Bronchiolitis Obliterans"**: The clinical and pathological hallmark of chronic lung transplant rejection, characterized by the fibrotic occlusion of small airways.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   **The Immunological Privilege of the Liver**: The liver transplant is remarkably resistant to both hyperacute and acute antibody-mediated rejection compared to kidney or heart transplants. The liver possesses a dual blood supply, a massive sinusoidal endothelial surface area that dilutes antibody binding, and continuously secretes soluble HLA Class I molecules into the circulation that act as "decoys" to bind and neutralize circulating DSAs before they can bind to the graft tissues. Additionally, liver tissue continuously produces Kupffer cells that clear immune complexes.
    *   **Direct vs. Indirect Pathway Lifespan**: It was historically believed that the direct pathway was the sole driver of transplant rejection. However, because donor dendritic cells cannot replicate and eventually die out within weeks of transplantation, the direct pathway naturally terminates. It is the indirect pathway (mediated by recipient APCs continuously presenting shed donor antigens) that persists indefinitely and serves as the primary driver of chronic rejection and chronic allograft vasculopathy.
    *   **Mismatches that "Do Not Matter"**: In corneal transplantation, HLA matching is typically not performed, and immunosuppression is not required. The cornea represents an immunologically privileged site due to: (1) a complete lack of blood and lymphatic vessels, preventing host lymphocytes from reaching the graft and donor antigens from reaching lymph nodes, (2) the local expression of Fas Ligand (CD178) which induces apoptosis in infiltrating host T cells, and (3) high local levels of immunosuppressive TGF-β in the anterior chamber of the eye.
