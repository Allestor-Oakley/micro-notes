### Inborn Errors of Innate Immunity (e.g., CGD, LAD)

#### Pathogenesis & Molecular Defect
*   **Underlying Immune Defect / Mutation:**
    *   Inborn errors of innate immunity (IEIs) are a heterogeneous group of congenital, monogenic disorders caused by loss-of-function (LOF) or gain-of-function (GOF) mutations in genes encoding structural, signaling, or regulatory components of the innate immune system.
    *   **Chronic Granulomatous Disease (CGD)**: Caused by mutations in genes encoding subunits of the phagocyte (NADPH) oxidase (phagocyte oxidase) enzyme complex. The X-linked recessive form is caused by mutations in the CYBB gene encoding the 91-kD alpha subunit of cytochrome b558, also known as gp91phox. The remaining autosomal recessive forms are caused by mutations in CYBA (encoding p22phox), NCF1 (p47phox), or NCF2 (p67phox).
    *   **Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency Type 1 (LAD-1)**: Caused by autosomal recessive mutations in the ITGB2 gene encoding the CD18 beta chain, which is the shared beta2 subunit of leukocyte integrins (LFA-1 (CD11aCD18), MAC-1 (CD11bCD18), and p150,95 (CD11cCD18)).
    *   **Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency Type 2 (LAD-2)**: Caused by autosomal recessive mutations in the SLC35C1 gene encoding the Golgi GDP-fucose transporter-1, required for importing fucose into the Golgi apparatus where it is incorporated into sialyl Lewis X (CD15s).
    *   **Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency Type 3 (LAD-3)**: Caused by autosomal recessive mutations in the FERMT3 gene encoding Kindlin-3, a cytoskeletal protein that binds to the cytoplasmic tails of integrins and regulates "inside-out" signaling and integrin activation.
    *   **Chediak-Higashi Syndrome (CHS)**: Caused by autosomal recessive mutations in the LYST gene (lysosomal trafficking regulator), which controls intracellular trafficking and fusion of lysosomal vesicles.
    *   **Toll-Like Receptor (TLR) Signaling Pathway Defects**: Mutations disrupting Toll-like receptor signaling cascades and type I interferon responses.
        *   *MyD88 and IRAK4 Defects*: Autosomal recessive mutations in MyD88 or IRAK4 (encoding IL-1 receptor-associated kinase 4), disrupting signaling downstream of most TLRs (except TLR3) and the IL-1 receptor.
        *   *UNC93B Defects*: Autosomal recessive mutations in UNC93B, an endoplasmic reticulum membrane chaperone protein essential for delivering the nucleic acid-sensing TLRs (TLRs 3, 7, 8, 9) from the ER to endosomal membranes.
        *   *TLR3, TRIF, TBK1, and TRAF3 Defects*: Autosomal recessive (or dominant-negative in some cases) mutations in TLR3, TRIF (adaptor), TBK1 (kinase), or TRAF3 (E3 ligase), disrupting TLR3-mediated type I interferon and antiviral signaling.
    *   **NK Cell Deficiencies**:
        *   *Classical NK Cell Deficiency*: Autosomal dominant mutations in the GATA2 gene encoding the GATA2 transcription factor, leading to a loss of bone marrow precursors and a total absence of mature NK cells, monocytes, and dendritic cells.
        *   *Functional NK Cell Deficiency*: Autosomal recessive mutations in the FCGR3A gene encoding CD16 (FcγRIIIA).
*   **Inheritance Pattern:**
    *   Autosomal recessive: LAD-1, LAD-2, LAD-3, Chediak-Higashi syndrome, MyD88 deficiency, IRAK4 deficiency, homozygous UNC93B mutations, and the autosomal recessive forms of CGD (p47phox, p67phox, p22phox).
    *   X-linked recessive: The most common form of Chronic Granulomatous Disease (~two-thirds of cases, CYBB mutation on X chromosome).
    *   Autosomal dominant: Classical NK cell deficiency (GATA2 mutation), and certain TLR3, TBK1, or TRAF3 mutations (exhibiting dominant-negative effects or haploinsufficiency).
*   **Cellular & Molecular Consequences:**
    *   **CGD**: Severe impairment of the phagocyte (NADPH) oxidase complex prevents the chemical reduction of molecular oxygen into superoxide anions (O2-) and downstream reactive oxygen species (ROS), such as hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and hypohalous acids (via myeloperoxidase). Phagocytes (neutrophils and macrophages) can ingest microbes but cannot execute the "respiratory burst" or generate the oxygen radicals required to destroy them.
    *   **LAD-1**: Deficient or absent surface expression of CD11/CD18 beta2 integrins. This abolishes the firm, stable adhesion of leukocytes (especially neutrophils) to intercellular adhesion molecules (such as ICAM-1) on cytokine-activated endothelial cells. It also impairs the T cell-APC immunological synapse (LFA-1/ICAM-1 interaction) and inhibits iC3b-mediated phagocytosis due to the lack of MAC-1 (CD11bCD18) and CD11cCD18.
    *   **LAD-2**: Complete absence of sialyl Lewis X (CD15s) on neutrophils and other leukocytes. Because sialyl Lewis X is the primary carbohydrate ligand for endothelial E-selectin (CD62E) and P-selectin (CD62P), its absence completely prevents the initial low-affinity "rolling" step of leukocyte migration, arresting leukocyte recruitment. The fucose metabolism defect also leads to a failure to synthesize the H glycan core of ABO antigens, resulting in a Bombay blood group phenotype.
    *   **LAD-3**: Disruption of the "inside-out" signaling pathway that couples chemokine receptor activation to integrin conformational changes. Leukocytes express integrins (such as LFA-1 and VLA-4) but cannot activate them to undergo high-affinity binding to endothelial ICAM-1 and VCAM-1, preventing firm adhesion and transmigration. Additionally, Kindlin-3 deficiency in platelets impairs GPIIb/IIIa integrin activation, abolishing platelet aggregation and fibrinogen binding despite normal platelet numbers.
    *   **Chediak-Higashi Syndrome**: Defective lysosomal vesicle trafficking leads to unregulated organelle fusion, forming giant, dysfunctional lysosomes and granules in neutrophils, monocytes, CTLs, NK cells, melanocytes, and platelets. Neutrophils and monocytes exhibit severe defects in chemotaxis, phagocytosis, and degranulation. Cytolytic granules in CTLs and NK cells cannot move along microtubules or fuse with the plasma membrane, completely abrogating granule-mediated cytolysis. Melanosome mistrafficking prevents melanin transfer to keratinocytes, causing albinism.
    *   **TLR Pathway Defects**:
        *   *MyD88/IRAK4 Deficiencies*: Impair the recruitment of the MyD88-IRAK4-IRAK1 signaling complex downstream of TLRs (except TLR3) and the IL-1 receptor. This prevents downstream TRAF6 activation, IκB kinase (IKK) activation, and the nuclear translocation of NF-κB, completely blocking the transcription and secretion of proinflammatory cytokines (such as TNF, IL-1, IL-6, and IL-12).
        *   *UNC93B and TLR3/TRIF/TBK1/TRAF3 Deficiencies*: Abolish endosomal TLR localization and TRIF-dependent signaling. This prevents TANK-binding kinase 1 (TBK1) activation, halting the phosphorylation and nuclear translocation of interferon regulatory factors 3 and 7 (IRF3/IRF7), resulting in a complete failure to synthesize type I interferons (IFN-α/IFN-β) in response to viral nucleic acids.
    *   **NK Cell Deficiencies**:
        *   *GATA2 Deficiency*: Eliminates critical bone marrow precursor populations, resulting in a progressive and severe depletion of peripheral NK cells, monocytes, and dendritic cells.
        *   *CD16 Deficiency*: Eliminates the surface expression or signaling of FcγRIIIA on NK cells, abolishing Antibody-Dependent Cellular Cytotoxicity (ADCC) and globally compromising NK cell lytic activity.
*   **Mechanism of Tissue Damage:**
    *   **CGD**: Because neutrophils and macrophages are unable to kill ingested microbes, persistent antigenic stimulation drives chronic cell-mediated immune responses. CD4+ T helper cells release IFN-γ, which continuously activates macrophages. These activated macrophages, unable to clear the intracellular niche, cluster around infected sites, forming progressive, pathological **granulomas** in the skin, liver, lungs, gastrointestinal tract, and urinary tract. This causes localized tissue compression, obstruction, and chronic inflammatory organ damage.
    *   **LAD-1, LAD-2, LAD-3**: The fundamental inability of neutrophils to transmigrate through postcapillary venules prevents their recruitment to infected tissues. In the absence of neutrophils, tissue infections expand unchecked, causing localized microbial-mediated tissue necrosis and ulceration with a complete **absence of pus** (which is primarily composed of dead neutrophils).
    *   **Chediak-Higashi Syndrome**: Uncontrolled macrophage activation and systemic hyperinflammation. Due to the lytic granule defect, CTLs and NK cells cannot kill virally infected cells (such as cells infected with EBV). The continuous, futile attempt to clear the infection triggers persistent, high-level IFN-γ secretion by CTLs. Persistent IFN-γ drives unchecked, systemic classical macrophage activation, resulting in the phagocytosis of erythrocytes, platelets, and leukocytes (hemophagocytosis) in the spleen, liver, and bone marrow, culminating in **Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis (HLH)**.
    *   **TLR Pathway Defects**: Tissue injury is primarily mediated by uncontrolled viral replication (e.g., Herpes Simplex Virus in the temporal lobes of the brain in TLR3/UNC93B deficiencies, causing necrotic hemorrhagic encephalitis) or bacterial invasion (e.g., invasive pneumococcal disease in MyD88/IRAK4 deficiencies, leading to localized tissue necrosis, meningitis, and septic shock due to delayed microbial clearance).
*   **Environmental, Infectious, or Commensal Triggers:**
    *   **CGD**: Triggered by exposure to **catalase-positive** pathogens. Organisms like Staphylococcus aureus, Aspergillus species (e.g., Aspergillus fumigatus), Nocardia, Serratia marcescens, and Burkholderia cepacia produce catalase, which degrades the trace amount of hydrogen peroxide they produce. Catalase-negative organisms (such as Streptococcus pneumoniae) produce H2O2 as a by-product of their own metabolism, which the CGD host cell can capture and utilize via host myeloperoxidase to kill the microbe. Catalase-positive pathogens destroy their own H2O2, depriving the host cell of any reactive oxygen substrate.
    *   **LADs**: Triggered by common environmental and skin bacteria/fungi (such as Staphylococcus aureus, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and Candida albicans) invading the skin, mucosal membranes, or gingiva.
    *   **Chediak-Higashi**: Latent or acute herpesvirus infections, particularly **Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV)**, act as a primary trigger driving the transition of CHS into the fatal "accelerated phase" (HLH).
    *   **TLR Pathway Defects**:
        *   *MyD88/IRAK4 Deficiencies*: Triggered by invasive encapsulated pyogenic bacteria, particularly **Streptococcus pneumoniae** and Neisseria meningitidis.
        *   *TLR3/UNC93B/TRIF/TBK1 Deficiencies*: Selective triggering by **Herpes Simplex Virus 1 (HSV-1)**, which infects the central nervous system to cause herpes simplex encephalitis.
    *   **Classical NK Deficiencies**: Triggered by herpesviruses (CMV, EBV, varicella-zoster virus) and papillomaviruses (HPV).

#### Clinical Presentation
*   **Age of Onset:**
    *   Chronic Granulomatous Disease, LAD-1, LAD-2, LAD-3, and Chediak-Higashi syndrome typically present in early infancy or early childhood with recurrent infections.
    *   LADs present uniquely at birth with delayed separation of the umbilical cord.
    *   MyD88 and IRAK4 deficiencies present with severe, invasive bacterial infections in early infancy, but the frequency and severity of infections decrease as the child grows older (typically after 5 to 10 years of age) due to compensation by adaptive humoral and cellular immunity.
*   **Classic Phenotype & Susceptibilities:**
    *   **CGD**: Patients present with recurrent, invasive bacterial and fungal infections of the skin, liver, lymph nodes, and respiratory tract, presenting as skin abscesses, pneumonia, lymphadenitis, and hepatic abscesses. Aspergillus infections are highly invasive and represent the leading cause of death. Granulomas can cause esophageal, gastric outlet, or urinary tract obstruction.
    *   **LAD-1**: Characterized by recurrent, severe bacterial and fungal infections of the skin and mucosal surfaces (e.g., severe gingivitis, periodontitis, cutaneous ulcers), impaired wound healing (atrophic scars), and a complete **absence of pus** in wounds despite high white blood cell counts.
    *   **LAD-2**: Presents with similar recurrent infections and mucosal ulcers as LAD-1, but is accompanied by severe developmental delay, cognitive impairment, dysmorphic facial features, short stature, and microcephaly.
    *   **LAD-3**: Combines the severe, recurrent infections of LAD-1 with a life-threatening, severe **bleeding disorder** (easy bruising, petechiae, mucosal bleeding, intracranial hemorrhage) requiring frequent blood transfusions.
    *   **Chediak-Higashi**: Presents with recurrent, severe pyogenic infections, **partial oculocutaneous albinism** (silvery hair, pale skin, photophobia, nystagmus), progressive peripheral sensory-motor neuropathy, and mild bleeding tendencies.
    *   **MyD88 and IRAK4 Deficiencies**: Susceptibility to recurrent, invasive pyogenic bacterial infections, particularly invasive pneumococcal disease (pneumonia, meningitis, septic arthritis, sepsis) and severe staphylococcal or pseudomonal infections.
    *   **TLR3/UNC93B Deficiencies**: Highly selective susceptibility to **Herpes Simplex Encephalitis (HSE)**, with recurrent or localized necrotic temporal lobe brain lesions, while immunity to other common pathogens remains entirely normal.
    *   **NK Cell Deficiencies**: Severe, recurrent, or disseminated infections with herpesviruses (causing severe varicella, CMV mononucleosis, or herpes simplex lesions) and papillomaviruses (causing refractory, extensive cutaneous and mucosal warts (HPV)).
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:**
    *   **Delayed Umbilical Cord Separation**: Neonatal umbilical cord retention exceeding 3 to 4 weeks post-birth (often up to months), accompanied by omphalitis, is highly pathognomonic for **LAD-1** and **LAD-3** (and occasionally LAD-2).
    *   **Cold Abscesses**: Recurrent subcutaneous and deep tissue abscesses (often due to S. aureus) that lack the typical warm, erythematous, inflammatory signs, characteristic of CGD.
    *   **Silvery Hair and Giant Lysosomes**: The physical presence of metallic-silvery hair in an infant, combined with the presence of massive, **giant intracellular peroxidase-positive granules** in granulocytes on peripheral blood smear, is pathognomonic for **Chediak-Higashi Syndrome**.
    *   **Extreme Leukocytosis with Absent Pus**: Circulating neutrophil counts exceeding 50,000 to 100,000/μL in an infant with active, deep tissue necrosis but a total absence of localized inflammatory pus.
*   **Associated Syndromes or Triggers:**
    *   **Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) / Accelerated Phase**: The life-threatening, hyper-inflammatory phase of Chediak-Higashi syndrome (usually triggered by EBV), characterized by high fever, hepatosplenomegaly, generalized lymphadenopathy, cytopenias, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), elevated ferritin, and tissue infiltration by non-neoplastic lymphocytes and hemophagocytic macrophages.
    *   **Bombay Blood Group Phenotype**: Observed in **LAD-2** patients due to the complete lack of fucosylation of the H antigen core of ABO blood group carbohydrates.
    *   **Blau Syndrome**: An autoinflammatory disorder caused by gain-of-function mutations in NOD2, presenting with a triad of granulomatous arthritis, uveitis, and skin rash (distinct from Crohn's disease which is associated with NOD2 loss-of-function variants).

#### Laboratory & Diagnostics
*   **CBC / Lymphocyte Subsets:**
    *   **LADs (LAD-1, LAD-2, LAD-3)**: Characterized by marked, persistent **neutrophilic leukocytosis** (often 20,000 to 100,000/μL) even in the absence of active infection, due to the inability of neutrophils to adhere and leave the vascular compartment.
    *   **Chediak-Higashi**: Shows variable neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia, which progress to severe pancytopenia during the HLH accelerated phase.
    *   **NK Cell Deficiencies (GATA2 Deficiency)**: Shows a profound absence of peripheral blood CD56+ CD3- NK cells, accompanied by progressive monocytopenia, dendritic cell depletion, and B-cell lymphopenia. CD16 deficiency shows normal NK cell numbers but absent CD16 expression.
    *   **MyD88 / IRAK4 / TLR3 Deficiencies**: Standard CBC and lymphocyte subset panels are completely normal.
*   **Immunoglobulin Levels:**
    *   Immunoglobulin levels (IgG, IgM, IgA, IgE) are typically normal or elevated (polyclonal hypergammaglobulinemia due to chronic infection) in CGD, LADs, CHS, and TLR pathway defects.
    *   GATA2 deficiency can show progressive hypogammaglobulinemia as B cell numbers decline.
*   **Flow Cytometry Findings:**
    *   **CGD (DHR Assay)**: Flow cytometric analysis of dihydrorhodamine 123 (DHR) oxidation is the diagnostic standard. Neutrophils are loaded with nonfluorescent DHR and stimulated with phorbol myristate acetate (PMA). Normal neutrophils oxidize DHR to green fluorescent rhodamine, yielding a strong fluorescent signal. CGD neutrophils show a complete failure of DHR oxidation (no green fluorescence). The assay also identifies X-linked carrier females, who display a characteristic bimodal cell population (one normal, one mutated population) due to random X-chromosome inactivation.
    *   **LAD-1**: Flow cytometry reveals a complete absence or severe reduction of surface **CD18** (beta2 integrin) on peripheral blood leukocytes, along with the absence of associated alpha chains CD11a (LFA-1), CD11b (MAC-1), and CD11c.
    *   **LAD-2**: Flow cytometry demonstrates a complete absence of **CD15s (sialyl Lewis X)** on neutrophils and other leukocytes.
    *   **LAD-3**: Flow cytometry shows normal expression levels of CD18 and CD11a, CD11b, CD11c, but Kindlin-3 expression is absent under Western blot or specialized intracellular flow staining.
    *   **CD16 Deficiency**: Flow cytometry shows normal NK cell counts (CD56+ CD3-) but a complete absence of cell surface **CD16 (FcγRIIIA)**.
*   **Functional Assays & Genetic Testing:**
    *   **Nitroblue Tetrazolium (NBT) Slide Test**: A classic functional assay for CGD. Neutrophils are stimulated with PMA in the presence of NBT. Normal cells reduce the yellow, soluble NBT dye into insoluble blue-black formazan crystals, which are visible microscopically. CGD cells fail to reduce NBT and remain yellow.
    *   **Platelet Aggregation Assays**: In **LAD-3**, platelet counts are normal, but ex vivo platelet aggregation assays show a profound, complete failure of aggregation in response to ADP, epinephrine, collagen, and arachidonic acid (resembling Glanzmann's thrombasthenia).
    *   **Type I Interferon Release Assays**: For TLR3 or UNC93B deficiencies, patient PBMCs stimulated ex vivo with TLR3 agonists (such as poly(I:C)) show a complete failure to secrete type I interferons (IFN-α/IFN-β).
    *   **Genetic Testing**: Next-generation sequencing panels are used to identify disease-causing mutations in CYBB, NCF1, NCF2, CYBA (for CGD); ITGB2 (LAD-1); SLC35C1 (LAD-2); FERMT3 (LAD-3); LYST (Chediak-Higashi); MyD88, IRAK4, UNC93B, TLR3, TRIF, TBK1 (TLR defects); or GATA2 (classical NK deficiency).

#### Management & Therapeutics
*   **Acute Management:**
    *   **Aggressive Antimicrobial Therapy**: Prompt, high-dose intravenous administration of pathogen-specific antibiotics and antifungals (e.g., Voriconazole or Amphotericin B for Aspergillus; Meropenem, Linezolid, or Bactrim depending on the bacterial species).
    *   **Surgical Drainage**: Incision and drainage of deep tissue or hepatic abscesses in CGD.
    *   **HLH Treatment Protocol**: For CHS patients entering the accelerated phase, emergency administration of the HLH-94/2004 protocol (etoposide, dexamethasone, and cyclosporine A) or an IFN-γ-neutralizing monoclonal antibody (Emapalumab) to halt systemic macrophage hyperactivation.
*   **Curative / Definitive Treatment:**
    *   **Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (HSCT / BMT)**: HSCT is the absolute curative standard of care for CGD, LAD-1, LAD-3, Chediak-Higashi syndrome (ideally performed before the onset of the HLH accelerated phase), GATA2 deficiency, and severe MyD88/IRAK4 deficiencies. Restoring donor-derived myeloid and lymphoid stem cells corrects the cellular defects. In CGD, achieving at least 15% donor myeloid chimerism is sufficient to restore full resistance to infections.
    *   **Gene Therapy**: Autologous HSCT utilizing lentiviral or retroviral vectors to insert functional wild-type copies of the mutated gene (e.g., CYBB for X-linked CGD) into the patient's own hematopoietic stem cells ex vivo is in active clinical trials and represents a promising curative therapy.
*   **Contraindications (e.g., live vaccines):**
    *   **Live Attenuated Vaccines are Strictly Contraindicated**: Do NOT administer live viral or bacterial vaccines (such as MMR, varicella, oral polio, rotavirus, or the **BCG vaccine**) to patients with CGD, LADs, Chediak-Higashi, or any severe innate immunodeficiencies. In CGD or MSMD patients, the live-attenuated Mycobacterium bovis BCG vaccine can cause severe, disseminated, and potentially fatal mycobacterial disease.
    *   Avoid radiation exposure in patients with double-stranded DNA break repair defects who present with immunodeficiencies (such as Ataxia-Telangiectasia), as they are extremely sensitive to radiation-induced damage.
*   **Immunosuppression or Desensitization Protocols:**
    *   **Interferon-Gamma (IFN-γ) Prophylaxis**: Subcutaneous recombinant human IFN-γ (Actimmune) is administered three times weekly as a standard prophylactic treatment in CGD. IFN-γ enhances transcription of the gp91phox gene and other components of the oxidase complex, while also boosting non-oxidative microbicidal mechanisms of phagocytes.
    *   **Life-Long Antimicrobial Prophylaxis**: Standard of care for CGD, LADs, and CHS patients awaiting HSCT. Includes daily administration of **Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (Bactrim)** to prevent bacterial infections and an oral azole (such as **Itraconazole** or Posaconazole) to prevent invasive fungal infections (especially Aspergillus).
    *   **Oral Fucose Supplementation**: Oral fucose therapy can be highly beneficial in a subset of **LAD-2** patients, restoring Golgi fucosylation, sialyl Lewis X expression, and normalizing leukocyte counts and infection resistance, though it does not reverse the cognitive developmental delay.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    *   **"Inability to make pus"**: The classic clinical description for **LAD-1**, where wounds undergo extensive tissue necrosis but lack the white, neutrophil-rich exudate (pus) because neutrophils are physically locked within the blood vessels.
    *   **"Respiratory Burst"**: The rapid, oxygen-dependent metabolic process where activated phagocytes consume molecular oxygen to synthesize toxic superoxide anions via NADPH oxidase.
    *   **"Bombay Blood Group"**: The rare blood group phenotype characterized by the complete lack of H substance (fucose core) on erythrocytes, observed in LAD-2.
    *   **"Albinism and Metallic Hair"**: The clinical phenotype of partial oculocutaneous albinism presenting as metallic-silvery hair in an infant, indicative of Chediak-Higashi syndrome.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   **LAD-3 Platelet Paradox**: Unlike LAD-1 and LAD-2 where the defect is restricted to leukocyte migration, LAD-3 combines severe leukocyte adhesion deficiency with a profound bleeding disorder, despite having entirely **normal platelet counts**. This occurs because Kindlin-3 is also required to activate platelet integrin GPIIb/IIIa (αIIbβ3), abolishing platelet-fibrinogen binding and aggregation.
    *   **CATA-Negative Protective Phenomenon in CGD**: CGD patients are highly susceptible to catalase-positive bacteria (e.g., S. aureus) but are completely protected against catalase-negative bacteria (e.g., Streptococcus pneumoniae). Because catalase-negative bacteria cannot degrade the hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) they produce as a waste product of their own metabolism, the CGD phagocyte can capture the bacterial H2O2 and use its own intact myeloperoxidase to synthesize toxic hypohalous acid, effectively killing the pathogen. Catalase-positive pathogens degrade their own H2O2, leaving the CGD cell with no respiratory burst substrates.
    *   **LAD-2 Non-Immunological Phenotype**: Unlike LAD-1 which is a pure immunodeficiency, LAD-2 is a generalized congenital disorder of glycosylation. The GDP-fucose transporter defect impairs fucosylation across multiple organs, leading to severe cognitive developmental delay, short stature, microcephaly, and dysmorphic features, in addition to leukocyte rolling defects.
    *   **Absence of Fever in MyD88/IRAK4 Deficiencies**: Patients with MyD88 or IRAK4 mutations are strikingly **unable to mount a fever** during severe, invasive bacterial infections. This occurs because the IL-1 receptor utilizes the MyD88-IRAK4 signaling pathway; without it, IL-1 cannot signal to the hypothalamus to trigger fever, masking severe systemic infections.
