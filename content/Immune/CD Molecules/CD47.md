## CD47 (The "Don't Eat Me" Signal)
CD47 is a profoundly high-yield molecule in the study of innate immunity and macrophage regulation. It functions primarily as a protective, universal self-recognition marker that actively prevents the immune system from attacking and destroying normal, healthy host tissues.
### Structure and Classification
1. **Molecular Weight:** CD47 is a 47 to 52 kD integral membrane protein.
2. Protein Family:** It is a member of the **Immunoglobulin (Ig) superfamily**.
### Cellular Expression Profile
Unlike many CD molecules that are highly restricted to specific subsets of immune cells (like CD4 or CD8), CD47 has an extremely broad expression profile because it must protect the entire body. It is expressed on:
	- All hematopoietic cells
	- Epithelial cells
	- Endothelial cells
	- Fibroblasts
### Mechanism of Action: The "Don't Eat Me" Signal
- **The Macrophage Receptor (SIRPα):** Macrophages express a specific inhibitory receptor on their plasma membrane known as **SIRPα** (Signal Regulatory Protein alpha).
- **The Ligand Interaction:** CD47 on the surface of normal, healthy cells acts as the direct, specific ligand for this SIRPα receptor.
- **Inhibitory Signaling:** When a patrolling macrophage comes into physical contact with a healthy host cell, the CD47 on the host cell binds to the SIRPα on the macrophage. This binding engagement generates potent inhibitory signals within the macrophage.
- **Physiological Result:** These intracellular inhibitory signals act as a molecular brake, completely halting the macrophage's phagocytic machinery and preventing the engulfment and destruction of the cell. Because of this vital interaction, CD47 is universally characterized as the **"don't eat me" signal** to phagocytes.
### Additional Functions
While its role as an anti-phagocytic signal dominates its clinical and immunological importance, CD47 is also noted to play a role in several other physiological cellular processes, including:
- Leukocyte adhesion
- Leukocyte migration
- Leukocyte activation
