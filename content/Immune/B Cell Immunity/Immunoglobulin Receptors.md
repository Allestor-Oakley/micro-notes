## FcγR
### CD16
CD16 refers to a group of low-affinity receptors for the Fc region of immunoglobulin G (IgG), specifically known as FcγRIII. Within the immune system, this receptor exists in two structurally and functionally distinct isoforms, encoded by separate genes: CD16a (FcγRIIIA) and CD16b (FcγRIIIB). Both molecules belong to the immunoglobulin (Ig) superfamily and share a similar low binding affinity for IgG for IgG.
#### CD16a (FcγRIIIA)
CD16a is the primary activating Fc receptor responsible for triggering powerful cytotoxic responses against antibody-coated target cells.
- **Structure:** CD16a is a 50 to 70 kD integral transmembrane protein.
- **Signaling Adaptors:** Because the receptor itself must transmit activation signals into the cell, CD16a in NK cells noncovalently associates with one of two different intracellular signaling proteins: either the FcεRIγ chain or the ζ (zeta) chain. Both of these associated signaling subunits contain Immunoreceptor Tyrosine-based Activation Motifs (ITAMs) in their cytoplasmic tails, which are essential for initiating the intracellular kinase cascades that drive cell activation.
- **Cellular Expression:** It is prominently expressed on Natural Killer (NK) cells, but is also found on macrophages and dendritic cells (DCs).
- **Ligand Specificity and Binding:** CD16a specifically binds to the Fc regions of the IgG1 and IgG3 antibody subclasses. Because it is a low-affinity receptor, it does not effectively bind free, monomeric IgG circulating in the plasma. Instead, it only binds tightly to clustered IgG molecules that are aggregated on the surface of a microbe or an infected target cell.
- **Key Effector Function (ADCC):** The most highly tested physiological function of CD16a is its role as the master mediator of **Antibody-Dependent Cell-Mediated Cytotoxicity (ADCC)**. When the adaptive immune system produces IgG1 or IgG3 antibodies that coat a virus-infected cell, CD16a on an NK cell binds to the array of antibody Fc regions. This cross-linking activates the NK cell, prompting it to rapidly discharge its cytotoxic granules (containing perforin and granzymes) to induce apoptosis in the target cell, while simultaneously secreting activating cytokines such as IFN-γ. CD16a also assists macrophages in the phagocytosis of opsonized targets.
#### CD16b (FcγRIIIB)
CD16b functions primarily as an accessory receptor for neutrophils, assisting in the clearance of immune complexes.

- **Structure:** Like CD16a, CD16b is a 50 to 70 kD protein. However, it is fundamentally distinct in its membrane attachment: it is **not** a transmembrane protein. Instead, it is anchored to the outer leaflet of the plasma membrane via a glycophosphatidylinositol (GPI) linkage.
- **Cellular Expression:** CD16b expression is highly restricted; it is expressed exclusively on neutrophils.
- **Ligand Binding:** It is a low-affinity receptor that binds to the Fc region of IgG.
- **Key Effector Function:** Because CD16b is only GPI-linked and completely lacks both a transmembrane domain and a cytoplasmic signaling tail, it cannot independently transduce intracellular signals. Consequently, it mediates phagocytosis very inefficiently on its own. Its primary physiological role is to bind to IgG in immune complexes and act in synergy with another Fc receptor, FcγRII (CD32), to facilitate the full activation of neutrophils and enhance the clearance of opsonized pathogens.

### CD32
**CD32 (FcγRII)** CD32 refers to the **FcγRII** family of receptors. These are 40 kD integral membrane proteins belonging to the immunoglobulin (Ig) superfamily that function as low-affinity receptors for the Fc region of immunoglobulin G (specifically the IgG1 and IgG3 subclasses). Because of this low affinity, they do not efficiently bind free circulating monomeric IgG, but instead bind tightly to aggregated IgG molecules that are clustered on the surface of opsonized microbes or within immune complexes.

A unique structural hallmark of the CD32 family compared to other Fc receptors (like CD16/FcγRIII or CD64/FcγRI) is that they are **single-chain receptors**. They do not require separate, noncovalently associated signaling subunits (like the FcR γ chain or TCR ζ chain) to function; instead, their activating or inhibitory signaling motifs are located directly within their own intrinsic cytoplasmic tails.

Because of gene duplication and diversification, CD32 exists in three distinct isoforms—**FcγRIIA, FcγRIIB, and FcγRIIC**. While their extracellular ligand-binding domains and IgG specificities are highly similar, their cytoplasmic tails, cellular distribution, and immunological functions are fundamentally different.

#### The Activating Isoforms: CD32a (FcγRIIA) and CD32c (FcγRIIC)
- **Cellular Expression:**
    - _FcγRIIA:_ Widely expressed on innate immune cells, including neutrophils, mononuclear phagocytes (macrophages/monocytes), dendritic cells (DCs), eosinophils, and platelets.
    - _FcγRIIC:_ Expressed primarily on mononuclear phagocytes, neutrophils, and Natural Killer (NK) cells.
- **Signaling Mechanism:** The cytoplasmic tails of both the A and C isoforms contain **Immunoreceptor Tyrosine-based Activation Motifs (ITAMs)**.
- **Physiological Roles:** When these receptors are physically cross-linked by clustering around an IgG-coated microbe, the ITAMs are phosphorylated, delivering a potent activation signal into the cell.
    - In phagocytes (macrophages and neutrophils), this strictly promotes the engulfment and phagocytosis of the opsonized particles, followed by cellular activation to destroy the ingested target.
    - On dendritic cells, these activating receptors play a critical role in capturing antigen-antibody immune complexes, allowing the DC to internalize the antigen, process it, and present the protein fragments to T cells to drive adaptive immunity.
#### The Inhibitory Isoform: CD32b (FcγRIIB)
FcγRIIB is one of the most highly tested molecules in advanced immunology because it is the quintessential off-switch for humoral immunity.
- **Cellular Expression:** It is expressed on macrophages, DCs, neutrophils, mast cells, and notably, it is the **only Fc receptor expressed on B cells**.
- **Signaling Mechanism:** The cytoplasmic tail of FcγRIIB contains an **Immunoreceptor Tyrosine-based Inhibitory Motif (ITIM)**. When an antibody-antigen immune complex simultaneously binds to both the B-cell receptor (BCR) and the FcγRIIB receptor on the same B cell, the receptors co-aggregate. This cross-linking causes an SRC family kinase (like LYN) to phosphorylate the ITIM on FcγRIIB.
    - _The Phosphatase Pathway:_ The phosphorylated ITIM provides a docking site that recruits **SHIP** (SH2 domain-containing inositol 5-phosphatase).
    - SHIP directly antagonizes the PI3-kinase signaling cascade generated by the BCR. It does this by stripping a phosphate group from PIP3 (converting it back into PIP2) in the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane, completely halting the downstream activation of enzymes like BTK, PLCγ, and PDK1.
- **Physiological Role (Antibody Feedback):** This biochemical blockade creates a physiologic control mechanism known as **antibody feedback**. When a humoral immune response has successfully produced enough IgG antibodies, those secreted antibodies bind to the remaining circulating antigens to form immune complexes. These complexes bind to FcγRIIB on B cells, triggering the ITIM/SHIP cascade that terminates any further B-cell activation and halts antibody production.
- **Innate Immune Regulation:** On mast cells, co-aggregation of FcγRIIB with the highly activating IgE receptor (FcεRI) leads to the inhibition of mast cell degranulation, helping to regulate immediate hypersensitivity reactions.
- **Clinical and Pathological Significance:**
    - Mice engineered to lack the gene for FcγRIIB exhibit uncontrolled antibody production and massive B-cell hyperactivation.
    - In humans, specific genetic polymorphisms in the _FcγRIIB_ gene have been directly linked to a heightened susceptibility to developing the severe systemic autoimmune disease **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)**.
    - _Therapeutic Manipulation:_ The administration of Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)—a treatment using pooled human IgG at high doses for autoimmune diseases like immune thrombocytopenia—is thought to work partly by binding to and upregulating FcγRIIB on B cells and myeloid cells, actively delivering inhibitory signals that drastically dampen inflammation and autoantibody production.

### CD64
**CD64 (FcγRI)** CD64, functionally known as FcγRI, is a 72 kD integral membrane protein that belongs to the immunoglobulin (Ig) superfamily. It distinguishes itself as the major high-affinity Fc receptor utilized by phagocytes to recognize and bind immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies.

**1. Structure and Signaling Mechanism**
The functional CD64 receptor is a multi-chain complex composed of a ligand-binding chain and a distinct signaling dimer:
- **The Ligand-Binding α Chain:** The large extracellular amino-terminal region of the CD64 α chain physically folds into three tandem Ig-like domains, which are responsible for specifically binding to the Fc portion of IgG antibodies.
- **The FcR γ Chain Dimer:** To transmit intracellular signals, the α chain of CD64 must be noncovalently associated with a disulfide-linked homodimer of a signaling protein known as the FcR γ chain. This specific γ chain is a highly conserved signaling component that is also shared by the signaling complexes of other receptors, including FcγRIII (CD16a), FcαR (CD89), and the IgE receptor FcεRI.
- **ITAM Signaling:** The FcR γ chain features a very short extracellular amino terminus and a large cytoplasmic carboxyl terminus that is structurally homologous to the ζ (zeta) chain of the T-cell receptor (TCR) complex. Most importantly, similar to the TCR ζ chain, the cytoplasmic tail of the FcR γ chain contains an Immunoreceptor Tyrosine-based Activation Motif (ITAM). When multiple CD64 receptors are clustered together by binding to multivalent antigen-antibody complexes, these ITAMs couple the receptor aggregation directly to the activation of intracellular protein tyrosine kinases, resulting in robust cell activation.

**2. Ligand Specificity and Binding Kinetics**
- **High Affinity:** Unlike the low-affinity Fc receptors (such as CD16 and CD32), CD64 is a high-affinity receptor.    
- **Monomeric Binding:** Because of this exceptionally high affinity, CD64 possesses the unique capacity to bind free, monomeric IgG circulating in the plasma, whereas low-affinity receptors can effectively bind only to aggregated IgGs found in immune complexes.
- **Isotype Specificity:** In humans, CD64 specifically binds to the heavy chains of the IgG1 and IgG3 subclasses. In mice, the receptor preferentially binds to the IgG2a and IgG2b/2c antibody subclasses.

**3. Cellular Expression** CD64 is prominently expressed on cells of the myeloid lineage, primarily on mononuclear phagocytes (monocytes and macrophages) and neutrophils (especially when activated). It is also found on the surface of eosinophils. In mice, CD64 serves as a universal marker for all circulating blood monocytes, expressed alongside CD115 and CD11b.

**4. Physiological Roles and Effector Functions**
- **Phagocytosis of Opsonized Targets:** CD64 is the single most important receptor for the phagocytosis of opsonized particles. When the receptor binds to a microbe heavily coated with IgG1 or IgG3 molecules, it actively triggers the engulfment of the particle into a phagosome.
- **Phagocyte Activation:** Upon cross-linking by antibody-coated microbes, the ITAM-mediated signals delivered by CD64 potently stimulate the microbicidal activities of the phagocyte, directing it to completely destroy the ingested pathogen.
- **Antibody-Dependent Cellular Cytotoxicity (ADCC):** In addition to driving phagocytosis, CD64 signaling is also capable of mediating ADCC, assisting in the targeted destruction of antibody-coated cells.

## Fcα Receptor / FcαR
CD89, commonly known as the Fcα receptor (FcαR), is a 55 to 75 kD integral transmembrane protein belonging to the immunoglobulin (Ig) superfamily. It functions primarily as an activating receptor that links IgA-mediated humoral immune responses with the robust effector functions of innate phagocytic cells.

**1. Structure and Intracellular Signaling Mechanism**
- **Subunit Association:** The CD89 molecule itself constitutes the ligand-binding chain of the receptor. However, because it lacks intrinsic intracellular signaling motifs, it must noncovalently associate with a separate signaling component to function. Specifically, the FcαR α chain is associated with a disulfide-linked homodimer of a signaling protein known as the **FcR common γ chain**.
- **The Shared FcR γ Chain:** This γ chain is a highly conserved and critical signaling subunit utilized across multiple different immune receptor complexes. In addition to FcαR, it serves as the essential signaling component for the high-affinity IgG receptor FcγRI (CD64), the ADCC-mediating receptor FcγRIIIA (CD16a), and the high-affinity IgE receptor FcεRI.
- **ITAM-Mediated Activation:** The cytoplasmic tail of this associated FcR γ chain contains an Immunoreceptor Tyrosine-based Activation Motif (ITAM). When multiple FcαR molecules are physically clustered together by binding to multivalent targets (such as a microbe heavily coated with antibodies), nonreceptor tyrosine kinases of the SRC family phosphorylate the tyrosine residues within these ITAMs. This phosphorylation provides a docking site to recruit and activate downstream protein tyrosine kinases, directly coupling the extracellular receptor engagement to massive intracellular cellular activation cascades.

**2. Ligand Specificity and Binding Kinetics**
- **Binding Affinity:** FcαR is functionally classified as a low-affinity receptor, exhibiting an equilibrium dissociation constant (Kd​) of approximately 10−6 M. Because of this low affinity, it requires immune complexes or densely opsonized targets to trigger effective cross-linking.
- **Ligands:** As its functional name implies, FcαR is the specific receptor for the Fc region of **IgA** antibodies. Notably, it also possesses the capacity to bind to **IgM** antibodies, as well as to the acute-phase plasma pentraxin, **C-reactive protein (CRP)**.

**3. Cellular Expression Profile** The expression of CD89 is heavily concentrated on circulating and tissue-resident cells of the myeloid lineage. It is prominently expressed on:
- Neutrophils
- Monocytes
- Macrophages
- Eosinophils Additionally, expression of CD89 has been identified on specific, limited subsets of both T lymphocytes and B lymphocytes.

**4. Physiological Roles and Effector Functions**
- **Phagocytosis and Phagocyte Activation:** The primary and best-characterized effector function of CD89 is the robust activation of phagocytic cells. When IgA or IgM antibodies successfully bind to and coat an invading microbe, the FcαR on the surface of neutrophils, macrophages, or monocytes recognizes the bound antibodies. This engagement actively triggers the engulfment (phagocytosis) of the opsonized microbe and stimulates the microbicidal activities of the phagocyte to destroy the ingested target.
- **Unresolved Functions:** Despite its clearly defined capacity to bind IgA, IgM, and CRP and to trigger phagocytosis, some texts note that the complete scope of CD89's physiological functions within the broader immune system remains partially unclear.

## Fc Epsilon Receptors (FcεR) 
The Fc epsilon receptors are specialized molecules that bind to the Fc region of immunoglobulin E (IgE) antibodies. In the immune system, they exist in two structurally and functionally distinct classes: the high-affinity receptor **FcεRI**, which is the master regulator of immediate hypersensitivity (allergic) reactions, and the low-affinity receptor **FcεRII (CD23)**.

### FcεRI: The High-Affinity IgE Receptor
FcεRI is the principal receptor responsible for sensitizing mast cells and basophils, allowing them to use IgE as an antigen-specific surface receptor.
**Structure and Binding Kinetics**
- **Binding Affinity:** FcεRI possesses an extraordinarily high binding affinity for monomeric IgE, with an equilibrium dissociation constant (Kd​) of approximately 1×10−10 M. This affinity is higher than that of any other Fc receptor for its respective antibody ligand. Because of this, even though normal plasma IgE concentrations are extremely low (<5×10−10 M), the FcεRI receptors on mast cells are almost entirely occupied by IgE at all times, even in nonatopic (non-allergic) individuals.
- **Polypeptide Subunits:** On mast cells and basophils, the fully functional receptor is expressed as an αβγ2​ tetramer:
    - **The** α **Chain:** The amino-terminal extracellular portion of this chain contains two Ig-like domains that physically form the binding cleft for the IgE Fc region.
    - **The** β **Chain:** This chain acts as a signaling component. Its cytoplasmic carboxy-terminal domain contains a single Immunoreceptor Tyrosine-based Activation Motif (ITAM).
    - **The** γ **Chains:** These two identical polypeptide chains are linked by a disulfide bond and are structurally homologous to the ζ (zeta) chain of the T-cell receptor (TCR) complex. Each γ chain contains one ITAM in its cytoplasmic portion. This same γ chain is also used as the signaling subunit for FcγRI (CD64), FcγRIIIA (CD16a), and FcαR (CD89).
- **Eosinophil Expression:** Eosinophils also express FcεRI, but at very low levels, and they uniquely express it as an αγ2​ trimer (lacking the β chain). There is currently no convincing evidence that eosinophils can be actively triggered by antigen binding to this modified receptor.

**Mechanism of Sensitization and Activation**
- **Sensitization:** The simple binding of IgE to FcεRI does not activate the cell; it merely "sensitizes" it. Interestingly, the binding of IgE actively increases the surface expression of FcεRI, creating a positive feedback loop that amplifies the potential for IgE-mediated reactions.
- **Cross-linking:** Activation strictly requires that a multivalent antigen (allergen) binds to and physically cross-links multiple IgE molecules that are already attached to adjacent FcεRI receptors on the cell membrane.

**The Intracellular Biochemical Signaling Cascade** When an allergen cross-links the FcεRI complex, a massive, multifaceted intracellular signaling cascade is initiated, utilizing kinases similar to those found in T-cell and B-cell activation.
- **Proximal Kinase Activation:** The SRC family tyrosine kinases **LYN** and **FYN** are constitutively associated with the cytoplasmic tail of the FcεRI β chain. Upon receptor cross-linking, LYN rapidly phosphorylates the ITAMs located on both the β and γ chains.
- **SYK Recruitment:** The phosphorylated ITAMs on the γ chains serve as high-affinity docking sites for **SYK**, a critical tyrosine kinase. Once docked, SYK becomes activated and subsequently phosphorylates multiple downstream adaptor molecules and enzymes.
- **Parallel PI3K Activation:** Simultaneously, the FYN kinase phosphorylates a docking/adaptor protein called **GAB2**, which directly leads to the activation of Phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K).

**The Three Major Physiologic Responses to FcεRI Signaling** The coordinated activation of SYK and PI3K drives three distinct, highly regulated cellular effector functions:
1. **Degranulation (Immediate Release):**
    - SYK activates Phospholipase Cγ (PLCγ), which cleaves membrane PIP2 to generate IP3 and Diacylglycerol (DAG). IP3 triggers the massive release of Ca2+ from the endoplasmic reticulum, while DAG and Ca2+ jointly activate Protein Kinase C (PKC).
    - Activated PKC phosphorylates the myosin light-chain components of the actin-myosin network just beneath the plasma membrane, causing it to disassemble and allowing the preformed secretory granules to contact the plasma membrane.
    - The massive spike in intracellular Ca2+ activates calcium sensor proteins that promote the interaction of **SNARE proteins** on the granule and plasma membranes. Overcoming the inhibitory regulation of RAB3 GTPases, the SNARE complexes catalyze membrane fusion, causing the explosive exocytosis (within seconds) of preformed mediators like histamine, tryptase, and chymase.
2. **De Novo Lipid Mediator Synthesis:**
    - The combination of elevated Ca2+ and the activation of MAP kinases (like ERK, triggered downstream of the SYK/RAS cascade) activates the cytosolic enzyme **Phospholipase A2 (PLA2)**.
    - PLA2 hydrolyzes membrane phospholipids to yield arachidonic acid, which is rapidly shunted into the cyclooxygenase or lipoxygenase pathways to synthesize entirely new lipid mediators, most notably Prostaglandin D2 (PGD2) and Leukotriene C4 (LTC4).
3. **Cytokine Gene Transcription (Late-Phase Response):**
    - The complex web of adaptors and kinases eventually leads to the nuclear translocation of master transcription factors, specifically **NFAT**, **NF-κB**, and **AP-1** (via the c-JUN N-terminal kinase pathway).
    - These transcription factors initiate the robust gene transcription and subsequent secretion of a massive array of cytokines (e.g., IL-4, IL-5, IL-13, TNF), which orchestrate the delayed, leukocyte-rich late-phase inflammatory reaction.

**Attenuation of FcεRI Signaling** To prevent runaway anaphylactic responses, FcεRI signaling is tightly regulated by inhibitory receptors on the mast cell surface. The most important of these is **FcγRIIB** (CD32). When an IgG-containing immune complex co-aggregates FcγRIIB with FcεRI, the LYN kinase phosphorylates the ITIM on the cytoplasmic tail of FcγRIIB. This phosphorylated ITIM recruits the lipid phosphatase **SHIP** (SH2 domain–containing inositol 5-phosphatase), which strips phosphate groups from membrane lipids (PIP3 to PIP2), effectively dismantling the necessary signaling complexes and halting mast cell degranulation.

### FcεRII (CD23): The Low-Affinity IgE Receptor
While FcεRI dominates allergic pathophysiology, the immune system also utilizes a second, structurally unrelated IgE receptor.
- **Structure and Family:** Unlike the Ig-superfamily structure of FcεRI, FcεRII (also known as CD23) is a 45 kD integral membrane protein that belongs to the **C-type mammalian lectin family**.
- **Binding Affinity:** It is a low-affinity receptor, binding to IgE with a Kd​ of approximately 1×10−7 M (a thousand times weaker than FcεRI).
- **Cellular Expression:** It is primarily expressed on activated B lymphocytes, monocytes, macrophages, eosinophils, and Langerhans cells. Its expression is heavily induced by the Th2 cytokine IL-4.
- **Physiologic Role:** Despite its presence across several leukocyte populations, the precise biological and immunological functions of FcεRII (CD23) remain largely unknown.

## Fcα/μ Receptor 
The Fcα/μ receptor is a specialized immunoglobulin-binding molecule that is distinctly categorized among the "trafficking" Fc receptors, distinguishing it from the classic signaling receptors such as the Fcγ and Fcε families.

**1. Structure and Signaling Mechanism**
The most defining biochemical characteristic of the Fcα/μ receptor is **its complete lack of intracellular signaling capacity**. Unlike the activating or inhibitory Fc receptors that rely on Immunoreceptor Tyrosine-based Activation Motifs (ITAMs) or Inhibitory Motifs (ITIMs), the Fcα/μ receptor possesses absolutely no signaling motifs within its structure.

**2. Ligand Specificity and Binding Kinetics** 
As its name implies, the Fcα/μ receptor exhibits dual isotype specificity, meaning it is capable of binding to the Fc regions of both IgM and IgA antibodies.
- **IgM Binding:** It functions primarily as a high-affinity receptor for IgM, exhibiting an equilibrium dissociation constant (Kd​) of approximately 10−9 M.
- **IgA Binding:** While it also recognizes IgA, its binding affinity for IgA is significantly lower than its affinity for IgM.

**3. Cellular Expression Profile**
Compared to the broad myeloid distribution of receptors like [[CD32]] or [[CD64]], the expression of the Fcα/μ receptor is highly restricted both by cell type and by anatomic location. It is expressed exclusively on B lymphocytes and macrophages, and this expression is heavily concentrated specifically at mucosal sites.

**4. Physiological Roles and Effector Functions** Despite lacking known signal transduction motifs and being formally grouped with trafficking receptors (such as the Poly-Ig receptor and the neonatal Fc receptor, FcRn), the primary documented function of the Fcα/μ receptor is to mediate the opsonization of microbes that have been coated with IgM or IgA antibodies. This targeted opsonization specifically at mucosal surfaces aids the localized mucosal immune cells in handling and clearing restricted pathogens.

**High-Yield The Complete Network of IgM Receptors**
The Fcα/μ receptor is not the only molecule in the immune system capable of binding IgM. Three other known IgM-interacting receptors:
- **The Poly-Ig Receptor (Trafficking):** Like the Fcα/μ receptor, the Poly-Ig receptor is a high-affinity (Kd​∼10−9 M) trafficking receptor. However, it is expressed exclusively on mucosal epithelial cells rather than immune cells. It binds strictly to multimeric IgM and dimeric IgA, and its sole function is to transport these secretory immunoglobulins from the basolateral surface across the epithelial barrier into the lumen via transcytosis.
- **CD89 / FcαR (Signaling):** As previously noted, this low-affinity receptor (Kd​∼10−6 M) is expressed heavily on neutrophils, monocytes, macrophages, and eosinophils. While primarily an IgA receptor, it can also bind IgM. Unlike the Fcα/μ receptor, CD89 contains an ITAM-bearing FcR γ chain and actively triggers phagocytosis and massive phagocyte activation.
- **TRIM21 (Cytosolic):** TRIM21 is an unusual cytosolic Fc receptor found inside nucleated cells. While it is primarily a high-affinity receptor for IgG, it also possesses the capacity to bind to the Fc regions of internalized IgM and IgA with low affinity. If a virus coated with IgM manages to enter the cell's cytosol, TRIM21 binds the antibody and uses its intrinsic ubiquitin E3 ligase activity to mark the entire viral particle-antibody complex for rapid proteasomal degradation.
