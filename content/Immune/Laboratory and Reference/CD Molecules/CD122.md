## CD122 (IL-2/15Rβ / Interleukin-2/15 Receptor Beta Chain)
CD122 is a 75 kD integral membrane protein that functions as a critical signal-transducing subunit for the receptors of two distinct cytokines: Interleukin-2 (IL-2) and Interleukin-15 (IL-15). As a member of the type I cytokine receptor family, its primary role is to transduce intracellular signals that drive the proliferation, survival, and differentiation of key lymphocyte populations.

**1. Structure and Receptor Complex Assembly**
Unlike the unique alpha chains (such as [[CD25]] for IL-2), CD122 does not function in isolation. Instead, it must noncovalently associate with the common $\gamma$ chain ($\gamma_c$, or [[CD132]]) to form functional receptor complexes:
- **The IL-2 Receptor (IL-2R):** CD122 constitutes the $\beta$ chain of the IL-2 receptor. Resting naive T cells and Natural Killer (NK) cells constitutively express the dimeric IL-2R$\beta\gamma_c$ complex. This baseline dimeric receptor possesses a moderate (or intermediate) binding affinity for IL-2, with an equilibrium dissociation constant ($K_d$) of approximately $10^{-9}$ M.
- **The High-Affinity IL-2 Receptor:** Upon activation by an antigen and costimulators, T cells dramatically upregulate the expression of the IL-2R$\alpha$ chain ([[CD25]]) and, to a lesser extent, increase their expression of the CD122 $\beta$ chain. The newly synthesized $\alpha$ chain associates with the pre-existing $\beta\gamma_c$ dimer to form the complete, heterotrimeric IL-2R$\alpha\beta\gamma_c$ complex. The addition of the $\alpha$ chain alters the receptor's kinetics, increasing its affinity for IL-2 by 100-fold to a $K_d$ of approximately $10^{-11}$ M, which allows the vigorously responding T cells to capture low physiological concentrations of IL-2 to drive massive clonal expansion.
- **The IL-15 Receptor (IL-15R):** CD122 is also an indispensable component of the IL-15 receptor, acting as the shared $\beta$ chain alongside the $\gamma_c$ chain ([[CD132]]) and a unique IL-15R$\alpha$ chain.

**2. Intracellular Signaling Mechanism** 
While the $\alpha$ chains of these cytokine receptors are primarily responsible for enhancing ligand binding affinity, CD122 (along with CD132) is fundamentally responsible for intracellular signal transduction. The cytoplasmic domains of the $\beta$ and $\gamma_c$ chains physically engage the **JAK-STAT (Janus kinase–signal transducers and activators of transcription)** signaling pathway. Upon ligand binding and receptor clustering, associated JAK kinases phosphorylate the receptor tails, recruiting and activating STAT transcription factors that subsequently translocate to the nucleus to drive the expression of genes required for lymphocyte growth and survival.

**3. Trans-Presentation of IL-15**
An immunologically unique and highly tested mechanism involving CD122 is how it receives IL-15 signals. IL-15 can be expressed on the surface of dendritic cells (DCs) bound tightly to the IL-15R$\alpha$ chain. The DC then presents this bound IL-15 _in trans_ to adjacent target cells (such as NK cells or memory CD8+ T cells) that express the CD122 ($\beta$) and CD132 ($\gamma_c$) subunits. This trans-presentation activates signaling pathways via CD122/CD132 on the NK cell, heavily promoting NK cell proliferation and the production of the cytokine IFN-$\gamma$.

**4. Clinical and Therapeutic Applications**
Because the expression of different IL-2 receptor chains dictates how a cell responds to IL-2, CD122 is a major target for experimental immunotherapies.
- **Targeting Effector Cells in Cancer:** The complete high-affinity trimeric receptor ($\alpha\beta\gamma_c$) is constitutively expressed on immunosuppressive regulatory T cells (Tregs), whereas the dimeric receptor ($\beta\gamma_c$, relying heavily on CD122) is found on resting effector T cells and NK cells. In cancer immunotherapy, administering systemic, wild-type IL-2 can inadvertently stimulate Tregs (via CD25), thereby suppressing the desired anti-tumor immune response. To circumvent this, researchers are designing mutated, recombinant versions of IL-2 engineered to selectively bind _only_ to the IL-2R$\beta\gamma_c$ (CD122/CD132) dimeric receptor complex. The objective of this tailored therapy is to selectively activate and massively expand the patient's cytolytic effector T cells and NK cells without engaging CD25 and feeding the suppressive Tregs.
