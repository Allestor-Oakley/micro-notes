## CD45 (Leukocyte Common Antigen / LCA / B220)
CD45, also known as leukocyte common antigen (LCA) or B220, is a critical regulatory integral membrane protein expressed on all hematopoietic cells. It is a uniquely _activating_ phosphatase in signal transduction, and its alternatively spliced isoforms are the premier clinical markers used to distinguish naive from memory lymphocytes.
### Structure and Signaling Mechanism (The Activating Phosphatase)
- **Family Classification:** CD45 belongs to the protein tyrosine phosphatase receptor family and structurally contains extracellular regions characteristic of the fibronectin type III family.
- **The Paradox of its Function:** In the immune system, the vast majority of phosphatases (such as SHP1, SHP2, and SHIP) serve to _attenuate_ or inhibit lymphocyte signaling by stripping activating phosphate groups off of signaling intermediates. CD45 is the major exception to this rule: it is a tyrosine phosphatase that actively _facilitates_ and is required for both T-cell and B-cell activation.
- **Biochemical Mechanism:** The cytoplasmic tail of the CD45 receptor contains tandem protein tyrosine phosphatase domains. In resting lymphocytes, SRC family kinases (such as **LCK** and **FYN** in T cells) are held in an inactive state partly because they have phosphate groups attached to specific _inhibitory_ tyrosine residues. CD45 actively dephosphorylates these specific inhibitory tyrosine residues. By removing the molecular "brake," CD45 allows the SRC family kinases to transition into their active states, priming the T cell or B cell so that it can rapidly initiate signal transduction pathways upon antigen receptor engagement.
### Isoforms and Splicing (Markers of Antigen Exposure)
CD45 exists in multiple molecular weight isoforms (ranging from 180 to 220 kD), which are generated by alternative splicing of the _CD45R_ gene transcripts. The specific isoform expressed on the cell surface is dynamically altered based on the cell's lineage and its history of antigen exposure.
In humans, the expression of these isoforms is the classic method used to distinguish naive T cells from those that have been previously activated.
- **CD45RA (220 kD isoform):**
    - This heavy isoform is prominently expressed on **naive T cells**, B cells, and monocytes.
    - _High-Yield Exception:_ While it is the classic marker for "naive" cells, CD45RA is also uniquely re-expressed on a specific, highly differentiated subset of human CD8+ effector memory T cells. Because they are effector memory cells that express the RA isoform, they are specifically termed **TEMRA cells** (T effector memory RA+).
- **CD45RO (180 kD isoform):**
    - This is the shortest isoform. Following activation by an antigen, newly generated effector and memory T cells alter their RNA splicing to express CD45RO instead of CD45RA.
    - It serves as the defining surface marker for **memory T cells** and activated effector T cells.
    - It is also found on a subset of B cells, monocytes, and macrophages.
- **CD45RB (190, 205, and 220 kD isoforms):**
    - These intermediately sized isoforms are expressed on B cells and a specific subset of T cells.
### Clinical and Experimental Nuance
While the CD45RA versus CD45RO distinction is a foundational concept for distinguishing naive from memory T-cell populations in humans, it is important to note that this phenotypic division is not absolutely rigid. Experimental evidence has documented that there can be interconversion between CD45RA+ and CD45RO+ T-cell populations.
