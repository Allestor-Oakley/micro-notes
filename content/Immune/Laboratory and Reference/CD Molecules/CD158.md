## CD158 (Killer Cell Immunoglobulin-like Receptors / KIRs)
CD158 refers to a critical and highly diverse family of receptors collectively known as **Killer Cell Immunoglobulin-like Receptors (KIRs)**. These are 50 to 58 kD integral membrane proteins that play a profound role in regulating the cytolytic activity and cytokine secretion of innate and adaptive immune cells.

**1. Structure and Classification**
- **Family:** As their name implies, KIRs belong to the **Immunoglobulin (Ig) superfamily**. Their extracellular ligand-binding regions are characterized by the presence of a structural domain known as an "Ig fold".
- **Nomenclature (DL vs. DS):** The nomenclature for specific KIRs (such as KIR2DL or KIR2DS) gives insight into their structure. The "D" refers to the number of extracellular Ig domains (e.g., 2D or 3D). The "L" refers to a "Long" cytoplasmic tail, which is associated with inhibitory functions. The "S" refers to a "Short" cytoplasmic tail, which is associated with activating functions.

**2. Cellular Expression Profile**
- **Natural Killer (NK) Cells:** CD158 receptors are most prominently expressed on NK cells, where they constitute the largest group of NK cell inhibitory receptors.
- **T Lymphocytes:** They are also expressed on a specific subset of T cells, most notably CD8+ Cytotoxic T Lymphocytes (CTLs).

**3. Ligand Specificity**
- The primary extracellular ligands for CD158 receptors are **Major Histocompatibility Complex Class I (MHC-I) molecules** (specifically HLA-A, HLA-B, and HLA-C in humans).
- When expressed on CD8+ CTLs, these receptors recognize MHC-I molecules on target cells but, crucially, they bind them independently of the specific peptide being displayed in the MHC cleft.

**4. Dual Functionality: Inhibitory vs. Activating Receptors** The CD158 family is fundamentally divided by its intracellular signaling capabilities, functioning as either a brake or an accelerator for cell activation:
- **The Inhibitory KIRs (e.g., KIR2DL, KIR3DL):**
    - **Signaling Mechanism:** These receptors contain **Immunoreceptor Tyrosine-based Inhibition Motifs (ITIMs)** directly within their long cytoplasmic tails.
    - **Physiological Role:** When these inhibitory KIRs bind to normal MHC-I molecules on the surface of healthy host cells, the ITIMs are phosphorylated. This transduces potent inhibitory signals (via the recruitment of phosphatases) that override any activating signals, actively preventing the NK cell or CTL from attacking and killing the normal, healthy cell. They act as a surveillance mechanism to verify "self".
- **The Activating KIRs (e.g., KIR2DS, KIR3DS):**
    - **Signaling Mechanism:** These receptors have short cytoplasmic tails that lack inhibitory motifs. However, to transduce signals, their transmembrane regions noncovalently associate with a separate signaling adaptor protein known as **DAP12**.
    - **Physiological Role:** DAP12 contains **Immunoreceptor Tyrosine-based Activation Motifs (ITAMs)**. When the activating KIR binds to its ligand (which may include certain HLA-C or HLA-B molecules, or potentially uncharacterized pathogen-encoded ligands), the associated ITAMs on DAP12 are phosphorylated, driving an intracellular kinase cascade that stimulates the NK cell to kill the target cell and secrete cytokines.

**5. Genetics, Polymorphism, and Clinical Significance** A uniquely testable feature of the CD158/KIR family is its complex genetics.
- **High Polymorphism:** The _KIR_ genes are highly polymorphic, meaning numerous allelic variants exist across the human population.
- **Haplotypes:** These genes are inherited together in blocks called _KIR haplotypes_. There are two major haplotypes (along with some rarer ones).
- **Individual Diversity:** Because of this genetic arrangement, the exact number and ratio of activating versus inhibitory CD158 receptors vary drastically from person to person. Consequently, the threshold for NK cell activation differs among individuals.
- **Pathology:** Certain _KIR_ haplotypes are clinically associated with an increased susceptibility to specific disorders, including spontaneous abortion and uveitis (a form of eye inflammation).
