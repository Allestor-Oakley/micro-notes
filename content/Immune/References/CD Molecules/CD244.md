**CD244 (2B4)** CD244, most frequently referred to in immunology as **2B4**, is a critical regulatory receptor that modulates the cytolytic activity of specific lymphocyte subsets.
### Structure and Classification

- **Molecular Weight and Family:** CD244 is a 41 kD integral membrane protein that belongs to the Immunoglobulin (Ig) superfamily.
- **Subfamily:** More specifically, it is a member of the [[CD2]]/CD48/[[CD58]] family, and it belongs to a specialized subgroup of costimulatory receptors known as the **SLAM (Signaling Lymphocytic Activation Molecule)** family. Like all SLAM family members, it contains extracellular Ig domains and a long cytoplasmic tail designed for intracellular signaling.
### Cellular Expression Profile 
Unlike broader receptors, the expression of CD244 is restricted primarily to cytotoxic subsets of lymphocytes. It is prominently expressed on:
- [[NK Cell|Natural Killer (NK) cells]]
- CD8+ [[T-Cell|T cells]]
- γδ [[T-Cell|T Cells]]
### Ligand Specificity and Function
- The primary function of CD244 is to modulate the cytolytic (cell-killing) activity of [[NK Cell]].
- It functions as a receptor for the molecule CD48.
### Intracellular Signaling Mechanism 
The signaling cascade of CD244 is a highly testable concept because of its reliance on unique motifs and adaptor proteins:
- **ITSMs:** Unlike many receptors that use ITAMs or ITIMs, the cytoplasmic tail of 2B4 contains **Immunoreceptor Tyrosine-based Switch Motifs (ITSMs)**.
- **The SAP Adaptor:** To transduce signals, the ITSM motifs on 2B4 must bind to a specific intracellular adaptor protein known as **SAP**.
- **Kinase Recruitment:** Once SAP binds to the receptor, it induces activating signals by recruiting and engaging the SRC family tyrosine kinase **FYN**.
### Clinical Significance: X-Linked Lymphoproliferative Syndrome (XLP)
- The reliance of 2B4 on the SAP adaptor has profound clinical implications. A genetic mutation in the SAP adaptor protein causes a severe immunodeficiency known as **X-linked lymphoproliferative syndrome (XLP)**.
- Because SAP is nonfunctional, 2B4 cannot transmit its activating signals. This defective 2B4 signaling directly contributes to the severe immune deficits seen in XLP patients, which are characterized by an inability to mount effective antibody and cytotoxic T-cell responses (particularly against viral infections like the [[EBV|Epstein-Barr Virus]]).
