### CD58

#### Structure & Genetics
*   **Molecular Structure & Subunits:** CD58 (also known as leukocyte function-associated antigen 3, or LFA-3) is a 55–70 kD cell-surface glycoprotein that belongs to the Immunoglobulin (Ig) superfamily of proteins. Its extracellular region consists of two homologous Ig-like domains: an amino-terminal variable (V)-like domain that mediates ligand binding, and a constant (C)-like domain adjacent to the cell membrane. CD58 is structurally unique because it is expressed on cell surfaces in two biochemically distinct isoforms: either as a glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-linked protein anchored in the outer leaflet of the lipid bilayer, or as a classical transmembrane polypeptide containing a single hydrophobic membrane-spanning segment and a short cytoplasmic tail.
*   **Gene Locus & Rearrangement:** CD58 is germline-encoded and does not undergo somatic gene recombination (V(D)J rearrangement) or somatic hypermutation. The specific chromosomal gene locus is N/A.
*   **Associated Chaperone Proteins:** N/A
*   **Polymorphisms & Isotypes:** Exists in two distinct membrane-anchored structural isoforms (isotypes) generated via alternative splicing of its pre-mRNA: the GPI-anchored isoform and the transmembrane isoform. Genetic polymorphisms in its extracellular domains are N/A.

#### Expression & Binding
*   **Cells Expressing this Molecule:** Broadly expressed on both hematopoietic and nonhematopoietic cell lineages. This includes professional antigen-presenting cells (dendritic cells, macrophages, B lymphocytes), vascular endothelial cells, epithelial cells, fibroblasts, and erythrocytes (red blood cells).
*   **Primary Ligands / Antigens:** CD2 (LFA-2), a 50 kD Ig superfamily glycoprotein constitutively expressed on T lymphocytes, developing thymocytes, and Natural Killer (NK) cells.
*   **Binding Kinetics & Affinity:** CD58 interacts with CD2 with low affinity (the dissociation constant Kd is in the micromolar range), which is a key biophysical characteristic of cell-adhesion molecules. This low monomeric affinity prevents irreversible cell-cell locking, allowing lymphocytes to dynamically and reversibly scan the membranes of APCs or target cells. When clustered, the CD58-CD2 interaction achieves high-avidity attachment.
*   **Co-receptors Required for Binding:** N/A
*   **Role as a Transplant, Blood Group, or Tumor Antigen:**
    - **Transplant Immunology:** CD58-CD2 interactions serve as a major accessory pathway in transplant rejection. CD58 expressed on donor vascular endothelial cells or donor-derived APCs binds CD2 on recipient T cells, stabilizing the alloreactive immunological synapse and delivering costimulatory signals that drive acute vascular and cellular graft rejection.
    - **Erythrocyte Expression:** Expressed on human erythrocytes, which historically mediated the binding of sheep red blood cells to human T lymphocytes in the diagnostic sheep red blood cell (SRBC) "E-rosette" assay (sheep erythrocytes express a CD58 homolog that binds human CD2). It does not function as a classical human blood group antigen.
    - **Tumor Immunosurveillance:** CD58 functions as an important immunosurveillance target; its expression is often lost or downregulated on tumor cells, permitting them to evade NK-cell and CTL-mediated cytolysis.

#### Signal Transduction
*   **Intracellular Signaling Motifs:**
    - **GPI-anchored isoform:** Completely lacks a cytoplasmic tail and thus contains no cytosolic signaling motifs.
    - **Transmembrane isoform:** Possesses a short cytoplasmic tail that does not contain classical activating (ITAM), inhibitory (ITIM), or switch (ITSM) motifs.
*   **Associated Kinases & Adaptor Proteins:** CD58 lacks intrinsic catalytic/kinase activity and does not directly recruit cytosolic kinases or adaptor proteins. However, its binding to CD2 on T cells or NK cells drives CD2 clustering, which recruits the Src-family tyrosine kinase FYN to the proline-rich cytoplasmic tail of CD2 and physically associates CD2 with the CD3ζ signaling chains of the T-cell receptor (TCR) complex.
*   **Downstream Transcription Factors Activated:** Does not directly activate transcription factors within the expressing cell. However, by engaging and activating CD2 on opposing lymphocytes, it indirectly facilitates the downstream activation of T-cell and NK-cell transcription factors, including **NFAT**, **AP-1 (Fos/Jun)**, and **NF-κB**, leading to transcription of cytokines (e.g., IL-2, IFN-γ) and survival proteins.

#### Functional Outcomes
*   **Cellular Response to Ligation:**
    - **Intercellular Adhesion:** Bridges opposing cell membranes to stabilize physical contact between APCs (or target cells) and lymphocytes (T cells and NK cells), contributing directly to the structural assembly of the immunological synapse.
    - **T-Cell Costimulation:** Ligation of CD2 by CD58 delivers Signal 2 costimulatory inputs that work synergistically with TCR-mediated Signal 1 to lower the activation threshold of naive T cells, promoting lymphocyte survival (via upregulation of Bcl-xL and Bcl-2), secretion of IL-2, and clonal expansion.
    - **Enhancement of Cytotoxicity:** Stabilizes the immunological synapse between Cytotoxic T Lymphocytes (CTLs) or NK cells and target cells, establishing a sealed secretory cleft that allows for the polarized and precise exocytosis of lytic granules (perforin, granzymes) to lyse infected or malignant target cells.
*   **Role in Immune Cascade:** Functions as a critical biophysical and biochemical accessory pathway during both the induction phase of adaptive immunity (naive CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell priming by mature DCs in lymph nodes) and the effector phase (killing of target cells by CTLs and NK cells in peripheral tissues, and CD4+ T-cell-mediated classical activation of macrophages).
*   **Regulation & Down-modulation:** N/A

#### Clinical & Pharmacologic Relevance
*   **Associated Immunodeficiencies (if mutated):** N/A
*   **Targeted Biologic Therapies (Monoclonal Antibodies):** Therapeutic manipulation of the CD2-CD58 pathway is used to block injurious immune reactions (such as graft rejection or autoimmune flares). Soluble fusion proteins consisting of the extracellular ligand-binding domain of CD58 linked to the Fc portion of human IgG (e.g., alefacept) bind to and block CD2 on T lymphocytes, preventing costimulatory engagement by cell-surface CD58 and inhibiting T-cell-mediated tissue inflammation.
*   **Pathogen Sabotage or Mimicry:**
    - **Viral Evasion:** Certain chronic viruses (such as HIV and Epstein-Barr Virus/EBV) downregulate the expression of CD58 on infected host cells, preventing stable immune synapse formation with CTLs or NK cells and allowing the virus to escape cytolytic clearance.
    - **Tumor Evasion:** Many human cancers downregulate cell-surface CD58 as a mechanism of immune evasion, rendering them resistant to CD2-dependent NK-cell and CTL-mediated destruction.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - **"Immunological Synapse Anchor":** Describes its principal biophysical role in stabilizing and sealing the zone of physical contact between immune cells.
    - **"Micromolar Adhesion Glycoprotein":** Highlights the low-affinity monomeric binding kinetics of CD58-CD2 interactions, which are evolutionarily optimized to permit rapid, non-destructive surface scanning by lymphocytes.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - **Evolutionary Absence in Rodents:** A major evolutionary exception is that rodent species (including mice and rats) completely lack a functional CD58 gene due to evolutionary loss. In rodents, the primary ligand for CD2 is **CD48** (another GPI-anchored Ig superfamily molecule of the CD2 family), whereas in humans, CD2 binds with high specificity to CD58. Consequently, murine models of T-cell activation and adhesion cannot be directly translated to human CD58-dependent clinical immunology.
