CD44 is an 80 to greater than 100 kD highly glycosylated integral membrane protein. Its primary known ligand is hyaluronan, a major macromolecular component of the extracellular matrix. The molecule is broadly expressed across various cell types in the body, prominently including leukocytes and erythrocytes.

In the context of the immune system, CD44 serves several critical physiological and developmental functions:
**1. Leukocyte Adhesion and Effector T-Cell Retention** CD44 
functions fundamentally as an adhesion molecule that mediates the binding of leukocytes to endothelial cells and to the extracellular matrix. Its expression is highly dynamic during an adaptive immune response. When naive T cells are activated by antigens, they significantly upregulate the expression of CD44 on their cell surface. By tightly binding to hyaluronan, this increased CD44 expression helps to physically retain the activated effector T cells within extravascular tissues, specifically concentrating them at sites of active infection and tissue damage where they are needed to eliminate microbes.

**2. Phenotypic Marker for Early T-Cell Development** 
In developmental immunology, CD44 is extensively utilized alongside CD25 (the IL-2 receptor alpha chain) to categorize and track the earliest stages of T-cell maturation within the thymic cortex. During these early phases, immature thymocytes lack the expression of the T-cell receptor (TCR), CD4, and CD8, earning them the designation of "Double-Negative" (DN) cells. The dynamic surface expression of CD44 characterizes their precise maturational progression:
- **DN1 Stage:** The earliest, largely undifferentiated thymocytes that have recently arrived in the thymus from the bone marrow are CD44+ and CD25−.
- **DN2 Stage (Pro-T Cell):** As these immature cells definitively commit to the T-cell lineage, they retain CD44 expression but also upregulate CD25, making them CD44+ CD25+.
- **DN3 Stage (Pre-T Cell):** At this critical stage, the cells actively rearrange their TCR beta, gamma, and delta genes. Coinciding with this gene rearrangement, they downregulate CD44, becoming CD44− CD25+.
- **DN4 Stage:** Following the successful productive rearrangement of the TCR beta chain and the reception of survival signals through the pre-TCR complex, the developing thymocytes progress to a CD44− CD25− state before they transition to the CD4+ CD8+ double-positive stage.
