### CD95 (FAS / The Death Receptor)

CD95, almost universally referred to in immunology as **FAS**, is the receptor component of this pair.
- **Structure and Family:** CD95 is an integral membrane protein that functions as a **homotrimer composed of 45 kD chains**. It is a prominent member of the **Tumor Necrosis Factor Receptor (TNFR) superfamily**. Like other members of this superfamily, its extracellular portion features conserved cysteine-rich domains required for ligand binding.
- **Cellular Expression Profile:** Because the immune system must have the ability to eliminate virtually any infected or damaged cell in the body, CD95 has a very **broad expression profile** across many different cell types. Within specialized immune compartments, it is notably expressed at exceptionally high levels on **Mucosa-Associated Invariant T (MAIT) cells**, a unique subset of CD8+ [[T-Cell|T cells]] found abundantly in the liver and gastrointestinal tract.
- **Physiological Function:** The sole established function of CD95 is to act as a death receptor. When it binds to its specific ligand (CD178), it delivers potent intracellular signals that rapidly drive the cell into apoptotic death.
### CD178 (FAS Ligand / FASL)
CD178, formally known as **FAS Ligand (FASL)**, is the specific biological trigger for the CD95 receptor.
- **Structure and Family:** CD178 belongs to the **Tumor Necrosis Factor (TNF) superfamily** of cytokines. Unlike soluble cytokines, CD178 functions primarily as a membrane-bound protein. Structurally, it is expressed as a **homotrimer composed of 31 kD subunits**.
- **Cellular Expression Profile:** In stark contrast to the broadly expressed CD95 receptor, the expression of CD178 (FASL) is highly restricted and tightly regulated. It is predominantly expressed exclusively on the surface of **activated T cells**.
- **Physiological Function:** CD178 serves as a targeted weapon. When an activated T cell expressing CD178 encounters a cell expressing CD95, it physically engages the receptor to trigger the death of that target cell.
### The Intracellular Signaling Mechanism (The Apoptotic Cascade)
The interaction between these molecules initiates the classic extrinsic "death receptor" pathway of apoptosis:
1. **Receptor Engagement:** Membrane-bound CD178 (FASL) homotrimers on the surface of an activated T cell physically bind to the preformed trimeric CD95 (FAS) receptors on the surface of a target cell.
2. **Adaptor Recruitment:** This trimeric cross-linking induces a conformational change in the intracellular domains of the CD95 receptor. The cytoplasmic tail of CD95 recruits specific intracellular adaptor proteins.
3. **Caspase Activation:** The recruitment of these adaptors provides a critical docking and activation site that leads to the proteolytic cleavage and activation of **caspase-8**.
4. **Execution of Apoptosis:** Active caspase-8 functions as a master initiator enzyme, cascading down to activate effector caspases that systematically dismantle the target cell's structural and genetic components, resulting in apoptotic cell death.
### Immunological Significance
The CD95/CD178 (FAS/FASL) signaling axis is absolutely fundamental to immune homeostasis and defense, contributing heavily to two major processes:
- **Target Cell Elimination:** It provides a direct, contact-dependent mechanism for cytotoxic T lymphocytes to destroy specific target cells, such as those infected by viruses or undergoing malignant transformation.
- **Self-Tolerance and Lymphocyte Development:** The death receptor pathway is a massive pillar of immune regulation. It is actively utilized during lymphocyte development to eliminate highly self-reactive cells, thereby establishing and maintaining immunologic self-tolerance. Furthermore, it helps physically eliminate previously activated lymphocytes once an infection has been cleared, a necessary process to prevent runaway, tissue-damaging inflammation.
