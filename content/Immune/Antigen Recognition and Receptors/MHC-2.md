## HLA Class II Molecules
**Structure and Subtypes** Unlike Class I, HLA Class II molecules are composed of two distinct MHC-encoded, highly polymorphic chains: an α chain (32-34 kD) and a β chain (29-32 kD).
- **Classical Subtypes:** The classical human class II loci are **HLA-DP**, **HLA-DQ**, and **HLA-DR**. Each locus contains separate _A_ and _B_ genes that encode the α and β chains, respectively (e.g., DRα and DRβ). In humans, the β chain possesses the highest degree of genetic polymorphism.
- **Nonclassical (Intracellular) Subtypes:** The Class II region also encodes unique nonclassical molecules involved strictly in antigen processing rather than cell surface display, specifically **HLA-DM** and **HLA-DO**.

**Expression Profile** The expression of HLA Class II molecules is highly restricted. They are constitutively expressed only on specialized, "professional" antigen-presenting cells (APCs), predominantly dendritic cells, macrophages, B lymphocytes, and thymic epithelial cells. Their expression can be massively induced on APCs and some vascular endothelial or epithelial cells by IFN-γ.

### Physiological Functions
- **Antigen Presentation to CD4+ [[T Cell|T Cells]]:** Classical HLA-DP, -DQ, and -DR molecules specialize in capturing and presenting peptides derived from extracellular proteins that have been internalized (via phagocytosis or endocytosis) into acidic vesicles, such as late endosomes and lysosomes. Within these vesicles, proteins are degraded by cathepsins, and the peptides are loaded onto HLA Class II molecules. Because the ends of the Class II peptide-binding cleft are open, they bind substantially longer peptides, ranging from 13 to 25 amino acids or more (with 12 to 16 residues being optimal). They display these peptides to the TCR and CD4 coreceptor of CD4+ helper [[T Cell|T lymphocytes]].
- **Directing Effector Responses:** By presenting extracellular antigens to CD4+ [[T Cell|T cells]], HLA Class II orchestrates the adaptive immune response. The activated CD4+ [[T Cell|T cells]] subsequently secrete cytokines to activate macrophages (eradicating phagocytosed microbes) or act on B cells (driving high-affinity, class-switched antibody production).
- **Thymic Selection:** Within the thymus, thymic epithelial cells express HLA Class II molecules to present self-peptides to developing thymocytes. This interactions governs the positive and negative selection of the CD4+ [[T Cell|T-cell]] repertoire, ensuring the generation of self-tolerant, functional T cells.
- **Functions of Nonclassical Class II Subtypes:**
    - **HLA-DM:** During biosynthesis in the ER, the Class II cleft is physically blocked by the [[CD74|invariant chain (Ii)]] to prevent premature binding of cytosolic peptides. After transport to the lysosome, the invariant chain is degraded, leaving a small blocking fragment called CLIP. **HLA-DM** is a nonpolymorphic protein localized to lysosomes that acts as an enzymatic peptide exchanger; it physically binds to the HLA Class II molecule, ejects the CLIP fragment, and facilitates the loading of high-affinity antigenic peptides.
    - **HLA-DO:** An intracellular dimeric molecule that binds to HLA-DM in lysosomes and serves as a negative regulator of HLA-DM-catalyzed peptide exchange.

### High-Yield Clinical and Genetic Correlates
Because HLA genes are the most polymorphic genes in the human genome, specific alleles dictate an individual's immune responsiveness (functioning as Immune Response or _Ir_ genes).

Consequently, inheriting certain extended HLA haplotypes is the strongest genetic risk factor for developing specific autoimmune diseases. For instance, carrying the **HLA-B27** (Class I) allele confers an extraordinarily high odds ratio (>100) for developing Ankylosing Spondylitis. Furthermore, specific Class II heterodimers are closely linked to autoimmunity: the **HLA-DRB1****0401-DQA1****0301-DQB1*0302** haplotype heavily predisposes patients to Type 1 Diabetes, while alleles containing a "shared epitope" sequence in **HLA-DRB1** strongly predispose to Rheumatoid Arthritis.
