## HLA Class I Molecules
**Structure and Subtypes** All HLA Class I molecules are heterodimers composed of a highly polymorphic heavy α chain (which contains α1, α2, and α3 domains) noncovalently linked to a nonpolymorphic, non-MHC-encoded 12-kD protein known as β2-microglobulin.
- **Classical Subtypes:** The classical human class I genes are **HLA-A**, **HLA-B**, and **HLA-C**. They are codominantly expressed, meaning an individual inherits and expresses alleles for each from both parents, maximizing the variety of presented peptides.
- **Nonclassical Subtypes (Class I-like):** The HLA locus also encodes structurally related nonclassical molecules, including **HLA-E**, **HLA-F**, **HLA-G**, **MIC-A**, and **MIC-B**. Additionally, **CD1** and **MR1** function as highly specialized MHC class I-like molecules.

**Expression Profile** Classical HLA Class I molecules are constitutively expressed on virtually all nucleated cells. Their expression is profoundly upregulated by type I interferons (IFN-α and IFN-β) during early viral infections, and by the cytokine IFN-γ, which serves to amplify immune recognition.
### Physiological Functions
- **Antigen Presentation to CD8+ T Cells:** The primary function of classical HLA-A, -B, and -C molecules is to bind peptides derived from cytosolic proteins and present them to the T-cell receptor (TCR) and CD8 coreceptor on CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs). These proteins are degraded by the cytosolic proteasome, and the resulting peptides are transported into the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) via the TAP transporter, where they are loaded onto the HLA Class I cleft. Because the cleft is closed at its ends, it specifically accommodates short peptides of 8 to 11 amino acids. This pathway allows CD8+ CTLs to survey all nucleated cells for intracellular viral infections, mutated tumor antigens, or cross-presented antigens (captured by dendritic cells), subsequently killing the targeted cells.
- **Regulation of Natural Killer (NK) Cells:** HLA Class I molecules act as critical ligands for both inhibitory and activating receptors on NK cells, such as the Killer Cell Immunoglobulin-like Receptors (KIRs). Healthy host cells express self HLA Class I, which binds to inhibitory KIRs on NK cells to suppress their cytolytic action. Viruses and tumor cells frequently downregulate HLA Class I to evade CD8+ T cells; NK cells detect this "missing self" signal and immediately destroy the abnormal cells.
- **Functions of Nonclassical Class I Subtypes:**
    - **HLA-G:** Exclusively expressed on placental trophoblast cells. Because it is nonpolymorphic, it prevents maternal NK cells from attacking the fetal tissue, establishing essential immune privilege at the maternal-fetal interface.
    - **HLA-E:** Binds specific leader sequence peptides cleaved from other newly synthesized classical HLA Class I molecules. It presents these fragments on the surface to the CD94/NKG2A (inhibitory) and CD94/NKG2C (activating) receptors on NK cells, essentially providing proof that the cell is actively producing normal MHC-I.
    - **CD1 Family:** These class I-like molecules lack a peptide-binding cleft but feature a hydrophobic pocket designed to capture and display nonpeptide antigens, specifically lipids and glycolipids (such as those from the cell walls of mycobacteria) to invariant Natural Killer T (iNKT) cells.
    - **MR1 (MHC class I–related protein 1):** A nonpolymorphic molecule that presents unique bacterial and fungal metabolites derived from the riboflavin (vitamin B2) synthesis pathway to a specialized subset of lymphocytes known as Mucosa-Associated Invariant T (MAIT) cells.
