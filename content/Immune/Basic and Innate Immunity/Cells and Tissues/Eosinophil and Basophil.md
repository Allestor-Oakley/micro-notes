### Eosinophil and Basophil
#### Origin & Maturation
*   **Lineage & Precursors:** Eosinophils and basophils are lineage-committed, terminally differentiated blood granulocytes belonging to the myeloid lineage. In adult postnatal life, both cells arise from multipotent hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) in the bone marrow.
    - *Eosinophil lineage:* Under the influence of colony-stimulating factors and interleukins, common myeloid-megakaryocyte-erythroid progenitors commit to the eosinophil lineage, forming colony-forming units for eosinophils (CFU-eo). These commit to eosinophiloblast precursors, which subsequently mature into mature neutrophilic-like segmented cells with distinctive cytoplasmic eosinophilic granules.
    - *Basophil lineage:* Common myeloid progenitors differentiate into committed basophil progenitors (CFU-b), which progress through the basophiloblast precursor stage in the bone marrow to become mature blood basophils.
    - *Key Cytokines:* Granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) and interleukin-3 (IL-3) promote the early differentiation of both lineages from immature myeloid progenitors. Interleukin-5 (IL-5) serves as the principal, highly specific lineage growth and differentiation factor that selectively drives eosinophil commitment and maturation from myeloid precursors in the bone marrow. In contrast, mature basophil development from its committed precursors is primarily driven by IL-3.
*   **Site of Development & Maturation:** Postnatal development and maturation of both eosinophils and basophils occur exclusively within the spongelike reticular framework of the red bone marrow of flat bones (such as the ribs, sternum, vertebrae, and iliac crests). Fetal precursors develop in the embryonic yolk sac blood islands and fetal liver before hematopoiesis transitions to the bone marrow near birth. Once fully mature, both cell types are released from the bone marrow directly into the vascular circulation.
*   **Key Transcription Factors for Differentiation:** N/A (The sources do not specify the master transcriptional regulators responsible for directing the commitment of common myeloid progenitors to either the eosinophil or basophil lineages, detailing their development primarily as cytokine-driven by IL-5, IL-3, and GM-CSF).
*   **Selection & Tolerance Mechanisms:** Neither eosinophils nor basophils express somatically recombined antigen receptors (such as TCRs or BCRs); thus, they do not undergo central clonal selection (positive or negative selection) or receptor editing in the generative lymphoid organs. Non-clonal self-tolerance is maintained by the expression of inhibitory surface receptors that contain immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibition motifs (ITIMs) in their cytoplasmic tails. Phosphorylation of these ITIM tyrosines by Src-family kinases recruits SH2 domain-containing tyrosine phosphatases (such as SHP-1 and SHP-2) or lipid phosphatases (such as SHIP) that dephosphorylate activating signaling intermediates, attenuating cell activation. For example, humans express Siglec-8, an ITIM-containing receptor on eosinophils that binds specific sialic acid ligands on healthy host tissues to deliver inhibitory signals. They also express CD47, which serves as a "don't eat me" signal by engaging SIRPα on phagocytes to protect healthy host cells from auto-phagocytosis or tissue-destructive degranulation.
*   **Circulation & Extravasation Dynamics:**
    - *Basophils:* Constitute less than 1% (normally 0.5% or less) of circulating blood leukocytes. They circulate in the blood with a short lifespan of only a few days and are normally not present in healthy peripheral tissues, but they can be recruited into tissue sites of active type 2 inflammation.
    - *Eosinophils:* Constitute approximately 2% (ranging from 1% to 5%) of total circulating blood leukocytes. They circulate in the bloodstream for a few hours before migrating into tissues. Under steady-state conditions, some eosinophils are resident in peripheral tissues, particularly in the mucosal linings of the respiratory, gastrointestinal (except the esophagus under normal conditions), and genitourinary tracts. Their tissue numbers increase dramatically during inflammatory or infectious reactions due to recruitment from the blood. Their lifespan in tissues spans days to weeks.
    - *Extravasation Cascade (Tissue Homing):* Recruitment of both cell types to sites of helminthic infection or late-phase allergic reactions is mediated by a multi-step adhesion cascade in postcapillary venules:
        1. *Margination and Rolling:* Locally produced cytokines TNF and IL-1, along with the epithelial alarmins IL-25, IL-33, and TSLP, activate endothelial cells to express E-selectin (CD62E). Histamine released from activated tissue mast cells or basophils triggers the rapid translocation of P-selectin (CD62P) from Weibel-Palade bodies to the endothelial cell luminal surface. Sialyl Lewis X carbohydrate ligands (such as PSGL-1/CD162) on the surface of eosinophils and basophils bind weakly to E- and P-selectins, causing the cells to margination and roll along the endothelium.
        2. *Integrin Activation:* Chemokines displayed on the endothelial lumen bind to G protein-coupled chemokine receptors on the rolling leukocytes. For eosinophils, the chemokine eotaxin (CCL11), produced abundantly by mucosal epithelial and stromal cells in response to IL-4 or IL-13, binds to the chemokine receptor CCR3 (CD193). For both cells, other chemoattractants include complement anaphylatoxin C5a (binding to CD88), lipid mediator platelet-activating factor (PAF), and leukotriene B4 (LTB4). This GPCR signaling activates leukocyte integrins, transforming them from a low-affinity bent conformation to a high-affinity extended conformation.
        3. *Firm Adhesion:* Eosinophils and basophils express very late antigen 4 (VLA-4; α4β1 integrin; CD49d/CD29) and Mac-1 (CD11b/CD18). IL-4 and IL-13 produced by Th2 cells and ILC2s selectively upregulate vascular cell adhesion molecule 1 (VCAM-1, the ligand for VLA-4) on endothelial cells. Firm binding of VLA-4 to VCAM-1, and Mac-1/LFA-1 to intercellular adhesion molecule 1 (ICAM-1) and ICAM-2, arrests the rolling cells.
        4. *Transmigration (Diapedesis):* The cells flatten and migrate through interendothelial junctions (paracellular transmigration) into the extravascular tissue space, a process mediated by homophilic CD31 (PECAM-1) interactions and reversible opening of endothelial adherens junctions.

#### Receptors & Surface Markers
*   **Defining CD Markers:**
    - *Eosinophils:* CD11b (integrin αM chain), CD18 (integrin β2 chain), CD49d (integrin α4 chain of VLA-4), CD125 (IL-5 receptor α chain), CD193 (CCR3, eotaxin receptor), CD89 (FcαR), CD162 (PSGL-1), CD35 (type 1 complement receptor, CR1), CD23 (FcεRII), and CD62L (L-selectin).
    - *Basophils:* CD123 (IL-3 receptor α chain), CD125 (IL-5 receptor α chain), CD193 (CCR3), CD11b, CD18, CD49d, CD162, CD88 (C5a receptor GPCR), and CD62L. Unlike mast cells, mature basophils do not express CD117 (c-KIT).
*   **Antigen Recognition Receptors:**
    - *Eosinophils:* Express Fc receptors for IgA (FcαR; CD89) and IgG (high-affinity FcγRI/CD64 and low-affinity FcγRIIA/CD32), complement receptors (CR1/CD35, CR3/CD11bCD18, CR4/CD11cCD18), and Toll-like receptors (TLRs). Eosinophils express low levels of FcεRI, but because it is expressed primarily as an αγ2 trimer lacking the signaling β chain, there is no convincing evidence that eosinophils can be activated by IgE-antigen complexes. Eosinophils also express the low-affinity IgE receptor FcεRII (CD23).
    - *Basophils:* Express exceptionally high levels of the high-affinity IgE receptor FcεRI. FcεRI is a tetrameric complex composed of one α chain (which binds the Fc region of IgE), one β chain, and two identical disulfide-linked γ chains (which transduce activation signals). Due to its very high affinity (Kd ~10^-10 M), these receptors are fully occupied by monomeric IgE even at low serum concentrations, coating basophils with IgE. Basophils also express TLRs and complement receptors (such as CD88 and C3aR).
*   **Co-stimulatory & Inhibitory Receptors:**
    - *Eosinophils:* Express Siglec-8 (human) and other sialic-acid-binding lectins that contain cytosolic ITIMs to transduce inhibitory signals.
    - *Basophils:* Express the low-affinity inhibitory IgG receptor FcγRIIB (CD32b) containing a cytosolic ITIM. Co-clustering of FcγRIIB with FcεRI by IgG-IgE-allergen complexes leads to LYN-mediated phosphorylation of the ITIM, which recruits the lipid phosphatase SHIP to degrade membrane PIP3 to PIP2, thereby blocking downstream calcium mobilization and PKC activation to terminate basophil responses.
*   **Cytokine & Chemokine Receptors:**
    - *Eosinophils:* Express the high-affinity IL-5 receptor (composed of a unique ligand-binding CD125/IL-5Rα chain and the common β chain CD131/βc shared with IL-3 and GM-CSF receptors). They also express the IL-3 receptor (CD123/CD131), GM-CSF receptor (CD116/CD131), IL-4 receptor (CD124/CD132), IL-13 receptor (CD213a1/CD132), and CCR3 (CD193).
    - *Basophils:* Express the high-affinity IL-3 receptor (composed of CD123/IL-3Rα and the common β chain CD131/βc), the IL-5 receptor (CD125/CD131), IL-4 receptor, IL-13 receptor, and CCR3 (CD193).
*   **Tissue Homing & Adhesion Molecules (Integrins/Selectins):** PSGL-1 (CD162), VLA-4 (CD49d/CD29), Mac-1 (CD11b/CD18), CR4 (CD11c/CD18), and L-selectin (CD62L).

#### Activation & Differentiation
*   **Primary Activation Signals (Signal 1, 2, 3):**
    - *Eosinophils:*
        - *Signal 1 (Cytokine Activation):* Ligation of the IL-5 receptor (CD125/CD131) by IL-5 produced by Th2 cells and ILC2s. This is the primary signal for mature eosinophil survival and activation.
        - *Signal 2 (Opsonin / PRR Recognition):* Ligation of FcαR (CD89) or FcγR by IgA- or IgG-coated particles, or engagement of TLRs by microbial PAMPs.
        - *Signal 3 (Chemokine & Complement Cues):* Binding of CCL11 (eotaxin) to CCR3, or complement fragments C5a and C3a to their GPCRs.
    - *Basophils:*
        - *Signal 1 (Allergen Cross-linking):* Multivalent antigen (allergen) cross-linking adjacent IgE molecules bound to the high-affinity FcεRI receptors on the cell surface.
        - *Signal 2 (Anaphylatoxins):* Direct binding of complement cleavage fragments C5a and C3a (anaphylatoxins) to G protein-coupled receptors (such as CD88) on the cell surface.
        - *Signal 3 (Growth Factor Priming):* Priming by IL-3 binding to its receptor (CD123/CD131), which significantly enhances their sensitivity and degranulation response to other stimuli.
*   **Signal Transduction Cascades:**
    - *FcεRI-Mediated Activation (Basophils):*
        1. Allergen-mediated cross-linking of IgE-FcεRI aggregates the receptors, activating the Src-family tyrosine kinase LYN (associated with the FcεRI β chain) and FYN. LYN phosphorylates the ITAMs in the cytoplasmic tails of the FcεRI β and γ chains.
        2. Tyrosine kinase SYK is recruited to the phosphorylated ITAMs of the γ chains, becomes activated, and phosphorylates adaptor proteins including LAT and SLP65 (BLNK).
        3. Phospholipase Cγ (PLCγ) is recruited to the LAT complex and activated. PLCγ catalyzes the hydrolysis of membrane PIP2 to yield IP3 and DAG.
        4. IP3 triggers the release of intracellular calcium from the endoplasmic reticulum. The depletion of these ER calcium stores is sensed by STIM1, which oligomerizes and opens cell surface calcium release-activated calcium (CRAC) channels (composed of ORAI proteins), resulting in a sustained influx of extracellular calcium into the cytosol.
        5. Elevated cytosolic calcium activates the calmodulin-dependent phosphatase calcineurin, which dephosphorylates cytoplasmic NFAT, causing it to translocate to the nucleus.
        6. DAG and calcium activate protein kinase C (PKC). Activated PKC phosphorylates the myosin light-chain component of actin-myosin complexes beneath the plasma membrane, causing disassembly. This allows cytoplasmic granules to move to the cell cortex. SNARE proteins on the granule membranes and plasma membrane interact to form a multimeric complex that catalyzes membrane fusion, triggering exocytosis (degranulation) within seconds of activation.
        7. FYN phosphorylates the docking adaptor GAB2, activating PI3-kinase. PI3-kinase converts membrane PIP2 to PIP3. PIP3 recruits PH-domain-containing proteins, including PDK1 and AKT, promoting survival. This pathway also activates cytosolic phospholipase A2 (PLA2) via MAP kinases. PLA2 hydrolyzes membrane phospholipids to release arachidonic acid, which is converted via the 5-lipoxygenase pathway into cysteinyl leukotrienes (LTC4, LTD4, LTE4).
        8. Small G proteins (RAS, RAC) are activated via exchange factors (such as SOS recruited by GRB2), initiating a MAP kinase cascade (RAF-MEK-ERK and RAC-JNK), which phosphorylates AP-1 and NF-κB, stimulating the transcription and secretion of cytokines (TNF, IL-4, IL-13, IL-5).
    - *IL-5 and IL-3 Receptor Signaling (JAK-STAT in both):*
        - Cytokine binding to the receptor induces dimerization of the unique α chain with the common β chain (βc). This activates receptor-associated Janus kinase 2 (JAK2), which phosphorylates tyrosine residues on the βc cytoplasmic tail.
        - These phosphotyrosines recruit SH2 domain-containing STAT transcription factors (primarily STAT3 and STAT5). STATs are phosphorylated by JAK2, form homodimers, and translocate to the nucleus to induce transcription of target genes, including anti-apoptotic BCL-2 and BCL-XL to promote survival.
*   **Polarization & Subsets:** Eosinophils and basophils do not differentiate into polarized helper T-cell-like subsets. They exit the bone marrow as a single, mature, terminally differentiated effector population. However, steady-state tissue-resident eosinophils (such as those in the lamina propria of the gastrointestinal tract) can exhibit phenotypic differences compared to inflammatory eosinophils recruited during acute type 2 reactions, adapting to local homeostatic cues.

#### Effector Functions & Secretory Profile
*   **Primary Effector Mechanisms:**
    - *Eosinophils:*
        - *Toxic Granule Protein Release (Degranulation):* Activated eosinophils undergo exocytosis of preformed cytoplasmic granules containing highly basic, cationic proteins:
            - **Major Basic Protein (MBP):** A highly basic polypeptide with no known enzymatic activity. It binds to and disrupts the cell wall/membrane of bacteria and the integument of helminths, and is highly toxic to host tissue cells.
            - **Eosinophil Cationic Protein (ECP):** A cationic protein that possesses ribonuclease activity, toxic to helminths, bacteria, and host tissue cells.
            - **Eosinophil Peroxidase (EPO):** An enzyme distinct from neutrophil myeloperoxidase. It catalyzes the oxidation of halide ions (like chloride or bromide) by hydrogen peroxide to form highly toxic hypohalous acids (hypochlorous or hypobromous acid), killing helminths, protozoa, and host cells.
            - **Lysosomal Hydrolases & Lysophospholipase:** Degrade microbial and helminthic cell walls and contribute to tissue remodeling.
        - *Charcot-Leyden Crystal Formation:* Eosinophils contain large amounts of the cytosolic protein **galectin-10**. Upon activation and cell lysis, galectin-10 aggregates to form bipryamidal hexagonal crystals known as **Charcot-Leyden crystals**. These crystals are found at sites of chronic type 2 inflammation (such as the sputum/bronchi of asthmatic patients). Charcot-Leyden crystals are highly proinflammatory: they activate the NLRP3 inflammasome in macrophages and prime dendritic cells to induce Th2 differentiation.
        - *Lipid Mediator Production:* Synthesize and release cyclooxygenase and lipoxygenase products: prostaglandins, platelet-activating factor (PAF), and **cysteinyl leukotrienes (LTC4, LTD4, LTE4)**, which cause prolonged, intense bronchoconstriction, vascular leak, and airway mucus secretion.
    - *Basophils:*
        - *Immediate Degranulation:*
            - **Histamine:** A vasoactive amine that binds H1, H2, and H3 receptors. Histamine contracts endothelial cells in postcapillary venules, causing vascular leak, plasma leakage (edema), and swelling. It stimulates endothelial production of vasodilators (NO, prostacyclin) causing vasodilation, and contracts bronchial/intestinal smooth muscle.
            - **Chondroitin Sulfate & Acidic Proteoglycans:** Bind and stabilize preformed basic mediators in granules.
            - **Neutral Proteases:** Cleave extracellular proteins, causing tissue damage and remodeling.
        - *Lipid Mediator Production:* Rapid synthesis of **LTC4, LTD4, LTE4 (cysteinyl leukotrienes)** and **Platelet-Activating Factor (PAF)**, causing prolonged bronchoconstriction, endothelial cell retraction, and mucosal fluid secretion.
        - *Cytokine Secretion:* Basophils are notable early producers of IL-4 and IL-13, which are critical for driving naive CD4+ T cells to polarize into Th2 cells, and driving B-cell immunoglobulin class switching to IgE.
*   **Key Cytokines Secreted:**
    - *Eosinophils:* IL-4 (drives Th2 differentiation), TGF-β (drives tissue repair, fibroblast collagen deposition, and subepithelial fibrosis), and chemokines (CXCL8/IL-8, IL-10, CCL5/RANTES, CCL3/MIP-1α, eotaxin).
    - *Basophils:* IL-4 and IL-13 (essential for B-cell class switching to IgE and Th2 skewing), IL-5 (promotes eosinophil production/activation), IL-3, TNF, and CCL3/MIP-1α.
*   **Target Cells & Pathogens:**
    - *Pathogens:* Parasitic **helminths** (nematodes, trematodes, cestodes) which are too large to be phagocytosed by neutrophils or macrophages. IgE-dependent degranulation of basophils/mast cells and IL-5-mediated activation of eosinophils release cationic proteins (MBP, ECP) that destroy the helminthic integument.
    - *Target Cells:* Bronchial and intestinal smooth muscle cells (induced to contract), endothelial cells (induced to leak/dilate), mucosal epithelial cells (induced by IL-13 to secrete excess mucus), and fibroblasts (stimulated by TGF-β to lay down collagen, causing tissue fibrosis).
*   **Memory Generation & Lifespan:** Neither eosinophils nor basophils belong to the adaptive immune system; they have germline-encoded receptors and do not generate immunological memory. Basophils are very short-lived (lifespan: **days**). Eosinophils survive slightly longer in tissues (lifespan: **days to weeks**).

#### Pathologic Relevance
*   **Role in Hypersensitivity or Autoimmunity:**
    - *Type I (Immediate) Hypersensitivity (Allergy, Asthma, and Atopy):*
        - Basophils circulate and are sensitized by IgE binding to their FcεRI receptors. Upon allergen re-exposure, allergen cross-linking of IgE-FcεRI triggers basophil activation and degranulation. Basophils contribute to immediate vascular and smooth muscle reactions (edema, bronchoconstriction, hives, anaphylaxis).
        - Eosinophils are the dominant, characteristic effector cells of the **late-phase reaction** of type I hypersensitivity, which develops 2 to 4 hours (maximal at 24 hours) after allergen challenge. Cytokines produced by mast cells (TNF) and Th2 cells (IL-4) upregulate E-selectin and VCAM-1 on endothelial cells. Eosinophils bind VCAM-1 via VLA-4 and are attracted into tissues by CCL11 (eotaxin) binding to CCR3. In the tissue, IL-5 activates eosinophils to release major basic protein (MBP) and eosinophil cationic protein (ECP), causing severe local host tissue damage.
        - In **atopic asthma**, persistent type 2 inflammation is driven by Th2 cells, ILC2s, and mast cells, leading to chronic recruitment of eosinophils. Eosinophil-derived cysteinyl leukotrienes (LTC4, LTD4, LTE4) cause prolonged bronchoconstriction and airway smooth muscle hypertrophy/hyperreactivity. Eosinophil-derived TGF-β drives subepithelial fibrosis (airway remodeling). Released galectin-10 forms Charcot-Leyden crystals in the bronchi, which activate the macrophage NLRP3 inflammasome, further amplifying inflammation.
*   **Microbial Evasion of this Cell Type:** N/A (The sources do not detail specific molecular mechanisms of microbial evasion that selectively target eosinophils or basophils, unlike intracellular pathogens that survive in macrophages or block MHC-I on tumor/viral infected cells).

*   **Key Experimental Markers:**
    - *Eosinophils:* Segmented nucleus (often bilobed), CD11b+, CD125+ (IL-5Rα), CD193+ (CCR3), CD49d+, Siglec-8+, MBP/ECP positive in granules, myeloperoxidase negative (but eosinophil peroxidase positive).
    - *Basophils:* Multilobed nucleus obscured by granules, CD123+ (IL-3Rα), CD125+, CD193+, CD203c+, FcεRI+, c-KIT (CD117) *negative* (distinguishes them from mast cells).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - *Hyper-IgE Syndromes (Job Syndrome):* Primary immunodeficiency caused by dominant-negative mutations in **STAT3** (autosomal dominant) or mutations in **DOCK8** (autosomal recessive). Characterized by severe eczema, marked eosinophilia, staphylococcal "cold" abscesses, recurrent pulmonary infections, and extremely elevated serum IgE. STAT3 mutation impairs IL-6, IL-10, IL-17, IL-21, and IL-22 signaling, preventing Th17 development (causing staphylococcal infection susceptibility due to lack of neutrophil recruitment). DOCK8 is a guanine nucleotide exchange factor required for cytoskeletal rearrangements and maintaining STAT3 phosphorylation.
    - *Omenn Syndrome:* A severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID) variant caused by hypomorphic (leaky) mutations in **RAG1 or RAG2**. Although T and B cells are mostly absent, there is an outgrowth of a few oligoclonal, self-reactive, activated T cells that secrete IL-4 and IL-5, driving severe eosinophilia and extremely high serum IgE levels (leading to neonatal erythroderma, desquamation, hepatosplenomegaly, and lymphadenopathy).
    - *STAT6 Gain-of-Function (STAT6 GOF):* Autosomal dominant mutation in STAT6 that causes hyperactive signaling downstream of the IL-4 and IL-13 receptors, forcing massive type 2 polarization. Patients present with severe, early-onset eczema, asthma, multiple food allergies, extremely high IgE, and eosinophilic gastrointestinal disease.
    - *Wiskott-Aldrich Syndrome (WAS):* Caused by mutations in the WASP gene regulating actin cytoskeletal polymerization. WAS platelets are small and microthrombocytopenic. Patients have eczema, elevated IgE and eosinophilia, and severe immunodeficiency.
    - *IPEX Syndrome (Immunodysregulation, Polyendocrinopathy, Enteropathy, X-linked):* Caused by mutations in **FOXP3**, preventing the development of functional regulatory T cells (Tregs). Results in unregulated activation of helper T cells (including Th2 cells), leading to multi-organ autoimmunity, severe allergic diseases, elevated IgE, and eosinophilia.
    - *Inhibitors / Targeted Biologics for Type 2 High Asthma:*
        - *Mepolizumab and Reslizumab:* Monoclonal antibodies that bind and neutralize soluble **IL-5**.
        - *Benralizumab:* Monoclonal antibody that binds to **IL-5Rα (CD125)** on eosinophils and basophils, inducing antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) mediated by NK cells to deplete eosinophils.
        - *Dupilumab:* Monoclonal antibody that blocks **IL-4Rα (CD124)**, which is the shared receptor subunit for both IL-4 and IL-13, thereby blocking both IL-4 and IL-13 signaling and inhibiting IgE class switching and eosinophil recruitment.
        - *Tezepelumab:* Monoclonal antibody that binds and blocks **TSLP** (thymic stromal lymphopoietin), an epithelial alarmin that activates dendritic cells and ILC2s to drive type 2 inflammation.
        - *Omalizumab:* Monoclonal antibody that binds to the Fc portion of free IgE, preventing it from binding to FcεRI on mast cells and basophils (neutralizing IgE).
