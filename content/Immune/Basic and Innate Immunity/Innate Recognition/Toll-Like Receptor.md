### Toll-Like Receptors (TLRs)
#### Structure & Genetics
*   **Molecular Structure & Subunits:** Toll-like receptors (TLRs) are dimeric integral membrane glycoproteins. They consist of an extracellular (or endosomal luminal) ligand-binding domain composed of 16 to 28 leucine-rich repeat (LRR) modules and a cytoplasmic Toll/Interleukin-1 Receptor (TIR) domain essential for initiating signaling cascades. Each LRR module is composed of 20 to 30 amino acids containing conserved LxxLxLxxN motifs (where L is leucine, x is any amino acid, and N is asparagine) and variable residues. The variable residues reside on the convex surface formed by α helices and β turns or loops, creating a structural architecture that binds directly to diverse pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) or their associated adaptors. Upon ligand binding, TLRs undergo physical conformation changes that drive homodimerization (e.g., TLR2/TLR2, TLR3/TLR3, TLR4/TLR4, TLR5/TLR5, TLR9/TLR9) or heterodimerization (e.g., TLR1/TLR2, TLR2/TLR6). These combinations expand the spectrum of recognizable molecular structures.
*   **Gene Locus & Rearrangement:** TLRs are germline-encoded, non-clonal receptors that do not undergo somatic rearrangement or V(D)J recombination. Human genomes express 10 functional TLR genes (TLR1 through TLR10). In contrast, mice express functional TLRs homologous to human TLR1 through TLR9, plus three additional TLRs (TLR11, TLR12, and TLR13), but they completely lack a functional TLR10 gene.
*   **Associated Chaperone Proteins:**
    - **UNC93B (UNC93B1):** An endoplasmic reticulum membrane protein required for delivery of the nucleic acid-sensing endosomal TLRs (TLR3, TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9) from the endoplasmic reticulum, where they are synthesized, to endosomal membranes. UNC93B is also critical for functional signaling of these receptors.
    - **MD2 (Myeloid Differentiation Protein 2):** An extracellular accessory protein that binds directly to the lipid A component of bacterial lipopolysaccharide (LPS). MD2 forms a complex with LPS, which then physically engages TLR4 on the cell membrane to initiate intracellular signaling.
    - **CD14:** A glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-linked membrane glycoprotein or soluble protein that binds LPS-LPS Binding Protein (LBP) complexes in extracellular fluids and delivers LPS to the MD2-TLR4 complex, facilitating activation.
    - **LBP (LPS-Binding Protein):** A soluble protein in blood and extracellular fluid that recognizes and binds bacterial LPS, presenting it to CD14.
*   **Polymorphisms & Isotypes:** TLR genes display several single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) across human populations that correlate with altered susceptibility to infectious or autoimmune diseases. For instance, a gain-of-function variant of the endosomal single-stranded RNA-recognizing receptor TLR7 is a known monogenic cause of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) in humans. TLR10 remains the least understood human isotype; unlike all other TLRs that are proinflammatory, there is emerging evidence that TLR10 acts in an anti-inflammatory manner.

#### Expression & Binding
*   **Cells Expressing this Molecule:** TLRs are widely expressed on professional antigen-presenting cells (APCs) including classical and plasmacytoid dendritic cells, monocytes, and macrophages. They are also highly expressed on neutrophils, mast cells, B lymphocytes, vascular endothelial cells, and mucosal epithelial cells (such as intestinal epithelial cells).
*   **Primary Ligands / Antigens:**
    - *Pathogen-Associated Molecular Patterns (PAMPs):*
        - **TLR1/TLR2 Heterodimer:** Bacterial triacyl lipopeptides.
        - **TLR2/TLR6 Heterodimer:** Bacterial diacyl lipopeptides.
        - **TLR2/TLR2 Homodimer:** Gram-positive bacterial cell wall peptidoglycan, lipoteichoic acid, and fungal zymosan.
        - **TLR3/TLR3 Homodimer:** Double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) typical of replicating viruses.
        - **TLR4/TLR4 Homodimer:** Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) from Gram-negative bacterial outer membranes.
        - **TLR5/TLR5 Homodimer:** Flagellin, the monomeric subunit of motile bacterial flagella.
        - **TLR7/TLR7 and TLR8/TLR8 Homodimers:** Single-stranded RNA (ssRNA) rich in guanosine and uridine, characteristic of viral genomes.
        - **TLR9/TLR9 Homodimer:** Unmethylated cytosine-guanine (CpG) dinucleotide repeats, common in prokaryotic and viral DNA but rare and methylated in vertebrate genomes.
    - *Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns (DAMPs) / Endogenous Ligands:*
        - **TLR2 and TLR4:** Bind extracellular Heat Shock Proteins (HSPs) (stress-induced chaperones) and High-Mobility Group Box 1 (HMGB1) (an abundant intracellular DNA-binding protein), both of which are released into the extracellular environment from damaged or necrotic host cells.
*   **Binding Kinetics & Affinity:** TLRs bind their ligands with fixed, non-clonally distributed affinities. Unlike adaptive antigen receptors (antibodies/TCRs) that undergo affinity maturation, TLR ligand-binding specificities are evolutionarily determined and rely on the physical alignment of conserved LRR convex loops with matching PAMP/DAMP structures. Ligation induces receptor dimerization, bringing their intracellular TIR domains into close proximity to initiate signaling.
*   **Co-receptors Required for Binding:** Heterodimers require mutual co-receptor pairing (e.g., TLR1 with TLR2; TLR2 with TLR6). TLR4 requires the sequential cooperative binding of LBP, CD14, and MD2 to efficiently bind LPS and signal.
*   **Role as a Transplant, Blood Group, or Tumor Antigen:** While TLRs are not polymorphic blood group antigens, their activation plays a vital role in transplantation and tumor biology. During transplantation, tissue ischemia and reperfusion release endogenous DAMPs (such as HSPs and HMGB1) that ligate donor and recipient TLR2/4 on dendritic cells. This activates APCs, inducing the upregulation of costimulators (CD80/CD86) and MHC molecules, which primes alloreactive recipient T cells to drive acute or chronic graft rejection. In tumor immunology, tumor-derived factors can engage TLRs on tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), modulating their polarization between the M1-like (antitumor) and M2-like (protumor) states.

#### Signal Transduction
*   **Intracellular Signaling Motifs:** Toll/Interleukin-1 Receptor (TIR) domain. Located in the cytoplasmic tail of all TLR proteins, the TIR domain is the conserved, homologous intracellular scaffold required to recruit downstream TIR-containing adaptor proteins.
*   **Associated Kinases & Adaptor Proteins:**
    - **MyD88 (Myeloid Differentiation Primary Response 88):** A TIR domain-containing adaptor protein engaged by all TLRs except TLR3. MyD88 contains a death domain that recruits members of the Interleukin-1 Receptor-Associated Kinase (IRAK) family.
    - **TRIF (TIR Domain-Containing Adaptor Inducing IFN-β):** A TIR-containing adaptor utilized exclusively by TLR3 and TLR4.
    - **IRAK4 and IRAK1:** Serine-threonine kinases recruited to MyD88. IRAK4 phosphorylates and activates IRAK1. Activated IRAKs auto-phosphorylate and dissociate from the receptor complex to activate TRAF6.
    - **TRAF6 (TNF Receptor-Associated Factor 6):** An E3 ubiquitin ligase recruited by IRAKs. TRAF6 catalyzes the formation of lysine-63 (K63)-linked polyubiquitin chains, forming a scaffold that recruits and activates the IκB kinase (IKK) complex and MAP kinases.
    - **TRAF3:** Engaged in endosomal TLR7/8/9 MyD88 signaling and TLR3/4 TRIF signaling. It forms a scaffold to recruit and activate the kinase TBK1.
    - **TBK1 (TANK-Binding Kinase 1):** A serine-threonine kinase activated downstream of TRIF or in endosomal TRAF3 complexes. TBK1 phosphorylates the transcription factors IRF3 and IRF7.
    - **IKK Complex (IKKα, IKKβ, and NEMO/IKKγ):** Activated downstream of TRAF6. IKKβ phosphorylates the cytosolic inhibitor IκBα, targeting it for polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, thereby releasing NF-κB.
*   **Downstream Transcription Factors Activated:**
    - **NF-κB (typically p50/p65 heterodimers):** Translocates to the nucleus to drive the transcription of genes encoding proinflammatory cytokines (TNF, IL-1β, IL-6), chemokines (CCL2, CXCL8), and endothelial adhesion molecules (E-selectin).
    - **IRF3 and IRF7:** Phosphorylated by TBK1, they form homodimers and translocate to the nucleus to drive the high-level transcription of Type I Interferon (IFN-α, IFN-β) genes.
    - **AP-1 (Activator Protein 1, JUN/FOS) and CREB:** Activated via downstream Mitogen-Activated Protein (MAP) kinase cascades (ERK, JNK, p38) to cooperate with NF-κB in transcribing inflammatory mediators.

#### Functional Outcomes
*   **Cellular Response to Ligation:**
    - *Macrophages and Neutrophils:* Ligation initiates classical (M1-like) activation, characterized by a major respiratory burst (assembly of the phagocyte NADPH oxidase to produce ROS), upregulation of inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) to generate nitric oxide, release of lysosomal enzymes, and secretion of acute-phase cytokines (TNF, IL-1, IL-6).
    - *Dendritic Cells:* Triggers DC maturation. Up-regulates cell surface MHC class I and II, costimulatory molecules (CD80, CD86, CD40), and the chemokine receptor CCR7. This prompts the DC to lose tissue adhesiveness and migrate via afferent lymphatics into the T-cell zones of draining lymph nodes.
    - *B Lymphocytes:* Ligation of TLRs (TLR5, 7, and 9 in humans) B cells enhances BCR-mediated activation, drives B-cell proliferation, and promotes heavy-chain class switching and T-independent antibody production against microbial carbohydrates and lipids.
    - *Intestinal Epithelial Cells:* Stimulates homeostatic maintenance of the mucosal barrier by tightening interendothelial tight junctions (translocation of zona occludens 1 and occludin), promoting goblet cell mucus (MUC2) secretion, and stimulating Paneth cell release of defensins and REGIII antimicrobial peptides.
*   **Role in Immune Cascade:** TLRs act as the primary bridge linking innate pathogen sensing to the activation of adaptive immunity. By driving dendritic cell maturation, TLR signaling "licenses" DCs to express the high levels of costimulators (CD80/CD86, CD40) and cytokines (such as IL-12) required to prime and polarize naive CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes into functional effector Th1, Th17, and cytotoxic T cell lineages.
*   **Regulation & Down-modulation:** TLR-mediated inflammatory responses are tightly regulated to prevent systemic endotropic shock and local tissue destruction:
    - *IL-10 Feedback:* Activated macrophages produce IL-10, which binds to IL-10R in an autocrine/paracrine manner, activating STAT3 to transcriptionally inhibit the expression of TNF, IL-1, IL-6, and IL-12.
    - *Inhibitory Receptors:* Phosphorylated ITIM-containing receptors (such as FcγRIIB) recruit SHP-1/2 or SHIP phosphatases to dephosphorylate activating intermediates in the TLR/MyD88 signaling complex.
    - *Autophagy:* Autophagy gene products (such as ATG16L1) actively regulate and limit the secretion of active IL-1β by target cells, preventing excessive inflammasome-mediated inflammation.
    - *Ubiquitin-Mediated Degradation:* E3 ubiquitin ligases can ubiquitinate key signaling components (such as IRAKs or TRAF6) for proteasomal degradation, terminating the signaling cascade.

#### Clinical & Pharmacologic Relevance
*   **Associated Immunodeficiencies (if mutated):**
    - **MyD88 and IRAK4 Deficiencies:** Autosomal recessive loss-of-function mutations in MyD88 or IRAK4 severely compromise canonical signaling downstream of most TLRs (except TLR3) and the Interleukin-1 Receptor (IL-1R). Clinically, children present with severe, recurrent, invasive pyogenic bacterial infections early in life (most notably pneumococcal pneumonia, meningitis, and septicemia caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae and Neisseria meningitidis). A diagnostic hallmark of these patients is their *inability to mount a fever* or elevated systemic acute-phase reactants (such as CRP) in the setting of severe illness. Susceptibility to pyogenic infections typically decreases later in life due to compensation by mature adaptive antibody and T-cell responses.
    - **UNC93B Deficiency:** Autosomal recessive mutations in the UNC93B gene prevent the transport of endosomal nucleic acid-sensing TLRs (TLR3, 7, 8, and 9) from the ER to endosomes, abolishing endosomal TLR signaling and Type I IFN production. Patients present with highly selective susceptibility to *recurrent, life-threatening Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV) encephalitis* localized in the brain.
    - **TLR3, TRIF, TBK1, and TRAF3 Mutations:** Autosomal recessive (or autosomal dominant dominant-negative) mutations selectively compromise TLR3/TRIF-dependent Type I IFN production in response to double-stranded viral RNA. These mutations present with a clinical phenotype identical to UNC93B deficiency, manifesting as isolated *Herpes Simplex Virus encephalitis*.
    - **NEMO (IKKγ) Deficiency:** X-linked recessive hypomorphic mutations in the NEMO gene impair canonical NF-κB activation downstream of both TLR and CD40 signaling, as well as developmental pathways. This causes Anhidrotic Ectodermal Dysplasia with Immunodeficiency (EDA-ID). Affected males present with abnormal ectoderm-derived structures (sparse hair, conical teeth, and an absence of sweat glands) and suffer from severe infections with encapsulated pyogenic bacteria, atypical mycobacteria (Mendelian Susceptibility to Mycobacterial Disease / MSMD), viruses, and the opportunistic fungus Pneumocystis jirovecii.
*   **Targeted Biologic Therapies (Monoclonal Antibodies):** While therapeutic TLR agonists (such as imiquimod, a TLR7 agonist used for viral warts and basal cell carcinoma) are in use, clinical biologics targeting the downstream products of TLR activation are cornerstone therapies:
    - **Infliximab, Adalimumab, and Etanercept:** Monoclonal antibodies or fusion proteins that bind and neutralize soluble and membrane-bound TNF, which is heavily transcribed downstream of TLR/NF-κB signaling, used to treat rheumatoid arthritis, Crohn's disease, and psoriasis.
    - **Tocilizumab:** A monoclonal antibody that blocks the IL-6 receptor, used to treat severe systemic inflammation, cytokine release syndrome, and rheumatoid arthritis.
*   **Pathogen Sabotage or Mimicry:**
    - *Intracellular Evasion:* Mycobacterium tuberculosis, Mycobacterium leprae, and Legionella pneumophila survive inside macrophages by actively blocking the fusion of lysosomes with the phagosome, thereby evading degradative processing and preventing the release of nucleic acids to endosomal TLRs.
    - *Phagosomal Escape:* Listeria monocytogenes produces a pore-forming toxin, listeriolysin O, which lyses the phagosomal membrane, allowing the bacteria to escape into the host cell cytoplasm, avoiding endosomal TLR detection.
    - *Antioxidant Enzymes:* Catalase-positive pathogens (such as Staphylococci) synthesize abundant catalase to convert hydrogen peroxide into water, neutralizing the toxic reactive oxygen species generated in the respiratory burst.
    - *Decoy Receptors:* Poxviruses and other viruses encode soluble decoy receptors for TNF and IL-1, or synthesize viral homologs of the immunosuppressive cytokine IL-10 (such as viral IL-10 encoded by EBV) to suppress local host endothelial activation and TLR-driven inflammatory recruitment.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Pattern Recognition Receptors (PRRs)", "Leucine-rich repeat (LRR) modules", "LxxLxLxxN motif", "Toll/IL-1 Receptor (TIR) domain", "MyD88-dependent vs. TRIF-dependent signaling", "Inside-out signaling", "Antiviral state", "Cold abscesses" (HIES/Job syndrome susceptibility, though HIES is driven by STAT3 mutations, it impairs downstream TLR-stimulated cytokines like IL-6/IL-17).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - *TLR10 is anti-inflammatory:* While all other TLRs promote inflammatory cascades, human TLR10 appears to function as an inhibitory or anti-inflammatory receptor, though its precise mechanisms remain poorly understood.
    - *B-cell TLR4 species difference:* Murine B cells express functional TLR4 on their cell surface and undergo direct activation in response to LPS, whereas human B cells completely lack cell surface TLR4 expression and do not respond directly to LPS (expressing instead only TLR5, 7, and 9).
    - *Failure to mount fever:* Patients with MyD88 or IRAK4 deficiencies do not mount a fever during severe, life-threatening bacterial infections because MyD88-dependent transcription of the pyrogenic cytokines TNF, IL-1, and IL-6 is abolished. These cytokines are required to act on the hypothalamus to induce prostaglandin E2, the molecular mediator of fever.
