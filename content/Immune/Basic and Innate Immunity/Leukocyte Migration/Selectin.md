The selectin family of CD molecules—specifically CD62E, CD62L, and CD62P—are plasma membrane carbohydrate-binding adhesion molecules that are fundamentally responsible for the initial, low-affinity attachment of circulating leukocytes to the endothelial cells lining postcapillary venules.

The extracellular domains of selectins are structurally similar to C-type lectins, meaning they bind specific carbohydrate structures in a calcium-dependent manner. Because their interaction with their ligands features a low binding affinity (Kd​∼100μm) and a very fast off-rate, the selectin-ligand bonds are easily and repeatedly disrupted by the sheer physical force of flowing blood. This rapid forming and breaking of bonds causes leukocytes to be pushed along the endothelial surface in a characteristic "rolling" motion. To maximize the efficiency of these interactions, leukocytes strategically express selectins and selectin ligands at the very tips of their microvilli.

## CD62
### CD62E (E-selectin)
- **Structure and Expression:** CD62E is a 115 kD protein expressed specifically on the surface of endothelial cells.
- **Activation and Kinetics:** Unlike P-selectin, E-selectin is not stored preformed. It must be newly synthesized and expressed on the endothelial cell surface, a process that takes approximately 1 to 2 hours. This induction is heavily stimulated by inflammatory cytokines—specifically Interleukin-1 (IL-1) and Tumor Necrosis Factor (TNF)—which are produced by tissue-resident sentinel cells (like macrophages and dendritic cells) in response to infection, as well as by direct exposure to microbial products like lipopolysaccharide (LPS).
- **Ligand Specificity:** E-selectin binds to complex sialylated carbohydrates related to the Lewis X family, specifically sialyl Lewis X (e.g., CLA-1). These carbohydrate ligands are displayed on glycoproteins present on the surface of neutrophils, monocytes, and previously activated effector and memory T cells.
- **Physiological Role:** CD62E mediates the rolling and initial adhesion of leukocytes to the endothelium, directing the homing of these immune cells specifically into inflamed tissues.

### CD62P (P-selectin)
- **Structure and Expression:** CD62P is a 140 kD selectin expressed by both endothelial cells and platelets.
- **Activation and Kinetics:** CD62P is unique in that it is stored preformed within the cytoplasmic granules of endothelial cells. When the endothelium is exposed to histamine (released from mast cells) or thrombin (generated during blood coagulation), these granules rapidly fuse with the plasma membrane. This allows P-selectin to be redistributed to the luminal surface almost instantaneously during an acute inflammatory reaction or vascular injury.
- **Ligand Specificity:** The major ligand for P-selectin is P-selectin glycoprotein ligand 1 (PSGL-1, also known as CD162). PSGL-1 is a leukocyte membrane glycoprotein that undergoes posttranslational modification to display the crucial sialyl Lewis X tetrasaccharide. It is expressed on neutrophils, monocytes, and effector/memory T cells.
- **Physiological Role:** Like E-selectin, P-selectin facilitates the rapid rolling and adhesion of leukocytes to the endothelium and to platelets at sites of inflammation and tissue injury.

### CD62L (L-selectin)
- **Structure and Expression:** CD62L is a 74–95 kD selectin that is uniquely expressed on the surface of leukocytes themselves, rather than on endothelial cells. It is found on neutrophils, monocytes, naive B cells, naive T cells, central memory T cells, and some Natural Killer (NK) cells.
- **Ligand Specificity:** CD62L binds to sialomucins expressed on endothelial cells, specifically recognizing a determinant known as 6-sulfo sialyl Lewis X.
- **Physiological Roles:**
    - **Lymphocyte Homing:** CD62L is absolutely required for the homing of naive T and B lymphocytes into secondary lymphoid organs (such as lymph nodes). It mediates the rolling of naive lymphocytes along specialized blood vessels known as high endothelial venules (HEVs). The specific sialomucin ligands on HEVs that bind L-selectin are collectively referred to as peripheral node addressin (PNAd). Depending on the anatomic location, PNAd is displayed by different molecules: GlyCAM-1 and CD34 on lymph node HEVs, and MAdCAM-1 on HEVs in gut-associated lymphoid tissues like Peyer patches.
    - **Inflammatory Recruitment:** On neutrophils, L-selectin promotes adhesion to endothelial cells at sites of inflammation because the expression of its sialomucin ligands on the vessel wall is upregulated by inflammatory cytokines like IL-1 and TNF.
- **Regulation:** Following antigen activation in the lymph node, newly generated effector T cells drastically reduce their expression of L-selectin. This downregulation prevents effector T cells from endlessly recirculating back into lymphoid organs, allowing them to instead seek out peripheral sites of infection.

## Clinical Significance: Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency Type 2 (LAD-2)
The critical nature of the selectin family in immune defense is highlighted by the disease Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency Type 2 (LAD-2). LAD-2 is a rare autosomal recessive disorder caused by a mutation in a GDP-fucose transporter required to synthesize sialyl Lewis X. Because the patients' leukocytes lack sialyl Lewis X, they physically cannot bind to E-selectin or P-selectin on activated endothelium. This causes a complete failure of the selectin-mediated "rolling" phase, resulting in a profound inability to recruit neutrophils and other leukocytes to sites of tissue injury, leading to severe, recurrent bacterial and fungal infections.
