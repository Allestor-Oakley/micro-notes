### Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) Class I Molecule
#### Structure & Genetics
*   **Molecular Structure & Subunits:**
    - MHC Class I molecules are heterodimeric cell surface glycoproteins consisting of two polypeptide chains:
        1.  **Polymorphic α (Heavy) Chain:** A transmembrane glycoprotein (approximately 44 kDa) composed of three extracellular domains (α1, α2, and α3), a hydrophobic transmembrane region, and a short carboxyl-terminal cytoplasmic tail.
        2.  **Non-polymorphic β2-microglobulin (β2m):** A small, non-glycosylated protein (approximately 12 kDa) that is non-covalently associated with the α3 domain of the heavy chain. β2m does not span the cell membrane.
    - **Peptide-Binding Cleft (Groove):** The extracellular **α1 and α2 domains** interact to form the peptide-binding cleft. The cleft consists of a flat floor formed by eight antiparallel β-pleated strands and two parallel α-helical walls. The ends of the MHC Class I peptide-binding cleft are physically **closed**, imposing a strict size constraint that accommodates only short linear peptides of **8 to 11 amino acids** in length.
    - **CD8 Coreceptor Binding Site:** The extracellular **α3 domain** is highly conserved among all classical MHC Class I molecules and folds into an immunoglobulin-like domain. This domain contains a specific loop that serves as the binding site for the **CD8** coreceptor expressed on cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs).
    - **Obligate Association with β2m:** The non-covalent association of the α heavy chain with β2-microglobulin is essential for the stable folding, trafficking, and cell surface expression of the MHC Class I complex. In the absence of β2m, the α heavy chain is unstable, undergoes misfolding in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), and is rapidly targeted for retrotranslocation and proteasomal degradation.
*   **Gene Locus & Rearrangement:**
    - Classical MHC Class I molecules are germline-encoded, non-clonal receptors that do not undergo somatic rearrangement, gene duplication, or V(D)J recombination.
    - In humans, MHC Class I molecules are encoded within the **Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC)** locus (designated as HLA, Human Leukocyte Antigen) located on the short arm of **chromosome 6** (6p21.3).
    - In mice, they are encoded within the **H-2** locus on **chromosome 17**.
    - **Classical MHC Class I Loci:** There are three highly polymorphic classical loci in humans: **HLA-A, HLA-B, and HLA-C**. In mice, the classical MHC Class I loci are **H-2K, H-2D, and H-2L**.
    - **Non-classical MHC Class I Loci:** Non-classical MHC Class I genes (also called MHC Class Ib molecules) reside within the same locus. In humans, these include **HLA-E, HLA-F, and HLA-G**. In mice, they include Qa and TL. Additionally, the MHC-I-like CD1 family of lipid-presenting molecules is structurally homologous but encoded on human chromosome 1.
*   **Associated Chaperone Proteins:**
    - Synthesis, assembly, and peptide loading of MHC Class I molecules occur within the lumen of the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and are guided by a highly regulated network of molecular chaperones:
        - **Calnexin:** A membrane-bound ER chaperone that binds to newly synthesized, free MHC Class I α heavy chains to stabilize them and facilitate their folding before association with β2-microglobulin.
        - **Calreticulin:** A soluble ER chaperone that replaces calnexin once β2m binds to the α heavy chain, stabilizing the folded α-β2m heterodimer.
        - **ERp57:** A thiol oxidoreductase that forms a stable complex with calreticulin and participates in stabilizing the empty peptide-binding cleft by establishing disulfide bonds.
        - **Tapasin:** An ER-transmembrane glycoprotein that physically links the empty MHC Class I heterodimer to the TAP transporter, forming the **Peptide Loading Complex (PLC)**. Tapasin acts as a critical peptide editor, facilitating the selection and loading of high-affinity peptides into the cleft.
        - **TAP1 and TAP2 (Transporter associated with Antigen Processing):** An ATP-dependent heterodimeric ABC transporter in the ER membrane. TAP actively pumps peptides generated by the cytosolic proteasome (and trimmed by cytosolic peptidases) from the cytoplasm into the ER lumen.
        - **ERAP1 and ERAP2 (Endoplasmic Reticulum Aminopeptidases):** ER-resident aminopeptidases that trim peptides that are too long at their amino-terminal ends to fit the 8-11 amino acid size requirement of the MHC Class I cleft.
*   **Polymorphisms & Isotypes:**
    - MHC Class I is one of the most polymorphic genes in mammalian genomes, with thousands of classical HLA-A, HLA-B, and HLA-C alleles identified in human populations.
    - **Localization of Polymorphic Residues:** Polymorphic amino acid residues are highly concentrated within the **α1 and α2 domains**, specifically lining the floor and α-helical walls of the peptide-binding cleft (known as pocket residues). These variations alter the electrostatic charge, hydrophobicity, and physical dimensions of the pockets within the cleft, thereby dictating the specific **anchor residues** required in a peptide to bind a given HLA allele.
    - **Co-dominant Expression:** MHC Class I alleles are expressed co-dominantly. Every nucleated cell simultaneously expresses classical MHC Class I molecules transcribed from both the maternal and paternal alleles (up to six different classical MHC-I molecules per cell: two HLA-A, two HLA-B, and two HLA-C), maximizing the repertoire of peptides that can be presented to the immune system.

#### Expression & Binding
*   **Cells Expressing this Molecule:**
    - Classical MHC Class I molecules are constitutively expressed on **virtually all nucleated cells** in the body, as well as on platelets.
    - Non-nucleated mature red blood cells do not express MHC Class I (or express only trace levels derived from progenitor cells).
    - **Upregulation by Interferons:** Expression is heavily upregulated by cytokines produced during early innate and adaptive immune responses, most notably **Type I Interferons (IFN-α, IFN-β)** and **Type II Interferon (IFN-γ)**. These cytokines activate the JAK-STAT pathway, prompting the nuclear translocation of transcription factors (such as IRF1) that bind the MHC Class I promoter to increase transcription of the classical heavy chains, β2m, TAP1/2, tapasin, and immunoproteasome subunits (LMP2, LMP7, MECL1).
    - **Tissue Privilege:** Expression is exceptionally low in certain immune-privileged sites, such as brain neurons, corneal epithelial cells, and placental trophoblasts, to minimize the risk of destructive T-cell responses.
*   **Primary Ligands / Antigens:**
    - Classical MHC Class I molecules present **short linear peptides (8 to 11 amino acids)** derived from intracellular (endogenous) proteins. These include:
        - Cytosolic viral proteins during viral replication.
        - Cytosolic or vesicular bacterial/protozoal proteins.
        - Mutated self-proteins in malignant cells (**tumor neoantigens**).
        - Normal, non-mutated self-proteins (essential for central and peripheral tolerance).
    - **Anchor Residues:** Peptides bind to the MHC Class I cleft via specific **anchor residues** (typically hydrophobic or basic amino acids) located at their amino-terminus (typically position 2, P2) and carboxyl-terminus (typically position 9 or 11, P9/P11). These anchor residues dock into complementary polymorphic pockets at the ends of the cleft, while the central residues of the peptide arch upward to make physical contact with the TCR.
*   **Binding Kinetics & Affinity:**
    - **Low Dissociation Rate (High Stability):** Once a high-affinity peptide is loaded into the cleft in the ER, the peptide-MHC Class I complex is highly stable, exhibiting a very slow dissociation rate (extremely long half-life of days on the cell surface, Kd ranging from 10^-6 to 10^-9 M). This ensures that the peptide-MHC complex remains intact on the cell membrane long enough to be scanned by circulating CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes.
    - **Broad Peptide-Binding Specificity (One-to-Many):** Unlike the highly specific, clonal antigen-binding of BCRs or TCRs, a single classical MHC Class I molecule can bind many different peptides, provided they possess the matching anchor residues (broad specificity).
*   **Co-receptors Required for Binding:**
    - **CD8 (αβ heterodimer or αα homodimer):** A coreceptor expressed selectively on cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs). CD8 binds to the conserved **α3 domain** of the classical MHC Class I heavy chain. This binding stabilizes the physical interaction between the TCR and the peptide-MHC Class I complex, and positions the CD8-associated Src-family tyrosine kinase **LCK** proximal to the TCR-associated CD3 and ζ (zeta) chains to initiate intracellular signal transduction.
*   **Role as a Transplant, Blood Group, or Tumor Antigen:**
    - **Alloreactivity and Transplant Rejection:** Classical MHC Class I molecules are the primary targets of host CD8+ T-cell-mediated cellular rejection in vascularized allografts.
        - *Direct Allorecognition:* Recipient CD8+ T cells recognize intact, non-self donor classical MHC Class I molecules expressed on donor APCs or parenchymal cells.
        - *Indirect Allorecognition:* Donor classical MHC Class I molecules are shed, captured, and processed by recipient dendritic cells, and presented as donor-derived peptide fragments on host MHC Class II molecules to recipient CD4+ helper T cells.
    - **Tumor Antigen Downregulation:** Tumors frequently downregulate classical MHC Class I or β2m expression to evade recognition and cytolysis by tumor-specific CD8+ CTLs, a major mechanism of immune evasion.

#### Signal Transduction
*   **Intracellular Signaling Motifs:**
    - Classical MHC Class I heavy chains do not possess intrinsic enzymatic activity or specific signaling motifs (such as ITAMs or ITIMs) in their short cytoplasmic tails. They act as presentation scaffolds rather than signaling receptors.
    - However, ligation of the peptide-MHC Class I complex by a specific TCR on a CD8+ CTL clusters the **TCR-CD3-ζ complex**, which contains cytoplasmic **ITAMs** (zeta chains contain three ITAMs each, CD3 chains contain one each, totaling 10 ITAMs per TCR complex).
*   **Associated Kinases & Adaptor Proteins (on T-Cell side):**
    - **LCK:** Src-family tyrosine kinase associated with the CD8 coreceptor. CD8 binding to the MHC-I α3 domain brings LCK proximal to phosphorylate the ITAMs of CD3 and ζ chains.
    - **ZAP-70:** Tyrosine kinase recruited to phosphorylated ζ chain ITAMs. ZAP-70 phosphorylates the adaptors **LAT** and **SLP-76**, assembling a scaffold that recruits **ITK** and **PLCγ1** to initiate the T-cell activation cascade.
*   **Downstream Transcription Factors Activated (on T-Cell side):**
    - Ligation triggers three primary downstream transcription factors:
        - **NFAT:** Activated via the PLCγ1-IP3-Calcium-Calcineurin pathway, translocating to the nucleus to drive CTL survival, activation, and IL-2 production.
        - **NF-κB:** Activated via the PLCγ1-DAG-PKCθ pathway, releasing NF-κB (p50/p65) for nuclear import.
        - **AP-1 (JUN/FOS):** Activated via Ras/Rac-MAP kinase cascades.

#### Functional Outcomes
*   **Cellular Response to Ligation:**
    - **Target Cell Apoptosis:** Ligation of classical MHC Class I on a virus-infected, damaged, or tumor cell by a CD8+ CTL triggers immunological synapse formation and directional exocytosis of cytolytic granules:
        - **Perforin:** Polymerizes in the target cell membrane or endosomal membrane, forming pores that facilitate the entry of granzymes into the target cell cytosol.
        - **Granzymes:** Active serine proteases (principally granzyme B) that cleave host cytosolic substrates, directly cleaving and activating executioner caspase-3 and the BH3-only protein BID (generating truncated tBID). tBID triggers mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization, causing cytochrome c release, apoptosome assembly, and rapid, non-inflammatory apoptosis.
        - **FAS-FASL Interaction:** Activated CTLs express **FAS Ligand (FAS-L/CD178)**, which binds to **FAS (CD95)** on the target cell, activating initiator caspase-8 to trigger extrinsic apoptosis.
*   **Role in Immune Cascade:**
    - **Surveillance against Intracellular Pathogens and Malignancy:** MHC Class I peptide display is the primary mechanism by which the immune system monitors the health of intracellular compartments, identifying and clearing host cells harboring replicating viruses, cytosolic bacteria (e.g., Listeria monocytogenes), or somatic mutations.
    - **Cross-Presentation:** Classical dendritic cells (specifically the **cDC1** subset, which expresses the chemokine receptor **XCR1** and the C-type lectin receptor **CLEC9A**) can capture extracellular, dying, virally infected or tumor cells, endocytose them, and route these exogenous antigens into the cytosolic pathway. These antigens are then loaded onto classical MHC Class I molecules. This physiological exception allows cDC1 to present exogenous viral/tumor antigens to naive CD8+ T cells, providing the necessary signal 1 and costimulatory signals (B7-1/2 binding CD28) to prime naive CTLs.
*   **Regulation & Down-modulation:**
    - **Negative Feedback via Inhibitory Receptors on NK Cells:** Soluble or membrane classical MHC Class I molecules act as ligands for inhibitory receptors expressed on Natural Killer (NK) cells. These include **KIRs** (Killer Immunoglobulin-like Receptors, such as KIR2DL and KIR3DL) and the **CD94/NKG2A** heterodimer.
        - KIRs and CD94/NKG2A possess cytoplasmic **ITIMs**. Binding to MHC Class I triggers Src-family kinase-mediated phosphorylation of these ITIM tyrosines, which recruits the tyrosine phosphatase **SHP-1**. SHP-1 dephosphorylates activating signaling intermediates, blocking NK cell activation. If classical MHC-I expression is downregulated (as seen in viral infections or tumors), this inhibitory signal is lost ("missing-self"), prompting NK-mediated cytolysis.

#### Clinical & Pharmacologic Relevance
*   **Associated Immunodeficiencies (if mutated):**
    - **Bare Lymphocyte Syndrome Type I (MHC Class I Deficiency):** A rare primary immunodeficiency caused by autosomal recessive mutations in the genes encoding **TAP1, TAP2, or tapasin**.
        - *Pathophysiology:* Affected individuals cannot transport cytosolic peptides into the ER, preventing peptide loading and resulting in a profound loss of classical MHC Class I expression on the cell surface (typically less than 5% of normal levels).
        - *Clinical Manifestation:* Patients present with chronic, recurrent bacterial infections of the respiratory tract (bronchitis, bronchiectasis, sinusitis) and chronic necrotizing skin ulcers (granulomatous vasculitic lesions on the face and extremities), accompanied by a severe reduction in circulating mature CD8+ T cells. Surprisingly, they are *not* highly susceptible to most viral infections because their NK cells remain functional and are activated by the loss of MHC Class I ("missing-self") to clear virus-infected cells.
*   **Targeted Biologic Therapies (Monoclonal Antibodies):**
    - **Checkpoint Blockade Immunotherapy:** Therapeutic monoclonal antibodies (anti-PD-1, anti-PD-L1, anti-CTLA-4) do not target MHC-I directly but rescue exhausted tumor-specific CD8+ CTLs, enabling them to recognize tumor neoantigens displayed on classical MHC Class I molecules and kill tumor cells.
*   **Pathogen Sabotage or Mimicry:**
    - Viruses have evolved highly efficient molecular mechanisms to downregulate classical MHC Class I expression and evade CD8+ CTL clearance:
        - **TAP Inhibition:** Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV) produces the protein **ICP47**, which binds directly to the cytosolic face of TAP, blocking peptide transport into the ER.
        - **ER Retrotranslocation & Degradation:** Cytomegalovirus (CMV) produces the proteins **US2** and **US11**, which bind MHC Class I heavy chains in the ER and retrotranslocate them into the cytosol, where they are targeted for proteasomal degradation.
        - **ER Retention:** Adenovirus produces the protein **E3-19K**, which binds MHC Class I heavy chains in the ER, physically retaining them and preventing their transport to the Golgi and cell surface.
        - **Accelerated Endocytosis:** HIV produces the accessory protein **Nef**, which accelerates the internalization and lysosomal degradation of cell surface HLA-A and HLA-B molecules.
        - **Inhibitory Mimicry:** Cytomegalovirus encodes a non-classical MHC-I homolog (such as UL18 or peptide complexes displaying HLA-E leader sequences) that binds specifically to NK cell inhibitory receptors, preventing NK-mediated lysis of virus-infected cells.

*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - **HLA-B27 and Ankylosing Spondylitis:** A strong genetic association exists between the classical MHC Class I allele **HLA-B27** and the development of Ankylosing Spondylitis (a chronic inflammatory arthropathy). The precise mechanism is thought to involve the tendency of HLA-B27 heavy chains to misfold, form homodimers (via a free cysteine residue at position 67) that accumulate in the ER, and trigger the unfolded protein response (UPR) and IL-23/IL-17 pathway-driven inflammation.
    - **HLA-G and Placental Tolerance:** HLA-G is a non-classical MHC Class I molecule expressed selectively on extravillous cytotrophoblast cells at the maternal-fetal interface. Because it is largely non-polymorphic, it does not trigger alloreactivity in maternal T cells. Instead, HLA-G binds to the inhibitory receptor **ILT2 (LILRB1)** on maternal NK cells, macrophages, and T cells, active suppressing maternal immune responses to protect the semi-allogeneic fetus.
    - **HLA-E and HLA-E leader peptides:** HLA-E is a non-classical MHC Class I molecule that displays a highly restricted peptide repertoire derived from the leader sequences of classical HLA-A, B, and C heavy chains. Surface HLA-E binds to the CD94/NKG2A inhibitory receptor on NK cells. This serves as an elegant mechanism for NK cells to monitor the overall integrity of classical MHC-I synthesis in the host cell: if classical MHC-I synthesis is disrupted (as occurs during viral infection or tumor transition), HLA-E expression drops, relieving the CD94/NKG2A inhibitory brake and triggering NK cell cytolysis.
