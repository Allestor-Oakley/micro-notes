### Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) Class II Molecule

#### Structure & Genetics
*   **Molecular Structure & Subunits:** Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) Class II molecules are expressed as membrane-bound, disulfide-linked heterodimers on the cell surface. They are composed of two noncovalently associated, polymorphic polypeptide chains: a 32- to 34-kD α chain and a 29- to 32-kD β chain. The extracellular portion of each polypeptide chain is folded into two domains, designated α1 and α2 for the α chain, and β1 and β2 for the β chain. The peptide-binding cleft (or groove) of the class II molecule is formed by the interaction and spatial juxtaposition of the amino-terminal α1 and β1 domains. Specifically, four strands of the floor of the cleft and one of the helical walls are formed by the α1 domain, while the other four strands of the floor and the second helical wall are formed by the β1 domain. The floor of the cleft consists of an eight-stranded, antiparallel β-pleated sheet, and the walls are formed by two parallel α-helices. Because the ends of the peptide-binding cleft are open in class II molecules (unlike the closed cleft of class I molecules), it can accommodate larger peptides of 13 to 25 amino acid residues or more (optimal length for fitting into the cleft being 12 to 16 residues) in an extended, linear conformation that can protrude from the ends of the cleft. Both the α and β chains span the membrane, each possessing a hydrophobic transmembrane region of approximately 25 amino acid residues, immediately followed by basic amino acid clusters and short hydrophilic cytoplasmic tails. Fully assembled, stable cell surface expression of MHC Class II requires a trimeric complex consisting of one α chain, one β chain, and a bound antigenic peptide.
*   **Gene Locus & Rearrangement:** The genes encoding both the α and β chains of MHC Class II molecules are polymorphic, codominantly expressed, and located entirely within the MHC locus. In humans, the MHC is located on the short arm of chromosome 6 and occupies a segment of approximately 3500 kilobases (kb). Within the class II region of the human HLA locus, there are three classical loci: HLA-DP, HLA-DQ, and HLA-DR. The DP and DQ loci contain separate functional genes designated A and B (DPA1, DPB1, DQA1, DQB1) encoding the α and β chains, respectively. The DR locus on each chromosome contains a single gene for the DRα chain (DRA) and variable numbers of genes encoding the DRβ chain (DRB1, and typically one to three of the duplicated DRB3, DRB4, or DRB5 genes). In mice, the MHC is located on chromosome 17, and the class II region consists of two loci: I-A (encoding Aα and Aβ chains) and I-E (encoding Eα and Eβ chains). MHC Class II genes do NOT undergo somatic gene rearrangement or recombination (unlike immunoglobulin and T-cell receptor genes). The high diversity of MHC Class II molecules in a population is solely the result of extreme inherited germline polymorphism. The level of MHC Class II expression on the cell surface is primarily determined by the rate of gene transcription. The master transcriptional regulator of class II genes is the Class II Transcription Activator (CIITA). CIITA is a non-DNA-binding protein that is inducible by Interferon-gamma (IFN-γ). It functions by coordinating and stabilizing the assembly of a multi-protein complex of transcription factors (including RFX) on the class II promoter, facilitating efficient transcription of DP, DQ, and DR genes.
*   **Associated Chaperone Proteins:** The biosynthesis, assembly, and transport of MHC Class II molecules are regulated by several specialized chaperone proteins within distinct cellular compartments:
    *   **Calnexin**: An endoplasmic reticulum (ER)-resident chaperone that assists in the proper folding and coordinate assembly of newly synthesized α and β chains.
    *   **Invariant Chain (Ii / CD74)**: A non-polymorphic homotrimer (composed of 30-kD subunits, existing as 33-, 35-, and 41-kD isoforms) that associates with newly formed class II αβ heterodimers within the ER. The invariant chain contains a segment that physically binds to and blocks the peptide-binding cleft of each class II molecule, preventing newly synthesized MHC Class II from prematurely binding self peptides present in the ER. Additionally, the cytoplasmic tail of Ii contains sorting signals that target the class II-Ii complex for transport from the trans-Golgi network to late endosomal/lysosomal compartments where endocytosed antigens are degraded.
    *   **Cathepsins**: Proteolytic enzymes in the endosomal/lysosomal compartments that degrade the invariant chain, leaving only a small 24-amino-acid remnant occupying the peptide-binding cleft.
    *   **CLIP (Class II-Associated Invariant Chain Peptide)**: The 24-amino-acid remnant of the invariant chain that remains bound in the peptide-binding cleft after proteolytic cleavage of Ii, serving as a placeholder to prevent premature peptide binding or class II denaturation before antigenic peptide loading.
    *   **HLA-DM (DM / H-2M in mice)**: A dimeric, non-polymorphic class II-like glycoprotein encoded within the MHC that colocalizes with MHC Class II in late endosomes and lysosomes. HLA-DM does not bind peptides or express on the cell surface; instead, it acts as a peptide exchanger. It physically binds to the β chain of MHC Class II and dislodges CLIP or loosely bound, low-affinity peptides from the cleft, allowing high-affinity antigenic peptides to bind. This "peptide editing" favors the surface display of highly stable peptide-MHC complexes.
    *   **HLA-DO (DO)**: A dimeric, non-polymorphic class II-like molecule that binds to HLA-DM in lysosomes and acts as a negative regulator, inhibiting DM-catalyzed peptide exchange until it is dissociated or downmodulated under inflammatory stimuli.
*   **Polymorphisms & Isotypes:** MHC Class II genes are the most polymorphic genes present in the mammalian genome, with over 6000 alleles identified for human class II genes alone. This extreme polymorphism is concentrated primarily within the α1 and β1 domains that form the walls and floor of the peptide-binding cleft. In human MHC Class II, the DRA chain is virtually nonpolymorphic (monomorphic) across the human population; therefore, the polymorphism of the HLA-DR molecule is determined almost entirely by variations in the DRβ chain (encoded by DRB genes). In contrast, both the α and β chains of HLA-DP and HLA-DQ are highly polymorphic. Polymorphic residues encode different amino acids in the pockets of the floor and helices of the walls of the cleft, directly altering the shape, charge, and hydrophobic properties of the pocket. This variation determines which specific peptide anchor residues can fit into the cleft, giving each MHC Class II allele unique peptide-binding specificity. Classical isotypes in humans include HLA-DR, HLA-DQ, and HLA-DP, while classical mouse isotypes include I-A and I-E.

#### Expression & Binding
*   **Cells Expressing this Molecule:** Under physiological conditions, MHC Class II molecule expression is highly restricted and found primarily on professional antigen-presenting cells (APCs) and specialized immunological tissues:
    *   **Dendritic Cells (DCs)**: Expressed constitutively at high levels on conventional classical dendritic cells (cDC1, cDC2), Langerhans cells, and monocyte-derived DCs. Expression is further upregulated during DC maturation.
    *   **Macrophages (Mononuclear Phagocytes)**: Expressed at low levels constitutively, but highly inducible to high levels of expression upon activation by cytokines, particularly IFN-γ.
    *   **B Lymphocytes**: Expressed constitutively, allowing B cells to present endocytosed antigens to helper T cells.
    *   **Thymic Epithelial Cells**: Expressed constitutively on cortical and medullary thymic epithelial cells (mTECs), where they present self-peptides to developing thymocytes during positive and negative selection.
    *   **Vascular Endothelial Cells**: Expressed in humans and inducible by inflammatory cytokines.
    *   **Other Cell Types**: Expression of MHC Class II can be induced on virtually any nucleated cell type during an active inflammatory response by IFN-γ-mediated activation of the CIITA transcription factor.
*   **Primary Ligands / Antigens:**
    *   **Peptide Antigens**: The primary physiological ligands are linear peptides of 10 to 30 residues or more (optimal length 12 to 16 residues) derived from extracellular or vesicular proteins. These proteins are internalized into the APC via endocytosis, phagocytosis, or macropinocytosis, and are subsequently digested by lysosomal/endosomal proteases (such as cathepsins) in acidic compartments.
    *   **Bacterial Superantigens**: Non-peptide toxins secreted by bacteria (e.g., Staphylococcal enterotoxin B (SEB), Toxic Shock Syndrome Toxin-1 (TSST-1)). Superantigens bind to MHC Class II molecules outside the peptide-binding cleft in an allele-nonspecific manner, while simultaneously binding to the variable region of specific families of TCR β chains (TCR Vβ), leading to massive, polyclonal T-cell activation.
*   **Binding Kinetics & Affinity:** The binding of peptides to MHC Class II molecules is a noncovalent interaction characterized by a broad specificity: a single MHC Class II allele can bind many different peptides that share common structural features (anchor residues), but only one peptide can occupy the cleft at any given time. Once a peptide is loaded into the cleft, the association is stable with a very slow off-rate. This slow dissociation rate corresponds to extremely long half-lives for the peptide-MHC complex (ranging from hours to many days), which ensures that the peptide remains displayed on the cell surface long enough to be scanned by recirculating CD4+ T lymphocytes. HLA-DM regulates this process by dislodging low-affinity peptides and CLIP, selecting for peptides with the highest binding affinity.
*   **Co-receptors Required for Binding:** CD4 serves as the selective co-receptor for MHC Class II-restricted antigen recognition. CD4 is a transmembrane glycoprotein monomer expressed on helper T lymphocytes, regulatory T cells (Tregs), and some monocytes/macrophages. The two amino-terminal extracellular Ig-like domains of the CD4 molecule bind specifically to a nonpolymorphic pocket or concavity created by the α2 and β2 domains of the MHC Class II heterodimer. This binding stabilizes the physical interaction between the TCR and the peptide-MHC Class II complex on the APC. Intracellularly, the cytoplasmic tail of CD4 is associated with Lck, a Src family tyrosine kinase. CD4 binding recruits Lck into close physical proximity with the TCR complex, allowing Lck to phosphorylate the immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motifs (ITAMs) of the CD3 and ζ signaling chains, initiating T-cell activation.
*   **Role as a Transplant, Blood Group, or Tumor Antigen:** MHC Class II molecules represent a major histocompatibility barrier in transplantation. Due to the extreme polymorphism of HLA-DR, DQ, and DP loci, grafts exchanged between non-identical individuals are rapidly recognized as foreign and rejected by T cells (allograft rejection). Rejection can be mediated by direct allorecognition (recipient T cells directly recognize intact allogeneic MHC Class II molecules on donor APCs) or indirect allorecognition (recipient APCs internalize, process, and present donor MHC Class II-derived peptides on self-MHC Class II molecules to recipient CD4+ T cells). In clinical kidney and organ transplantation, HLA matching of the HLA-DR locus (along with HLA-A and HLA-B) is a key predictor of graft survival. In autoimmune pathology, specific MHC Class II alleles are strongly associated with increased susceptibility to diseases (odds ratio / relative risk):
    *   **Rheumatoid Arthritis**: Highly associated with HLA-DRB1 alleles containing a conserved sequence at amino acid positions 70-74, termed the "shared epitope" (SE).
    *   **Type 1 Diabetes**: Strongly associated with HLA-DRB1\*0301-DQA1\*0501-DQB1\*0201 (DR3) and DRB1\*0401-DQA1\*0301-DQB1\*0302 (DR4) haplotypes; heterozygous individuals (DR3/DR4) have a synergistically increased risk.
    *   **Celiac Disease**: Strongly associated with the DQA1\*0501-DQB1\*0201 haplotype (encoding HLA-DQ2) and HLA-DQ8.
    *   **Multiple Sclerosis**: Associated with the HLA-DRB1\*1501 allele.
    *   **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)**: Associated with HLA-DRB1\*0301 and DRB1\*1501.

#### Signal Transduction
*   **Intracellular Signaling Motifs:** N/A. MHC Class II molecules do not possess intrinsic enzymatic activity, nor do they contain classical activation (ITAMs) or inhibitory (ITIMs) signaling motifs in their short cytoplasmic tails.
*   **Associated Kinases & Adaptor Proteins:** Although MHC Class II molecules lack intrinsic signaling domains, their ligation on the APC (by TCR, antibodies, or superantigens) can trigger "back-signaling" into the APC. Ligation induces the translocation of MHC Class II molecules into specialized membrane lipid rafts, where they associate with nonreceptor tyrosine kinases of the Src family (such as Lyn or Fyn). This leads to tyrosine phosphorylation of various cytoplasmic substrates, activation of phospholipase C gamma (PLCγ), mobilization of intracellular calcium, and activation of protein kinase C (PKC) isoforms. Additionally, the invariant chain (CD74), before being cleaved, acts as a signaling receptor for the cytokine MIF (Macrophage Migration Inhibitory Factor) when expressed on the cell surface, initiating survival pathways.
*   **Downstream Transcription Factors Activated:** Back-signaling through MHC Class II on APCs leads to the downstream activation and nuclear translocation of transcription factors such as NF-κB, AP-1, and CREB. This activation drives the upregulation of costimulatory molecules (such as B7-1 and B7-2) and the transcription of inflammatory cytokines (such as IL-12), enhancing the antigen-presenting capacity and survival of the APC.

#### Functional Outcomes
*   **Cellular Response to Ligation:** Ligation of the peptide-MHC Class II complex by a specific TCR and the CD4 co-receptor on a CD4+ T lymphocyte triggers a downstream signaling cascade in the T cell. The recruitment of Lck leads to the phosphorylation of CD3 and ζ chain ITAMs, recruiting and activating the tyrosine kinase ZAP-70. ZAP-70 phosphorylates scaffold adaptors (such as LAT and SLP-76), recruiting enzymes that activate three major downstream signaling pathways:
    *   **Calcium-Calmodulin Pathway**: Activates the phosphatase calcineurin, which dephosphorylates the transcription factor NFAT, allowing its translocation to the nucleus.
    *   **Ras/Rac MAPK Pathway**: Leads to the activation of ERK and JNK kinases, inducing the expression and activation of Fos and Jun, which dimerize to form the transcription factor AP-1.
    *   **PKCθ Pathway**: Leads to the phosphorylation of IκB and its subsequent degradation, releasing the transcription factor NF-κB for nuclear translocation.
    These transcription factors act synergistically to induce the transcription of interleukin-2 (IL-2), the high-affinity IL-2 receptor α chain (CD25), and anti-apoptotic proteins (such as Bcl-2 and Bcl-XL), driving T-cell survival, clonal proliferation, and differentiation.
*   **Role in Immune Cascade:** MHC Class II molecules are the central orchestrators of CD4+ T-cell-mediated adaptive immunity:
    *   **Th1 Differentiation & Macrophage Activation**: Naive CD4+ T cells that recognize peptide-MHC-II on dendritic cells in the presence of IL-12 differentiate into Th1 cells. Effector Th1 cells then recognize the same peptide-MHC-II complexes on macrophages, activating them via IFN-γ secretion and CD40L-CD40 interactions to destroy phagocytosed intracellular pathogens.
    *   **Tfh Differentiation & B-Cell Help**: CD4+ T cells that differentiate into T follicular helper (Tfh) cells interact with B cells presenting peptide-MHC-II complexes. This interaction drives B-cell proliferation, germinal center formation, somatic hypermutation, antibody class switching, and affinity maturation.
    *   **Th17 Differentiation**: In the presence of pro-inflammatory cytokines, naive CD4+ T cells differentiate into Th17 cells, which secrete IL-17 to recruit neutrophils and clear extracellular bacterial and fungal infections.
    *   **Thymic Selection**: In the thymus, MHC Class II expression on cortical epithelial cells mediates positive selection, directing double-positive thymocytes that weakly recognize self-peptide-MHC-II to commit to the CD4+ helper lineage. Medullary thymic epithelial cells (mTECs) expressing MHC-II present self-antigens to induce clonal deletion (negative selection) of highly self-reactive CD4+ T cells, maintaining central tolerance.
*   **Regulation & Down-modulation:** To prevent continuous, unregulated T-cell activation, the expression of MHC Class II molecules on the surface of APCs is strictly regulated:
    *   **MARCH-1**: In resting APCs (especially immature dendritic cells and B cells), a membrane-associated ubiquitin E3 ligase called MARCH-1 continuously ubiquitinates the cytoplasmic tail of the MHC Class II β chain, targeting the class II molecules for internalization and degradation in the lysosomes, keeping surface expression low.
    *   **Microbial & Cytokine Modulation**: During an infection, the encounter of APCs with pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs via TLRs) and inflammatory cytokines (such as type I IFNs and IFN-γ) shuts down the transcription and expression of MARCH-1. This increases the half-life and stability of peptide-MHC Class II complexes on the cell surface, ensuring optimal presentation of microbial peptides to naive T cells.
    *   **HLA-DO Regulation**: HLA-DO acts as an inhibitor of HLA-DM. Under resting conditions, DO binds to DM in lysosomes and prevents peptide exchange. Upon cellular activation or exposure to inflammatory cytokines, the relative level of DM increases compared to DO, releasing DM to actively catalyze CLIP displacement and load antigenic peptides.

#### Clinical & Pharmacologic Relevance
*   **Associated Immunodeficiencies (if mutated):**
    *   **MHC Class II Deficiency (Bare Lymphocyte Syndrome Type II - BLS II)**: A rare, autosomal recessive, life-threatening primary immunodeficiency.
        *   **Underlying Genetic Defect**: The mutations do not occur in the structural HLA class II genes themselves. Instead, BLS II is caused by mutations in genes encoding the crucial transcriptional machinery required for MHC Class II expression. The most commonly mutated genes include those encoding:
            *   **CIITA** (Class II Transcription Activator) - the master transcriptional co-activator.
            *   **RFX5** (Regulatory Factor X5).
            *   **RFXANK** (RFX-Associated Ankyrin-containing protein).
            *   **RFXAP** (RFX-Associated Protein).
        *   **Cellular Consequences**: There is a complete failure to express classical MHC Class II molecules (HLA-DP, DQ, DR) on the surface of B cells, macrophages, and dendritic cells, and an inability of IFN-γ to induce class II expression on non-lymphoid cells.
        *   **Clinical & Immunological Phenotype**: Because of the lack of MHC Class II expression on thymic cortical epithelial cells, positive selection of CD4+ T cells is severely impaired, leading to a profound deficiency of mature CD4+ helper T cells in the periphery. Since helper T cells are required for both cell-mediated immunity and T-dependent antibody production, patients suffer from a severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID) phenotype. This presents in infancy with recurrent, severe, and fatal viral, bacterial, fungal, and protozoal infections, particularly in the gastrointestinal and respiratory tracts.
*   **Targeted Biologic Therapies (Monoclonal Antibodies):**
    *   **Anti-CD20 (Rituximab)**: Used extensively to treat B-cell lymphomas and autoimmune diseases by depleting B cells, which are major MHC Class II-expressing APCs.
    *   **Anti-CD40L (CD154) / Anti-CD40 Monoclonal Antibodies**: Biological agents designed to block the costimulatory interaction between MHC Class II-restricted CD4+ T cells and APCs/B cells, utilized in transplantation and autoimmune therapy.
    *   **Anti-CD4 Monoclonal Antibodies**: Evaluated clinically to inhibit MHC Class II-restricted T-cell co-receptor function and block HIV entry.
*   **Pathogen Sabotage or Mimicry:** Numerous pathogens have evolved mechanisms to evade immune surveillance by targeting the MHC Class II presentation pathway:
    *   **Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)**: Downregulates cell surface MHC Class II expression and selectively infects and destroys CD4+ T cells (which recognize MHC-II), crippling helper T-cell-mediated immunity.
    *   **Cytomegalovirus (CMV)**: Produces specific proteins that retain newly synthesized MHC Class II molecules within the ER or Golgi, or direct them to lysosomes for premature degradation.
    *   **Mycobacterium tuberculosis**: Inhibits the fusion of phagosomes with lysosomes within macrophages and actively blocks the IFN-γ-induced transcription of CIITA, preventing the display of mycobacterial peptides on MHC Class II.
    *   **Helicobacter pylori**: Upregulates the host ubiquitin E3 ligase MARCH-1 or directly targets MHC Class II molecules to induce their endocytosis and lysosomal degradation, reducing surface expression.
    *   **Bacterial Superantigens (SEB, TSST-1)**: Exploit MHC Class II molecules as high-affinity receptors. By binding to the outer surface of the class II molecule (outside the cleft) and cross-linking it with specific TCR Vβ chains, they bypass the normal antigen processing and clonal selection machinery. This induces massive, non-protective polyclonal activation of up to 20% of all T cells, causing toxic shock syndrome characterized by systemic cytokine release and shock.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    *   **"CLIP" (Class II-Associated Invariant Chain Peptide)**: The molecular "placeholder" that protects the empty cleft from binding premature peptides.
    *   **"Peptide Editing"**: The process catalyzed by HLA-DM where low-affinity peptides are continuously exchanged for high-affinity peptides in the lysosome.
    *   **"MHC Restriction"**: The fundamental immunological rule that T lymphocytes can only recognize foreign peptide antigens when they are bound and presented by self-MHC molecules.
    *   **"Immune Response (Ir) Genes"**: The historical genetic term for what was later discovered to be the polymorphic MHC Class II structural genes.
    *   **"Codominant Expression"**: The phenomenon where both maternal and paternal HLA alleles are expressed simultaneously, maximizing the array of peptides an individual can present.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   **Presentation of Cytosolic Antigens (Autophagy)**: Although the MHC Class II pathway conventionally processes endocytosed extracellular/vesicular antigens, cytosolic proteins can be presented on MHC Class II. This occurs through autophagy (such as macroautophagy or chaperone-mediated autophagy), where cytosolic self-proteins or viral proteins are delivered into endosomal/lysosomal compartments, degraded, and loaded onto MHC Class II.
    *   **Lack of Polymorphism in HLA-DRA**: While most HLA Class II chains are highly polymorphic, the human HLA-DRA gene (encoding the DRα chain) is virtually monomorphic (nonpolymorphic) in the human population. Thus, all HLA-DR diversity resides almost entirely in the DRB chains (DRβ).
    *   **Endothelial Cell Presentation**: Human vascular endothelial cells can express MHC Class II under the influence of IFN-γ and can present antigens to activate memory CD4+ T cells, whereas murine endothelial cells express very little MHC Class II and are poor antigen presenters.
