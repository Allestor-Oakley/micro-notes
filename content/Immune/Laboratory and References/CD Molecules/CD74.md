### CD74

#### Structure & Genetics
*   **Molecular Structure & Subunits:** CD74, also known as the MHC class II invariant chain (Ii), exists as 33-, 35-, and 41-kD isoforms. It forms a homotrimer composed of three subunits, each of which physically binds and associates with one newly synthesized MHC class II αβ heterodimer.
*   **Gene Locus & Rearrangement:** Germline-encoded; does not undergo somatic gene recombination (V(D)J rearrangement) or somatic hypermutation. The specific chromosomal gene locus is N/A.
*   **Associated Chaperone Proteins:** Directly associates with newly synthesized MHC class II α and β chains in the rough endoplasmic reticulum (ER). Its folding and assembly are also aided by ER-resident chaperones such as calnexin.
*   **Polymorphisms & Isotypes:** Exists in three distinct molecular weight isoforms (isotypes) generated via alternative splicing of its primary pre-mRNA: 33-kD, 35-kD, and 41-kD isoforms. Polymorphisms in the coding sequence are N/A.

#### Expression & Binding
*   **Cells Expressing this Molecule:** Constitutively expressed on professional antigen-presenting cells (APCs) of the immune system, including B lymphocytes, dendritic cells (DCs), and macrophages/mononuclear phagocytes. It is also expressed on cortical and medullary thymic epithelial cells.
*   **Primary Ligands / Antigens:** Binds newly synthesized, peptide-empty MHC class II molecules (including human HLA-DR, HLA-DQ, HLA-DP; and murine I-A, I-E) within the endoplasmic reticulum.
*   **Binding Kinetics & Affinity:** Binds newly synthesized MHC class II αβ heterodimers with high affinity, blocking their peptide-binding clefts to prevent premature peptide binding within the ER.
*   **Co-receptors Required for Binding:** N/A
*   **Role as a Transplant, Blood Group, or Tumor Antigen:**
    - **Transplant Immunology:** Essential for the transport and assembly of MHC class II molecules, which are the primary targets of CD4+ T cell-mediated allorecognition and acute/chronic graft rejection.
    - **Tumor Marker:** Used as an immunohistochemical marker to identify certain B-cell malignancies, leukemia, or lymphoma, as well as some myeloid leukemias. It does not function as a classical blood group antigen.

#### Signal Transduction
*   **Intracellular Signaling Motifs:** The cytoplasmic tail of CD74 contains endosomal targeting/sorting signals (di-leucine-based motifs) that mediate the intracellular trafficking of the MHC-II–Ii complex from the trans-Golgi network to late endosomes and lysosomes. It does not contain classical activating (ITAM) or inhibitory (ITIM/ITSM) signaling motifs.
*   **Associated Kinases & Adaptor Proteins:** Does not directly associate with TCR/BCR-associated kinases like LCK or LYN. It interacts with clathrin-associated adaptor complexes that guide the intracellular sorting of the vesicle-bound MHC-II–Ii complex.
*   **Downstream Transcription Factors Activated:** N/A (Does not directly activate downstream transcription factors, as it functions primarily as an intracellular chaperone and sorting molecule).

#### Functional Outcomes
*   **Cellular Response to Ligation:**
    - **Prevention of Premature Peptide Binding:** By occupying the peptide-binding cleft of newly synthesized MHC-II molecules in the ER, CD74 blocks the cleft and prevents MHC-II from accepting peptides encountered in the ER, leaving those ER-derived peptides (transported by TAP) to associate with MHC-I molecules.
    - **Intracellular Sorting and Transport:** Guides the newly synthesized MHC-II αβ heterodimers from the trans-Golgi network to late endosomes and lysosomes (the MHC class II compartment or MIIC), where internalized/endocytosed proteins have been proteolytically degraded into peptides.
    - **Degradation and Generation of CLIP:** Within the acidic endosomal/lysosomal vesicles, CD74 is proteolytically cleaved by acidic proteases (such as cathepsins, specifically cathepsin S in most peripheral APCs, and cathepsin L in cortical thymic epithelial cells of rodents), leaving only a 24-amino-acid remnant called class II–associated invariant chain peptide (CLIP) sitting in the peptide-binding cleft. This enzymatic cleavage also removes the transmembrane portion and cytoplasmic tail of CD74, which prevents tethering of MHC-II molecules to the lysosomal membrane and allows MHC-II–peptide complexes to bud out of the vesicles and go to the cell surface.
*   **Role in Immune Cascade:** Essential for the generation of functional peptide-MHC class II complexes on the cell surface of APCs. This pathway is critical for:
    - Positive selection and lineage commitment of mature CD4+ T helper cells in the thymic cortex.
    - Activation and priming of naive CD4+ helper T cells by mature dendritic cells in secondary lymphoid organs.
    - Providing helper T-cell signals (via CD40L-CD40 and cytokines) to B cells for germinal center formation, somatic hypermutation, and antibody isotype switching, and to macrophages for classical activation (M1) to kill intracellular pathogens.
*   **Regulation & Down-modulation:** After CD74 is proteolytically cleaved in lysosomes, the remaining CLIP peptide must be removed to allow antigenic peptide binding. This is catalyzed by HLA-DM (or H-2M in mice), which acts as a peptide exchanger, facilitating the removal of CLIP and the loading of higher-affinity peptides to the MHC-II cleft. HLA-DO binds to HLA-DM in lysosomes and negatively regulates its function, preventing peptide exchange until DM is released.

#### Clinical & Pharmacologic Relevance
*   **Associated Immunodeficiencies (if mutated):**
    - **MHC Class II Deficiency (Bare Lymphocyte Syndrome):** An autosomal recessive immunodeficiency caused by mutations in transcription factors regulating MHC class II gene expression (including CIITA, RFX5, RFXANK, and RFXAP). Since CD74/Ii transcription is coordinately regulated with classical MHC class II genes, this leads to a complete lack of HLA-DR, DQ, DP, and CD74 expression on B lymphocytes, macrophages, and dendritic cells. Affected individuals show defective positive selection of CD4+ T cells, a profound reduction in mature CD4+ T cells, and an inability of IFN-γ to induce MHC class II expression, resulting in fatal susceptibility to severe infections.
    - **Cathepsin L Deficiency:** In rodents, loss of cathepsin L results in an inability of cortical thymic epithelial cells (cTECs) to degrade CD74 (Ii) to CLIP, which blocks positive selection and leads to a major reduction in CD4+ single-positive T cells.
*   **Targeted Biologic Therapies (Monoclonal Antibodies):** Monoclonal antibodies targeting CD74 or the CLIP-MHC class II complex have been explored for immunotherapy of B-cell malignancies (such as multiple myeloma, chronic lymphocytic leukemia, and non-Hodgkin lymphomas) or autoimmune diseases, but are not specified in detail in the sources.
*   **Pathogen Sabotage or Mimicry:** Many intracellular pathogens and viruses target the MHC class II pathway to evade CD4+ T-cell detection. Some pathogens can secrete inhibitors of lysosomal cathepsins, thereby blocking the proteolytic cleavage of CD74 (Ii) to CLIP, which prevents empty MHC-II molecules from loading antigenic peptides and blocks their expression on the cell surface.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - **"MHC Class II Invariant Chain":** Describing its principal cellular function as a constant, nonpolymorphic protein that associates with newly synthesized MHC class II α and β chains.
    - **"CLIP (Class Class II-associated Invariant Chain Peptide)":** The 24-amino-acid remnant of CD74 that remains in the peptide-binding cleft of MHC-II molecules after lysosomal proteolysis.
    - **"Intracellular Sorting Chaperone":** Highlights its biophysical role in targeting newly synthesized MHC-II complexes from the Golgi apparatus to the MIIC compartment.
*   **Key Experimental Markers:**
    - **CD74 (Invariant Chain):** Used as an intracellular marker to stain and identify late endosomal and lysosomal vesicles containing the MHC class II compartment (MIIC).
    - **CLIP-Loaded MHC Class II:** Specific antibodies targeting CLIP-MHC class II complexes can be used to assess the efficiency of HLA-DM-mediated peptide exchange.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - **Species and Tissue-Specific Cathepsin Usage:** While most peripheral APCs (B cells, macrophages, dendritic cells) in both humans and rodents utilize cathepsin S to degrade the invariant chain (CD74) to CLIP in lysosomal compartments, cortical thymic epithelial cells (cTECs) in rodents utilize cathepsin L to generate unique MHC class II-associated peptides for positive selection. Mice lacking cathepsin L show a severe defect in T-cell positive selection due to failure of Ii degradation in cTECs, whereas T-cell positive selection in humans may exhibit different enzymatic dependencies.
