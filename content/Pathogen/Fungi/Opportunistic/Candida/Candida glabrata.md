### Candida glabrata

#### Mycology & Structural Characteristics
*   **Cell Wall Composition & Membrane:** The cell wall contains mannans (recognized by host Toll-like receptor 4), 1,3-beta-D-glucan (recognized by Dectin-1 receptors), and chitin. The cell membrane contains ergosterol as its major sterol component.
*   **Thermal Dimorphism & Environmental Triggers:** Not thermally dimorphic. It is a commensal organism and part of the normal human microbiome of the gastrointestinal tract, mucous membranes, and skin.
*   **Microscopic Morphology (Yeast Form):** Exists exclusively as oval yeastlike forms (3-5 μm) that reproduce by the formation of buds (blastoconidia).
*   **Microscopic Morphology (Mold / Hyphal Form):** N/A
*   **Macroscopic & Colony Morphology:** Variable morphology; colonies are usually pasty, white to tan, and opaque. It can be identified specifically by its positive reaction to the rapid trehalose test.

#### Virulence Factors & Pathogenesis
*   **Enzymes & Proteases:** Secretes degradative enzymes including proteases and phospholipases that facilitate host tissue invasion.
*   **Host Immune Evasion:** Exhibits a strong propensity for both intrinsic and acquired resistance to azoles (e.g., [[fluconazole]]) and other commonly used antifungal agents, providing a distinct survival advantage in hospitalized patients exposed to these drugs. Additional virulence factors include cellular adherence capabilities, cell-surface hydrophobicity, cell wall mannans, and phenotypic switching.
*   **Thermal Tolerance Mechanisms:** Readily grows and proliferates at mammalian body temperatures (37°C).
*   **Toxins:** N/A
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Innate host defenses, specifically neutrophils and [[Monocyte|monocytes]], provide crucial protection against systemic infection. Dissemination and invasive disease are heavily linked to severe immunosuppression, prolonged neutropenia, hematologic malignancies, stem cell or solid organ transplantation, and extensive gastrointestinal mucosal damage induced by cytotoxic chemotherapy.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Infection:** Endogenous opportunistic infections. Usually presents as simple mucosal colonization, mucocutaneous candidiasis, oral thrush, or vulvovaginitis. Also a frequent cause of urinary tract infections, including cystitis and pyelonephritis.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Endocarditis, pericarditis, meningitis, brain abscesses, endophthalmitis, hepatosplenic candidiasis, peritonitis, osteomyelitis, arthritis, and visceral abscesses.
*   **Disseminated Disease:** Hematogenous dissemination and candidemia (bloodstream infections) with a high propensity to seed deep tissues of target organs. Transfer of the organism from the gastrointestinal mucosa to the bloodstream requires overgrowth of the yeasts in their commensal habitat coupled with a mucosal breach.
*   **Opportunistic Presentations:** A prominent and emerging cause of nosocomial central line-associated bloodstream infections (CLABSI) in adult intensive care units (ICUs). It is a truly "emerging" cause of bloodstream infections, disproportionately affecting specific high-risk groups: solid organ transplant recipients (38.4% of *Candida* isolates), stem cell transplant recipients (32.7%), solid tumor patients (28.9%), and hematologic malignancy patients (25.5%).
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions:** N/A

#### Epidemiology & Exposures
*   **Geographic Endemicity:** Distributed worldwide. Represents approximately 18.4% of all *Candida* bloodstream isolates globally, with the highest regional prevalences in North America (25.3%) and Europe (16.0%), followed by the Asia-Pacific region (14.2%) and Latin America (8.8%).
*   **Environmental Reservoir:** Endogenous opportunist. Functions as normal microbial flora colonizing the human gastrointestinal mucosa, vaginal mucosa, skin, and nails.
*   **High-Risk Activities & Occupations:** Prolonged hospitalization in intensive care units, use of central venous or indwelling urinary catheters, total parenteral nutrition, broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy, acute hemodialysis, abdominal surgery, gastrointestinal perforations, or anastomotic leaks.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** The widespread and extensive clinical use of [[fluconazole]] has selectively eliminated more susceptible *Candida* species (like *C. albicans*) and precipitated the emergence and overgrowth of the highly azole-resistant *C. glabrata* across global healthcare settings.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Microscopic Visualization & Special Stains:** Clinical specimens (e.g., tissue biopsies) stained with Gomori Methenamine Silver (GMS), Periodic Acid-Schiff (PAS), or Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) reveal budding yeast cells but lack any pseudohyphae or true hyphae.
*   **Wet Mounts:** Saline swabs, corneal scrapings, or mucosal scrapings can be examined directly using a 10-20% potassium hydroxide (KOH) and calcofluor white preparation to visualize the budding yeasts via fluorescence.
*   **Culture Media & Growth Speed:** Grows readily on standard mycologic media. Can be isolated and differentiated using selective chromogenic media (e.g., CHROMagar Candida).
*   **Serology & Antigen Testing:** Can be rapidly identified directly from positive blood culture bottles using Peptide Nucleic Acid-Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization (PNA-FISH). Specifically, the Yeast Traffic Light PNA-FISH kit targets species-specific rRNA sequences, causing *C. glabrata* (along with *C. krusei*) to fluoresce red. It triggers a positive result on the 1,3-beta-D-glucan (Fungitell) assay and mannan antigen tests (LA, EIA). Definitive identification is also achieved via Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption Ionization-Time of Flight Mass Spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS), the rapid trehalose test, carbohydrate assimilation panels, and gene sequencing.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **Targeting Membrane / Ergosterol:** *C. glabrata* demonstrates significant intrinsic and acquired resistance to azoles. It shows moderate activity/resistance to [[fluconazole]] (++), [[itraconazole]] (++), and [[isavuconazole]] (+++), with somewhat better but still variable activity to [[voriconazole]] (+++). It exhibits reliable activity with occasional resistance to [[amphotericin B]] (+++) and moderate activity/resistance to [[flucytosine]] (++).
*   **Targeting Cell Wall / Glucan:** Highly susceptible and very active (++++) against the echinocandins (e.g., [[Anidulafungin]], [[Caspofungin]], [[Micafungin]], and rezafungin).
*   **Alternative / Cutaneous Treatments:** Topical application of systemically active antifungal agents for superficial mucosal and cutaneous infections. Removal of indwelling central venous or urinary catheters is critical for resolving catheter-associated fungemia.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Formulations:** Because *C. glabrata* is frequently resistant to [[fluconazole]], empirical therapy for invasive candidiasis or candidemia in critically ill patients, especially those with prior azole exposure or hematologic malignancies, heavily relies on echinocandins as first-line therapy.
*   **Prophylaxis & Prevention:** Strict catheter/line management and the judicious use of broad-spectrum antibacterial and antifungal ([[fluconazole]]) agents to prevent selective endogenous overgrowth.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Rapid trehalose test, Yeast Traffic Light PNA-FISH (fluoresces red), [[fluconazole]] resistance, emerging nosocomial pathogen, solid organ transplant pathogen (38.4% of isolates).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike *[[Candida albicans]]* and almost all other clinically significant *Candida* species (which readily produce pseudohyphae and/or true hyphae), *C. glabrata* is unique in that it EXCLUSIVELY forms budding yeasts and does NOT produce pseudohyphae or true hyphae in clinical specimens or culture.
