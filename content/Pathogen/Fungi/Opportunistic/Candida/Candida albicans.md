### Candida albicans
#### Mycology & Structural Characteristics
*   **Cell Wall Composition & Membrane:** The fungal cell wall is composed of chitin, glycoproteins, and sugar polymers including mannans (e.g., 1,6-alpha-mannose) and glucans (e.g., 1,3-beta-glucan and 1,6-beta-glucan). The cell membrane contains ergosterol.
*   **Thermal Dimorphism & Environmental Triggers:** *Candida albicans* is dimorphic and capable of rapid morphological transitions depending on environmental conditions like pH and temperature. In culture or tissue, it grows as a yeast, but it can quickly switch to true hyphae (germ tubes) when incubated in serum at 37°C for 60 to 90 minutes. It exhibits phenotypic switching, allowing a single strain to change reversibly among several morphotypes (e.g., from smooth white colonies to fuzzy/hairy colonies) to adapt to different environmental microniches.
*   **Microscopic Morphology (Yeast Form):** The yeast form (blastoconidia) consists of oval, budding cells measuring 3 to 6 μm in diameter. They stain strongly Gram-positive. The buds are typically hydrophilic.
*   **Microscopic Morphology (Mold / Hyphal Form):** *Candida albicans* uniquely produces both pseudohyphae (chains of elongated budding cells that are pinched or constricted at the septations) and true hyphae (germ tubes, which are thinner, uniform, and lack the pinched septations). On nutritionally deficient media, it characteristically produces large, spherical terminal chlamydospores. The germ tubes are hydrophobic.
*   **Macroscopic & Colony Morphology:** On standard agar media at room temperature or 37°C, it typically produces soft, opaque, cream-colored, domed colonies with a distinct yeasty odor within 24 hours. Submerged pseudohyphal growth may be apparent below the agar surface.

#### Virulence Factors & Pathogenesis
*   **Enzymes & Proteases:** The fungus secretes a family of 10 aspartyl proteinases (SAP) that hydrolyze host proteins and degrade host cell membranes and immunoglobulins, facilitating the breaching of connective tissue barriers. It also secretes phospholipase (PLB1) from yeasts and pseudohyphae, which damages host cells and aids in tissue invasion.
*   **Host Immune Evasion:** *Candida* heavily utilizes phenotypic switching to rapidly alter cell wall glycoproteins, proteolytic enzyme secretion, and susceptibility to oxidative damage. It forms resilient biofilms shielded by an extracellular matrix on biological and prosthetic surfaces (like indwelling catheters), which mechanically block host immune cells and antifungal drugs. The pseudohyphal morphotype makes the organism physically difficult for phagocytes to ingest. Furthermore, *Candida* surface glycoproteins may suppress the host immune response.
*   **Thermal Tolerance Mechanisms:** Readily grows and replicates at 37°C within the mammalian host.
*   **Toxins:** N/A
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Protection against *Candida* relies heavily on intact skin/mucosal barriers and innate immune responses. Host cell pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) such as dectin-1 (binding 1,3-beta-glucan) and Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR-4, binding 1,2-beta-mannan) detect the yeast, triggering Th17 and Th1 responses. This induces IL-17, TNF-alpha, IFN-gamma, and granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF), leading to the massive activation and recruitment of neutrophils and macrophages. Consequently, patients with profound neutropenia, advanced [[HIV]]/AIDS (CD4 < 100 cells/μL), solid organ or stem cell transplants, or those taking systemic corticosteroids and immunosuppressants are at uniquely high risk for invasive disease. Defects in T-lymphocyte responsiveness to *Candida* specifically lead to Chronic Mucocutaneous Candidiasis (CMC).

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Infection:** Simple mucosal colonization or superficial mucocutaneous candidiasis. Oropharyngeal candidiasis (thrush) presents as patchy, white, cottage cheese-like pseudomembranous plaques on the tongue, lips, gums, or palate that leave a raw, bleeding surface when scraped. Vaginal invasion leads to vulvovaginitis characterized by irritation, pruritus, and a creamy discharge. Cutaneous infections occur in moist, occluded intertriginous areas (axillae, groin, toe webs, inframammary folds) as erythematous vesiculopustular rashes with satellite pustules. Severe diaper rash is common in newborns.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** The fungus can infect the nails (onychomycosis) and nail folds (paronychia), often presenting as painful erythematous swelling mimicking pyogenic bacterial infections. Angular cheilitis presents as sore fissures at the corners of the mouth. Chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis presents in childhood (often linked to autoimmunity and hypoparathyroidism) with highly disfiguring, unremitting, crusty, granulomatous keratitic lesions on the skin, mucosa, and scalp.
*   **Disseminated Disease:** Hematogenous dissemination (candidemia) can lead to seeding of deep target organs. Hepatosplenic candidiasis (chronic disseminated candidiasis) occurs on recovery from neutropenia, marked by high spiking fevers, elevated alkaline phosphatase, and multiple lucencies in the liver and [[spleen]]. Endocarditis features large, friable vegetations with high embolic risk. Central nervous system (CNS) candidiasis can mimic bacterial meningitis. Hematogenous seeding also causes renal abscesses, papillary necrosis, vertebral osteomyelitis, endophthalmitis, and chorioretinitis.
*   **Opportunistic Presentations:** *Candida* is the most common cause of central line-associated bloodstream infections in adult ICUs. Intraabdominal candidiasis (peritonitis) is a severe complication following gastrointestinal surgery, anastomotic leaks, perforated appendicitis, or acute necrotizing pancreatitis.
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions:** White "cottage cheese" plaques in the oropharynx. Intertriginous erythematous rashes with distinct satellite pustules.

#### Epidemiology & Exposures
*   **Geographic Endemicity:** Cosmopolitan; worldwide distribution.
*   **Environmental Reservoir:** The primary reservoir is the normal human microbiome; *Candida albicans* endogenously colonizes the gastrointestinal mucosa (from mouth to rectum), vagina, urethra, skin, and nails in 25% to 50% of healthy humans. It has also been isolated from environmental air, water, and soil.
*   **High-Risk Activities & Occupations:** Prolonged hand immersion in water (homemakers, bartenders, cooks, fish handlers) heavily predisposes to interdigital candidiasis. The widespread use of broad-spectrum antibacterial drugs promotes massive endogenous overgrowth by eradicating competing normal bacterial flora.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Most infections are endogenous, but exogenous nosocomial transmission is frequent. Hand-to-catheter transfer by healthcare workers is a major source of hospital outbreaks in ICUs and neonatal units.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Microscopic Visualization & Special Stains:** Budding yeast cells, pseudohyphae, and true hyphae can be visualized via Gram stain (Gram-positive) or in histopathological tissue sections using Periodic acid-Schiff (PAS), Gomori methenamine silver (GMS), and Gridley stains.
*   **Wet Mounts:** Rapid bedside diagnosis of mucosal or cutaneous lesions is achieved by examining scrapings/secretions in 10% to 20% potassium hydroxide (KOH), ideally mixed with calcofluor white, allowing the chitin-rich fungal elements to fluoresce brilliantly under a fluorescent microscope.
*   **Culture Media & Growth Speed:** The organism grows rapidly (within 24 to 48 hours) as cream-colored, pasty colonies on standard mycologic media (Sabouraud dextrose agar) and most bacteriologic media at room temperature or 37°C. Blood cultures utilizing lysis-centrifugation tubes or continuous-monitoring systems generally yield growth within 3 to 5 days, though sensitivity for deep disseminated candidiasis is only roughly 50%.
*   **Serology & Antigen Testing:** Detection of circulating 1,3-beta-D-glucan (Fungitell assay, positive at >= 80 pg/mL) and *Candida* mannan/anti-mannan via enzyme immunoassay (EIA) or latex agglutination are useful adjuncts for diagnosing systemic disease. Molecular identification can be achieved via T2Candida magnetic resonance assay (detects directly from whole blood with 1 CFU/mL limit), PNA-FISH, or MALDI-TOF MS. Presumptive biochemical identification relies heavily on the classic germ tube test (hyphae formation in serum at 37°C), rapid trehalose test (RAT), carbohydrate assimilation panels, and CHROMagar Candida (where *C. albicans* strictly produces green colonies).

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **Targeting Membrane / Ergosterol:** [[Fluconazole]] is highly effective and widely used for primary therapy and step-down consolidation for *C. albicans*, as primary resistance is exceedingly rare (unlike *C. glabrata* or *C. krusei*). Other active azoles include [[voriconazole]], [[posaconazole]], and [[itraconazole]]. Intravenous [[amphotericin B]] (deoxycholate or lipid formulations) is utilized for severe, refractory, or critical systemic cases.
*   **Targeting Cell Wall / Glucan:** Echinocandins ([[caspofungin]], [[micafungin]], [[anidulafungin]]) inhibit 1,3-beta-D-glucan synthase and are potent, fungicidal first-line agents for invasive candidiasis and candidemia. Resistance to [[echinocandins]] occurs via point mutations in the *fks1* gene.
*   **Alternative / Cutaneous Treatments:** Topical [[nystatin]], [[clotrimazole]], [[miconazole]], econazole, and [[ketoconazole]] are standard for oral thrush and vulvovaginal candidiasis. [[Flucytosine]] may be used in combination regimens for deep infections. Ibrexafungerp, a novel glucan synthase inhibitor, maintains activity against some *fks* mutants.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Formulations:** For central line-associated candidemia or deep abscesses, successful management absolutely mandates removal of the infected nidus (i.e., pulling the infected vascular catheter or surgically draining the abscess). Prolonged azole therapy can select for efflux pump overexpression (MDR/CDR genes) or target enzyme alterations (ERG11 gene mutations/overexpression), leading to resistance.
*   **Prophylaxis & Prevention:** Rigorous control of broad-spectrum antibiotics, intensive catheter care, and strict hand hygiene are the most critical preventive measures. Primary antifungal prophylaxis with oral [[fluconazole]] is administered to high-risk neutropenic leukemia patients, allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant recipients, and high-risk solid organ transplant recipients.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Thrush", "cottage cheese patches", "germ tube test", "pseudohyphae AND true hyphae", "terminal chlamydospores", "CHROMagar green colonies", "phenotypic switching", "ALS glycoproteins".
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** While virtually all pathogenic *Candida* species are restricted to forming budding yeasts and pseudohyphae in vivo, *C. albicans* breaks this rule by being dimorphic and capable of producing distinct true hyphae (germ tubes) alongside pseudohyphae. Furthermore, on nutritionally deficient media like cornmeal agar, *C. albicans* is unique among the common species for producing massive, thick-walled terminal chlamydospores.
