### Chromoblastomycosis

#### Mycology & Structural Characteristics
*   **Cell Wall Composition & Membrane:** Fungal cell walls contain chitin, glucans, mannans, and melanin (naturally pigmented/dematiaceous). Cell walls are thick and dark brown or chestnut-brown due to melanin deposition. Melanin allows the fungi to withstand environmental stresses such as drought, heat, or cold, and protects against host defenses.
*   **Thermal Dimorphism & Environmental Triggers:** Not thermally dimorphic. Instead of thermal dimorphism, traumatic inoculation into host tissue induces morphogenesis into tissue-form muriform cells (sclerotic bodies / Medlar bodies / copper pennies / fumagoid cells) dividing by internal transverse and vertical septation in one or two planes, regardless of temperature.
*   **Microscopic Morphology (Yeast Form):** In tissue, organisms do not form true budding yeasts; instead, they form diagnostic chestnut-brown, thick-walled, spherical to oval cells (4–12 $\mu$m or 6–12 $\mu$m in diameter) termed muriform cells, sclerotic bodies, Medlar bodies, "copper pennies", or fumagoid cells. They divide by internal septation (fission along single or multiple vertical and horizontal planes) rather than budding. Delayed cell separation yields clusters of 4 to 8 cells. In superficial crusts, exudates, or skin scrapings, cells may germinate into septate, branching pigmented hyphae. Exophiala species may form yeastlike cells in freshly isolated colonies.
*   **Microscopic Morphology (Mold / Hyphal Form):** Dematiaceous, branched, septate hyphae with melanized (light to dark brown) cell walls. Agents are morphologically diverse and differentiated in culture by their specific modes of conidiation:
    *   *Phialophora verrucosa*: Produces vase-shaped or flask-shaped phialides with cup-shaped collarettes; mature oval conidia accumulate around the phialide opening.
    *   *Fonsecaea pedrosoi*: Polymorphic; forms short branching chains of blastoconidia (Cladosporium-type), sympodial conidia (Rhinocladiella-type), or phialides with collarettes (Phialophora-type).
    *   *Fonsecaea compacta*: Forms spherical blastoconidia with broad connecting bases in compact structures smaller than *F. pedrosoi*.
    *   *Cladophialophora carrionii* (formerly *Cladosporium carrionii*): Produces elongated conidiophores with long, branching, acropetalous chains of oval conidia formed by distal budding.
    *   *Rhinocladiella aquaspersa*: Produces elliptical to clavate conidia laterally or terminally from lengthening conidiogenous cells via sympodial process.
*   **Macroscopic & Colony Morphology:** In culture on Sabouraud dextrose agar (SDA) or inhibitory mold agar (IMA), colonies grow as slow-growing, compact, woolly, gray, olive, deep brown to black molds with a velvety, often wrinkled or folded surface.

#### Reproduction, Spores & Life Cycle
*   **Asexual Reproduction & Spores (Conidia / Sporangiospores):** Asexual conidia (mitospores) produced from specialized conidiogenous cells or vegetative hyphae depending on the genus and species: phialoconidia extruded from phialides (*P. verrucosa*), blastoconidia formed in acropetalous branching chains (*C. carrionii*, *F. pedrosoi*, *F. compacta*), or sympodial conidia (*R. aquaspersa*, *F. pedrosoi*). In host tissue, asexual reproduction occurs by internal transverse and longitudinal division (isodiametric cleavage/fission) of muriform cells.
*   **Sexual Reproduction & Spores (Teleomorph):** Etiologic agents belong to the Order Chaetothyriales, Class Sordariomycetes / Eurotiomycetes, Phylum Ascomycota. Teleomorphs / sexual cycles are not observed in clinical isolates or routine laboratory cultures.
*   **Ecological Niche & Reservoir:** Saprophytes found in the natural environment residing in soil, woody plants, vegetation, forest detritus, plant debris, and decaying organic matter in tropical and subtropical regions.

#### Virulence Factors & Pathogenesis
*   **Enzymes & Proteases:** Secretion of cell wall-degrading enzymes and proteases that facilitate survival in tissue; ability to digest gelatin varies between pathogenic species (pathogenic species cannot digest gelatin, distinguishing them from saprophytic dematiaceous molds).
*   **Host Immune Evasion:** Cell wall melanin protects fungi against oxidative burst, microbicidal peptides, and host immune defenses. The thick-walled muriform cell structure (sclerotic body) resists [[neutrophil]] and [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophage]] phagocytic killing and destruction, allowing persistence within giant cells and [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]].
*   **Thermal Tolerance Mechanisms:** Ability to proliferate at 37°C in host tissue (saprophytic dematiaceous molds fail to grow at 37°C). Production of cell wall melanin provides thermal stability and protection against environmental heat and drought.
*   **Toxins:** N/A
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** No specific underlying immunodeficiency or predisposing condition is required; infections occur in immunocompetent individuals. Immunity depends on cell-mediated responses (Th1/Th17). Histopathology reveals suppurative and granulomatous tissue reaction with microabscesses containing neutrophils, [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]], giant cells, pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia, and dermal fibrosis.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Infection:** Initiated by traumatic inoculation (e.g., thorn prick, wood splinter, insect bite, or abrasion) of soil or plant debris contaminated with fungal elements, usually on exposed extremities. Begins as a small, painless or mildly pruritic erythematous papule or warty nodule that slowly enlarges over months to years.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Clinical forms include verrucous warty lesions, raised plaques with central scarring, psoriasiform, rupioid, sporotrichoid localized lesions, cystic or mycetoma-like lesions. Satellite lesions can occur secondary to autoinoculation or local lymphatic spread. Rare facial, ear, trunk, neck, or shoulder lesions.
*   **Disseminated Disease:** Hematogenous dissemination to distant organs or deep viscera is extremely rare. Deep tissue invasion is rare.
*   **Opportunistic Presentations:** N/A
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions:** Large, hyperkeratotic, warty, "cauliflower-like" exophytic growths clustered in a region, studded with characteristic surface "black dots" representing hemopurulent exudate and transepidermal elimination of fungal elements, tissue, and erythrocytes.

#### Epidemiology & Exposures
*   **Geographic Endemicity:** Endemic primarily in tropical and subtropical regions with high annual rainfall (e.g., Central and Northern South America (Brazil, Costa Rica, Mexico, Cuba, Venezuela), Madagascar, East coast of Southern Africa, Indonesia, Japan, India, Australia, West Pacific). Local climatic factors influence species distribution: *Fonsecaea pedrosoi* predominates in humid, high-rainfall regions (e.g., wet areas of Madagascar, Central/South America) involving lower extremities; *Cladophialophora carrionii* predominates in arid, low-rainfall regions (e.g., dry areas of Madagascar, Australia, South Africa) involving upper extremities. *Phialophora verrucosa* occurs in tropical climatic zones; *Rhinocladiella aquaspersa* is rare in Latin America.
*   **Environmental Reservoir:** Soil, woody plants, vegetation, forest detritus, and decaying plant matter.
*   **High-Risk Activities & Occupations:** Agricultural workers, barefoot agrarian farmers, foresters, and outdoor laborers in rural tropical areas.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Designated as a Neglected Tropical Disease (NTD) by the World Health Organization (WHO). Not communicable from person to person; no isolation required. Prevented by wearing protective shoes/footwear and protective clothing during agricultural work.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Microscopic Visualization & Special Stains:** Diagnostic finding in tissue sections stained with Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E), Periodic Acid-Schiff (PAS), or Gomori Methenamine Silver (GMS) is the presence of chestnut-brown, thick-walled, spherical, single or clustered muriform cells (sclerotic bodies / Medlar bodies) measuring 4–12 $\mu$m (or 6–12 $\mu$m) in diameter within microabscesses, [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]], or giant cells. Tissue shows suppurative and granulomatous inflammation, pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia, acanthosis, dermal fibrosis, and transepidermal elimination. Fontana-Masson stain can confirm cell wall melanin.
*   **Wet Mounts:** Examination of skin scrapings, crusted exudate, or material aspirated from surface "black dots" mounted in 10%–20% potassium hydroxide (KOH) reveals characteristic thick-walled, multiseptate, brown muriform cells ("copper pennies"). Dermoscopy can directly visualize "black dots" and muriform cells.
*   **Culture Media & Growth Speed:** Specimens (skin scrapings, crusts, biopsy tissue) should be inoculated onto Sabouraud Dextrose Agar (SDA) or Inhibitory Mold Agar (IMA) supplemented with antibiotics (cycloheximide and [[chloramphenicol]]) and incubated at 25°C–30°C and 37°C. Molds are slow-growing, taking 1 to 4 weeks to form compact, dark olive-green to black velvet-like colonies. Speciation based on microscopic mode of conidiogenesis. Pathogenic species grow at 37°C and fail to digest gelatin.
*   **Serology & Antigen Testing:** No standard commercially available serologic tests. [[ELISA and Immunoassays|ELISA]] has been evaluated for monitoring therapeutic evolution and antibody response in *Cladophialophora carrionii* infections.
*   **Molecular Testing & Advanced Identification:** [[PCR]] assays have been developed for specific identification of *Fonsecaea* species and *C. carrionii*. Nucleic acid sequencing (e.g., ribosomal RNA ITS region sequencing) is used in reference laboratories for definitive species-level identification when morphological conidiogenesis is complex or equivocal.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **Targeting Membrane / Ergosterol:** First-line systemic triazole antifungal therapy: [[Itraconazole]] (200–400 mg daily orally) for 6 to 12 months or longer until clinical resolution. [[Posaconazole]] (long-term) or [[voriconazole]] used in refractory cases or as alternatives. [[Terbinafine]] (500–1000 mg daily orally; acts on squalene epoxidase) is an effective first-line agent. Combination therapy of [[itraconazole]] plus [[terbinafine]] or flucytosine ([[Flucytosine|5-fluorocytosine]]) is used in late, extensive, or refractory cases. [[Ketoconazole]] has been used historically.
*   **Targeting Cell Wall / Glucan:** Echinocandins ([[caspofungin]], [[micafungin]], [[anidulafungin]]) are inactive / not indicated for dematiaceous molds causing chromoblastomycosis.
*   **Alternative / Cutaneous Treatments:** Application of local physical heat (thermotherapy using heat-retaining gels, pocket hand-warmers, or local heating devices) applied comfortably to the skin. Local cryotherapy (e.g., liquid nitrogen) or physical destructive techniques can be combined with systemic antifungal chemotherapy.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Formulations:** Complete surgical excision with wide margins is indicated only for small, localized early lesions before extensive tissue involvement. Surgery is generally inadvisable for extensive lesions due to risk of spreading infection within the surgical scar. Systemic drug therapy should precede and follow surgical or destructive interventions and be continued for several months after clinical cure to prevent common relapses.
*   **Prophylaxis & Prevention:** No vaccine or pharmacological prophylaxis available. Primary prevention relies on public health education, wearing protective footwear (shoes/boots), and wearing protective clothing to cover legs and arms during rural agricultural or forest work.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Copper pennies", Medlar bodies, sclerotic bodies, muriform cells, fumagoid cells, "cauliflower-like" verrucous lesions, surface "black dots", dematiaceous mold, transepidermal elimination, WHO Neglected Tropical Disease, *Fonsecaea pedrosoi*, *Cladophialophora carrionii*, *Phialophora verrucosa*.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   Unlike most systemic or subcutaneous mycoses that reproduce in tissue by budding yeast cells, endospores, or hyphae, the agents of chromoblastomycosis uniquely reproduce in tissue by internal transverse and vertical septation (fission/isodiametric division) forming multi-planar muriform cells ("copper pennies").
    *   Morphological diversity in culture (multiple types of conidiogenesis within the same isolate, e.g., *Fonsecaea*) contrasts with uniform, identical muriform cell morphology across all etiologic species in tissue.
    *   Although caused by dematiaceous molds that can cause opportunistic invasive infections in immunocompromised hosts, chromoblastomycosis occurs almost exclusively in immunocompetent individuals following traumatic outdoor exposure.
    *   High rainfall dictates *Fonsecaea pedrosoi* prevalence on lower extremities in wet tropical areas, whereas low rainfall dictates *Cladophialophora carrionii* prevalence on upper extremities in arid tropical/subtropical areas.
