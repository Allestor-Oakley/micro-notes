### Entomophthoromycosis

#### Mycology & Structural Characteristics
*   **Cell Wall Composition & Membrane:** Fungal cell walls contain chitin, glucans, and structural polysaccharides. Cell walls are thin, poorly staining, and surrounded in host tissue by intensely eosinophilic Splendore-Hoeppli material (antigen-antibody complexes and proteinaceous debris).
*   **Thermal Dimorphism & Environmental Triggers:** Non-thermally dimorphic. Organisms exist as saprophytic molds in the environment and produce hyphae or hyphal fragments in tissue. Implantation via percutaneous trauma (*Basidiobolus ranarum*) or inhalation of spores (*Conidiobolus coronatus*) into host tissue induces a chronic, non-angioinvasive, eosinophilic granulomatous reaction.
*   **Microscopic Morphology (Yeast Form):** N/A
*   **Microscopic Morphology (Mold / Hyphal Form):** In tissue sections and in culture, fungal elements appear as broad, thin-walled, poorly staining, pauciseptate hyphae or short hyphal fragments measuring 6 to 25 $\mu$m in diameter, with nonparallel sides and random branching. Although septa are infrequent, they are more prominent than those seen in Mucoraceae.
*   **Macroscopic & Colony Morphology:** Rapidly growing, flat, waxy, powdery, or woolly colonies on standard mycologic media at 25°C–30°C. *Basidiobolus ranarum* produces gray to yellowish, waxy/powdery colonies with an earthy odor and forcible sporangiospore ejection. *Conidiobolus coronatus* produces flat, waxy to powdery, white-to-tan colonies with satellite colonies formed by forcibly expelled conidia.

#### Reproduction, Spores & Life Cycle
*   **Asexual Reproduction & Spores (Conidia / Sporangiospores):** Asexual sporangiospores or single conidial spores are borne singly on short sporophores and are forcibly ejected when mature.
*   **Sexual Reproduction & Spores (Teleomorph):** *Basidiobolus* produces sexual zygospores formed by the fusion of compatible mating types/gametangia with prominent beaklike appendages. *Conidiobolus* species form smooth or villous conidia and zygospores. Belong to Phylum Basidiobolomycota (Order Basidiobolales) and Phylum Entomophthoromycota (Order Entomophthorales).
*   **Ecological Niche & Reservoir:** Environmental saprophytes present in decaying plant debris, leaf litter, and soil in tropical environments. *Basidiobolus ranarum* also inhabits the gastrointestinal tract and intestinal contents of small reptiles, amphibians (frogs, lizards), and fish. *Conidiobolus coronatus* is a plant saprophyte and an insect pathogen.

#### Virulence Factors & Pathogenesis
*   **Enzymes & Proteases:** Secretes hydrolytic enzymes and lipases that facilitate local tissue invasion and expansion through subcutaneous tissue planes.
*   **Host Immune Evasion:** Hyphae trigger a dense, localized eosinophilic [[Acute Inflammatory Response|inflammatory response]] and become encased in thick, intensely eosinophilic Splendore-Hoeppli material (antigen-antibody-complement complexes). This protective coat shields the hyphal fragments from host cellular degradation.
*   **Thermal Tolerance Mechanisms:** Capable of proliferating at 37°C in host subcutaneous tissues.
*   **Toxins:** N/A
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Occurs in immunocompetent hosts without known underlying immunodeficiencies, metabolic acidosis, or underlying predisposing factors. The host tissue response is characterized by eosinophilic abscesses, non-caseating granulomatous inflammation, and dense subcutaneous fibrosis. Hyphae are strictly **non-angioinvasive** (lacking vascular invasion, thrombosis, or ischemic tissue necrosis).

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Infection:**
    *   **Subcutaneous Basidiobolomycosis (*Basidiobolus ranarum*):** Follows traumatic percutaneous implantation of fungal elements from plant debris or soil into subcutaneous tissue. Begins as a small, firm, non-tender subcutaneous nodule that gradually expands to form a large, disk-shaped, rubbery or woody, movable, painless mass localized to the proximal limbs or limb girdles (thighs, buttocks, hips, pelvis, shoulders, trunk).
    *   **Rhinofacial Conidiobolomycosis (*Conidiobolus coronatus*):** Follows inhalation of aerosolized fungal spores or local mucosal implantation. Begins within the nasal cavity or inferior turbinates and spreads slowly to involve the subcutaneous tissues of the nose, upper lip, paranasal sinuses, and face, presenting as a hard, painless, progressive swelling.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:**
    *   **Gastrointestinal Basidiobolomycosis:** Rare invasive presentation involving the gastrointestinal tract (stomach, small intestine, colon), presenting with abdominal pain, [[fever]], palpable abdominal mass, and peripheral eosinophilia, closely mimicking gastrointestinal malignancy, inflammatory bowel disease, or [[appendicitis]]. Reported sporadically worldwide (including a cluster in Arizona, USA).
    *   **Cutaneous Ulceration:** Large *Basidiobolus* subcutaneous masses may expand locally and eventually ulcerate the overlying skin.
    *   **Rhinofacial Deformity:** *C. coronatus* infection produces dramatic, fixed facial disfigurement affecting the nasal bridge, cheek, upper and lower face, and orbit.
*   **Disseminated Disease:** Hematogenous dissemination or deep visceral spread is extremely rare; infection remains strictly localized to the subcutaneous tissues, face, or GI tract.
*   **Opportunistic Presentations:** N/A
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions:** Large, rubbery, disk-shaped, painless, movable subcutaneous mass on the thighs/buttocks in a child (*B. ranarum*) or hard, painless, progressive swelling of the upper lip and nasal bridge causing severe facial deformity in an adult (*C. coronatus*).

#### Epidemiology & Exposures
*   **Geographic Endemicity:**
    *   Prevalent in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide.
    *   Most commonly reported in Central, East, and West Africa, and India.
    *   *Basidiobolus ranarum*: Reported in Africa, India, Middle East, Asia, Europe, and USA (Arizona for GI form).
    *   *Conidiobolus coronatus*: Reported in Latin America/West Indies, Central/West Africa, and India.
*   **Environmental Reservoir:** Leaf and plant debris, soil, decaying organic matter, intestinal tract of amphibians (frogs) and reptiles (lizards).
*   **High-Risk Activities & Occupations:**
    *   *B. ranarum*: Primarily affects **children** (80% < 20 years old), with a male-to-female ratio of 3:1. Acquired via traumatic inoculation (insect bites, scratches, toilet habits using leaves/plant material).
    *   *C. coronatus*: Primarily affects **young adults** (20–40 years old), with a male-to-female ratio of 10:1. Acquired via inhalation of fungal spores.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Not transmissible from person to person; no isolation required. Prevention involves avoiding contact with decaying plant matter, proper wound hygiene, and protective clothing in endemic tropical areas.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Microscopic Visualization & Special Stains:** Biopsy of infected tissue is mandatory for definitive diagnosis. Histopathology stained with Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) reveals focal clusters of granulomatous inflammation, dense eosinophilic infiltration (eosinophilic abscesses), and sparse, poorly stained, thin-walled, broad hyphae or hyphal fragments (6–25 $\mu$m wide) surrounded by prominent, radiating, intensely eosinophilic **Splendore-Hoeppli material**. Hyphae stain weakly with Gomori Methenamine Silver (GMS) and Periodic Acid-Schiff (PAS), but are often better visualized on H&E. Absence of vascular invasion (non-angioinvasive).
*   **Wet Mounts:** Direct KOH wet mounts of tissue aspirates or biopsies demonstrate broad, pauciseptate, hyaline hyphal fragments.
*   **Culture Media & Growth Speed:** Inoculated onto standard mycologic media (Sabouraud dextrose agar without cycloheximide, as cycloheximide may inhibit growth). Organisms grow rapidly (within 2 to 5 days) at 25°C–30°C and 37°C. *Basidiobolus* forms flat, yellowish to gray, waxy or powdery colonies that produce a characteristic musky or earthy odor and forcible ejection of sporangiospores against the petri dish lid. *Conidiobolus* produces flat, waxy, glabrous to powdery, white-to-tan colonies with satellite colonies formed by expelled conidia.
*   **Serology & Antigen Testing:** No commercially available serologic or antigen tests exist.
*   **Molecular Testing & Advanced Identification:** [[PCR]] and ribosomal DNA (ITS region) sequencing can be performed in reference laboratories for species-level identification of *Conidiobolus* and *Basidiobolus* isolates.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **Targeting Membrane / Ergosterol:**
    *   **First-Line Medical Therapy:** Oral **[[itraconazole]]** (100–400 mg daily) is highly effective and considered the treatment of choice for both *Basidiobolus* and *Conidiobolus* infections.
    *   **Classic Alternative Agent:** Oral **saturated solution of potassium iodide (SSKI)**, used historically and still effective (especially in resource-limited settings), often given in combination with [[itraconazole]].
    *   **Alternative Azoles:** [[Posaconazole]], [[voriconazole]], or [[ketoconazole]] may be utilized in refractory cases or as alternatives. [[Amphotericin B]] has variable activity and is generally less effective for subcutaneous entomophthoromycosis than for Mucorales infections.
*   **Targeting Cell Wall / Glucan:** Echinocandins ([[caspofungin]], [[micafungin]], [[anidulafungin]]) are inactive and not indicated.
*   **Alternative / Cutaneous Treatments:** Complete surgical excision is effective for small, localized, disk-shaped *Basidiobolus* masses. However, extensive surgical debulking is generally avoided in *Conidiobolus* rhinofacial disease due to severe residual scarring and fibrosis.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Formulations:** Medical therapy with [[itraconazole]] and/or SSKI should be continued for several months until complete resolution of clinical masses. Facial reconstructive surgery is frequently required in *Conidiobolus* cases after eradication of the fungus to correct extensive facial fibrosis and deformity.
*   **Prophylaxis & Prevention:** No vaccine or pharmacological prophylaxis available. Prevention relies on avoiding traumatic injury with soil/plant debris in endemic tropical areas and maintaining proper wound hygiene.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Subcutaneous entomophthoromycosis, *Basidiobolus ranarum*, *Conidiobolus coronatus*, Splendore-Hoeppli phenomenon, eosinophilic abscesses, broad pauciseptate non-angioinvasive hyphae, disk-shaped rubbery mass (thighs/buttocks in children), rhinofacial swelling (upper lip/nasal bridge in adult males), forcible spore ejection, SSKI + [[itraconazole]].
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   Unlike Mucorales (e.g., *[[Rhizopus]]*, *[[Mucor]]*) which cause acute, rapidly fatal, highly angioinvasive, necrotizing infections in immunocompromised or diabetic hosts, Entomophthorales (*Conidiobolus*, *Basidiobolus*) cause chronic, indolent, **non-angioinvasive**, granulomatous infections in **immunocompetent** hosts.
    *   *Basidiobolus ranarum* predominantly affects young **children** on the lower extremities/buttocks (male:female 3:1), whereas *Conidiobolus coronatus* predominantly affects young **adults** on the face/nose (male:female 10:1).
    *   Histopathologically, hyphae are sparse and weakly staining with GMS/PAS, but are surrounded by prominent, radiating, intensely eosinophilic **Splendore-Hoeppli material** within an [[Eosinophil and Basophil|eosinophil]]-rich granulomatous infiltrate.
    *   *Basidiobolus ranarum* can mimic gastrointestinal malignancy (GI basidiobolomycosis) in both children and adults.
