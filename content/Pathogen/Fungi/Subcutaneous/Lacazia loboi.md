### Lacazia loboi

#### Mycology & Structural Characteristics
*   **Cell Wall Composition & Membrane:** Fungal cell wall is thick, doubly contoured, double-refractile, and hyaline. Cell wall contains chitin, glucans, and mannans characteristic of the order Onygenales and family Ajellomycetaceae. In Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) stained tissue sections, the thick refractile cell wall surrounds one or more hematoxylinophilic nuclei. Stains intensely with Gomori Methenamine Silver (GMS) and Periodic Acid-Schiff (PAS) stains. Unstained fungal cells appear refractile.
*   **Thermal Dimorphism & Environmental Triggers:** Uncultivable ascomycete fungus. Population genetic analysis indicates it is a member of the family Ajellomycetaceae (closely related to [[Paracoccidioides brasiliensis]]) that possesses genetic defects in pathways required for yeast-to-hypha morphologic transition. Percutaneous traumatic inoculation of fungal elements from environmental soil, vegetation, or water into host skin initiates a chronic, indolent dermal infection in which the fungus exists exclusively in a yeastlike morphologic state.
*   **Microscopic Morphology (Yeast Form):** In dermis and subcutaneous tissue, organisms appear as spherical, oval, or lemon-shaped yeastlike cells measuring 6 to 12 $\mu$m in diameter (uniform size, approximately 10 $\mu$m). Fungi reproduce by sequential budding, forming single or branching chains of cells joined together by narrow, tubelike bridges. Individual cells may occasionally display one or two secondary buds (which can be mistaken for the "pilot-wheel" form of [[Paracoccidioides brasiliensis]]). Found both intracellularly (phagocytosed within multinucleated giant cells and [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]]) and extracellularly throughout the dermis.
*   **Microscopic Morphology (Mold / Hyphal Form):** N/A
*   **Macroscopic & Colony Morphology:** N/A

#### Reproduction, Spores & Life Cycle
*   **Asexual Reproduction & Spores (Conidia / Sporangiospores):** Reproduces in host tissues via sequential asexual budding, forming characteristic linear chains of spherical yeast cells linked by narrow tubelike bridges. Does not produce conidia, sporangiospores, or specialized asexual reproductive structures in vitro.
*   **Sexual Reproduction & Spores (Teleomorph):** Phylogenetically classified as an ascomycete fungus in the Order Onygenales, Family Ajellomycetaceae (genus Paracoccidioides / Lacazia). Sexual reproductive structures (teleomorph) have not been observed.
*   **Ecological Niche & Reservoir:** Believed to be a saprophyte residing in soil, vegetation, and water in tropical rain forests (e.g., Amazon rain forest). Paracoccidioides ceti infects both marine and freshwater dolphins (e.g., bottle-nosed dolphins in Florida, Texas, South Brazil, Suriname River estuary, Spanish/French coasts), indicating an aquatic environment/reservoir. Experimental infection has been successfully reproduced by injecting infected tissue into hamsters and armadillos.

#### Virulence Factors & Pathogenesis
*   **Enzymes & Proteases:** N/A
*   **Host Immune Evasion:** Resistance to phagocytic destruction; thick, double-refractile cell walls allow yeast cells to survive and persist intracellularly within dermal [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]] and multinucleated giant cells for decades (up to 40–50 years) without triggering systemic symptoms or systemic dissemination.
*   **Thermal Tolerance Mechanisms:** Adapted to proliferate and persist at 37°C within human dermal and subcutaneous tissues.
*   **Toxins:** N/A
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Infection occurs in immunocompetent individuals following local cutaneous trauma. Host response is characterized by a diffuse, dispersed granulomatous infiltrate in the dermis and subcutaneous tissue consisting of multinucleated giant cells, histiocytes/[[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]], and epithelioid cells densely packed with phagocytosed fungal cells. Lack of extensive dermal fibrosis distinguishes it microscopically from true keloids. Infection does not involve internal organs, [[lymph nodes]], or mucous membranes, and patients have no systemic manifestations of disease.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Infection:**
    *   **Lobomycosis / Lacaziosis / [[Paracoccidioides brasiliensis|Paracoccidioidomycosis]] loboi (Jorge Lobo's Disease):** Follows percutaneous traumatic injury (e.g., thorn prick, insect bite, snakebite, cut during farming/jungle clearing) introducing fungal elements into exposed skin. Begins as a small, painless, raised erythematous papule or macule that enlarges very slowly over months to years.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:**
    *   **Polymorphic Cutaneous Lesions:** Dermal lesions are polymorphic, including macules, papules, keloidal nodules, plaques, verrucous lesions, and ulcerated lesions, all of which may coexist in a single patient. The **nodular keloid-like lesion** is the most common presentation. Lesions commonly affect traumatized, exposed skin areas such as the face, ears (helix of ear), arms, legs, feet, and back. Autoinoculation can lead to local cutaneous spread.
    *   **Squamous Cell Carcinoma Transformation:** Longstanding, chronic lobomycosis lesions (persisting over decades) rarely undergo malignant transformation into squamous cell carcinoma.
*   **Disseminated Disease:** Infection remains strictly confined to the skin and subcutaneous tissues without hematogenous or visceral dissemination to internal organs, mucous membranes, or deep [[lymph nodes]].
*   **Opportunistic Presentations:** N/A
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions:** Slowly evolving, painless, shiny, dusky red or violaceous keloid-like nodules and plaques on exposed extremities, face, or ears, with hypesthesia or mild pruritus, in a patient with exposure to tropical rain forests.

#### Epidemiology & Exposures
*   **Geographic Endemicity:**
    *   Human disease (Paracoccidioides loboi) is endemic exclusively in tropical rain forests of Central and South America: central and western Brazil (Amazon region), Bolivia, Colombia, Costa Rica, Ecuador, Guyana, French Guiana, Mexico, Panama, Peru, Suriname, and Venezuela. Isolated imported cases reported in Holland and Canada/USA following travel to endemic regions.
    *   Dolphin disease (Paracoccidioides ceti) occurs in coastal waters and estuaries: Florida, Texas coast, Spanish and French coasts, South Brazilian coast, and Suriname River estuary.
*   **Environmental Reservoir:** Soil, thick tropical vegetation, plant debris, and aquatic environments (freshwater and marine habitats).
*   **High-Risk Activities & Occupations:** Predominantly affects men involved in outdoor agricultural and jungle activities: farmers, rubber tappers/workers, miners, hunters, and foresters. Peak age of onset is 20 to 40 years (though affects all age groups). Dolphin-to-human transmission has been reported in a single instance (dolphin trainer/handler), but there is **no human-to-human transmission**.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Not communicable from person to person; no isolation required. Prevention involves wearing protective clothing and footwear during farming and jungle clearing, and avoiding direct contact with infected dolphins.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Microscopic Visualization & Special Stains:**
    *   **Histopathology:** Skin biopsy of dermal lesions stained with Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E), Periodic Acid-Schiff (PAS), or Gomori Methenamine Silver (GMS) demonstrates a diffuse, superficial and deep granulomatous infiltrate in the dermis and subcutaneous tissue composed of multinucleated giant cells, [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]], and epithelioid cells. Unstained fungal cells show thick, double-refractile cell walls. Stains intensely with GMS and PAS. H&E reveals thick hyaline cell walls and hematoxylinophilic nuclei.
    *   **Diagnostic Microscopic Finding:** Presence of spherical to oval yeastlike cells (6 to 12 $\mu$m or ~10 $\mu$m) arranged in **characteristic single or branching chains joined by narrow, tubelike bridges**.
*   **Wet Mounts:** Microscopic examination of exudate, skin scrapings, or lesion aspirates mounted in 10%–20% potassium hydroxide (KOH) reveals characteristic chains of thick-walled, spherical yeast cells linked by tubelike bridges.
*   **Culture Media & Growth Speed:** **Uncultivatable**: Lacazia loboi / Paracoccidioides loboi **cannot be cultured in vitro** on artificial mycologic media or cell culture. Inoculation of clinical specimens onto media yields negative growth.
*   **Serology & Antigen Testing:** No commercially available serologic or antigen detection tests exist.
*   **Molecular Testing & Advanced Identification:** [[PCR]] assays targeting 18S, 28S, and internal transcribed spacer (ITS) ribosomal DNA regions or specific genes have been developed for phylogenetic analysis and research identification in reference laboratories.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **Targeting Membrane / Ergosterol:** Systemic antifungal agents ([[itraconazole]], [[ketoconazole]], [[amphotericin B]]) demonstrate **poor or no clinical efficacy** against Paracoccidioides loboi in widespread disease. [[Clofazimine]] (an antimicrobial agent used in leprosy) has been tried in widespread cases with limited success.
*   **Targeting Cell Wall / Glucan:** Echinocandins are inactive and not indicated.
*   **Alternative / Cutaneous Treatments:** **Complete surgical excision** with wide margins is the **only effective treatment** for localized, early skin lesions.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Formulations:** Widespread or multifocal disease responds poorly to surgical intervention (high recurrence rate in surgical scars) and does not respond to standard systemic antifungal chemotherapy.
*   **Prophylaxis & Prevention:** No vaccine or pharmacological prophylaxis available. Primary prevention relies on wearing protective clothing and footwear during agricultural and forest clearing work in tropical rain forests, avoiding skin trauma, and exercising care when handling marine or freshwater dolphins.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Lacazia loboi, Paracoccidioides loboi, Paracoccidioides ceti, Jorge Lobo's disease / Jorge Lobo disease, lobomycosis, lacaziosis, keloid-like nodules, chains of yeast cells connected by tubelike bridges, uncultivatable in vitro, dolphins (aquatic reservoir), Amazon rain forest, no human-to-human transmission.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   **Uncultivatable Fungus:** Unlike almost all other medical mycoses (except [[Pneumocystis jirovecii]] and Rhinosporidium seeberi), Lacazia loboi / Paracoccidioides loboi **cannot be grown on artificial culture media in vitro**.
    *   **Morphologic Distinction:** Chains of spherical yeast cells connected by **narrow tubelike bridges** distinguish Paracoccidioides loboi from [[Paracoccidioides brasiliensis]] (multiple buds in "pilot-wheel" arrangement), [[Blastomyces dermatitidis]] (broad-based single budding), [[Histoplasma capsulatum]] (intracellular small yeasts without chains), and Coccidioides (spherules with endospores).
    *   **Keloid Misdiagnosis:** Grossly resembles keloids, but histopathologically lacks severe dermal fibrosis and instead features a dense granulomatous infiltrate filled with giant cells and fungal chains.
    *   **Taxonomic Reclassification:** Formerly known as Lacazia loboi (or Loboa loboi), recent molecular phylogenetic studies reclassified the human pathogen as Paracoccidioides loboi and the dolphin pathogen as Paracoccidioides ceti (Family Ajellomycetaceae).
