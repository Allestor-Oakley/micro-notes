### Pthirus pubis

#### Arthropod Anatomy & Physical Morphology
*   **Adult Physical Features & Size:** Small, greyish-white or reddish-brown, dorsally flattened, crab-shaped ectoparasitic insect measuring approximately 1.1 to 2.0 mm in length (characteristically shorter and broader than head or body lice). The thorax and abdomen are fused together into a short, wide segment, giving the parasite a distinct crab-like appearance. Possesses 3 pairs of legs; the middle and posterior pairs of legs are substantially thicker and terminate in large, powerful, heavy thumb-like claws adapted specifically for grasping widely spaced, coarse human hair shafts.
*   **Mouthparts & Feeding Apparatus:** Piercing-sucking mouthparts (haematophagous apparatus) adapted for solenophagy; penetrates host skin capillaries to feed exclusively on human blood four to five times per day.
*   **Egg / Nit Morphology & Attachment:** Translucent, oval eggs (nits) measuring approximately 0.8 mm in length, tightly cemented to the base of coarse hair shafts near the skin surface via a durable, water-insoluble chitinous cement substance secreted by the adult female.
*   **Immature Stages:** Nits hatch after 7 to 10 days into miniature, active, 6-legged blood-feeding nymphs that undergo three nymphal molts (instars) over 2 to 3 weeks before maturing into adult crab lice.

#### Lifecycle, Habitat & Host Attachment
*   **Primary Host Range:** Humans (obligate permanent ectoparasite; humans are the only natural host).
*   **Developmental Stages:** Three-stage lifecycle occurring entirely on human hair and skin: Egg (nit) -> Nymph (3 instars) -> Adult male / female. Total lifecycle duration is approximately 3 to 4 weeks.
*   **Attachment Mechanics & Burrowing Behavior:** Grasps coarse, widely spaced hair shafts (pubic hair, perianal hair, coarse thigh/abdominal hair, axillary hair, eyebrows, eyelashes, beard) using powerful clawed middle and hind legs. Remains stationary attached to hair roots while inserting mouthparts into dermal capillaries to feed; does not burrow beneath the epidermal surface.
*   **Environmental Survival Off-Host:** Sluggish and poorly adapted for locomotion off human hair; dies rapidly within 24 to 48 hours when separated from the human body due to starvation and desiccation.

#### Pathogenesis & Vector Dynamics
*   **Direct Dermatologic Damage:** Puncturing of dermal capillaries and injection of lytic, anticoagulating salivary secretions during blood feeding cause localized tissue irritation, microscopic feeding puncta, petechial macules, and secondary breakdown of local cutaneous barriers.
*   **Hypersensitivity & Immune Response:** Salivary proteins injected during repeated blood meals trigger host allergic hypersensitivity reactions, producing intense local itching (pruritus) and pathognomonic bluish-gray or slate-blue, non-blanching macules called **maculae caeruleae** (blue spots / tache noire) on the thighs, lower abdomen, or trunk, resulting from the enzymatic action of louse saliva on host hemoglobin.
*   **Pathogens Transmitted as a Vector:** N/A (Unlike the body louse *[[Pediculus humanus corporis|Pediculus humanus humanus]]*, the crab louse *Pthirus pubis* / *Phthirus pubis* does **not** serve as a vector for *[[Rickettsia prowazekii]]*, *Borrelia recurrentis*, or *Bartonella quintana*).
*   **Host Defense & Predisposing Factors:** Close personal body-to-body contact; sexual activity among adults; parent-child close physical interactions; sharing infested towels, clothing, or bedding.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Presentations
*   **Primary Infestation Presentation:** Intense itching (pruritus) concentrated in the pubic, genital, and groin regions, often accompanied by visible crawling insects or tiny dark specks of digested blood (louse feces / scybala) on underwear.
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions & Distribution Pattern:** Distribution targets areas of coarse body hair (pubic, perianal, thighs, abdomen, chest, axillae, beard, mustache, and eyelashes in young children (pediculosis ciliaris / phthiriasis palpebrarum)). Pathognomonic signs include **maculae caeruleae** (slate-gray or sky-blue macules 0.5 to 1 cm in diameter) on the lower abdomen or thighs, and translucent nits glued to coarse hair bases.
*   **Secondary Complications:** Excoriations from scratching lead to secondary bacterial skin infections (pyoderma, impetigo, folliculitis, cellulitis); blepharitis, conjunctival inflammation, or secondary bacterial eyelid infection in eyelash infestation (phthiriasis palpebrarum).
*   **Laboratory & Dermoscopic Findings:** Dermoscopy or direct microscopic examination confirms 6-legged crab-shaped adult lice with large claws clutched onto hair shafts, or oval operculated nits attached near the hair root.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Geographic Distribution:** Worldwide / Cosmopolitan distribution across all human populations.
*   **Transmission Route:** Transmitted predominantly via direct, close personal body-to-body contact, particularly sexual contact between adults, or parent-child physical interactions. Fomite transmission via shared clothing, towels, bedding, or toilet seats occurs less frequently.
*   **Institutional & Crowding Risks:** Highest prevalence among sexually active adolescents and young adults; high transmission in close-quarter living conditions or shared sleeping facilities.
*   **Public Health & Outbreak Management:** When pubic lice are detected, all sexual partners from the preceding month and household contacts must be evaluated and treated simultaneously; comprehensive screening for co-existing sexually transmitted infections (STIs, including *[[Neisseria gonorrhoeae]]*, *[[Chlamydia trachomatis]]*, *[[Treponema pallidum pallidum|Treponema pallidum]]*, and [[HIV]]) is mandatory due to frequent sexual co-transmission.

#### Advanced Diagnostics
*   **Direct Examination & Skin Scrapings:** Inspection of coarse hair under direct light or comb examination. Removed lice or hair shafts examined under low-power (x100) light microscopy demonstrate characteristic crab-like morphology (broad fused body, thumb-like claws) or oval nits with opercula.
*   **Dermoscopy Features:** Dermoscopy demonstrates reddish-brown, 6-legged crab-like insects clutching coarse hair shafts with their large middle and posterior claws, or translucent oval nits attached near the skin-hair junction.
*   **Biopsy Findings:** Biopsy is rarely required; if performed, reveals superficial upper dermal edema, perivascular lymphocytic and eosinophilic infiltrates around hair follicles, and localized dermal red blood cell extravasation at feeding sites.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Eradication
*   **Topical Scabicides & Pediculicides:**
    *   *Pyrethrins with piperonyl butoxide* or *Permethrin 1% lotion/cream:* First-line topical pediculicidal agents. Applied thoroughly to affected coarse hair areas (pubic, perianal, thighs, abdomen, axillae), left on for 10 minutes, and thoroughly washed off. Must be **repeated 7 to 10 days later** (at least 7 days apart) to kill newly hatched nymphs.
    *   *Alternative Topical Agents:* 1% gamma benzene hexachloride (lindane) shampoo or 0.5% malathion lotion (used if primary treatments fail; lindane is contraindicated in infants, young children, pregnant/lactating women, and individuals with seizure disorders); 1% malathion powder.
    *   *Eyelash Infestation (Phthiriasis Palpebrarum):* Occlusive petrolatum (white petroleum) ointment applied to eyelid margins 2 to 4 times daily for 8 to 10 days to suffocate lice and nits, followed by manual removal using fine forceps. (Pediculicidal chemical lotions/shampoos MUST NOT be applied to the eyes).
*   **Oral Systemic Treatments:** Oral [[Ivermectin]] (200 µg/kg PO, repeated in 7 to 14 days) is indicated for refractory, severe, or widespread infestations.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Re-application Schedules:** A single topical application does not reliably kill all unhatched eggs; repeating treatment **7 to 10 days later** is mandatory to eliminate newly emerged nymphs before they mature and reproduce. All sexual partners from the preceding 30 days **must be treated simultaneously on the same day** to prevent cyclic reinfestation ("ping-pong" infection).
*   **Environmental & Laundry Decontamination Protocols:** Clothing, bedding, and towels used during the 2 to 3 days prior to treatment must be machine washed in hot water (>60°C / 140°F) and dried on the hot dryer cycle for at least 20 minutes; non-washable items should be sealed in airtight plastic bags for at least 2 weeks.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Crab louse, *Pthirus pubis*, *Phthirus pubis*, pubic louse, pediculosis pubis, crab-like claws, maculae caeruleae (slate-gray / blue macules), pediculosis ciliaris / phthiriasis palpebrarum (eyelash infestation), petrolatum ointment for eyelids, mandatory STI co-screening, [[permethrin]] 1% cream / pyrethrins.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   *Crab-like Morphological Specialization:* Unlike head and body lice (*Pediculus humanus*), which are elongated, *Pthirus pubis* possesses a short, broad body with fused thoracic and abdominal segments resembling a tiny ocean crab, with heavy claws specialized exclusively for clutching widely spaced coarse hair rather than fine head hair.
    *   *Complete Lack of Bacterial Vector Capacity:* While the [[Pediculus humanus corporis|body louse]] is a major vector of epidemic typhus (*[[Rickettsia prowazekii]]*), trench fever (*Bartonella quintana*), and louse-borne relapsing fever (*[[Borrelia recurrentis]]*), the crab louse (*Pthirus pubis*) is completely non-vectorial and transmits NO systemic bacterial pathogens.
    *   *Eyelash Infestation Treatment Protocol:* Standard pediculicidal chemical shampoos cannot be applied near the eyes due to severe ocular chemical irritation; instead, eyelash infestation (phthiriasis palpebrarum) is managed with suffocating petrolatum ointment applied to eyelid margins for 8 to 10 days alongside manual extraction using fine forceps.
    *   *Mandatory STI Screening:* Diagnosis of pubic lice in an adult serves as an epidemiological indicator for other sexually transmitted infections (STIs), requiring a full diagnostic workup for co-transmitted pathogens such as *[[Neisseria gonorrhoeae]]*, *[[Chlamydia trachomatis]]*, *[[Treponema pallidum pallidum|Treponema pallidum]]*, and [[HIV]].
