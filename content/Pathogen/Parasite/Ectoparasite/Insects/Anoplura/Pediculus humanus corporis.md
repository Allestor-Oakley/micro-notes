### Pediculus humanus corporis

#### Arthropod Anatomy & Physical Morphology
*   **Adult Physical Features & Size:** Small, elongated, dorsally flattened, wingless ectoparasitic insect measuring approximately 2.0 to 4.0 mm in length (slightly larger on average than the [[Pediculus humanus capitis|head louse]] *Pediculus humanus capitis*). Possesses 3 pairs of legs attached to the fused thorax, each terminating in a strong tarsal claw adapted for clutching textile fibers and clothing threads. Body color ranges from greyish-white to reddish-brown (after blood feeding).
*   **Mouthparts & Feeding Apparatus:** Piercing-sucking mouthparts (solenophagous feeding apparatus) adapted for penetrating dermal blood capillaries; feeds exclusively on human blood four to five times per day.
*   **Egg / Nit Morphology & Attachment:** Translucent, oval or flask-shaped eggs (nits) measuring approximately 0.8 mm in length, fitted with an operculum (cap) containing aeropyles. Unlike head or pubic lice, body louse females glue their nits primarily to the internal seams, linings, and folds of clothing fibers worn next to the skin using a durable, water-insoluble chitinous cement substance.
*   **Immature Stages:** Nits hatch after 7 to 10 days into miniature, active, 6-legged blood-feeding nymphs (first instar) that undergo three nymphal molts (instars) over 1 to 2 weeks before maturing into adult body lice.

#### Lifecycle, Habitat & Host Attachment
*   **Primary Host Range:** Humans (obligate permanent ectoparasite; humans are the only natural host).
*   **Developmental Stages:** Three-stage lifecycle occurring on human clothing and skin: Egg (nit) -> Nymph (3 instars) -> Adult male / female. Total lifecycle from egg to adult takes approximately 2 to 3 weeks.
*   **Attachment Mechanics & Burrowing Behavior:** Resides and clings to textile fibers inside the seams, folds, and inner surfaces of clothing worn close to the body. Moves from clothing to host skin surface only to feed four to five times daily; does NOT burrow beneath the epidermal surface or live permanently on skin or hair.
*   **Environmental Survival Off-Host:** Obligate ectoparasite dependent on frequent human blood meals and host body heat. Can survive off the human body in clothing without blood feeding for up to several days to 1 week under cool conditions, but rapidly dehydrates and dies when exposed to heat >60°C for 15 to 30 minutes.

#### Pathogenesis & Vector Dynamics
*   **Direct Dermatologic Damage:** Puncturing of dermal capillaries and injection of lytic, anticoagulating salivary secretions during blood feeding cause localized mechanical trauma, small erythematous macules, papules, petechiae, and secondary excoriations from scratching.
*   **Hypersensitivity & Immune Response:** Injected salivary proteins trigger host allergic hypersensitivity reactions, producing intense, severe body itching (pruritus corporis). Chronic, long-term infestation combined with continuous scratching leads to diffuse post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, lichenification, and skin thickening—a condition classically termed **vagabond's disease** (vagabond's skin).
*   **Pathogens Transmitted as a Vector:** Body lice (*Pediculus humanus humanus* / *Pediculus humanus corporis*) are premier, highly dangerous biological vectors for three major systemic human bacterial diseases:
    *   *[[Rickettsia prowazekii]]:* Causative agent of epidemic (louse-borne) typhus (transmitted when louse feces or crushed lice are rubbed into bite wounds or mucous membranes).
    *   *[[Borrelia recurrentis]]:* Causative agent of louse-borne relapsing fever (transmitted when crushed infected lice release spirochetes onto abraded skin).
    *   *Bartonella quintana:* Causative agent of trench fever / five-day fever and bacillary angiomatosis.
    *   *Emerging Pathogens:* Body lice can also harbor and potentially transmit *[[Acinetobacter baumannii]]* and *[[Serratia marcescens]]*.
*   **Host Defense & Predisposing Factors:** Poverty, homelessness, unhoused living conditions, disaster zones, war/refugee camps, overcrowding, lack of access to clean water or laundry facilities, and wearing dirty clothing continuously for weeks without changing or washing.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Presentations
*   **Primary Infestation Presentation:** Intense, severe body itching (pruritus corporis), particularly on covered skin areas touching clothing seams. Presence of crawling lice or nits embedded in the inner seams and linings of shirts, trousers, or underwear.
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions & Distribution Pattern:** Small, red, intensely pruritic papules, wheals, linear excoriations, and petechiae concentrated on the trunk, neck, shoulders, waistline, belt line, flanks, buttocks, and thighs (areas where clothing fits tightly or seams press against skin). Chronic infestation exhibits **vagabond's disease** (extensive dark hyperpigmentation, scaling, and lichenified skin thickening).
*   **Secondary Complications:** Secondary bacterial skin infections (impetigo, pyoderma, folliculitis, cellulitis, bacteremia) resulting from vigorous scratching; severe, potentially fatal systemic outbreaks of epidemic typhus, relapsing fever, or trench fever in vulnerable populations.
*   **Laboratory & Dermoscopic Findings:** Direct visual inspection of inner clothing seams, hems, and pockets reveals live, 6-legged, crawling adult lice, nymphs, or operculated nits glued to textile threads.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Geographic Distribution:** Worldwide / Cosmopolitan distribution, particularly prevalent in cold climates, war-torn regions, refugee settlements, homeless shelters, and areas affected by humanitarian crises or extreme poverty.
*   **Transmission Route:** Transmitted primarily via direct, close person-to-person body contact, or indirectly via sharing infested clothing, coats, towels, or bedding.
*   **Institutional & Crowding Risks:** High risk of explosive transmission in overcrowded, unsanitary living conditions, such as homeless shelters, refugee camps, military barracks, prisons, and disaster relief centers.
*   **Public Health & Outbreak Management:** Mass laundering or heat treatment of clothing and bedding; provision of clean clothing changes; mass dusting with insecticidal powders (e.g., 1% malathion powder or [[permethrin]] dust) in refugee camps, war zones, or epidemic typhus outbreak settings; public health sanitation and hygiene education.

#### Advanced Diagnostics
*   **Direct Examination & Skin Scrapings:** Careful inspection of the inner seams, cuffs, collar folds, and waistbands of clothing turned inside out. Examination under low-power (x100) light microscopy confirms 6-legged adult louse morphology or operculated nits attached to garment fibers. Skin scrapings are non-diagnostic because lice reside on clothing.
*   **Dermoscopy Features:** Dermoscopy of clothing seam fibers demonstrates live 6-legged adult body lice, active nymphs, or translucent oval nits anchored to textile threads.
*   **Biopsy Findings:** Biopsy of skin bite lesions is rarely indicated; if performed, demonstrates superficial perivascular lymphocytic, neutrophilic, and eosinophilic dermal infiltrates, upper dermal edema, and focal extravasation of red blood cells around capillary puncture tracks.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Eradication
*   **Topical Scabicides & Pediculicides:** Chemical pediculicidal application directly to human skin is **rarely necessary** because body lice live on clothing rather than skin or body hair. However, if lice are observed actively crawling on host body hair, topical application of *1% [[Permethrin]] cream* or *Pyrethrins with piperonyl butoxide* to the body (left on for 10 minutes and washed off) can be utilized.
*   **Oral Systemic Treatments:** Oral [[Ivermectin]] (200 µg/kg PO as a single dose, repeated in 7 to 10 days) is indicated for severe, widespread, or refractory body lice infestations in crowded populations or unhoused individuals where regular clothing changes and laundering are unfeasible.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Re-application Schedules:** Primary therapeutic focus is **environmental and garment decontamination** rather than personal chemical treatment. Simply changing clothing, bathing with soap and warm water, and decontaminating garments completely cures the infestation.
*   **Environmental & Laundry Decontamination Protocols:**
    *   *Heat Decontamination:* All clothing, underwear, towels, and bed linen used by the infested person must be machine washed in hot water (>60°C / 140°F) and dried on the hot dryer cycle for at least 20 to 30 minutes. (Exposing lice and nits to heat >60°C for 15 to 30 minutes reliably kills all developmental stages).
    *   *Storage Protocol:* Non-washable items or clothing should be sealed in airtight plastic bags for at least 2 weeks (14 days) to ensure all emerging nymphs starve and die.
    *   *Chemical Dusting:* In mass outbreak or refugee settings, dusting clothing and bedding with 1% malathion powder or [[permethrin]] dust provides effective vector control.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Body louse, *Pediculus humanus corporis*, *Pediculus humanus humanus*, clothing louse, pediculosis corporis, inner clothing seams, vagabond's disease (hyperpigmented skin), epidemic typhus (*[[Rickettsia prowazekii]]*), louse-borne relapsing fever (*[[Borrelia recurrentis]]*), trench fever (*Bartonella quintana*), heat clothing >60°C for 15-30 min, homeless / refugee outbreaks.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   *Clothing Habitat Exception:* Unlike head lice (*P. h. capitis*) and pubic lice (*[[Pthirus pubis|Phthirus pubis]]*) which reside and attach eggs directly to human hair shafts, body lice live, hide, and lay their eggs (nits) on the fibers and inner seams of **clothing**, visiting host skin only to feed 4 to 5 times daily.
    *   *Exclusive Epidemic Vector Status:* Body lice are the ONLY human lice ecotype capable of serving as major biological vectors for severe, fatal human bacterial epidemics: epidemic typhus (*[[Rickettsia prowazekii]]*), louse-borne relapsing fever (*[[Borrelia recurrentis]]*), and trench fever (*Bartonella quintana*).
    *   *Non-Pharmacological Primary Cure:* The primary curative treatment for body lice infestation does not require applying pediculicidal chemicals to human skin; rather, it is accomplished by personal hygiene (bathing) and laundering, heating (>60°C), or changing infected clothing and bedding.
