### Pediculus humanus capitis

#### Arthropod Anatomy & Physical Morphology
*   **Adult Physical Features & Size:** Small, elongated, dorsally flattened, wingless ectoparasitic insect measuring approximately 2.0 to 3.0 mm in length. Possesses 3 pairs of legs attached to the fused thorax, each terminating in a specialized tarsal claw adapted for grasping human hair shafts. Color ranges from greyish-white to reddish-brown (after blood feeding).
*   **Mouthparts & Feeding Apparatus:** Piercing-sucking mouthparts (solenophagous feeding apparatus) adapted for penetrating dermal capillaries and feeding exclusively on human blood four to five times per day.
*   **Egg / Nit Morphology & Attachment:** Translucent, oval or flask-shaped eggs (nits) measuring approximately 0.8 mm in length, fitted with an operculum (cap) containing aeropyles. Nits are firmly glued to the base of scalp hair shafts near the skin-hair junction via a water-insoluble chitinous cement substance secreted by the female louse. Unhatched nits are opaque/yellowish-white, while empty nit shells appear transparent or white after hatching.
*   **Immature Stages:** Nits hatch after 7 to 10 days into miniature, active, 6-legged blood-feeding nymphs (first instar) that undergo three nymphal molts (instars) over 8 to 12 days before developing into sexually mature adult head lice.

#### Lifecycle, Habitat & Host Attachment
*   **Primary Host Range:** Humans (obligate permanent ectoparasite; humans are the only natural host).
*   **Developmental Stages:** Three-stage lifecycle occurring entirely on human hair and scalp: Egg (nit) -> Nymph (3 instars) -> Adult male / female. Total lifecycle from egg to adult takes approximately 2 to 3 weeks.
*   **Attachment Mechanics & Burrowing Behavior:** Clings to scalp hair shafts using claws on all 6 legs; prefers hair behind the ears and at the nape of the neck. Remains on hair and scalp skin surface to feed on host blood; does NOT burrow under host skin.
*   **Environmental Survival Off-Host:** Obligate ectoparasite that requires frequent blood meals (4–5 times daily) and host body heat. Dies rapidly within 24 to 48 hours when detached from human scalp hair due to starvation and desiccation.

#### Pathogenesis & Vector Dynamics
*   **Direct Dermatologic Damage:** Puncturing of scalp skin by piercing-sucking mouthparts during blood feeding causes localized mechanical tissue trauma, focal petechiae, and small erythematous papules.
*   **Hypersensitivity & Immune Response:** Salivary proteins injected during feeding trigger host allergic hypersensitivity reactions, producing severe, intense itching (pruritus) of the scalp, posterior neck, and postauricular skin. Sensitization and pruritus may take weeks to develop during initial infestation.
*   **Pathogens Transmitted as a Vector:** Unlike body lice (*[[Pediculus humanus corporis|Pediculus humanus humanus]]*), head lice are generally considered poor biological vectors of epidemic typhus (*[[Rickettsia prowazekii]]*) or louse-borne relapsing fever (*[[Borrelia recurrentis]]*). However, *Bartonella quintana* (agent of trench fever) has been detected in head lice from populations in Nepal, the United States, and Ethiopia.
*   **Host Defense & Predisposing Factors:** High prevalence among school-aged children (ages 3–11 years), institutional daycare/school settings, close head-to-head contact during play, sharing combs, brushes, hats, or hair accessories, and family co-sleeping arrangements.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Presentations
*   **Primary Infestation Presentation:** Intense scalp itching (pruritus capitis), especially behind the ears and at the nape of the neck, leading to frequent scratching. Presence of visible crawling lice or nits firmly attached to scalp hair.
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions & Distribution Pattern:** Pruritic, red papules, excoriations, and nits firmly glued to hair shafts within 1 cm of the scalp, concentrated around the occipital region and postauricular scalp.
*   **Secondary Complications:** Excoriations from vigorous scratching can lead to secondary bacterial skin infections (pyoderma, impetigo, folliculitis, cellulitis caused by *[[Staphylococcus aureus]]* or *[[Streptococcus pyogenes]]*), regional posterior cervical lymphadenopathy, matted hair, and crusting.
*   **Laboratory & Dermoscopic Findings:** Direct visual identification of live, crawling 6-legged adult lice or nymphs, or operculated nits attached near the scalp. Dermoscopy reveals live embryos within intact nits or empty, translucent shells with flat opercular ends glued to hair shafts.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Geographic Distribution:** Worldwide / Cosmopolitan distribution across all socioeconomic groups and geographic regions.
*   **Transmission Route:** Transmitted primarily via direct, head-to-head contact with an infested individual. Indirect transmission via fomites (shared hats, hair ribbons, combs, brushes, pillows, bedding, or towels) occurs less frequently.
*   **Institutional & Crowding Risks:** High transmission in elementary schools, daycare centers, summer camps, crowded households, and institutional settings where children interact closely.
*   **Public Health & Outbreak Management:** Screening close family members and school contacts; treating only individuals with confirmed active infestation (live lice or viable nits near scalp); avoiding routine exclusionary "no-nit" policies in schools; public health education on avoiding head-to-head contact and sharing hair items.

#### Advanced Diagnostics
*   **Direct Examination & Skin Scrapings:** Wet combing of damp hair using a fine-toothed louse comb (with teeth spaced 0.2–0.3 mm apart) is 4 to 5 times more effective for detecting live head lice than direct visual inspection. Microscopic examination of hair shafts confirms attached nits or adult louse morphology.
*   **Dermoscopy Features:** Handheld dermoscopy demonstrates dark, oval, convex nits containing viable embryos or empty translucent nit shells attached to hair shafts via a chitinous collar, as well as 6-legged adult lice clinging to hair fibers.
*   **Biopsy Findings:** Scalp biopsy is rarely indicated; if performed, demonstrates intraepidermal puncture tracks, focal upper dermal edema, extravasated red blood cells, and superficial perivascular lymphocytic and eosinophilic infiltrates around hair follicles.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Eradication
*   **Topical Scabicides & Pediculicides:**
    *   *[[Permethrin]] 1% Lotion / Cream:* First-line topical OTC pediculicide. Applied to washed, towel-dried hair, left on for 10 minutes, and rinsed off. Must be **repeated 7 to 10 days later** (at least 7 days apart) to kill newly hatched nymphs.
    *   *Pyrethrins with Piperonyl Butoxide:* First-line topical OTC agent. Applied to dry hair, left on for 10 minutes, and thoroughly washed out. Repeated 7 to 10 days later.
    *   *Alternative Topical Agents:* 0.5% Malathion lotion (applied to dry hair for 8–12 hours; highly ovicidal); 0.9% Spinosad suspension (applied for 10 minutes; ovicidal); 0.5% [[Ivermectin]] lotion (topical single application); 1% Gamma benzene hexachloride (lindane) shampoo (effective, but third-line due to neurotoxicity risks; contraindicated in infants, young children, pregnant/lactating women, and patients with seizure disorders).
*   **Oral Systemic Treatments:** Oral [[Ivermectin]] (200 µg/kg PO, repeated in 7 to 10 days) is indicated for refractory, severe, or resistant head lice infestations.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Re-application Schedules:** Because most non-ovicidal topical pediculicides spare unhatched eggs, repeating treatment **7 to 10 days later** (or on day 9) is crucial to kill newly emerged nymphs before they mature and lay eggs. Mechanical removal of nits using a fine-toothed louse comb after pediculicidal treatment enhances clearance. Examine all family contacts, but treat ONLY those with active infestation.
*   **Environmental & Laundry Decontamination Protocols:** Machine wash all clothing, hats, pillowcases, and bed linen used by the infested person during the 2 days prior to treatment in hot water (>60°C / 140°F) and dry on the hot dryer cycle for at least 20 to 30 minutes (or dry clean). Soaking combs and brushes in hot water (>60°C) for 5 to 10 minutes. Sealing non-washable items in airtight plastic bags for 2 weeks is sufficient to starve any emerging nymphs.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Head louse, *Pediculus humanus capitis*, pediculosis capitis, nits glued to hair shafts, fine-toothed louse comb, 4-5x more sensitive than visual inspection, 1% [[permethrin]], pyrethrins + piperonyl butoxide, 0.9% spinosad, 0.5% malathion, repeat treatment in 7-10 days, *Bartonella quintana* carrier in certain global regions.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   *Diagnostic Superiority of Louse Comb:* Visual examination of the scalp frequently misses light head louse infestations; fine-toothed comb detection on wet hair is 4 to 5 times more sensitive than manual visual inspection.
    *   *Selective Contact Treatment:* Unlike scabies or pubic lice where all household/sexual contacts are treated empirically on the same day, family members of a child with head lice should be examined, but ONLY those with confirmed active infestation (live lice or viable nits) should be treated.
    *   *Lack of Systemic Epidemic Vector Capacity:* Although morphologically nearly identical to the [[Pediculus humanus corporis|body louse]] (*Pediculus humanus humanus*), the head louse does NOT transmit epidemic typhus (*[[Rickettsia prowazekii]]*) or louse-borne relapsing fever (*[[Borrelia recurrentis]]*) under normal conditions, though it can harbor *Bartonella quintana*.
    *   *Short Off-Host Survival:* Head lice cannot survive more than 24 to 48 hours away from the human scalp, rendering extensive environmental fumigation or spraying of homes unnecessary.
