### Pthirus pubis

#### Arthropod Anatomy & Physical Morphology
*   **Adult Physical Features & Size:** Adult pubic or crab lice (*Pthirus pubis*, also spelled *Phthirus pubis*) are small, wingless, obligate ectoparasitic insects, dorsoventrally flattened, measuring 1.0 mm to 2.0 mm in length (specifically reported as 1.2 mm by 0.8 mm, or 1.3 mm to 2.0 mm; males are slightly smaller than females). Body shape is broadly squat, compact, and nearly as broad as it is long, giving it a characteristic crab-like appearance. Color ranges from pale whitish-grey or greyish-brown to dark grey, turning reddish-brown following a blood meal. The body comprises three regions: head, fused thorax, and broad abdomen. The head bears a pair of small black eyes and a pair of short, 5-segmented antennae. The broad, squat abdomen features prominent lateral abdominal protuberances (tubercles/processes) bearing stiff setae along the body margins. All three pairs of legs bear terminal claws; however, while the first pair of legs is slender with thin claws (used primarily to hold the skin during feeding), the second and third pairs (middle and hind legs) are significantly thicker, stout, and equipped with massive, thumb-like claws adapted specifically for grasping coarse hair fibers.
*   **Mouthparts & Feeding Apparatus:** Mouthparts consist of a short, retractable, snout-like sucking organ called the haustellum, armed internally with minute, recurved chitinous teeth that anchor to host skin during feeding. Internally, three delicate, needle-like piercing stylets are thrust through the haustellum into dermal capillaries. Saliva containing anticoagulants and local vasodilators is injected into the puncture wound to prevent blood clotting, after which host blood is drawn up into the digestive tract by the muscular pharyngeal pump. Pubic lice feed on human blood exclusively, taking 4 to 5 blood meals daily.
*   **Egg / Nit Morphology & Attachment:** Eggs (nits) are oval, translucent, shiny, yellowish-white or milky-white structures measuring approximately 0.8 mm in length. The distal anterior cap bears a distinct operculum perforated by small respiratory pores ("pepper pot" appearance). Eggs are cemented individually or in clusters (unlike head lice, several eggs may be laid on a single hair) to the base of coarse hair shafts in the genital and perianal regions using a quick-hardening, water-insoluble proteinaceous glue secreted by the female's accessory glands. Females lay approximately 3 eggs per day, totaling 30 to 50 eggs (up to 150 to 200 eggs) over an adult female lifespan of 17 to 30 days.
*   **Immature Stages:** Incomplete metamorphosis (hemimetabolous lifecycle) with 3 nymphal instars:
    *   *Nymph I:* Hatches from the egg after 6 to 8 days (7 to 8 days) by swallowing air to build internal pressure that pops off the operculum. Nymph I measures ~0.5 mm to 0.8 mm, possesses 6 legs with stout claws, resembles a miniature adult, and begins blood-feeding immediately.
    *   *Nymph II:* Arises after the first molt (2 to 4 days later), larger in size.
    *   *Nymph III:* Arises after the second molt (3 to 4 days later), further developing sexually before molting into a mature adult male or female. Total nymphal development spans 10 to 17 days (or 13 to 17 days).

#### Lifecycle, Habitat & Host Attachment
*   **Primary Host Range:** Humans are the sole natural host (*Pthirus pubis* is host-specific to humans; two distinct species of *Pthirus* exist on separate hosts: *Pthirus pubis* on humans and *Pthirus gorillae* on gorillas).
*   **Developmental Stages:** Incomplete metamorphosis: Egg -> Nymph I -> Nymph II -> Nymph III -> Adult (Male / Female). The total lifecycle from egg to adult requires 16 to 27 days (22 to 27 days). Adult lifespan averages 17 days for females and 22 days for males (up to 30 days).
*   **Attachment Mechanics & Burrowing Behavior:** Pubic lice do not burrow into skin or live in subcutaneous tissues. They reside permanently on coarse hair shafts close to the skin surface. The stout middle and hind legs with massive claws grip coarse hairs (pubic, perianal, axillary, chest, facial hair, eyelashes) in a vice-like grip, while the slender front legs hold the skin surface during blood feeding. Lice move sluggishly along hair shafts (considerably less active than *Pediculus* species) and cannot jump or fly.
*   **Environmental Survival Off-Host:** Pubic lice are obligate parasites requiring human body warmth and frequent blood meals (4–5 times daily). Off the human host at ambient room temperatures (e.g., 15°C to 20°C), adult lice and nymphs dehydrate and starve rapidly, dying in less than 24 to 48 hours. They do not infest rooms, carpets, furniture, beds, or pets.

#### Pathogenesis & Vector Dynamics
*   **Direct Dermatologic Damage:** Puncture wounds caused by piercing stylets, enzymatic irritation from injected salivary fluids, and deposition of louse feces around feeding sites induce localized dermal inflammation, vascular dilation, and feeding-site hemorrhagic spots.
*   **Hypersensitivity & Immune Response:** Clinical manifestations stem from host immediate (IgE-mediated) and delayed-type (cell-mediated) hypersensitivity reactions to louse salivary proteins and fecal secretions. Sensitization and severe pruritus typically become noticeable 2 to 3 weeks (or 14+ days) following initial infestation. A pathognomonic vascular sign—maculae caeruleae (slate-grey or pale blue non-blanching macules, 0.5 cm to 1 cm in diameter)—develops at bite sites on the thighs, lower abdomen, or buttocks, caused by the action of louse salivary enzymes/substances on host blood or altered bilirubin/hemoglobin pigments.
*   **Pathogens Transmitted as a Vector:** Under natural conditions, *Pthirus pubis* is NOT a biological vector for any human bacterial, viral, or protozoal pathogens (N/A). (Although pubic lice can harbor and transmit *[[Rickettsia prowazekii]]* under artificial laboratory conditions, and historical reports suggested involvement in Chinese typhus outbreaks, there is no evidence of natural pathogen transmission in humans).
*   **Host Defense & Predisposing Factors:** Risk factors include sexually active adults, multiple sexual partners, close physical contact, and co-infestation with other sexually transmitted infections (STIs/STDs) such as gonorrhea, trichomoniasis, chlamydia, syphilis, and [[HIV]]. In young children, infestation occurs via co-sleeping with infested parents or, critically, may be an indicator of child sexual abuse.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Presentations
*   **Primary Infestation Presentation:**
    *   *Pediculosis Pubis / Phthiriasis:* Intensely pruritic eruption in the pubic, perianal, or groin regions. Patients report crawling sensations and severe itching that is characteristically worse at night.
    *   *Phthiriasis Palpebrarum:* Ectoparasitic infestation of the eyelashes and eyelid margins (most common in young children or infants), presenting with eyelid pruritus, burning, lid margin edema, scaling, crusting, conjunctival irritation, and corneal epithelial keratitis. Often misdiagnosed as allergic, viral, or bacterial conjunctivitis, or seborrheic blepharitis.
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions & Distribution Pattern:** Primary lesions include red erythematous papules, excoriations, and pathognomonic maculae caeruleae (characteristic slate-grey or pale blue non-blanching macules on the lower abdomen, thighs, buttocks, or trunk). Louse feces appear as reddish-brown or dark pepper-like flecks on underwear or skin. Preferred distribution: pubic hair, perianal hair, thighs, lower abdomen, axillae, chest hair, beard, moustache, and eyelashes (cilia). Fine scalp hair is rarely or never involved in adults.
*   **Secondary Complications:** Intense scratching causes excoriation, secondary bacterial superinfections (impetigo contagiosa, pyoderma, folliculitis, [[cellulitis]]), regional lymphadenopathy, blepharoconjunctivitis, marginal keratitis, loss of eyelashes (madarosis), and psychological distress. In pediatric phthiriasis palpebrarum, failure to recognize child sexual abuse is a critical medical-legal risk.
*   **Laboratory & Dermoscopic Findings:** Direct visual or microscopic demonstration of crab-like adult lice (1.0–2.0 mm, broad body, massive claws on middle/hind legs), active nymphs, or translucent oval eggs (nits) glued to coarse hair shafts. Dermoscopy (entomodermoscopy) or stereomicroscopy reveals crab-shaped lice anchored to hair shafts, translucent eggs with opercula containing dark embryos, or empty white tubular nits. Host DNA extracted via [[PCR]] from louse blood meals can be used in forensic medicine (rape/sexual assault investigations). Evaluation for co-existing STIs (gonorrhea, chlamydia, syphilis, [[HIV]], trichomoniasis) is mandatory in all adult cases.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Geographic Distribution:** Worldwide / cosmopolitan, affecting all races, climates, and socioeconomic groups.
*   **Transmission Route:** Primary transmission occurs via close, direct person-to-person physical contact, predominantly through sexual intercourse among adults ("papillons d'amour"). In children, transmission occurs via close non-sexual physical contact (e.g., sleeping in the same bed as infested parents) or via child sexual abuse. Fomite transmission via shared bedding, towels, or clothing is rare due to short off-host survival (<48 h), and transmission from toilet seats is extremely rare.
*   **Institutional & Crowding Risks:** Elevated in sexually active populations, STI clinics, overcrowded households, homeless shelters, and dormitories.
*   **Public Health & Outbreak Management:** Contact tracing and simultaneous examination/treatment of all sexual partners and close household contacts are essential. Public health policies emphasize evaluating all affected adults for co-existing STIs and reporting suspected child sexual abuse in pediatric eyelash infestations. Environmental fogging or room spraying is unnecessary and contraindicated because pubic lice die within 48 hours off the host.

#### Advanced Diagnostics
*   **Direct Examination & Skin Scrapings:** Visual inspection of coarse body hair using a magnifying glass or hand lens (x10) or stereomicroscopy. Lice and nits can be removed using fine forceps or by cutting the infested hair shaft with scissors (avoiding scissors around eyes). For eyelash infestation (phthiriasis palpebrarum), direct slit-lamp examination or magnifying lens allows visualization of adult lice and nits clinging to cilia; lice can be extracted using fine jeweler's forceps or by applying occlusive petrolatum.
*   **Dermoscopy Features:** Entomodermoscopy (x10 to x40 magnification) reveals broad, crab-shaped lice with massive claws gripping hair shafts, translucent unhatched eggs with opercula, or empty white nits cemented to hair fibers. Dermoscopy of skin bite sites demonstrates maculae caeruleae as faint, slate-grey or bluish non-vascular macules.
*   **Biopsy Findings:** Skin biopsy is rarely indicated but demonstrates superficial dermal edema, extravasated erythrocytes, and a sparse perivascular lymphohistiocytic infiltrate with scattered eosinophils.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Eradication
*   **Topical Scabicides & Pediculicides:**
    *   *Topical Pediculicides:* [[Permethrin]] 1% cream rinse or 5% cream, Pyrethrins with piperonyl butoxide, or Dimethicone 4% formulations applied to affected hairy areas (pubic, perianal, groin, thighs, trunk, axillae), left on for 10 minutes (or overnight for [[permethrin]] 5%), and thoroughly washed off.
    *   *Lindane 1% Shampoo / Lotion:* Historical alternative, but no longer recommended due to potential neurotoxicity.
    *   *Eyelash / Ocular Treatment (Phthiriasis Palpebrarum):* Insecticidal creams/lotions are strictly contraindicated near eyes. Eyelash infestations are treated by applying plain occlusive petrolatum ointment (white petrolatum / Vaseline) 2 to 4 times daily for 8 to 10 days to suffocate lice and eggs, combined with manual mechanical extraction using blunt fine forceps under a slit lamp.
*   **Oral Systemic Treatments:**
    *   *Oral [[Ivermectin]]:* Single dose of 200 μg/kg, repeated after 7 to 10 days (Day 1 and Day 8–10) to kill newly hatched nymphs. Highly effective for widespread or refractory body/pubic louse infestations and eyelash involvement.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Re-application Schedules:** A second pediculicidal treatment on Day 8 to 10 is mandatory because most topical agents are not fully ovicidal; the second application kills newly hatched nymphs before they mature sexually (nymphal stage 10–17 days). Historical shaving of pubic hair is no longer required or recommended. All sexual partners within the preceding month must be treated simultaneously regardless of symptoms.
*   **Environmental & Laundry Decontamination Protocols:** Environmental spraying with insecticides or foggers is unnecessary because lice die off-host within 48 hours. Clothing, underwear, towels, and bed sheets used during the preceding 2 to 3 days should be laundered in hot water (>50°C to 60°C) and dried on a hot dryer cycle for 20 to 30 minutes, or dry-cleaned. Non-washable items can be sealed in plastic bags for 2 weeks (48 hours is sufficient to starve lice).

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Papillons d'amour" (French nickname for pubic lice), "Maculae caeruleae" (slate-blue macules from salivary enzymes), "Phthiriasis palpebrarum" (eyelashes infestation in children/adults), "Phthirus pubis" vs "Pthirus pubis" (spelling anomaly: 'Pthirus' is the official generic taxon, 'phthiriasis' is the disease name), "Crab louse" (broad body and massive claws on middle/hind legs), "Forensic host DNA profiling" (extracting host DNA from louse blood meal in rape cases), "Mandatory STI screening", "Pediatric eyelash infestation as a sign of child sexual abuse".
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   Pubic lice (*Pthirus pubis*) have a unique broad, squat, crab-like shape with massive claws on their middle and hind legs, making them easily distinguishable from elongate head and body lice (*Pediculus humanus*).
    *   Unlike head lice, female pubic lice may deposit multiple eggs on a single coarse hair shaft.
    *   Pubic lice live almost exclusively on coarse body hair (pubic, perianal, axillary, chest, facial, eyelashes) and almost NEVER infest the finer scalp hair of adults.
    *   Pubic lice are NOT natural vectors of any human infectious diseases, unlike body lice (*[[Pediculus humanus corporis|Pediculus humanus humanus]]*).
    *   Eyelash infestation in children (*phthiriasis palpebrarum*) MUST be treated with occlusive petrolatum or manual extraction—NEVER with chemical pediculicides—and requires careful evaluation for potential child sexual abuse.
    *   Off-host survival of pubic lice is extremely short (<48 hours), making environmental insecticidal spraying or room decontamination completely unnecessary.
