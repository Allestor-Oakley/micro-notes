### Bedbugs

#### Arthropod Anatomy & Physical Morphology
*   **Adult Physical Features & Size:** Adult bed bugs measure approximately 4 to 5 mm or 5 to 8 mm in length by 3 mm in width; body is dorsoventrally flattened, oval, mahogany brown to reddish-brown in color, and possesses only vestigial wings/wing pads (incapable of flight); resemble unengorged ticks or very small cockroaches; adults possess a jointed 3-segmented proboscis folded ventrally under the head/body when not feeding, multi-segmented antennae, and prominent compound eyes.
*   **Mouthparts & Feeding Apparatus:** Long piercing-sucking proboscis adapted for siphoning blood, folded ventrally under the body when not in use; both male and female nymphs and adults are obligate hematophagous feeders, requiring blood as their sole source of nutrition; a blood meal lasts 3 to 10 minutes for complete engorgement to occur, and bugs bite 3 to 8 times to take a full meal; saliva contains anti-clotting and local irritant/anesthetic components so initial bites are usually unfelt.
*   **Egg / Nit Morphology & Attachment:** Cream-colored eggs are around 1 mm in length; female bed bug lays 3 to 4 eggs per day (or 5 to 8 eggs per week for 18 weeks at 23°C and 90% relative humidity, laying up to 50 to 150 eggs over a 4-week lifespan); eggs are cemented onto rough surfaces in dark hiding places/cracks and hatch within 6 to 12 days (or 9 to 12 days).
*   **Immature Stages:** Undergo incomplete metamorphosis (hemimetabolous lifecycle) with 5 juvenile instar stages (nymphs) before emerging as adults; 1st instar nymph hatches at ~1 mm in length; all 5 nymphal stages require a blood meal for growth and molting to the next instar stage.

#### Lifecycle, Habitat & Host Attachment
*   **Primary Host Range:** Humans, warm-blooded mammals (including bats), birds, and poultry/chickens.
*   **Developmental Stages:** Hemimetabolous lifecycle (egg -> 5 nymphal instars -> adult); at room temperature, a generation develops within 1 to 2 months, and adult bugs live for an additional 4 to 5 months; complete development from egg to adult requires blood meals at each instar stage.
*   **Attachment Mechanics & Burrowing Behavior:** Non-burrowing, non-permanent, temporary ectoparasites; bugs do not remain attached or live on human skin, but crawl onto sleeping hosts at night, pierce the skin with their proboscis, feed for 3 to 10 minutes, excrete superfluous blood liquid from the anus, and return to environmental hiding places.
*   **Environmental Survival Off-Host:** Strictly nocturnal activity reaching peak activity before dawn; hide during the day in dark cracks and crevices of walls, bed frames, headboards, box springs, mattresses, behind baseboards, pictures, mirrors, under carpets, inside furniture, and clothing/luggage; attracted to hosts by exhaled carbon dioxide (CO2), body heat (responding to temperatures 2°C or more above ambient temperature), and body odor; bugs produce aggregation, alarm, and sex pheromones that emit a characteristic distinct odor in heavy infestations; adult bugs and nymphs can survive extended periods of starvation without feeding (up to several months off-host).

#### Pathogenesis & Vector Dynamics
*   **Direct Dermatologic Damage:** Puncture bites from the proboscis cause mechanical cutaneous irritation, resulting in small red marks, localized wheals, or erythematous papules, often featuring a central punctum at the bite site.
*   **Hypersensitivity & Immune Response:** Salivary secretions injected during feeding trigger variable host cutaneous hypersensitivity responses ranging from mild pruritus to intense itching, localized swelling (appearing 15 minutes to 2 hours after biting), large erythematous wheals, purpuric macules, and severe bullous or urticarial eruptions; repeated bites can lead to systemic allergic reactions, lethargy, or pallor.
*   **Pathogens Transmitted as a Vector:** N/A (Bed bugs can mechanically carry microorganisms, but there is no conclusive evidence that they function as natural biological vectors for human pathogens in nature).
*   **Host Defense & Predisposing Factors:** Susceptibility and infestation spread are predisposed by international travel, staying in infested hotels or rooming houses, purchasing second-hand furniture, living in overcrowded housing, lack of physical barriers/screening, and high insecticide resistance in bug populations (e.g., resistance to pyrethroids, organophosphates, and neonicotinoids).

#### Clinical Syndromes & Presentations
*   **Primary Infestation Presentation:** Presents as intensely pruritic, swollen, burning erythematous papules, wheals, or hemorrhagic bullae on exposed skin surfaces; bites are typically noticed upon awakening in the morning.
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions & Distribution Pattern:** Recurrent bites are concentrated on exposed body regions (arms, legs, back, face around the eyes) and characteristically present in linear or clustered arrangements ("breakfast, lunch, and dinner" pattern); presence of small dark brown/black fecal specks ("fly specks"/blood spots) on bedding, mattresses, or walls and a distinct bug odor are characteristic.
*   **Secondary Complications:** Secondary bacterial superinfections (impetigo, ecthyma, cellulitis, lymphangitis) due to excoriation from intense scratching; chronic heavy infestations can lead to iron deficiency anemia; significant psychological sequelae including severe anxiety, sleep deprivation, and mental health impact.
*   **Laboratory & Dermoscopic Findings:** N/A for specific blood/skin tests; clinical diagnosis is confirmed by identifying live bugs or fecal spots; trained dogs can detect bug infestations via odor.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Geographic Distribution:** Worldwide (cosmopolitan distribution in temperate and tropical regions); *Cimex lectularius* is predominant in temperate regions, whereas *Cimex hemipterus* is primarily tropical.
*   **Transmission Route:** Passive mechanical transport of blood-fed bugs or eggs hidden in luggage, clothing, second-hand furniture, or bedding during travel; or active crawling migration of bugs between adjacent rooms/apartments through wall cracks and utility conduits.
*   **Institutional & Crowding Risks:** High risk in family dwellings, multi-unit apartments, rooming houses, hotels, healthcare facilities, college dormitories, homeless shelters, jails, and agricultural operations (chicken houses).
*   **Public Health & Outbreak Management:** Control is exceptionally challenging due to widespread insecticide resistance (to pyrethroids, organophosphates, neonicotinoids); eradication requires integrated pest management combining powerful vacuum cleaning, high-temperature steam treatments, heat treatments, applying residual insecticides, sealing wall cracks/crevices, and thoroughly washing bedding.

#### Advanced Diagnostics
*   **Direct Examination & Skin Scrapings:** Gross identification of captured bugs (4 to 8 mm long, oval, dorsoventrally flattened, mahogany/reddish-brown, wingless, 3-segmented proboscis); inspection of bedding, mattress seams, and bed frames for dark fecal spots, cast nymphal skins, or live bugs; trained bed bug detection dogs can identify hidden infestations.
*   **Dermoscopy Features:** N/A.
*   **Biopsy Findings:** Histology of a bite lesion demonstrates superficial epidermal puncture and perivascular dermal inflammatory infiltrate with lymphocytes, histiocytes, and eosinophils.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Eradication
*   **Topical Scabicides & Pediculicides:** N/A (Bed bugs do not live or colonize on host skin and do not respond to scabicides/pediculicides).
*   **Oral Systemic Treatments:** Direct bite management is supportive and palliative, employing topical antipruritics, oral antihistamines, mild topical corticosteroids to relieve itching and inflammation, and systemic antibiotics if secondary bacterial infections occur.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Re-application Schedules:** N/A.
*   **Environmental & Laundry Decontamination Protocols:** Washing all clothing and bedding in hot water (>60°C for 15-30 minutes or tumble drying on high heat for 20-30 minutes); keeping luggage closed and elevated in hotel rooms; avoiding second-hand furniture; applying residual chemical insecticides combined with physical steam, vacuuming, and heat treatment to infested rooms and bed frames.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Cimex lectularius", "Cimex hemipterus", "mahogany brown", "dorsoventrally flattened", "vestigial wing pads", "linear or clustered bite pattern", "breakfast, lunch, and dinner pattern", "fecal specks on bedding", "attracted to CO2 and heat (>2°C above ambient)", "aggregation pheromones", "resurgence due to pyrethroid resistance".
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Bed bugs are completely wingless/non-aerial insects that cannot fly or jump (unlike fleas or [[mosquitoes]]); despite being obligate blood-sucking parasites that feed on humans, bed bugs do not live or stay on the host's body and are not natural biological vectors of any infectious disease.
