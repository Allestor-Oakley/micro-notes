### Mosquitoes

#### Arthropod Anatomy & Physical Morphology
*   **Adult Physical Features & Size:** Small, delicate, slender flying insects ranging from approximately 3 to 6 mm in length (~1/8 to 1/4 inch; average ~4 mm); body is divided into head, thorax, and abdomen; characteristically equipped with long, delicate legs, one pair of functional membranous wings covered with microscopic scales along the wing veins, one pair of vestigial hind wings modified as halteres for flight balance, long multi-segmented antennae (plumose in males, pilose in females), and a greatly elongated proboscis; adult species exhibit distinct color patterns, leg banding, or thoracic markings (e.g., lyre-shaped markings on *Aedes aegypti*).
*   **Mouthparts & Feeding Apparatus:** Highly specialized piercing-sucking mouthparts forming a elongated proboscis; when taking a blood meal, the female stabs two specialized internal stylets/tubes into the skin microvasculature: one tube injects salivary secretions containing anticoagulants and immunosuppressive enzymes to inhibit blood clotting and facilitate blood flow, while the second tube sucks up host blood; only adult female mosquitoes bite and ingest vertebrate blood (required as a protein source for the development and maturation of each clutch of 100 to 200 eggs); adult male mosquitoes lack skin-piercing capability and feed exclusively on plant juices, flower nectar, and sugars.
*   **Egg / Nit Morphology & Attachment:** Adult females deposit eggs on or near standing water or moist organic substrates (habitats include tree holes, salt marshes, tidewater pools, sewage effluent ponds, irrigated pastures, rainwater collections, ditches, wells, and artificial containers such as flowerpots, rain gutters, discarded tires, and water storage jars); depending on the genus, eggs are deposited either individually on water or moist soil (*Aedes*, *Anopheles*) or glued together in floating rafts containing 200 to 300 eggs (*Culex*).
*   **Immature Stages:** Aquatic larvae (commonly called "wigglers" or "wrigglers") progress through four larval instar stages over 7 to 14 days, reaching up to 12 mm (~0.5 inch) before pupating into active aquatic pupae ("tumblers"); *Culex* larvae possess an elongated posterior breathing siphon tube and hang at an angle or perpendicular to the water surface to breathe; *Anopheles* larvae lack a siphon tube, possess dorsal palmate hairs, and lie parallel to the water surface to obtain air.

#### Lifecycle, Habitat & Host Attachment
*   **Primary Host Range:** Attack humans, domestic animals, livestock, wild mammals, domestic and wild birds, and reptiles; host preference varies by species (e.g., *Anopheles gambiae* and *Aedes aegypti* are strongly anthropophilic; *Culex quinquefasciatus* and *Culex tarsalis* prefer domestic/wild birds and mammals).
*   **Developmental Stages:** Undergo complete metamorphosis (holometabolous lifecycle: egg -> larva (4 instars) -> pupa -> adult); aquatic larval development requires 7 to 14 days depending on ambient water temperature -> active pupa stage -> adult fly emerges at the water surface; adult male lifespan averages ~1 week, whereas adult female lifespan averages ~1 month in summer; females emerging in late summer can hibernate/aestivate in sheltered shelters through winter until spring.
*   **Attachment Mechanics & Burrowing Behavior:** Non-burrowing, temporary ectoparasites; females do not remain attached permanently but land on exposed skin, penetrate the epidermis with their proboscis, inject saliva into the vascular space, feed on blood for a few minutes until engorged, and depart.
*   **Environmental Survival Off-Host:** Require stagnant or slow-moving water collections for larval/pupal development; active flight and feeding occur between temperatures of >16°C and <33°C (optimal 20°C to 30°C with high relative humidity); sporogonic parasite development inside the vector cannot occur below 16°C or above 33°C, or at altitudes above 2000 meters; biting rhythms vary by genus (e.g., *Aedes* bite primarily during daylight/twilight; *Anopheles* and *Culex* bite primarily at night or dusk/midnight).

#### Pathogenesis & Vector Dynamics
*   **Direct Dermatologic Damage:** Proboscis insertion causes minor mechanical capillary damage, producing an immediate small, flat wheal surrounded by an erythematous flare, followed by delayed pruritic induration and localized inflammatory dermatitis.
*   **Hypersensitivity & Immune Response:** Salivary proteins and anticoagulants injected during probing provoke both immediate IgE-mediated wheal-and-flare reactions and delayed cell-mediated hypersensitivity reactions, leading to intense local itching, erythema, and edema; excoriation from scratching can lead to secondary bacterial skin infections.
*   **Pathogens Transmitted as a Vector:** Most important arthropod vector group in global medicine; serves as the obligate biological vector for:
    *   **Protozoa:** *[[Plasmodium falciparum]]*, *[[Plasmodium vivax]]*, *[[Plasmodium ovale]]* (*P. o. curtisi* and *P. o. wallikeri*), *[[Plasmodium malariae]]*, and *[[Plasmodium knowlesi]]* (transmitted by female *Anopheles* species).
    *   **Helminths:** *Wuchereria bancrofti*, *[[Brugia malayi]]*, and *[[Brugia timori]]* causing lymphatic filariasis (transmitted by *Anopheles*, *Culex*, *Aedes*, and *Mansonia* species); *[[Dirofilaria]] immitis* (dog heartworm) and other *[[Dirofilaria]]* spp.
    *   **Viruses:** [[Yellow fever virus]] (*Aedes aegypti*, *Aedes albopictus*); [[Dengue virus]] (*Aedes aegypti*, *Aedes albopictus*); [[West Nile virus]] (*Culex quinquefasciatus*, *Culex salinarius*); [[EEEV|Eastern equine encephalitis virus]] (*Ochlerotatus/Aedes sollicitans*, *Aedes vexans*, *Culiseta melanura*, *Coquillettidia perturbans*); Western equine encephalitis virus (*Culex tarsalis*); [[St. Louis encephalitis virus]] (*Culex nigripalpus*, *Culex quinquefasciatus*, *Culex salinarius*, *Culex tarsalis*); La Crosse encephalitis virus (*Aedes triseriatus*, *Aedes albopictus*); [[Chikungunya virus]].
*   **Host Defense & Predisposing Factors:** Human risk is heightened by proximity to stagnant water breeding sites (artificial containers, open water drums, unsealed wells, rice fields, deforestation puddles), outdoor nocturnal activity, lack of window screens or bed nets, elevated body heat, carbon dioxide exhalation, and dark clothing.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Presentations
*   **Primary Infestation Presentation:** Direct bite presents as an acute, pruritic, erythematous papule or localized wheal with a central puncture mark on exposed skin.
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions & Distribution Pattern:** Multiple scattered itchy papules on uncovered skin areas (arms, legs, face, neck); systemic vector-borne presentations include acute febrile paroxysms with chills, rigors, anemia, and splenomegaly ([[Plasmodium sp.|malaria]]); acute lymphangitis, lymphadenopathy, hydrocele, and chronic elephantiasis of limbs/genitalia (lymphatic filariasis); or acute febrile illness, retro-orbital pain, severe arthralgia/breakbone pain, rash, and encephalitis (arboviral infections).
*   **Secondary Complications:** Secondary bacterial superinfections (impetigo, cellulitis, abscesses) due to excoriation; severe life-threatening vector-borne complications including cerebral [[Plasmodium sp.|malaria]], severe malarial anemia, blackwater fever (hemoglobinuria), dengue hemorrhagic fever, or fatal viral encephalitis.
*   **Laboratory & Dermoscopic Findings:** Direct bite shows non-specific localized epidermal edema; vector-borne disease diagnosis relies on peripheral blood films showing *[[Plasmodium sp.]]* ring forms, schizonts, or banana-shaped gametocytes (*P. falciparum*); daytime/nighttime blood films showing sheathed microfilariae (*W. bancrofti*, *B. malayi*); or specific NAT/PCR and serology for arboviruses.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Geographic Distribution:** Worldwide (cosmopolitan distribution from tropical rainforests and deserts to temperate and arctic tundra regions); *Anopheles gambiae* is the primary [[Plasmodium sp.|malaria]] vector in sub-Saharan Africa; *Aedes aegypti* and *Aedes albopictus* are widespread throughout tropical, subtropical, and temperate urban zones.
*   **Transmission Route:** Biological transmission occurs when an infective female mosquito takes a blood meal, injecting pathogens (e.g., malarial sporozoites, filarial L3 larvae, or viral particles) contained within its salivary glands into the host's cutaneous capillaries or bite wound.
*   **Institutional & Crowding Risks:** High transmission risk in unscreened housing, informal settlements, refugee camps, agricultural/irrigation projects, deforestation camps, and flooded zones.
*   **Public Health & Outbreak Management:** Control relies on integrated vector management: physical source reduction (draining standing water, container management, floating polystyrene balls on wells), chemical indoor residual spraying (IRS) with pyrethroids, organochlorines, organophosphates, or carbamates, biological control (*Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis*, *Gambusia affinis* larvivorous fish), and personal protection (long-lasting insecticide-treated bed nets (LLINs/ITNs) impregnated with [[permethrin]]/deltamethrin or PBO, wire-mesh window screening, DEET repellents, and wearing long-sleeved clothing).

#### Advanced Diagnostics
*   **Direct Examination & Skin Scrapings:** Gross identification of captured adult mosquitoes (slender body, long delicate legs, single pair of scaled wings, long proboscis, plumose/pilose antennae) or aquatic larvae; *Anopheles* larvae are identified by their parallel resting position on water surfaces and presence of dorsal palmate hairs, whereas *Culex* larvae are identified by their long breathing siphon hanging at an angle.
*   **Dermoscopy Features:** N/A.
*   **Biopsy Findings:** Histology of a fresh bite lesion reveals acute focal dermal edema, epidermal spongiosis, and a superficial and deep perivascular inflammatory infiltrate composed of lymphocytes, histiocytes, and eosinophils surrounding the proboscis puncture site.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Eradication
*   **Topical Scabicides & Pediculicides:** N/A (Mosquitoes are free-flying ectoparasites and do not colonize host skin or respond to topical scabicides/pediculicides).
*   **Oral Systemic Treatments:** Direct bite management is supportive (topical antipruritics, oral antihistamines, mild topical corticosteroids, and local antiseptics/antibiotics for secondary bacterial infections); vector-borne pathogens require disease-specific systemic antimicrobials (e.g., artemisinin-based combination therapies (ACTs), [[Chloroquine]], [[Primaquine]] for [[Plasmodium sp.|malaria]]; [[diethylcarbamazine]] (DEC), [[ivermectin]], or [[albendazole]] for filariasis).
*   **Treatment Nuances & Re-application Schedules:** N/A.
*   **Environmental & Laundry Decontamination Protocols:** Application of insect repellents containing DEET (*N,N*-diethyl-*m*-toluamide), picaridin, or oil of lemon eucalyptus to exposed skin; wearing [[permethrin]]-treated protective clothing (effective through multiple machine washings); sleeping under long-lasting insecticide-treated bed nets (LLINs/ITNs); installing fine wire-mesh screens on windows and doors.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Wigglers/wrigglers" (larvae), "tumblers" (pupae), "proboscis with two tubes", "scales along wing veins", "Anopheles parallel resting position", "Culex breathing siphon", "palmate hairs", "long-lasting insecticide-treated nets (LLINs)", "indoor residual spraying (IRS)", "hatchet cell absent" (distinguishing mosquitoes/flies from [[tsetse flies]]).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Male mosquitoes are completely incapable of biting or feeding on blood because their mouthparts cannot pierce skin; they feed exclusively on plant nectar and sugars. *Anopheles* larvae lie parallel to the surface of the water to breathe because they lack a breathing siphon, whereas *Culex* larvae hang at an angle using a prominent posterior siphon.
