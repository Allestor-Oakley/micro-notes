### Mosquitoes

#### Arthropod Anatomy & Physical Morphology
*   **Adult Physical Features & Size:** Adult mosquitoes (family Culicidae) are slender, small to medium-sized dipterans, usually measuring 3.0 mm to 6.0 mm in length (ranging from 2.0 mm in small species up to 19 mm in Toxorhynchites, with wingspans reaching 24 mm). The body is distinctly divided into three tagmata: head, thorax, and abdomen. Mosquitoes are distinguished from other similar flies by three key features: (1) possession of a conspicuous, forward-projecting proboscis; (2) presence of numerous appressed scales on the thorax, legs, abdomen, and wing veins; and (3) a fringe of scales along the posterior wing border. They possess only one pair of functional, narrow fore-wings; the hind-wings are reduced to a pair of small, knob-like balancing organs called halteres. At rest, the wings lie closed scissor-like across the dorsum of the abdomen. The head bears a prominent pair of kidney-shaped compound eyes and filamentous 15-segmented antennae. Sexual dimorphism is marked: females have pilose antennae (bearing short, sparse whorls of hair), whereas males have plumose antennae (feathery, bearing dense long hairs used to detect female wing-beat frequencies). Palps arise below the antennae: in female Anophelinae, palps are as long as the proboscis and apically straight/pointed; in male Anophelinae, palps are as long as the proboscis and distinctly clubbed/swollen at their tips. In female Culicinae, palps are very short; in male Culicinae, palps are long (often turned upward and hairy distally) but never clubbed. The thorax is covered with dull, shiny, or iridescent scales forming species-specific decorative patterns (e.g., Aedes aegypti exhibits a lyre-shaped silver design on the scutum; Aedes albopictus possesses a single median white stripe; Aedes vittatus has six lateral white spots; Haemagogus and Sabethes exhibit bright metallic blue, green, or golden appressed scales with enlarged antepronotal lobes forming a collar; Sabethes often features conspicuous paddles of scales on its tarsi). The long, slender legs are scale-covered, often with dark and pale rings; tarsi end in simple or toothed claws. Culex mosquitoes possess a pair of fleshy pulvilli between the claws in addition to the hair-like empodium. The abdomen is 10-segmented (7 or 8 visible); Culicinae abdomens are densely covered with dark and pale scales, whereas Anophelinae abdomens are almost or entirely devoid of scales. The female abdomen terminates in a pair of finger-like cerci (tapered to a pointed tip in female Aedes), whereas the male abdomen terminates in prominent genital claspers. Internally, female Anophelinae possess a single spermatheca and a shorter middle salivary gland lobe, whereas female Culicinae possess 2 or 3 spermathecae and equal-length salivary gland lobes.
*   **Mouthparts & Feeding Apparatus:** The proboscis projects forward in both sexes, but only females bite and ingest blood (males feed exclusively on plant juices, sap, and nectar; Toxorhynchites proboscis is curved backwards in both sexes and incapable of piercing skin). The largest outer component is the gutter-shaped labium, which terminates in a pair of sensory labella and acts as a protective sheath encircling six internal piercing stylets:
    1.  *Labrum:* The uppermost, rigid stylet forming the primary food channel through which blood is drawn.
    2.  *Mandibles:* A pair of thin, needle-like cutting stylets.
    3.  *Maxillae:* A pair of flexible stylets with serrated or toothed tips that saw into host dermal tissue.
    4.  *Hypopharynx:* A central flattened stylet containing the salivary duct through which salivary secretions are injected into the wound.
    During blood feeding, the labium bends backward like a bow while the six stylets enter host skin. Saliva contains antihemostatic enzymes (producing localized tissue hematomas), potent anticoagulants (preventing blood clotting in mouthpart channels), vasodilators, and local anesthetic substances that minimize host pain and defensive reactions.
*   **Egg / Nit Morphology & Attachment:**
    *   *Anophelinae:* Laid singly directly on the water surface. Eggs are boat-shaped, measuring ~0.5 mm, and possess a pair of lateral, air-filled floats. They cannot survive desiccation and hatch in 2 to 3 days in tropical climates (7 to 14 days in temperate zones).
    *   *Culex:* Laid vertically in floating egg rafts comprising up to 300 cylindrical eggs held together by surface tension without adhesive glue. Cannot survive drying.
    *   *Aedes:* Laid singly on damp surfaces (mud, container walls, leaf axils) just above the water line. Eggs are dark, oval, covered with a reticulated shell pattern, and exhibit extreme desiccation resistance, surviving dry for months or years and hatching in installments upon submergence.
    *   *Mansonia:* Laid in sticky, star-like masses glued to the undersurface of floating aquatic vegetation (e.g., Pistia, Salvinia).
    *   *Haemagogus / Psorophora:* Black, ovoid, laid singly in tree-holes, rock-pools, or flooded pastures; capable of surviving desiccation.
*   **Immature Stages:** Complete metamorphosis (holometabolous lifecycle):
    *   *Larvae ("Wigglers"):* Aquatic, legless, active organisms with an enlarged thorax wider than the head or abdomen. 4 active larval instars. The head possesses compound eyes, antennae, and prominent mouthbrushes that sweep organic particles and microorganisms into the mouth. The roundish thorax bears long branched and unbranched setae. The 10-segmented abdomen bears 9 visible segments; the 10th segment terminates in caudal setae, a fan-like ventral brush, and 2 pairs of transparent, sausage-shaped anal papillae (regulating osmoregulation, not respiration).
        *   *Anophelinae Larvae:* Lack a respiratory siphon. Spiracles open directly on the dorsal surface of abdominal segment 8. Larvae lie parallel to the water surface, held by dorsal palmate (float) hairs and brown tergal plates on abdominal segments 1 to 8, alongside a lateral comb-like pecten.
        *   *Culicinae Larvae:* Possess a sclerotized respiratory siphon on segment 8 and hang at an angle from the water surface. Culex larvae have a long, narrow siphon with multiple subventral hair tufts and pecten. Aedes larvae have a short, barrel-shaped siphon with a single subventral hair tuft and comb scales. Mansonia and Coquillettidia larvae possess a specialized, conical, serrated siphon supplied with prehensile hairs designed to pierce air canals in the roots and stems of aquatic plants for respiration. Toxorhynchites larvae are large (12 to 18 mm), reddish, and predatory on other mosquito larvae.
    *   *Pupae ("Tumblers"):* Aquatic, comma-shaped, highly active, non-feeding stage. The head and thorax are fused into a cephalothorax containing compound eyes, folded wings, legs, and proboscis. Dorsally, the cephalothorax bears a pair of respiratory trumpets (short, broad, and conical in Anophelinae; longer and cylindrical in Culicinae). The abdomen terminates in a pair of oval, flattened paddles. Anophelinae pupae possess short, peg-like lateral spines on abdominal segments 2 to 7 (or 3 to 7), which are absent in Culicinae pupae.

#### Lifecycle, Habitat & Host Attachment
*   **Primary Host Range:** Female mosquitoes feed on humans, domestic livestock (cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, horses), wild mammals (monkeys, rodents), birds (herons, egrets, ibises, crows), and cold-blooded vertebrates (reptiles, amphibians). Male mosquitoes feed exclusively on plant sap, nectar, and fruit juices.
*   **Developmental Stages:** Holometabolous lifecycle: Egg -> 4 Larval Instars -> Pupa -> Adult. Duration from egg to adult takes as little as 7 to 10 days in warm tropical environments (e.g., Aedes or Culex), but extends to weeks or months in temperate or arctic climates. The female reproductive life is defined by the gonotrophic cycle: Unfed Adult -> Blood Meal -> Digestion & Ovarian Maturation (Blood-fed -> Half-gravid -> Gravid) -> Oviposition -> Unfed Adult. Each gonotrophic cycle requires 2 to 3 days in the tropics (7 to 14 days in cooler regions) and is repeated multiple times per female lifespan. Autogenous species can mature a first batch of eggs without a blood meal.
*   **Attachment Mechanics & Burrowing Behavior:** Mosquitoes do not burrow or remain permanently attached to host skin. Female mosquitoes land on exposed skin, insert their six mouthpart stylets into superficial dermal capillaries, inject salivary fluids, ingest blood until the abdomen distends into a bright red balloon, and detach within 1 to 3 minutes. Feeding periodicity varies by taxon: night-biting / crepuscular (Anopheles, Culex quinquefasciatus) vs. day-biting / diurnal (Aedes aegypti, Aedes albopictus, Aedes bromeliae, Haemagogus in forest canopy).
*   **Environmental Survival Off-Host:** Adults inhabit indoor domestic microclimates (endophilic: bedrooms, closets, dark corners) or outdoor natural vegetation and shelters (exophilic: bushes, tree-holes, animal burrows). Adults are highly sensitive to low humidity and desiccation. Overwintering strategies include diapausing eggs (Aedes), hibernating adult females in shelters (Anopheles maculipennis complex), or diapausing larvae in water bodies.

#### Pathogenesis & Vector Dynamics
*   **Direct Dermatologic Damage:** Mechanical puncture by maxillae/mandibles and enzymatic histolysis from injected saliva induce localized epidermal/dermal damage, capillary dilation, focal edema, and transient microvascular hemorrhages.
*   **Hypersensitivity & Immune Response:** Bite lesions are driven by host immediate (IgE-mediated wheal-and-flare) and delayed-type (cell-mediated papular) hypersensitivity reactions against mosquito salivary proteins. Symptoms include intense pruritus, erythematous macules, papules, and urticarial wheals (culicosis). Excoriation from scratching compromises the skin barrier.
*   **Pathogens Transmitted as a Vector:** Mosquitoes are premier biological vectors for protozoa, filarial nematodes, and over 150 arboviruses:
    *   *Protozoa (Malaria):* [[Plasmodium falciparum]], [[Plasmodium vivax]], [[Plasmodium ovale]], [[Plasmodium malariae]], and [[Plasmodium knowlesi]]. Transmitted exclusively by female Anopheles mosquitoes (e.g., Anopheles gambiae, An. funestus, An. stephensi, An. culicifacies, An. baimai, An. freeborni, An. quadrimaculatus). Sporozoites in salivary glands are inoculated during blood feeding.
    *   *Helminths (Lymphatic Filariasis & Dirofilariasis):* [[Wuchereria bancrofti]], [[Brugia malayi]], and [[Brugia timori]]. Transmitted by Culex quinquefasciatus (bancroftian filariasis in polluted urban waters), Anopheles species (rural Africa/Asia), Aedes species (Ae. polynesiensis, Ae. pseudoscutellaris in Pacific Islands), and Mansonia species (M. indiana, M. uniformis in swamp forest habitats). Ingested microfilariae exsheath in the stomach, penetrate the gut wall, migrate to thoracic muscles to develop into fat, sausage-shaped L1/L2 larvae, undergo two molts to become active L3 infective larvae (10 to 14 days), and migrate to the labium/proboscis to enter human skin via bite wounds. [[Dirofilaria|Dirofilaria immitis]] (dog heartworm / human pulmonary coin lesions) is transmitted by Culex, Aedes, and Anopheles.
    *   *Arboviruses (Flaviviruses, Alphaviruses, Bunyaviruses):*
        *   [[Yellow Fever Virus]] (Flavivirus): Aedes aegypti (urban cycle); Aedes africanus, Ae. bromeliae (African sylvan/sylvatic cycle); Haemagogus species (Hg. spegazzinii, Hg. janthinomys) and Sabethes chloropterus (South American sylvan cycle).
        *   [[Dengue Virus]] (DENV-1, -2, -3, -4; Flavivirus): Aedes aegypti (primary urban vector), Aedes albopictus (secondary vector).
        *   [[West Nile Virus]] (Flavivirus): Culex species (Cx. pipiens, Cx. quinquefasciatus, Cx. tarsalis, Cx. salinarius).
        *   [[Japanese Encephalitis Virus]] (Flavivirus): Culex tritaeniorhynchus, Cx. vishnui, Cx. pseudovishnui, Cx. gelidus (ricefield breeders).
        *   [[St. Louis Encephalitis Virus]] (Flavivirus): Culex nigripalpus, Cx. quinquefasciatus, Cx. tarsalis.
        *   [[Zika Virus]] (Flavivirus): Aedes aegypti, Aedes albopictus.
        *   [[Chikungunya Virus]] (Alphavirus): Aedes aegypti, Aedes albopictus.
        *   Eastern Equine Encephalitis ([[EEEV]]) Virus (Alphavirus): Culiseta melanura, Aedes vexans, Culex salinarius.
        *   Western Equine Encephalitis ([[WEEV]]) Virus (Alphavirus): Culex tarsalis, Aedes species.
        *   Venezuelan Equine Encephalitis Virus ([[VEEV]]) (Alphavirus): Culex (Melaniconion) species, Aedes taeniorhynchus, Psorophora confinnis.
        *   Ross Valley Virus / O'nyong nyong Virus (Alphaviruses).
        *   Rift Valley Fever Virus (Phlebovirus): Aedes and Culex species.
        *   La Crosse Encephalitis Virus (Bunyavirus): Aedes triseriatus.
    *   *Vector Transmission Mechanisms:* Ingested viruses penetrate the gut wall into hemolymph, invade salivary glands, multiply during an extrinsic incubation period (EIP) of 5 to 30 days (typically 8 to 15 days), and are injected in saliva during refeeding. Transovarial transmission occurs in Aedes and Haemagogus for certain arboviruses.
*   **Host Defense & Predisposing Factors:** Risk factors include occupational or recreational outdoor exposure at dawn, dusk, or night; living near unsealed water storage jars, polluted drains, flooded pastures, or ricefields; lack of window screening; and absence of insecticide-treated bed nets.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Presentations
*   **Primary Infestation Presentation:** Mosquito bite dermatitis (culicosis) presents as acute, intensely pruritic, erythematous papules or wheals on exposed skin surfaces (arms, legs, face, neck).
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions & Distribution Pattern:** Localized pruritic wheals with central puncta on exposed skin. Pathognomonic clinical presentations of vector-borne diseases include:
    *   *[[Plasmodium sp.|Malaria]]:* Paroxysmal high fever, shaking chills, sweating, severe anemia, splenomegaly, jaundice, and cerebral involvement (coma in P. falciparum).
    *   *Lymphatic Filariasis:* Acute filarial [[Lymphadenitis and Lymphangitis]], retrograde [[Lymphadenitis and Lymphangitis]], hydrocele, persistent lymphedema, and chronic elephantiasis of limbs and genitalia.
    *   *Dengue Fever:* Severe retro-orbital headache, excruciating "breakbone" myalgia and arthralgia, maculopapular rash, positive tourniquet test, bleeding diathesis, and plasma leakage in Dengue Hemorrhagic Fever (DHF) / Dengue Shock Syndrome (DSS).
    *   *Yellow Fever:* Biphasic illness with toxic phase featuring fever, jaundice, hematemesis ("black vomit"), melena, oliguria, and relative bradycardia (Faget's sign).
*   **Secondary Complications:** Excoriation from scratching leads to secondary bacterial pyoderma, impetigo, [[cellulitis]], and staphylococcal or streptococcal superinfections. Vector disease complications include cerebral [[Plasmodium sp.|malaria]], acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), severe hemorrhagic shock, viral [[encephalitis]], permanent lymphedema, and high mortality in vulnerable populations.
*   **Laboratory & Dermoscopic Findings:** Direct examination of bite site shows superficial spongiosis, dermal edema, and perivascular inflammatory infiltrates. Diagnostics for vector pathogens include: thick and thin blood films (Giemsa stain) for Plasmodium ring stages/gametocytes or sheathed Wuchereria/Brugia microfilariae; rapid diagnostic tests (RDTs / antigen detection); serology ([[ELISA and Immunoassays|ELISA]], IFA) or molecular RT-[[PCR]] for arboviruses (Dengue, Yellow Fever, West Nile, Chikungunya, Zika) and [[Plasmodium sp.|malaria]].

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Geographic Distribution:** Cosmopolitan and worldwide across all continents except Antarctica:
    *   *Anopheles:* Global across tropical, subtropical, and temperate regions. An. gambiae complex dominates sub-Saharan Africa; An. stephensi and An. culicifacies in India; An. baimai in Southeast Asia.
    *   *Aedes aegypti / Ae. albopictus:* Endemic throughout tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, with Ae. albopictus expanding rapidly into temperate Europe and the Americas.
    *   *Culex quinquefasciatus:* Ubiquitous across tropical and subtropical urban and semi-urban centers.
    *   *Haemagogus / Sabethes:* Restricted to tropical forests of Central and South America.
*   **Transmission Route:** Vector-borne transmission via salivary injection of sporozoites ([[Plasmodium sp.|malaria]]) or arboviruses, or mechanical deposition of L3 filarial larvae onto skin during blood meals by infected female mosquitoes. No direct person-to-person transmission occurs for mosquito bites.
*   **Institutional & Crowding Risks:** High risk in urban slums with inadequate drainage and sanitation (Culex quinquefasciatus), flooded agricultural areas and ricefield communities (An. culicifacies, Cx. tritaeniorhynchus), refugee camps, forestry/timber camps (Haemagogus, An. baimai), and military field operations.
*   **Public Health & Outbreak Management:** Integrated Vector Management (IVM) strategies:
    1.  *Larval Control & Source Reduction:* Eliminating breeding sites (covering or emptying water pots, clearing choked drains, removing discarded tires and tin cans); applying chemical larvicides (temephos organophosphate, methoprene insect growth regulator); applying biological larvicides (Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis [BTI], Bacillus sphaericus); releasing larvivorous fish (Gambusia affinis, Poecilia reticulata) or predatory Toxorhynchites larvae; applying floating polystyrene beads on soakaway pits, latrines, and septic tanks.
    2.  *Adult Vector Control:*
        *   *Insecticide-Treated Nets (ITNs) & Long-Lasting Insecticidal Nets (LLINs):* Bed nets impregnated with synthetic pyrethroids ([[permethrin]], deltamethrin, alpha-cypermethrin, lambda-cyhalothrin) or dual-action nets incorporating chlorfenapyr or piperonyl butoxide (PBO). LLINs remain effective for 3 to 5 years and tolerate multiple washings, providing physical and chemical protection.
        *   *Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS):* Applying long-lasting residual insecticides (pyrethroids, carbamates, organophosphates, DDT) to inner walls and ceilings of dwellings where endophilic mosquitoes rest before/after blood meals. Target site mutations (kdr, ace) and metabolic resistance require insecticide rotation.
        *   *Space Spraying & ULV Fogs:* Ultra-low-volume (ULV) aerosol spraying or thermal fogging using pyrethroids or organophosphates for rapid knock-down of exophilic adult mosquitoes during arboviral epidemics.

#### Advanced Diagnostics
*   **Direct Examination & Skin Scrapings:** Morphological identification of wild-caught adult mosquitoes, larvae, or pupae using stereomicroscopy or hand lens (x10 to x40 magnification):
    *   *Adult Identification:* Examination of wing scale patterns (speckled, blocked, or uniform), proboscis vs. palp length ratio, antennal plumosity (sexing), scutellar scale patterns, tarsal banding, presence/absence of pulvilli, and abdominal scale coverage.
    *   *Larval Identification:* Presence or absence of a respiratory siphon; siphon index and subventral hair tuft arrangements; presence of palmate (float) hairs and tergal plates (Anopheles); comb scale configuration and sclerotized comb plates; pecten teeth; anal papillae shape; and modified plant-piercing siphon structures (Mansonia).
*   **Dermoscopy Features:** Dermoscopy (x10 to x20 magnification) of an acute bite site demonstrates a central punctate hemorrhagic core surrounded by an elevated, pale, edematous wheal and an outer erythematous halo.
*   **Biopsy Findings:** Histopathological examination (H&E stain) of a mosquito bite site shows focal epidermal intracellular edema (spongiosis), marked papillary dermal edema, dilated superficial capillaries with extravasated red blood cells, and a dense perivascular lymphohistiocytic infiltrate containing abundant eosinophils.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Eradication
*   **Topical Scabicides & Pediculicides:** Topical scabicides or pediculicides are not used on human skin for mosquito bites. Personal protection relies on topical chemical repellents applied to exposed skin: DEET (N,N-diethyl-m-toluamide, 20% to 50%), Picaridin (KBR 3023), IR3535, or Oil of Lemon Eucalyptus (PMD). [[Permethrin]] 0.5% spray applied to clothing, footwear, uniforms, and bed nets functions as a durable contact acaricide/insecticide.
*   **Oral Systemic Treatments:**
    *   *Symptomatic Bite Relief:* Oral H1-antihistamines (cetirizine, loratadine, diphenhydramine) to control severe pruritus; topical hydrocortisone 1% cream or calamine lotion to suppress local inflammation.
    *   *Systemic Vector Disease Therapy:* [[Artemisinin-based combination therapies]] (ACTs; e.g., [[artemether]]-[[lumefantrine]], [[dihydroartemisinin]]-[[piperaquine]]), [[chloroquine]], [[primaquine]], or [[8-Aminoquinoline|tafenoquine]] for [[Plasmodium sp.|malaria]]; [[Diethylcarbamazine]], [[Ivermectin]], and [[Albendazole]] (single annual triple therapy) for lymphatic filariasis.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Re-application Schedules:** Topical skin repellents must be reapplied every 4 to 8 hours depending on chemical formulation and sweating. Conventional ITNs require insecticidal re-dipping every 6 to 12 months; LLINs do not require re-dipping and retain efficacy for up to 20 washes or 3 to 5 years.
*   **Environmental & Laundry Decontamination Protocols:** Fitting dwellings with window and door screening (6 to 8 holes/cm or 16 to 18 mesh/inch). Sleeping under LLINs tucked under mattresses before sunset. Laundering bed nets and clothing in hot water (>50°C) or drying on high heat. Eliminating standing water collections around residential premises.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Pilose antennae" (female) vs "Plumose antennae" (male feathery), "Proboscis with 6 stylets", "Gutter-shaped labium and labella", "Lyre-shaped silvery markings" (Aedes aegypti scutum), "Single median white stripe" (Aedes albopictus scutum), "Boat-shaped eggs with air-filled floats" (Anopheles), "Egg raft" (Culex), "Desiccation-resistant eggs" (Aedes), "Siphonless larva lying parallel to water surface" (Anopheles), "Palmate float hairs and tergal plates", "Siphon with subventral hair tufts" (Culex, Aedes), "Plant-piercing siphon" (Mansonia / Coquillettidia), "Sausage-shaped larva" (filarial L1/L2 in mosquito thoracic muscle), "Extrinsic incubation period", "Gonotrophic cycle", "Endophilic vs Exophilic", "Long-Lasting Insecticidal Nets (LLINs)", "Pyrethroid knockdown resistance (kdr) mutation", "Autogenous development".
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   Only adult female mosquitoes feed on blood; male mosquitoes feed exclusively on plant nectar and sap and are completely incapable of biting.
    *   Adult mosquitoes of the genus Toxorhynchites have a proboscis curved backwards in both sexes, cannot feed on blood or bite humans, and are medically harmless; their larvae are large and predatory on other mosquito larvae.
    *   Anophelinae larvae completely lack a respiratory siphon and lie parallel to the water surface, whereas Culicinae larvae possess a siphon and hang at an angle from the surface.
    *   Mansonia and Coquillettidia larvae and pupae do not surface to breathe atmospheric air; they use a serrated, modified siphon/trumpet to pierce the air-containing roots and stems of submerged aquatic plants.
    *   Aedes eggs can survive complete desiccation for months or years and hatch in installments when flooded, whereas Anopheles and Culex eggs die rapidly if desiccated.
    *   Mosquitoes do not transmit human pathogens through simple mechanical contamination alone (with rare exceptions); transmitted arboviruses, [[Plasmodium sp.|malaria]] parasites, and filarial worms undergo obligatory, essential developmental or multiplication cycles (extrinsic incubation period) inside the mosquito vector before becoming infective to humans.
