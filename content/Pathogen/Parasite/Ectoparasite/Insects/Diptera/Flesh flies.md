### Flesh flies

#### Arthropod Anatomy & Physical Morphology
*   **Adult Physical Features & Size:** Adult flies possess a body divided into head, thorax, and abdomen, one pair of functional membranous wings, one pair of vestigial hind wings modified as halteres for balance, three pairs of legs, and a single pair of antennae; adult thorax and abdomen characteristically exhibit longitudinal stripes or a checkerboard/spotted pattern; specific adult millimeter size is N/A in the provided sources.
*   **Mouthparts & Feeding Apparatus:** Adult mouthparts are adapted for feeding on feces, decaying meat, or fish; larvae possess powerful, chitinized mouthparts with oral hooks used to penetrate, tear, and feed on host tissue and fluids.
*   **Egg / Nit Morphology & Attachment:** Female flies do not lay eggs on the host; they are larviparous (ovoviviparous), developing eggs internally and depositing batches of 40 to 80 active, live first-stage larvae directly onto living or decaying host tissues, wounds, or orifices.
*   **Immature Stages:** Larvae (maggots) progress through three instar stages; characterized by chitinized mouth hooks, cuticular spines, and posterior spiracles (stigmal plates) housed within a distinct, deep posterior cavity or pit.

#### Lifecycle, Habitat & Host Attachment
*   **Primary Host Range:** Humans and warm-blooded domestic and wild animals (including sheep, cattle, horses, dogs, camels, geese, and poultry).
*   **Developmental Stages:** Undergo complete metamorphosis (holometabolous lifecycle: egg -> larva -> pupa -> adult); larviparous female deposits 1st instar larvae directly onto host tissue -> larvae feed and molt through 2nd and 3rd instars in host tissue -> mature 3rd instar larvae leave the host -> pupate in dry soil/environment -> adult flies emerge.
*   **Attachment Mechanics & Burrowing Behavior:** Larvae use chitinized oral hooks and body spines to anchor and burrow into host tissues, wounds, or mucous membranes; *Wohlfahrtia magnifica* larvae are obligate parasites that actively invade living tissues and natural orifices, whereas *Wohlfahrtia vigil* larvae produce localized dermal boil-like lesions.
*   **Environmental Survival Off-Host:** Adults survive off-host feeding on decaying organic substrate, carrion, and feces; mature larvae exit the host and require a dry, terrestrial environment or loose soil to complete pupation.

#### Pathogenesis & Vector Dynamics
*   **Direct Dermatologic Damage:** Larvae invade living or necrotic tissue, using powerful oral hooks to digest and destroy cutaneous, subcutaneous, or mucosal structures, resulting in furuncular boil-like lesions, ulcerative tissue erosion, deep cavitary destruction, or traumatic wound breakdown.
*   **Hypersensitivity & Immune Response:** Larval feeding and metabolic excretion/secretion trigger an intense local inflammatory cellular infiltration (neutrophils, lymphocytes, [[Plasma Cell|plasma cells]], and eosinophils), localized edema, throbbing pain, and severe pruritus; accidental crushing or squashing of larvae within the skin releases foreign antigens that provoke severe hypersensitivity reactions and localized foreign-body granulomas.
*   **Pathogens Transmitted as a Vector:** Act primarily as direct tissue parasites causing myiasis rather than biological vectors of specific pathogens; mechanical transport of environmental/fecal bacteria can occur, but specific vector transmission capacity is N/A in the provided sources.
*   **Host Defense & Predisposing Factors:** Nosocomial and community acquisition is predisposed by impaired consciousness, physical or mental disability, debilitation, open purulent or malodorous wounds, advanced periodontitis/poor oral hygiene, and lack of window screening or hygienic measures in rural or tropical settings.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Presentations
*   **Primary Infestation Presentation:** Cutaneous presentation begins as an itchy, red papule or nodule that enlarges into a furuncle-like lesion with a central pore through which serosanguineous or purulent fluid exudes and larval spiracles may be visualized; patients frequently report severe throbbing pain and a crawling sensation.
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions & Distribution Pattern:** Boil-like furuncular nodules with a central breathing pore; destructive wound/traumatic lesions; aural otomyiasis (dark bloody or foul discharge, crawling sensations, and buzzing in the ear canal); nasal myiasis (nasal obstruction, facial edema, and fever); oral orofacial myiasis; ocular myiasis; or accidental intestinal myiasis (diarrhea, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, and rectal bleeding following larval ingestion).
*   **Secondary Complications:** Secondary bacterial superinfections, septic ulcers, abscess formation, cellulitis, extensive tissue necrosis, lymphadenopathy, thrombophlebitis, and potential secondary tetanus.
*   **Laboratory & Dermoscopic Findings:** Peripheral blood eosinophilia, elevated total serum IgE, and systemic inflammatory markers; Doppler ultrasonography demonstrates the sub-epidermal larva; dermoscopy reveals a central opening with a larva protruding or extruding spiracles.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Geographic Distribution:** Worldwide; *Wohlfahrtia magnifica* is distributed from the Mediterranean across Europe to the former USSR and Mongolia; *Wohlfahrtia vigil* occurs in northern North America.
*   **Transmission Route:** Direct larviposition by female flies depositing live 1st instar larvae onto skin, open sores, or natural body openings (nose, ear, eye, mouth, vagina); or accidental oral ingestion of contaminated food causing enteric myiasis.
*   **Institutional & Crowding Risks:** Significant risk of health care-associated (nosocomial) myiasis among institutionalized, debilitated, non-responsive, or intubated patients with open wounds, tracheostomies, or malodorous discharges.
*   **Public Health & Outbreak Management:** Control relies on vector elimination (flypaper, electrocuting traps, baited traps), installing window screens, proper sanitization and disposal of garbage, animal excreta, and decaying organic matter, and applying DEET insect repellents.

#### Advanced Diagnostics
*   **Direct Examination & Skin Scrapings:** Gross recovery and morphological examination of larvae; species identification is based on chitinized mouth hooks, body spines, and posterior spiracular plates (stigmal plates) located inside a deep posterior cavity; extracted live larvae should be killed by immersion in hot (near-boiling, ~60°C) water for 30 seconds to prevent shrinkage/putrefaction and then preserved in 70% to 95% ethanol or 70% isopropyl alcohol (formalin is not recommended).
*   **Dermoscopy Features:** Visualization of a central cutaneous pore surrounded by dilated blood vessels from which the yellowish/dark posterior end or spiracles of the larva intermittently extrude.
*   **Biopsy Findings:** Histologic sections reveal a central tissue cavity or sinus tract containing larval cross-sections with chitinous cuticle, surrounded by tissue necrosis and a dense inflammatory infiltrate composed of neutrophils, lymphocytes, [[Plasma Cell|plasma cells]], and eosinophils.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Eradication
*   **Topical Scabicides & Pediculicides:** N/A (Free-living fly larvae causing myiasis do not respond to standard topical scabicides or pediculicides).
*   **Oral Systemic Treatments:** Treatment requires physical or surgical removal of larvae; application of asphyxiating substances (petroleum jelly, liquid paraffin, mineral oil, or turpentine-soaked cotton) over the central pore forces the larva to surface for forceps extraction; systemic oral [[ivermectin]] or oral/topical antibiotics may be used as adjuvants or for secondary bacterial infections.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Re-application Schedules:** Squeezing or squashing larvae inside the host skin must be strictly avoided during extraction, as dead or ruptured larval fragments trigger intense foreign-body reactions, severe inflammation, and secondary bacterial abscesses.
*   **Environmental & Laundry Decontamination Protocols:** Utilization of physical barriers (window screens, bed nets), fly traps, and insecticides; elimination of environmental decaying organic waste, garbage, and animal manure; application of DEET repellents to exposed skin.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Spotted flesh fly", "larviparous" (depositing live 1st instar larvae rather than eggs), "chitinized mouth hooks", "posterior spiracles in a deep pit/cavity", "furuncular myiasis", "traumatic wound myiasis", "otomyiasis", "nosocomial myiasis in debilitated patients".
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Female flesh flies are strictly larviparous and never lay unhatched eggs on the host; *Wohlfahrtia magnifica* is an obligate tissue parasite that only develops in living host tissues and never breeds in carrion or dead animal matter.
