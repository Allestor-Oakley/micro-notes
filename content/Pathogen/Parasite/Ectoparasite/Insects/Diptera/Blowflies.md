### Blowflies

#### Arthropod Anatomy & Physical Morphology
*   **Adult Physical Features & Size:** Medium to large dipterous flies (family Calliphoridae) measuring approximately 6 to 14 mm in length. Many species feature a characteristic metallic blue (bluebottles, e.g., *Calliphora* spp.), metallic green (greenbottles, e.g., *Lucilia* spp.), or dark metallic sheen (*Phormia regina*), while others are non-metallic. Possesses a distinct head, thorax, and abdomen, a single pair of functional membranous wings, halteres, and 3 pairs of jointed legs (6 legs total).
*   **Mouthparts & Feeding Apparatus:** Adult blowflies possess sponging-lapping mouthparts adapted for feeding on liquid organic fluids, nectar, and decaying organic matter. Larvae (maggots) possess chitinous anterior mouthhooks used to tear, rasp, and liquefy host tissue, coupled with salivary digestive enzymes.
*   **Egg / Nit Morphology & Attachment:** Adult female blowflies deposit clusters or batches of hundreds of smooth, elongated, yellowish-white oval eggs onto decaying organic matter, animal carcasses, unwashed suppurative wounds, or foul-smelling body openings.
*   **Immature Stages:** Complete metamorphosis (holometabolous lifecycle). Eggs hatch into legless, peg-shaped larvae (maggots) that progress through 3 larval instars. Third-instar larvae feature species-specific posterior spiracular plates (stigmal plates) with distinct slits and peritreme patterns used for diagnostic identification.

#### Lifecycle, Habitat & Host Attachment
*   **Primary Host Range:** Humans, domestic and wild mammals, birds, and decaying animal carcasses.
*   **Developmental Stages:** Four-stage lifecycle: Egg -> Larva (3 instars) -> Pupa (puparium in soil or dry material) -> Adult fly.
*   **Attachment Mechanics & Burrowing Behavior:** Female flies do not attach to hosts; they lay eggs on necrotic tissue, open suppurative wounds, or unwashed skin. Larvae burrow into damaged, necrotic, or sometimes healthy skin/tissue using anterior mouthhooks and backward-pointing cuticular spines, causing facultative or traumatic wound myiasis.
*   **Environmental Survival Off-Host:** Free-living adults fly in outdoor and indoor environments, attracted by odors of decomposition, feces, and open suppurative lesions. Pupae survive in soil or leaf litter.

#### Pathogenesis & Vector Dynamics
*   **Direct Dermatologic Damage:** Larval tissue invasion causes extensive mechanical destruction, liquefaction necrosis of tissue, foul-smelling purulent discharge, deep tissue destruction, and excavation of necrotic or living tissue.
*   **Hypersensitivity & Immune Response:** Secretion of larval proteolytic enzymes and metabolic excretions induces localized intense inflammatory infiltrates composed of neutrophils, lymphocytes, [[Plasma Cell|plasma cells]], and eosinophils, accompanied by local edema and erythema.
*   **Pathogens Transmitted as a Vector:** Adult blowflies act as mechanical vectors transferring enteric bacterial pathogens (*[[Escherichia coli]]*, *[[Salmonella typhi|Salmonella]]* spp., *[[Shigella]]* spp.), protozoan cysts (*[[Entamoeba histolytica]]*, *[[Giardia lamblia]]*), and helminth eggs from garbage and feces to human food or open wounds. Larvae cause myiasis rather than biological disease transmission.
*   **Host Defense & Predisposing Factors:** Open traumatic or surgical wounds, chronic cutaneous ulcers, neglected scalp or skin scratches, poor personal hygiene, homelessness, advanced age, physical or mental incapacitation, debilitation, and unwashed suppurative body discharges.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Presentations
*   **Primary Infestation Presentation:** Presence of visible, writhing maggots inside open traumatic wounds, ulcers, nasal cavities, ears, or skin lesions, accompanied by pain, crawling sensations, foul odor, and purulent discharge.
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions & Distribution Pattern:** Traumatic or facultative wound myiasis presenting as cavitary skin lesions or ulcerated wounds teeming with legless maggots; cutaneous, nasal, aural, ocular, gastrointestinal, or genitourinary tissue involvement.
*   **Secondary Complications:** Secondary bacterial infection (cellulitis, pyoderma, bacteremia), extensive tissue and bone destruction (e.g., erosion of facial bones or skull in severe cavitary cases), hemorrhage, and severe disfigurement.
*   **Laboratory & Dermoscopic Findings:** Direct visual or dermoscopic identification of active, motile, legless fly larvae inside tissue lesions. Histopathology shows cross-sections of larvae surrounded by dense neutrophilic, eosinophilic, and granulomatous tissue infiltrates.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Geographic Distribution:** Worldwide / Cosmopolitan distribution across all climatic zones.
*   **Transmission Route:** Accidental or facultative oviposition by gravid female blowflies onto exposed, foul-smelling wounds, body orifices, unwashed skin, or dirty clothing; or ingestion of fly eggs/larvae on contaminated food leading to pseudomyiasis/gastrointestinal myiasis.
*   **Institutional & Crowding Risks:** High risk of health care-associated (nosocomial) myiasis among debilitated, comatose, or bedridden hospital patients with open wounds or tracheostomies in warm environments where flies gain entry.
*   **Public Health & Outbreak Management:** Wound care and hygiene in hospitals and long-term care facilities; screening doors and windows to prevent fly entry; proper disposal of garbage and animal carcasses; sanitation and fly control.

#### Advanced Diagnostics
*   **Direct Examination & Skin Scrapings:** Extraction of larvae from lesions, followed by preservation in hot (60°C) 70% alcohol and microscopic examination of the posterior spiracular (stigmal) plates and anterior mouthhooks under low-power magnification to identify the specific fly family and genus.
*   **Dermoscopy Features:** Dermoscopy reveals motile, white-to-yellowish elongated larvae with prominent cuticular spine bands and dark anterior mouthhooks moving inside wound tissue.
*   **Biopsy Findings:** Histological sectioning of excised tissue reveals larval cross-sections with thick chitinous cuticles, internal muscle bands, respiratory tracheae, and surrounding intense inflammatory infiltrates composed of neutrophils, eosinophils, and lymphocytes.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Eradication
*   **Topical Scabicides & Pediculicides:** N/A for blowfly myiasis.
*   **Oral Systemic Treatments:** Systemic antibiotics are indicated for secondary bacterial wound infections. Oral [[Ivermectin]] can be used off-label in severe cavitary myiasis to paralyze larvae and facilitate removal.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Re-application Schedules:** Primary management requires manual or surgical extraction of all larvae using forceps, followed by thorough irrigation with sterile saline or antiseptic solutions (e.g., chlorhexidine), surgical debridement of necrotic tissue, and local wound care.
*   **Environmental & Laundry Decontamination Protocols:** Maintaining clean, covered wound dressings; installing window/door screens and fly traps in healthcare settings; washing soiled hospital bedding and clothing in hot water; and prompt removal and incineration of garbage and decaying organic waste.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Blowfly, Calliphoridae, *Calliphora* (bluebottle), *Lucilia* (greenbottle), *Phormia regina* (black blowfly), metallic fly, maggots, myiasis, traumatic wound myiasis, posterior spiracles / stigmal plates, medicolegal forensic entomology, post-mortem interval, human DNA source from third-instar larvae.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   *Medicolegal Forensic Entomology:* Third-instar blowfly larvae actively feeding on a human corpse are the primary tool used by forensic entomologists to calculate the Post-Mortem Interval (PMI / time of death) based on larval developmental rates, and crop contents of larvae serve as an excellent source of human DNA for victim identification.
    *   *Maggot Debridement Therapy (MDT):* Controlled, sterile larvae of greenbottle blowflies (*Lucilia sericata*) are intentionally applied to chronic, non-healing necrotic wounds (e.g., diabetic foot ulcers) because they selectively digest dead necrotic tissue and secrete antimicrobial enzymes without harming viable living tissue.
    *   *Facultative vs. Obligatory Myiasis:* Most common blowfly species (*Calliphora*, *Lucilia*, *Phormia*) cause facultative myiasis (feeding primarily on dead/necrotic tissue or carcasses), whereas specific primary screwworms (*Chrysomyia bezziana*, *Cochliomyia hominivorax*) cause obligatory myiasis by attacking healthy, living tissue.
