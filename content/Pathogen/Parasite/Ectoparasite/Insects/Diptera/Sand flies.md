### Sand flies

#### Arthropod Anatomy & Physical Morphology
*   **Adult Physical Features & Size:** Small, delicate, fragile flying insects measuring less than 5 mm in length (typically 1 to 4 mm, or 2 to 5 mm); body color ranges from dark-gray to dusty-brown or yellowish; characteristically covered in dense hair giving a distinctly fuzzy/hairy appearance; possess large dark eyes, long multi-segmented antennae, long maxillary palpi, and mouthparts that are at least as long as the head; equipped with long, slender legs and a single pair of delicate wings lacking scales; adult sand flies exhibit a characteristic resting posture wherein wings are held erect upward over the thorax at a 60° "V" angle; females possess a plump, cylindrical abdomen that tapers distally and terminates in two rounded, paddle-like cerci; taxonomy comprises six main genera, including *Phlebotomus*, *Sergentomyia*, and *Chinius* in the Old World, and *Lutzomyia*, *Brumptomyia*, and *Warileya* in the New World (with *Phlebotomus* and *Lutzomyia* serving as the primary medically important genera).
*   **Mouthparts & Feeding Apparatus:** Specialized cutting-slicing mouthparts adapted for pool feeding (telmophagy); female flies use blade-like mouthparts to slice into host skin, creating a superficial dermal wound and feeding on the pooled blood micro-hemorrhage; salivary gland secretions contain potent anticoagulants, vasodilators, lipophosphoglycans, and salivary proteins that maintain blood fluidity and actively enhance parasite infectivity in the host; both male and female sand flies feed on plant sugars, juices, and flower nectar, but only females take blood meals (which provide essential protein for egg maturation).
*   **Egg / Nit Morphology & Attachment:** Adult females require one or more blood meals to mature each batch/clutch of eggs; eggs are deposited in non-aquatic, cool, dark, humid microhabitats rich in organic matter (such as moist soil, cracks and crevices in masonry/walls, rodent burrows, rock crevices, stone walls, termite hills, and rubbish heaps); microscopic egg dimensions are N/A in the provided sources.
*   **Immature Stages:** Non-aquatic larvae progress through development in moist organic substrates over 30 to 60 days before undergoing pupation; specific larval instar morphological features and spiracular structures are N/A in the provided sources.

#### Lifecycle, Habitat & Host Attachment
*   **Primary Host Range:** Attack humans, domestic animals (dogs, cats), and wild animal reservoirs (rodents, gerbils, fat sand rats, rock and tree hyraxes, sloths, anteaters, opossums, armadillos, and wild carnivores/canids), as well as birds and reptiles.
*   **Developmental Stages:** Undergo complete metamorphosis (holometabolous lifecycle: egg -> larva -> pupa -> adult); non-aquatic larval development requires 30 to 60 days in moist organic matter -> pupal stage -> adult fly emerges; adult flies are weak fliers that move in short, characteristic "jerky hops" along walls, ceilings, or ground vegetation; female sand flies become infective to mammalian hosts 4 to 18 days (typically 7 to 10 days) after ingesting a blood meal containing amastigotes, during which time parasites transform into motile promastigotes and multiply in the midgut (subgenus *Leishmania*) or midgut and hindgut (subgenus *Viannia*), eventually migrating to the anterior gut/hypostome.
*   **Attachment Mechanics & Burrowing Behavior:** Non-burrowing, temporary ectoparasites; female flies do not remain permanently attached to host skin, but land briefly, slice the epidermis, inject saliva, feed on pooled blood, and depart.
*   **Environmental Survival Off-Host:** Strictly restricted to geographic regions where ambient temperatures exceed 15.6°C (60°F) for at least 3 months of the year; found from below sea level up to elevations of 2,800 meters or higher; nocturnal or crepuscular biters that become active at and after dusk; during daylight hours, flies rest in cool, dark, sheltered microhabitats (corners of dwellings, wall cracks, stone crevices, rodent burrows, termite mounds, tree hollows, and forest canopy leaf litter).

#### Pathogenesis & Vector Dynamics
*   **Direct Dermatologic Damage:** Slicing mouthparts cause direct mechanical epidermal and dermal capillary laceration, resulting in immediate localized hemorrhage, focal tissue irritation, pain, induration, and an erythematous wheal 1 to 2 cm in diameter accompanied by intense pruritus.
*   **Hypersensitivity & Immune Response:** Injected salivary components provoke both immediate and delayed-type hypersensitivity responses, characterized by intense itching, local erythema, and localized edema; in sensitized individuals, sand fly bites can trigger systemic allergic reactions including fever, retroorbital pain, nausea, and general malaise (sandfly fever / Phlebotomus fever).
*   **Pathogens Transmitted as a Vector:** Major biological vector for:
    *   **Protozoa (*Leishmania* spp.):** Transmits metacyclic promastigotes during blood feeding. Old World vectors (*Phlebotomus* spp.) transmit *L. donovani*, *L. infantum*, *L. major*, *L. tropica*, and *L. aethiopica*. New World vectors (*Lutzomyia* spp.) transmit *L. infantum* (*L. chagasi*), *L. braziliensis*, *L. mexicana*, *L. guyanensis*, *L. panamensis*, *L. amazonensis*, *L. pifanoi*, *L. venezuelensis*, *L. shawi*, *L. naiffi*, *L. lainsoni*, *L. peruviana*, and *L. colombiensis*.
    *   **Bacteria:** Transmits *[[Bartonella bacilliformis]]* (agent of Bartonellosis / Carrion disease / Oroya fever / verruga peruana).
    *   **Viruses:** Transmits Sandfly fever virus (*Phlebovirus* causing sandfly fever).
*   **Host Defense & Predisposing Factors:** Human risk is heightened by intrusion into natural sylvatic/zoonotic foci (forest clearing, construction, agricultural work, timber cutting, chicle harvesting), sleeping in poorly constructed housing with unplastered wall cracks or mud construction, proximity to animal reservoirs (rodent burrows, dog kennels), outdoor nocturnal exposure, and lack of fine-mesh bed nets or window screening.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Presentations
*   **Primary Infestation Presentation:** Direct bite presents as a small, intensely pruritic, red papule or indurated wheal with a central hemorrhagic puncture mark; Sandfly fever presents with sudden onset of high fever, severe frontal headache, retroorbital pain, anorexia, nausea, photophobia, and generalized myalgia.
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions & Distribution Pattern:** Localized erythematous bite papules on exposed skin (face, neck, arms, legs); associated vector-borne manifestations include cutaneous leishmaniasis ulcers (Oriental sore, Baghdad boil, Chiclero ulcer on ear cartilage) with raised indurated borders and central crusting; mucocutaneous destruction of nasal, oral, and pharyngeal cartilage (espundia); or visceral leishmaniasis (kala-azar) marked by irregular dromedary fever, massive hepatosplenomegaly, generalized lymphadenopathy, hypergammaglobulinemia, and severe pancytopenia.
*   **Secondary Complications:** Secondary bacterial superinfections of excoriated bite wounds; facial mutilation/airway destruction in mucocutaneous leishmaniasis; secondary fatal bacterial/viral infections or bleeding in visceral leishmaniasis.
*   **Laboratory & Dermoscopic Findings:** Direct bite site shows non-specific epidermal disruption and acute perivascular dermal inflammation; diagnostic laboratory findings for vector-borne leishmaniasis include microscopic identification of intracellular amastigotes (Leishman-Donovan bodies with nucleus and bar-shaped kinetoplast) inside tissue [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]] on Giemsa-stained tissue/[[Bone Marrow]]/splenic aspirates or skin biopsy, positive kDNA PCR, and isolation of flagellated promastigotes in NNN orSchneider's culture media.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Geographic Distribution:** Worldwide across tropical, subtropical, and temperate regions of Southern Europe, the Mediterranean basin, the Middle East, Sub-Saharan Africa, Central Asia, the Indian subcontinent, Central America, South America, and the southern United States (Texas and Arizona).
*   **Transmission Route:** Biological transmission occurs when an infective female sand fly takes a blood meal, regurgitating infective metacyclic promastigotes or bacterial/viral pathogens from its mouthparts/hypostome into the skin wound; rare alternative transmission routes for vector-borne leishmaniasis include direct lesion contact (leishmanization), blood transfusion, organ transplantation, needle sharing among intravenous drug users, and congenital transmission.
*   **Institutional & Crowding Risks:** High risk in rural poverty-stricken zones, informal settlements, refugee camps, displaced person camps in conflict zones (e.g., Kabul, Afghanistan), deforestation/construction camps, and housing with mud/thatched walls.
*   **Public Health & Outbreak Management:** Integrated vector control relies on indoor residual spraying (IRS) with insecticides (DDT, pyrethroids), environmental management (deep plowing over rodent burrow concentrations in arid zones, destroying rodent burrows near human dwellings, clearing rubbish heaps), using fine-mesh window screening and bed nets (mesh size must be smaller than standard [[Mosquitoes|mosquito]] netting due to tiny sand fly size <5 mm), applying [[permethrin]] to clothing and bed nets, applying DEET repellents to exposed skin, and controlling animal reservoir populations (stray dog culling, delousing/insecticide collars on dogs).

#### Advanced Diagnostics
*   **Direct Examination & Skin Scrapings:** Morphological identification of captured adult flies; key diagnostic parameters include small size (<5 mm), densely hairy/fuzzy body and wings, large compound eyes, erect wings held at a 60° "V" position over the thorax, long antennae and palpi, and short, jerky hopping flight pattern.
*   **Dermoscopy Features:** N/A.
*   **Biopsy Findings:** Histologic evaluation of direct bite sites demonstrates focal epidermal disruption, superficial dermal hemorrhage, and a dense perivascular acute and chronic inflammatory infiltrate composed of neutrophils, lymphocytes, and histiocytes; tissue biopsy of vector-borne leishmaniasis lesions reveals granulomatous dermal/submucosal inflammation with intracellular amastigotes inside reticuloendothelial [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]].

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Eradication
*   **Topical Scabicides & Pediculicides:** N/A (Sand flies are free-flying ectoparasites and do not colonize or burrow into host skin).
*   **Oral Systemic Treatments:** Direct bite management is palliative, utilizing topical antipruritics, oral antihistamines, local anesthetics, and antiseptics/antibiotics for secondary bacterial infections; systemic vector-borne leishmaniasis is treated with [[Liposomal Amphotericin B]], [[Sodium stibogluconate]], meglumine antimonate, [[Miltefosine]], or [[Pentamidine]].
*   **Treatment Nuances & Re-application Schedules:** N/A.
*   **Environmental & Laundry Decontamination Protocols:** Application of chemical repellents containing DEET (*N,N*-diethyl-*m*-toluamide) to exposed skin; wearing [[permethrin]]-treated protective clothing; sleeping under fine-mesh insecticide-treated bed nets (ITNs); residual insecticide spraying of dwelling walls; application of bacterial larvicides (*Bacillus thuringiensis* var. *israelensis*) to breeding sites.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Moth flies", "phlebotomines", "Phlebotomus", "Lutzomyia", "wings erect at 60° V-shape", "fuzzy/densely hairy body", "short jerky hops", "pool feeders", "telmophagy", "sandfly fever", "kala-azar vector", "Oriental sore vector", "espundia vector".
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Female sand flies are extremely tiny (<5 mm) and can easily pass through standard wire-mesh window screens and ordinary [[Mosquitoes|mosquito]] bed nets, requiring specialized extra-fine mesh netting for physical exclusion; lectins and toxins present in the plant diet of certain sand flies (e.g., *Phlebotomus papatasi*) have been shown to actively kill *Leishmania major* promastigotes within the fly gut, potentially decreasing parasite transmission in specific natural vegetation environments.
