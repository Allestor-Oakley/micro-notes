### Sand flies

#### Arthropod Anatomy & Physical Morphology
*   **Adult Physical Features & Size:** Adult phlebotomine sand flies (family Psychodidae, subfamily Phlebotominae) are minute, fragile, blood-sucking Diptera measuring 1.5 mm to 3.0 mm in length (rarely up to 5.0 mm). Body coloration ranges from pale sand-colored, yellowish, or dark grey to dusty brown. A hallmark feature is that the entire body—including the head, thorax, abdomen, legs, and wing veins—is densely covered with long, fine, erect hairs, giving the insect a characteristic "fuzzy" or "moth-like" appearance. The head bears a pair of relatively large, prominent black compound eyes and a pair of long, filamentous 16-segmented antennae composed of small bead-like segments bearing short hairs (similar in both sexes, non-plumose). The mouthparts are short and inconspicuous, flanked at their base by a pair of 5-segmented maxillary palps that droop conspicuously downward. The legs are noticeably long, slender, and stilt-like. Wings are lanceolate (leaf-shaped) in outline, distinct from all other biting flies. A pathognomonic behavioral and anatomical diagnostic feature is that at rest or while blood-feeding, sand flies hold their wings erected in a prominent "V"-shape at an angle of 40° to 60° over the thorax/body (never folded flat across the dorsum or roof-like over the body as in non-biting psychodid flies). Wing venation is unique to Phlebotominae: wing vein 2 branches twice (double branching of vein 2). Adult females possess a plump, cylindrical abdomen terminating in two rounded, paddle-like cerci; adult males feature prominent genital claspers at the abdominal tip. Medically important genera include *Phlebotomus* (restricted to the Old World), *Sergentomyia* (Old World, primarily feeds on reptiles and cold-blooded animals), *Lutzomyia* (restricted to the New World), and *Psychodopygus* (New World).
*   **Mouthparts & Feeding Apparatus:** Biting mouthparts are short, delicate, and hang downward from the ventral surface of the head. Only adult female sand flies possess functional cutting stylets and feed on blood; male sand flies feed exclusively on plant juices, sap, and fruit nectar. The feeding apparatus comprises six short, blade-like/lancet-like stylets (labrum, pair of mandibles, pair of maxillae/laciniae, and hypopharynx) enveloped posteriorly by a fleshy labium. Sand flies are pool feeders (telmophages): the serrated mandibles and maxillae cut and slice into host dermal tissue, lacerating superficial blood capillaries to form a small pool of blood (hematoma) that is drawn up into the digestive tract by the muscular pharyngeal pump. Saliva contains antihemostatic enzymes, potent anticoagulants, vasodilators (e.g., Maxadilatan in *Lutzomyia longipalpis*—the most potent vasodilator peptide known), and local irritants/allergenic proteins that prevent blood clotting, increase local blood flow, and enhance parasite infectivity in the host. Because their mouthparts are extremely short, sand flies are completely incapable of biting through fabric or clothing.
*   **Egg / Nit Morphology & Attachment:** Eggs are minute, measuring 0.3 mm to 0.4 mm in length, ovoid or elongated-oval in shape, and brown or dark black. Careful microscopic examination reveals a characteristic sculptured, reticulated, or raised mosaic-type ridge pattern on the eggshell. Eggs are deposited singly or in small batches of 30 to 70 eggs per oviposition in dark, cool, humid microhabitats rich in organic matter: small cracks and crevices in soil/ground, rodent burrows (e.g., *Rhombomys opimus* gerbil burrows), bases of termite mounds, masonry cracks, stone walls, stable floors, poultry houses, leaf litter, decaying forest vegetation, and between the buttress roots of rainforest trees. Eggs require a high-humidity microenvironment to survive; they cannot withstand complete desiccation and hatch in 4 to 20 days depending on ambient temperature. Eggs are never deposited in open water.
*   **Immature Stages:** Complete metamorphosis (holometabolous lifecycle) with 4 larval instars:
    *   *Larvae:* Mature 4th-instar larvae measure 3.0 mm to 6.0 mm in length, terrestrial or semi-aquatic, caterpillar-like, with a white or greyish-white body consisting of a well-defined black head and 12 visible body segments. The head bears small mandibles. Ventrally, abdominal segments bear small false legs (pseudopods). A pathognomonic diagnostic feature is that the head and all 12 body segments are covered with conspicuous, thick, stiff bristles with barbed/feathered stems and slightly enlarged, button-like tips called **matchstick hairs** (matchstick setae). The 12th (terminal) abdominal segment bears two pairs (4 total) of very long, dark, erect bristles called **caudal setae** / caudal bristles (1st-instar larvae possess only one pair of caudal setae). Larvae are terrestrial scavengers, feeding on decaying organic matter, leaf litter, fungi, animal feces, and dead arthropod bodies.
    *   *Pupae:* Non-feeding stage measuring 2.0 mm to 3.0 mm, resting in an almost erect position in humid dark crevices. The pupa does not completely cast off the 4th-instar larval skin; instead, the larval exuviae remains firmly attached to the posterior tip of the pupa. The presence of this attached larval skin, with its characteristic two pairs of long caudal setae and matchstick hairs, provides definitive diagnostic identification of a phlebotomine pupa. The pupal period lasts 6 to 13 days.

#### Lifecycle, Habitat & Host Attachment
*   **Primary Host Range:** Adult males and females feed on plant sap, nectar, and sugary secretions from plants and honeydew. In addition, adult females require a protein blood meal from terrestrial vertebrates (humans, domestic livestock including cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, horses, dogs, cats, wild rodents, gerbils, fat sand rats, rock hyraxes, tree hyraxes, sloths, anteaters, armadillos, opossums, spiny rats, wood rats, birds, reptiles, and amphibians) for ovarian egg maturation.
*   **Developmental Stages:** Complete holometabolous lifecycle: Egg -> 4 Larval Instars -> Pupa (with attached larval skin and caudal setae) -> Adult (Male / Female). Duration from egg to adult emergence ranges from 30 to 60 days under warm tropical conditions, but extends over several months or overwintering periods (as diapausing mature 4th-instar larvae in temperate or arid regions). Females take one or more blood meals per egg batch; a few species are autogenous (capable of maturing a first batch of eggs without a blood meal). Adult lifespan ranges from 2 to 6 weeks.
*   **Attachment Mechanics & Burrowing Behavior:** Sand flies do not burrow into host skin or remain permanently attached to host tissues. Females land on exposed bare skin, slice skin with short stylets, take blood meals for 2 to 5 minutes, and fly off. Biting is strictly nocturnal or crepuscular, peaking at dusk, dawn, and during night hours. However, in dark indoor rooms or shaded tropical forest canopies, biting can occur during overcast daytime hours. Flight behavior is highly characteristic: sand flies are weak fliers that move in short, jerky, hopping flights ("hopping flight") close to the ground, walls, or vegetation, taking frequent short landings before settling on a host. Light winds easily inhibit flight and biting activity. Because of short mouthparts, sand flies cannot bite through clothing; bites concentrate on uncovered skin areas (face, neck, forearms, hands, ankles, feet).
*   **Environmental Survival Off-Host:** During daylight hours, adult sand flies shelter in dark, cool, humid, wind-protected microhabitats: cracks/crevices in mud or brick walls, stone fissures, caves, rodent burrows, termite hills, tree-holes, animal shelters, forest leaf litter, and dark corners inside houses (endophilic species). Dispersal is limited: sand flies usually remain within 100 meters of their larval breeding sites, though flight distances up to 2.2 km have been recorded. In temperate regions, adults die off in autumn and species overwinter as diapausing 4th-instar larvae; in tropical zones, adults occur year-round or display wet/dry season population surges.

#### Pathogenesis & Vector Dynamics
*   **Direct Dermatologic Damage:** Slashing of dermal capillaries by blade-like stylets causes localized mechanical tissue trauma, minor hemorrhage, induration, and intense local pain. Bites produce small, inflamed, indurated wheals (1.0 cm to 2.0 cm in diameter) with central hemorrhagic spots (puncta) at feeding sites.
*   **Hypersensitivity & Immune Response:** Salivary proteins, Maxadilatan, and digestive enzymes injected during feeding elicit immediate (IgE-mediated) and delayed-type (cell-mediated) cutaneous hypersensitivity reactions. In sensitized individuals, bites cause an intensely pruritic, intolerable allergic dermatitis known in the Middle East as **harara** ("harara" / sandfly bite fever). Excoriation from scratching leads to loss of the skin barrier and secondary bacterial infections.
*   **Pathogens Transmitted as a Vector:** Sand flies are exclusive biological vectors for protozoa, bacteria, and arboviruses:
    *   *Leishmania Species (Leishmaniasis):* Biologically transmitted exclusively by female sand flies (*Phlebotomus* in the Old World; *Lutzomyia* and *Psychodopygus* in the New World).
        *   *Parasite Life Cycle in Vector:* Female sand flies ingest intracellular amastigotes (Leishman-Donovan bodies; 2–6 µm, non-flagellated, containing a nucleus and a dark rod-like kinetoplast) inside host [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]] during a blood meal. In the sand fly midgut, amastigotes transform into elongate, flagellated promastigotes (15–20 µm) and multiply by binary fission. Depending on the subgenus, development occurs either in the anterior alimentary tract (suprapylarian; subgenus *Leishmania*) or in the hindgut and midgut (peripylarian; subgenus *Viannia*). Promastigotes differentiate into infective metacyclic promastigotes, which migrate to the anterior midgut, foregut, and mouthparts (hypostome/proboscis) over 4 to 25 days (typically 5 to 7 days). Parasite-produced promastigote secretory gel (PSG) plugs the foregut, inducing the fly to probe repeatedly and regurgitate metacyclic promastigotes directly into the bite wound during subsequent blood meals. Vector competence is mediated by specific polymorphisms in parasite surface lipophosphoglycan (LPG) binding to sand fly midgut lectins.
        *   *Old World Vectors:* *Phlebotomus papatasi* (*L. major*), *Phlebotomus sergenti* (*L. tropica*), *Phlebotomus argentipes* (*L. donovani* in India/Bangladesh), *Phlebotomus orientalis* (*L. donovani* in Sudan), *Phlebotomus duboscqi*, *Phlebotomus ariasi* and *P. perniciosus* (*L. infantum* in the Mediterranean), *Phlebotomus pedifer* and *P. longipes* (*L. aethiopica* in East Africa), *Phlebotomus martini*.
        *   *New World Vectors:* *Lutzomyia longipalpis* species complex (*L. infantum / chagasi* in South America), *Lutzomyia flaviscutellata* (*L. amazonensis*, *L. pifanoi*), *Lutzomyia wellcomei* (*L. braziliensis*), *Lutzomyia umbratilis* (*L. guyanensis*), *Lutzomyia trapidoi*, *L. ylephiletor*, *L. gomezi*, and *Psychodopygus panamensis* (*L. panamensis*), *Lutzomyia olmeca* (*L. mexicana*), *Lutzomyia peruensis* and *L. verrucarum* (*L. peruviana*), *Lutzomyia anthophora* (wood rats in Texas and Arizona).
    *   *[[Bartonella bacilliformis]] (Bartonellosis / Carrión's Disease / Oroya Fever / Verruga Peruana):* Transmitted by *Lutzomyia verrucarum* and *Lutzomyia* species in high inter-Andean valleys (900–3,000 m altitude) of Peru, Ecuador, and Colombia.
    *   *Phleboviruses (Sandfly Fevers / Papataci Fever / Three-Day Fever):* Caused by seven viral serotypes of Phlebovirus transmitted by *Phlebotomus papatasi* and *P. perniciosus* in the Old World, and *Lutzomyia trapidoi* and *L. ylephiletor* in the New World. Transovarial transmission occurs from female sand flies to their progeny. Sandfly fever virus is recognized as a potential bioterrorism agent.
    *   *Vesiculoviruses / Rhabdoviruses:* Chandipura virus (causing acute pediatric [[encephalitis]] in India) and Vesicular stomatitis virus.
*   **Host Defense & Predisposing Factors:** Predisposing risks include living in substandard rural or peri-urban housing (mud walls with cracks, unplastered brick, dirt floors, thatched roofs), sleeping on floors or without fine-mesh bed nets, working near gerbil burrows or stone walls; outdoor forestry, road construction, agricultural clearing, timber cutting, rubber tapping ("chicleros" harvesting chicle latex), military deployments, and eco-tourism in endemic tropical forests.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Presentations
*   **Primary Infestation Presentation:**
    *   *Sandfly Bite Dermatitis / Harara:* Intensely pruritic, painful, indurated erythematous papules or wheals (1.0–2.0 cm) with central hemorrhagic puncta on exposed skin.
    *   *Primary Vector Disease Presentations:*
        *   *Cutaneous Leishmaniasis (CL / Oriental Sore / Baghdad Boil / Delhi Boil / Biskra Button / Aleppo Evil / Chiclero Ulcer / Uta / Pian Bois):* Red papule appearing at the bite site 2 weeks to 2 months post-exposure, enlarging into a crusted, painless ulcer with raised indurated borders. May present as single or multiple lesions, satellite papules, or destructive lesions on ear cartilage (chiclero ulcer).
        *   *Mucocutaneous Leishmaniasis (MCL / Espundia / Nariz de anta):* Late, highly destructive ulceration of nasopharyngeal mucous membranes, erosion of the nasal septum, perforation, and facial mutilation (caused primarily by *L. braziliensis*, *L. panamensis*, *L. guyanensis*).
        *   *Visceral Leishmaniasis (VL / Kala-azar / Dumdum Fever):* Severe systemic infection featuring protracted irregular fever, massive splenomegaly, hepatomegaly, pancytopenia, progressive cachexia, and hypergammaglobulinemia. Post-kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis (PKDL) presents as a late macular, papular, or nodular cutaneous rash following VL cure.
        *   *Diffuse Cutaneous Leishmaniasis (DCL):* Non-ulcerating macules, papules, or nodules spread over the entire body due to specific host cell-mediated immune anergy (caused by *L. aethiopica*, *L. amazonensis*, *L. pifanoi*).
        *   *Bartonellosis (Carrión's Disease):* Biphasic illness featuring an acute life-threatening febrile hemolytic anemia (Oroya fever) followed by chronic eruptive vascular skin nodules (Verruga peruana).
        *   *Sandfly Fever (Papataci Fever):* Acute febrile illness with sudden onset of high fever, severe frontal headache, retro-orbital pain, photophobia, myalgia, arthralgia, nausea, and leukopenia.
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions & Distribution Pattern:** Lesions concentrate on uncovered, exposed skin areas (face, forearms, hands, lower legs, ankles). Pathognomonic clinical signs include: "volcano-like" rolled-edge painless ulcers (CL), destruction of nasal septum / "tapir nose" (MCL), massive splenomegaly with hypergammaglobulinemia (VL), and slate-grey skin hyperpigmentation (kala-azar = "black fever").
*   **Secondary Complications:** Excoriation from scratching causes secondary bacterial pyoderma, impetigo, and [[cellulitis]]. Vector disease complications include fatal bacterial superinfections, severe anemia, facial destruction, total nasal collapse, septicemia, and near 100% mortality in untreated visceral leishmaniasis or acute Oroya fever.
*   **Laboratory & Dermoscopic Findings:**
    *   *Entomological Identification:* Minute size (<5.0 mm), body/wings densely covered in erect hairs, lanceolate wings held erect in a 40°–60° "V"-shape at rest, 16-segmented non-plumose antennae, 5-segmented drooping palps, double-branched 2nd wing vein. Larva: matchstick hairs, 2 pairs of caudal setae. Pupa: attached larval skin with caudal setae.
    *   *Parasitological Diagnostics:* Demonstration of intracellular Leishman-Donovan bodies (amastigotes, 2–6 µm, oval, containing a nucleus and rod-like kinetoplast) in Giemsa-stained tissue scrapings, fine-needle aspirates, or punch biopsies of ulcer edges (CL/MCL) or [[spleen]]/[[bone marrow]]/lymph node aspirates (VL); culture on NNN (Novy-MacNeal-Nicolle) medium yielding motile promastigotes; positive Montenegro leishmanin skin test (in CL/MCL; negative in VL and DCL); rK39 rapid immunochromatographic strip test for VL; blood films showing intraerythrocytic *[[Bartonella bacilliformis]]* bacilli during Oroya fever.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Geographic Distribution:** Worldwide in tropical, subtropical, and warm temperate zones where ambient temperatures exceed 15.6°C for at least 3 months of the year (between 50°N and 40°S latitude, from below sea level to 2,800+ meters in the Andes):
    *   *Old World Genera (Phlebotomus, Sergentomyia):* Mediterranean basin, Middle East, North Africa, Sub-Saharan Africa, Central Asia, Indian subcontinent, Southern Europe.
    *   *New World Genera (Lutzomyia, Psychodopygus):* Central America, South America, Mexico, and focal areas of the southern United States (Texas, Arizona).
*   **Transmission Route:** Biological vector transmission via the bite of an infected female sand fly depositing metacyclic promastigotes or *Bartonella* bacteria into skin wounds during pool-feeding. Transmission cycles:
    *   *Anthroponotic Cycle (Human-Vector-Human):* *L. donovani* in India/Bangladesh (kala-azar) and *L. tropica* in urban centers (e.g., epidemic CL in Kabul, Afghanistan).
    *   *Zoonotic Cycle (Animal-Vector-Human):* *L. infantum* (main reservoir: domestic dogs, foxes), *L. major* (reservoirs: desert gerbils *Rhombomys opimus*, fat sand rats *Psammomys obesus*), *L. aethiopica* (reservoirs: rock hyraxes *Procavia capensis*), *L. braziliensis* / *L. mexicana* / *L. guyanensis* / *L. panamensis* (reservoirs: forest sloths, anteaters, armadillos, spiny rats, opossums, wood rats).
    *   *Non-Vector Routes:* Rare transmission via blood transfusion, organ transplantation, congenital/vertical transmission, needle sharing in drug users, laboratory accidents, or direct contact with open sores ("leishmanization").
*   **Institutional & Crowding Risks:** High risk in impoverished rural villages, mud-plastered dwellings, displaced person/refugee camps (e.g., Kabul, South Sudan), agricultural settlements along forest fringes, mining and timber camps, and military deployments.
*   **Public Health & Outbreak Management:** Integrated Vector Control strategies:
    1.  *Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS):* Spraying indoor walls, animal shelters, chicken coops, and stone masonry with long-lasting synthetic pyrethroids (lambda-cyhalothrin, deltamethrin) or DDT.
    2.  *Insecticide-Treated Materials:* Using long-lasting insecticidal bed nets (LLINs / Olyset fine-mesh nets impregnated with [[permethrin]] or deltamethrin) to prevent nocturnal biting (sand flies can crawl through coarse standard mosquito nets unless fine-meshed or treated with pyrethroids).
    3.  *Environmental & Reservoir Control:* Destruction of rodent burrows near human settlements (deep plowing of gerbil burrows in Central Asia); plastering wall cracks and fissures with cement; applying deltamethrin-impregnated dog collars or spot-on insecticides to domestic dogs (for *L. infantum*); prompt active case detection and treatment to eliminate the human reservoir.

#### Advanced Diagnostics
*   **Direct Examination & Skin Scrapings:** Stereomicroscopic or low-power microscopic identification of wild-caught adult flies, matchstick-haired larvae, or pupae. Microscopic examination of Giemsa-stained impression smears or tissue scrapings from the active, indurated border of skin ulcers to identify intracellular amastigotes inside [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]].
*   **Dermoscopy Features:** Entomodermoscopy (x10 to x20 magnification) of an acute sand fly bite demonstrates a central punctate hemorrhagic core/crust surrounded by an edematous pale wheal and an outer erythematous halo. Dermoscopy of cutaneous leishmaniasis lesions demonstrates tear-like vessels, yellow structures, hyperkeratosis, central ulceration, and a "white starburst" pattern.
*   **Biopsy Findings:** Histopathological examination (H&E and Giemsa stains) of skin ulcer edge or mucosal biopsy shows hyperkeratosis, acanthosis, dermal edema, pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia, and a dense dermal lymphohistiocytic and granulomatous infiltrate containing epithelioid cells, [[Plasma Cell & Memory B Cell|plasma cells]], multinucleated giant cells, and numerous intracellular amastigotes (Leishman-Donovan bodies) within [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophage]] cytoplasm. Amastigotes measure 2.0 µm to 6.0 µm, displaying a characteristic diagnostic "dot-and-dash" appearance (spherical nucleus and dark rod-like kinetoplast). In visceral leishmaniasis, liver biopsy shows marked [[Monocyte and Macrophage|Kupffer cell]] hyperplasia packed with amastigotes, and [[spleen]]/[[bone marrow]] biopsies demonstrate massive reticuloendothelial cell hyperplasia laden with parasites.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Eradication
*   **Topical Scabicides & Pediculicides:** Topical scabicides or pediculicides applied to host skin are ineffective against sand flies. Personal protection relies on topical chemical insect repellents applied to exposed skin: DEET (N,N-diethyl-m-toluamide, 20% to 50%), Picaridin, IR3535, or neem oil. [[Permethrin]] 0.5% spray applied to clothing, uniforms, and fine-mesh bed nets provides durable contact repellent and insecticidal action.
*   **Oral Systemic Treatments:**
    *   *Symptomatic Bite Relief:* Topical calamine lotion, hydrocortisone 1% cream, or oral H1-antihistamines (cetirizine, loratadine, diphenhydramine) to relieve pruritus and allergic inflammation.
    *   *Systemic Chemotherapy for Transmitted Leishmaniasis & Bartonellosis:*
        *   **Visceral Leishmaniasis (VL):** [[Liposomal Amphotericin B]] (first-line drug of choice; single dose 10 mg/kg or multi-dose regimens); oral [[Miltefosine]] (2.5 mg/kg/day for 28 days); [[Paromomycin]] (aminosidine; 15 mg/kg/day IM for 21 days); [[Sodium Stibogluconate]] / Meglumine Antimonate (pentavalent antimonials; 20 mg Sb/kg/day IV/IM for 28 days, though high resistance exists in India).
        *   **Cutaneous & Mucocutaneous Leishmaniasis (CL/MCL):** Pentavalent antimonials (Intralesional or IV/IM [[Sodium Stibogluconate]] / Meglumine Antimonate 20 mg/kg/day for 20 to 28 days); oral [[Miltefosine]]; Liposomal [[Amphotericin B]]; [[Pentamidine]] isethionate.
        *   **Bartonellosis (Carrión's Disease):** [[Ciprofloxacin]] or [[Doxycycline]] for adults; [[Chloramphenicol]] plus beta-lactams for acute Oroya fever; [[Rifampin]] or [[Azithromycin]] for Verruga peruana.
        *   **Sandfly Fever:** Supportive symptomatic care (antipyretics, analgesics, fluid hydration).
*   **Treatment Nuances & Re-application Schedules:** Topical repellents must be reapplied every 4 to 8 hours during outdoor exposure. Bed nets MUST be fine-meshed or impregnated with synthetic pyrethroids ([[permethrin]]/deltamethrin) because tiny sand flies can physically crawl through standard coarse mosquito netting. Deltamethrin dog collars must be replaced every 6 months.
*   **Environmental & Laundry Decontamination Protocols:** Wearing light-colored, long-sleeved shirts and long trousers. Treating clothing with [[permethrin]]. Sealing window openings with fine-mesh insect screening impregnated with pyrethroids. Plastering mud wall cracks and sealing stone crevices in dwellings. Moving livestock pens and chicken coops away from sleeping quarters.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "V-shaped wing posture" (held erect at 40°–60° angle at rest), "Fuzzy / moth-like appearance" (dense body and wing hairs), "Hopping flight" (short, jerky flights), "Double branching of wing vein 2", "Matchstick hairs" (barbed larval setae with enlarged tips), "Caudal setae" (2 pairs on 4th-instar larva and attached pupal exuviae), "Maxadilatan" (most potent vasodilator peptide known, in *L. longipalpis* saliva), "Promastigote secretory gel (PSG)" (foregut plug forcing repeated probing), "Leishman-Donovan bodies" (amastigotes with nucleus and rod kinetoplast), "Harara" (severe allergic sandfly bite dermatitis), "Oriental sore / Baghdad boil / Delhi boil", "Chiclero ulcer" (destructive ear cartilage lesions in chicle rubber harvesters), "Espundia / Nariz de anta" (mucocutaneous facial destruction), "Montenegro leishmanin skin test", "Kala-azar" ("black fever" in Hindi), "Canine reservoir / Deltamethrin collar".
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   Only adult female sand flies bite and take blood meals; adult males feed exclusively on plant sugars and fruit nectar and are completely incapable of biting.
    *   Sand flies hold their wings erect in a characteristic "V"-shape (40°–60° angle) over their thorax at rest, unlike non-biting psychodid moth flies which fold wings flat or roof-like over the body.
    *   Standard coarse mosquito bed nets fail to exclude sand flies because of their minute size (<3.0 mm); bed nets MUST be fine-meshed or impregnated with pyrethroid insecticides ([[permethrin]]/deltamethrin) to prevent sand flies from crawling through.
    *   Sand fly larvae are strictly terrestrial/semi-aquatic scavengers residing in humid soil, leaf litter, and burrows; they do NOT develop in open water, streams, or aquatic ponds.
    *   Phlebotomine pupae retain the shed 4th-instar larval skin with its two pairs of long caudal setae attached to their posterior abdominal tip, which serves as a definitive pupal identification mark.
    *   In *Leishmania* transmission, infected sand flies develop a parasite-secreted promastigote secretory gel (PSG) plug in their foregut that blocks the lumen, forcing the fly to probe repeatedly and regurgitate infective metacyclic promastigotes directly into the host bite wound.
