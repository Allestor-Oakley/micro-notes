### Pyroglyphidae: House-dust Mites

#### Arthropod Anatomy & Physical Morphology
*   **Adult Physical Features & Size:** Very small, microscopic arachnids measuring approximately 0.3 mm in length. Body is oval, dorsoventrally flattened, translucent to creamy-white, with a finely striated cuticle. The body is divided into an anterior gnathosoma (bearing mouthparts) and a posterior idiosoma (comprising the podosoma and opisthosoma). Adults possess 4 pairs of legs (8 legs) equipped with fine tarsal claws and leg setae that allow them to cling firmly to carpet fibers and fabric strands. Sclerotization is minimal, and true eyes are absent.
*   **Mouthparts & Feeding Apparatus:** Gnathosoma features biting/chewing chelicerae and pedipalps adapted for scraping, crushing, and ingesting small particulate organic debris. Non-haematophagous (non-blood-sucking) and non-parasitic scavengers. Feed primarily on discarded human skin scales (dander), fungi growing on mattresses and floors, mold, fungal spores, semen, pollen grains, feathers, animal dander, and general organic debris.
*   **Egg / Nit Morphology & Attachment:** Eggs are minute, spherical to oval, translucent-white structures. Laid singly by female mites (1–3 eggs per day per female, totaling 40–80 eggs over a female lifespan) among bedclothes, mattresses, carpets, floor cracks, and dust particles. Eggs are non-sticky, non-attached to host tissues, and hatch in 6–12 days.
*   **Immature Stages:** Newly hatched 6-legged (3 pairs) larvae emerge from eggs and actively feed on organic debris. Larvae moult to give rise to the protonymph (first 8-legged nymphal stage), which then moults into the tritonymph (second 8-legged nymphal stage) before becoming an adult. Complete immature development (egg -> larva -> protonymph -> tritonymph -> adult) takes approximately 3–4 weeks. The protonymph stage possesses remarkable desiccation resistance and can survive for months at low relative humidities.

#### Lifecycle, Habitat & Host Attachment
*   **Primary Host Range:** Non-parasitic on living host tissue; free-living synanthropic commensals associated with human habitations, domestic pets (dogs, cats), and birds.
*   **Developmental Stages:** Four life cycle stages (holometabolous-like acarine developmental stages): Egg, Larva (6-legged), Nymph (protonymph and tritonymph, 8-legged), and Adult (8-legged). Female oviposition averages 1–3 eggs/day with total fecundity of 40–80 eggs. Eggs hatch after 6–12 days. Complete life cycle duration is 3–4 weeks (21–28 days) under optimal warm, humid conditions. Adult lifespan is approximately 1–2 months.
*   **Attachment Mechanics & Burrowing Behavior:** Do not burrow into human skin or attach to living host tissue. Cling tightly to carpet fibers, fabric threads, mattress ticking, pillow feathers, clothing, and soft toys using tarsal claws and leg setae.
*   **Environmental Survival Off-Host:** Entire life cycle occurs off-host in indoor microenvironments (beds, mattresses, carpets, upholstered furniture, bedroom closets/wardrobes, soft toys). Beds represent the primary and most important breeding site. High relative humidity (65–70%) is essential for mite survival, feeding, and reproduction; however, protonymphs can survive for months in dry environments (<50% RH). Killed by temperatures exceeding 55 °C, prolonged freezing, or desiccation.

#### Pathogenesis & Vector Dynamics
*   **Direct Dermatologic Damage:** No direct mechanical bite, puncture, tissue invasion, or burrowing damage. Direct contact with mites, fecal pellets, or secretions induces allergic contact dermatitis, atopic eczema (childhood eczema), pruritus, and erythematous skin reactions in sensitized individuals.
*   **Hypersensitivity & Immune Response:** Pathogenesis is driven entirely by Type I IgE-mediated [[Type I Hypersensitivity|immediate hypersensitivity]] reactions triggered by potent protein allergens contained within mite bodies, fecal pellets, and secretions (e.g., Der p 1, Der f 1). Inhalation or cutaneous contact with airborne mite feces and debris induces specific IgE antibody production, [[mast cell]] degranulation, histamine release, airway inflammation, bronchial hyperreactivity, allergic rhinitis, acute asthmatic paroxysms, allergic [[conjunctivitis]], and eczema flare-ups.
*   **Pathogens Transmitted as a Vector:** N/A
*   **Host Defense & Predisposing Factors:** Predisposing factors include genetic [[Type I Hypersensitivity|atopy]], high indoor relative humidity (>65%), poorly ventilated residential spaces, wall-to-wall carpeting, old mattresses, sleeping with soft toys, presence of pet dander, and settled indoor dust in homes and day care centers/schools. Mite density risk thresholds: typical dust contains ~300 mites/g; ideal mattress dust contains ~5,000 to 15,600 mites/g; densities >100 mites/g represent a risk factor for allergen sensitization (asthma, rhinitis, [[conjunctivitis]], eczema); densities >500 mites/g represent a major risk factor for acute asthma attacks in sensitized individuals.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Presentations
*   **Primary Infestation Presentation:** Allergic Asthma (wheezing, dyspnea, shortness of breath, chest tightness, nocturnal cough), Allergic Rhinitis (sneezing, rhinorrhea, nasal congestion, nasal pruritus), Atopic Eczema (pruritic, erythematous, papular, or vesicular skin lesions, dry scaling plaques, particularly in children), and Allergic [[Conjunctivitis]] (ocular pruritus, lacrimation, conjunctival injection, eyelid edema).
*   **Pathognomonic Lesions & Distribution Pattern:** No pathognomonic mite burrows or bite marks. Presentation consists of diffuse allergic eczema on flexural skin surfaces, face, or trunk, and allergic respiratory symptoms triggered by indoor dust exposure or bed making (which resuspends allergen-laden mite feces).
*   **Secondary Complications:** Excoriation from scratching eczema lesions leading to secondary bacterial pyoderma ([[Staphylococcus aureus]], [[Streptococcus pyogenes]]), impetigo, and [[cellulitis]]. Severe chronic refractory asthma, airway remodeling, status asthmaticus, and sleep disruption.
*   **Laboratory & Dermoscopic Findings:** Laboratory testing demonstrates elevated serum total IgE and specific IgE [[Antibodies, Immunoglobulins|antibodies]] against Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus or D. farinae (via RAST or ImmunoCAP assays), positive immediate skin prick test (wheal-and-flare reaction) to house-dust mite extracts, and peripheral blood eosinophilia. Dermoscopy of patient skin shows non-specific eczematous changes, erythema, and excoriation without burrows or parasites. Microscopic examination of house dust or mattress scrapings mounted in 10% KOH or mineral oil reveals 0.3 mm 8-legged oval mites with striated cuticles.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Geographic Distribution:** Cosmopolitan / Worldwide. Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus (European house-dust mite) occurs in Europe, North America, and worldwide. Dermatophagoides farinae (American house-dust mite) occurs in the USA, central/southern Europe, and worldwide. Euroglyphus maynei has an almost worldwide distribution.
*   **Transmission Route:** Non-communicable parasite. Exposure occurs via inhalation or direct skin contact with airborne live/dead mites, fecal particles, and structural fragments resuspended into dust storms during bed making, sweeping, or vacuuming.
*   **Institutional & Crowding Risks:** High prevalence in residential homes, carpeted day care facilities, schools, college dormitories, hotels, and healthcare facilities with high indoor humidity, upholstered furniture, and textile bedding.
*   **Public Health & Outbreak Management:** Focuses on indoor environmental allergen mitigation, maintaining indoor humidity below 50% RH, and integrated pest/dust management in homes, day care centers, and educational institutions.

#### Advanced Diagnostics
*   **Direct Examination & Skin Scrapings:** House dust samples collected via high-power vacuuming are sieved and examined microscopically in 10% KOH, lactic acid, or mineral oil to quantify mite density (mites per gram of dust). Skin scrapings from patients are negative for mites (differentiating dust mite [[Type I Hypersensitivity|allergy]] from Scabies / [[Sarcoptes scabiei]] or Demodex infestation).
*   **Dermoscopy Features:** Dermoscopy of patient skin demonstrates non-specific eczematous inflammation, epidermal scaling, and excoriation without burrows, delta-wing signs, or embedded parasites.
*   **Biopsy Findings:** Skin biopsy of atopic eczema lesions reveals hyperkeratosis, acanthosis, spongiosis, epidermal edema, and a superficial perivascular dermal inflammatory infiltrate rich in lymphocytes, histiocytes, and eosinophils, with complete absence of intralesional parasites.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Eradication
*   **Topical Scabicides & Pediculicides:** N/A
*   **Oral Systemic Treatments:** Oral antihistamines (cetirizine, loratadine, diphenhydramine) for allergic pruritus, rhinitis, and urticaria. Inhaled bronchodilators (albuterol) and inhaled corticosteroids for allergic asthma. Anti-parasitic agents ([[ivermectin]], [[albendazole]]) have no role.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Re-application Schedules:** Medical management addresses host IgE-mediated inflammatory symptoms, whereas environmental measures target mite reduction. Chemical acaricide sprays (benzyl benzoate, [[permethrin]], tannic acid) require re-application every 2–3 months to maintain reduced allergen levels.
*   **Environmental & Laundry Decontamination Protocols:** Washing bed linen and sheets in hot water above 55 °C or dry-cleaning reliably kills mites. Leaving electric blankets switched on at maximum temperature for 6 hours or more during the day kills mites in bedding. Enclosing mattresses, box springs, and pillows in plastic or Gore-Tex bed bags prevents mite infestation and blocks allergen escape. Treating carpets and sheets with benzyl benzoate, [[permethrin]] sprays, or sprays containing benzyl alcohol and tannic acid (which denatures protein allergens) reduces live mites and active allergens. Treating soft toys by freezing in a freezer, hot tumble drying, or washing with eucalyptus oil (0.2%–0.4%) significantly reduces live mites. Maintaining indoor relative humidity below 50% inhibits mite survival and breeding. Vacuuming carpets has limited effect on live mites (which cling firmly to fibers with tarsal claws) and may exacerbate allergies by generating mite-infested dust storms unless high-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) vacuum cleaners are used. Meta-analyses (Gøtzsche et al. 1998) indicate that individual physical or chemical control measures alone do not significantly reduce clinical asthma or [[Type I Hypersensitivity|allergy]] outcomes without comprehensive clinical management.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Dust storms during bed making" (resuspension of mite fecal pellets and bodies), "Protonymph desiccation resistance" (survival for months at low humidity), "Gore-Tex bed covers" (impermeable barrier for mattresses/pillows), "100 mites/g and 500 mites/g thresholds" (risk factors for sensitization vs acute asthma attacks), "Eucalyptus oil 0.2–0.4%" (soft toy decontamination), "Electric blanket 6-hour heat treatment" (thermal mite eradication), "Tannic acid allergen denaturant" (chemical inactivation of protein allergens).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** While most medically important mites actively bite, burrow, or parasitize living host tissue (e.g., Sarcoptes, Demodex, Trombiculidae), Pyroglyphidae house-dust mites are free-living, non-parasitic environmental scavengers that cause human disease exclusively through inhaled or contact protein allergens. While vacuuming removes immature flea stages, vacuuming carpets is ineffective for live house-dust mites (which cling firmly to carpet fibers with tarsal claws) and may exacerbate allergic symptoms by stirring up airborne dust storms unless HEPA-filtered. While acaricides effectively kill mites, acaricide-impregnated mattress covers may themselves trigger allergic reactions in sensitive individuals.
