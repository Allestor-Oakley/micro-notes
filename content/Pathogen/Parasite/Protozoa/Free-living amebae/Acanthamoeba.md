### Acanthamoeba spp.

#### Protozoan Biology & Microscopic Morphology
*   **Trophozoite Stage Features:** Uninucleate amoeboid form measuring 14 to 50 µm in diameter (usual range 15 to 45 µm, average diameter ~30 µm). Characterized by the presence of fine, spiky, thorn-like or spine-like hyaline pseudopodial projections called acanthapodia extending from the cell surface. The single nucleus contains a prominent, large, dense, centrally placed nucleolus/karyosome with no peripheral nuclear chromatin. The cytoplasm is finely granular and contains organelles typical of higher eukaryotic cells: smooth and rough endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi complex, free ribosomes, mitochondria with tubular cristae, digestive/food vacuoles, and a single contractile vacuole that pulsates (disappears and reappears) at regular intervals of 45 to 50 seconds. Feeds on bacteria, algae, and yeasts in the environment via phagocytosis using food-cups (amoebastomes) or pinocytosis of liquid nutrients. Lacks any flagellate stage in its life cycle.
*   **Cyst / Oocyst Stage Features:** Uninucleate, double-walled encysted form ranging in size from 6 to 30 µm (typically 10 to 25 µm; 10 to 20 µm in some species). The nucleus retains the large central karyosome seen in the trophozoite. Possesses a double cyst wall consisting of a proteinaceous, slightly wrinkled outer wall (ectocyst) and an inner endocyst made of cellulose that may be polyhedral, stellate (star-shaped), polygonal, hexagonal, oval, or round. The double walls meet at pores called ostioles, which are plugged with mucopolysaccharide. The cellulose-containing endocyst stains positive with Periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) (stains red), Gomori's methenamine silver (stains black), and Calcofluor white chemofluorescent dye. Cysts form under adverse environmental conditions (e.g., desiccation, food exhaustion) and are extremely resistant to biocides, desiccation (can survive desiccation for >20 years), antibiotics, chlorination, and low temperatures (0 to 2°C; can remain viable in water at 4°C for 24 to 25 years).
*   **Intracellular Morphologies:** N/A
*   **Locomotion Mechanics:** Locomotion is accomplished by extending and retracting fine, tapering, spiky hyaline pseudopodia termed acanthapodia. Progressive directional movement is slow and usually not prominent in direct wet mount preparations. Does not form flagella or undergo enflagellation under any environmental conditions.

#### Lifecycle & Transmission
*   **Definitive Host:** None. Acanthamoeba is a free-living amphizoic organism capable of surviving indefinitely in nature without a host cycle. Humans and animals act as accidental or opportunistic hosts.
*   **Intermediate Host & Vector Dynamics:** None required. Contact lenses and storage cases serve as mechanical vectors that transport amebae onto the corneal surface. Acanthamoeba also serves as an environmental host, reservoir, and incubator ("crib") for various pathogenic bacterial and viral endosymbionts (e.g., [[Legionella pneumophila]], [[Pseudomonas aeruginosa]], [[Escherichia coli]] O157, [[Mycobacterium avium]] / nontuberculous mycobacteria, [[Campylobacter jejuni]], [[Listeria monocytogenes]], [[Vibrio cholerae]], [[Francisella tularensis]], [[Burkholderia cepacia|Burkholderia]] spp., Mimivirus, and Pandoravirus inopinatum).
*   **Transmission Route:** Direct ocular inoculation via contact with contaminated water, soil, dust, or contaminated contact lens care products (e.g., homemade saline, unsterile lens cases). Inhalation or aspiration of airborne cysts/trophozoites in dust or aerosols through the nasal passages or lower respiratory tract. Direct entry into broken skin, skin ulcers, surgical wounds, corneal abrasions, or human/child bite wounds. Hematogenous dissemination from primary skin, sinus, or pulmonary lesions to the central nervous system (CNS) and other visceral organs.
*   **Infective Stage & Portal of Entry:** Both trophozoites and double-walled cysts are infective. Portals of entry include the eye (corneal epithelium/stroma), the skin (cuts, abrasions, ulcers, bite wounds), and the respiratory tract (nasal neuroepithelium, sinuses, lungs).
*   **Diagnostic Stage & Specimen Source:** Both trophozoites and cysts are present in diagnostic clinical specimens. Specimen sources include corneal scrapings, corneal biopsy, contact lenses and lens care solutions/cases, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), brain biopsy or autopsy tissue, skin biopsy/scrapings, skin ulcer exudates or pus, bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid, nasal/pharyngeal swabs, ear discharge, and feces.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Tissue Tropism & Cellular Invasion:** Demonstrates strong tropism for the corneal epithelium/stroma, central nervous system (cerebral cortex, subcortical white matter, temporal, parietal, and occipital lobes, basal ganglia, cerebellum, midbrain, brainstem), skin and subcutaneous tissue, lungs, nasal mucosa/sinuses, and vascular endothelium. Adhesion to host tissues is initiated by a 136-kDa mannose-binding protein (MBP) on the ameba surface binding to mannose-containing glycoproteins on host epithelial cells. Attachment to extracellular matrix proteins (collagen, fibronectin, laminin) is further mediated by a >200-kDa parasite adhesin and a laminin-binding protein. Adhered trophozoites destabilize epithelial tight junctions and migrate through paracellular spaces, disrupting the corneal barrier and blood-brain barrier.
*   **Cytopathic Effects & Tissue Destruction:** Produces tissue damage through both direct/contact-dependent cytolysis and indirect enzyme secretion. Secretes potent proteolytic enzymes including alkaline cysteine proteinases (more active in pathogenic strains), serine proteases, metalloproteases, and cytotoxic proteases. These proteases degrade extracellular matrix components (type I collagen, elastin, fibrinogen, laminin), digest basement membranes, cause cytolysis of corneal and neural tissue, and induce apoptosis/phagocytosis of host keratocytes and epithelial cells using amebostomes (food-cups). Generates reactive oxygen species/free radicals that destroy connective tissue. In the CNS, causes chronic granulomatous inflammation, focal necrosis, hemorrhagic necrosis, vasculitis, and perivascular cuffing of blood vessels by trophozoites and cysts. In the cornea, pathogenesis progresses through 4 stages: Stage 1 (epithelial breach without inflammation), Stage 2 (keratocyte depletion via phagocytosis/apoptosis), Stage 3 (PMN and [[macrophage]] inflammatory infiltration), and Stage 4 (stromal collagen thinning, necrosis, and ring infiltrate formation).
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** Transforms into a double-walled resistant cyst stage inside host tissues and environmental niches to survive antimicrobial agents and host immune mechanisms. Masks cell-surface antigens from cellular immune recognition, thereby suppressing [[macrophage]] function and preventing neutrophil-mediated killing. Activates the alternative complement pathway but resists complement-mediated cellular lysis by releasing transport-dependent extracellular matrix molecules and expressing complement-inhibitory surface proteins. Endosymbionts harbored within amebae enhance amebic pathogenic potential and corneal cytotoxicity.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Host defense relies on humoral immunity (serum IgM and IgG antibodies) and complement activation that promote opsonization and phagocytosis by neutrophils and [[Macrophage|macrophages]]. Ameba-specific IgA in tears protects against corneal infection by blocking amebic adherence to corneal epithelium. Neutrophils activated by lymphokines release lysosomal enzymes and reactive oxygen intermediates to lyse amebae. Predisposing factors for GAE and disseminated acanthamoebiasis include low CD4+ [[T Cell|T-cell]] counts (AIDS), organ transplantation (kidney, lung), cancer chemotherapy, radiation therapy, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), systemic corticosteroid therapy, chronic alcoholism, diabetes mellitus, renal failure, hematologic malignancies, and severe malnutrition. In AIDS patients with GAE, the typical granulomatous inflammatory response is minimal or entirely absent due to severe cellular immunodeficiency. In immunocompetent hosts, Acanthamoeba primarily causes keratitis (linked to contact lenses or ocular trauma) and rarely GAE.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Complications
*   **Acute Clinical Presentation:**
    - Acanthamoeba Keratitis (AK): Presents with severe, excruciating ocular pain (often disproportionate to early clinical findings), photophobia, intense lacrimation/tearing, foreign body sensation, decreased visual acuity, redness, conjunctival injection, corneal epithelial defects/ulceration, iritis, scleritis, and anterior chamber hypopyon.
    - Acute-onset GAE: Less common acute presentation characterized by sudden fever, severe headache, neck stiffness/pain, lethargy, and rapid progression.
*   **Chronic & Extra-Intestinal Disease:**
    - Granulomatous Amebic Encephalitis (GAE): Subacute or chronic, slowly progressive CNS infection with an unknown incubation period (weeks to months). Symptoms include persistent headache, low-grade fever, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, drowsiness, confusion, mental status changes, lethargy, stiff neck, seizures, ataxia, hemiparesis, sensory deficits, cranial nerve palsies, signs of increased intracranial pressure, cerebral edema, herniation, and death. Duration from onset to death ranges from 7 days to 5 months (survival usually ~1 month once neurological symptoms appear). Overall mortality is ~75% without CNS disease and 100% when cutaneous disease is accompanied by CNS involvement.
    - Cutaneous Acanthamoebiasis: Presents as hard, firm erythematous papules, subcutaneous nodules, purulent draining papulonodules, and chronic nonhealing indurated skin ulcers or abscesses. May occur on the face, trunk, arms, legs, or feet.
    - Amebic Sinusitis & Nasal Lesions: Chronic nasal congestion, purulent nasal discharge, sinus pain/tenderness, nasal septal ulcers, otitis, and intramuscular abscesses.
    - Disseminated Disease: Multi-organ involvement (skin, lungs, sinuses, brain, kidneys, bone) predominantly occurring in immunocompromised patients (e.g., AIDS, transplant recipients).
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:**
    - Ocular: Characteristic 360° or paracentral stromal ring infiltrate in the cornea and radial keratoneuritis (radial perineural infiltrates along corneal nerves) accompanied by severe ocular pain.
    - Histopathologic/Diagnostic: Demonstration of BOTH motile trophozoites (with spiky acanthapodia) AND double-walled cysts (wrinkled ectocyst, polygonal endocyst) simultaneously in tissue biopsy or CSF sediment (differentiating Acanthamoeba from [[Naegleria fowleri]], which exhibits only trophozoites in tissue).
*   **Host Immune Response & Laboratory Findings:**
    - CSF Findings in GAE: Lymphocytic pleocytosis (leukocyte count elevated, though in [[HIV]]/AIDS patients CSF may reveal zero cells), elevated protein concentration, and normal or slightly decreased glucose concentration. Gram stain is negative for bacteria, though background debris can cause false-positive interpretations.
    - Peripheral Blood: Mild to moderate leukocytosis in GAE/disseminated infection.
    - Serology: Serum IgG/IgM antibodies are present in many healthy individuals due to environmental exposure, rendering serologic antibody testing unreliable for active diagnosis. Ameba-specific tear IgA levels are significantly reduced in patients with Acanthamoeba keratitis compared to controls.

#### Epidemiology & Exposures
*   **Geographic Endemicity:** Worldwide distribution. Isolates identified across North America, Europe, Asia, Australia, Africa, and South America. The incidence of Acanthamoeba keratitis in the United Kingdom is 15 times higher than in the United States and 7 times higher than in Holland.
*   **Environmental Reservoir & Water Systems:** Ubiquitous in natural and built environments. Isolated from soil, freshwater lakes, ponds, rivers, hot springs, brackish water, sea water, air, dust, vegetation, and home aquariums. Common in man-made domestic water systems: tap water, residential plumbing, bottled mineral/distilled water, HVAC filters, and humidifiers. Found in hospital environments on medical equipment, eye wash units, hydrotherapy baths, dental irrigation units, and hemodialysis machines. Highly resistant to standard swimming-pool levels of chlorine.
*   **High-Risk Activities & Dietary Exposures:**
    - Contact Lens Wear: Leading risk factor for keratitis. High-risk practices include using homemade saline solutions, unapproved commercial lens cleaning solutions, wearing contact lenses while swimming, showering, or bathing, poor lens hygiene, and failing to clean storage cases.
    - Ocular Trauma: Agriculture/rural corneal trauma involving vegetative material, soil, or mud (major cause of AK in non-contact lens wearers in developing nations). Pre-existing corneal abrasions or prior incisional keratotomy.
    - Nasal Irrigation: Rinsing nasal sinuses with tap water or contaminated unsterilized saline using neti pots.
    - Environmental Exposures: Exposure to dusty environments (e.g., Harmattan season), gardening, or soil handling.
    - Dietary Exposures: N/A.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:**
    - Biosafety: Culture procedures for free-living amebae represent a high BSL-2 risk. Personnel must wear gloves, closed laboratory coats, use capped centrifuge tubes, and perform procedures inside a biological safety cabinet to prevent skin, ocular, or aerosol exposure.
    - Public Health Guidance for Lens Wearers: Educate wearers to (1) remove lenses before showering, swimming, or hot tub use, (2) wash and dry hands thoroughly before handling lenses, (3) use approved commercial disinfection solutions, (4) regularly scrub case interiors, expose cases to hot water (at least 70°C), and replace cases frequently.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Microscopy & Stains:**
    - Direct Wet Mount: Microscopic examination of corneal scrapings, CSF sediment, or skin scrapings under a slide with coverslip (not in a counting chamber, where amebae mimic leukocytes) using reduced light or phase-contrast optics to visualize motile trophozoites with acanthapodia and contractile vacuoles.
    - Permanent Histologic Stains:
      - Trichrome Stain & Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E): Visualizes trophozoites (large central karyosome, cytoplasmic vacuoles) and double-walled cysts in tissue sections.
      - Giemsa Stain & Wright's Stain: Stains amebic cytoplasm blue and nucleolus dense red/violet.
      - Calcofluor White Stain: Chemofluorescent dye that binds specifically to cellulose in the endocyst wall, emitting bright green/blue fluorescence under UV light. Trophozoites do not fluoresce.
      - Periodic Acid-Schiff (PAS): Stains the cyst wall red.
      - Gomori's Methenamine Silver: Stains the cyst wall black.
      - Gram Stain: Cysts appear as double-walled structures; beware of false-positive Gram stain interpretation where background cellular debris is misidentified as bacteria.
      - Lactophenol Cotton Blue (LPCB): Delineates cyst structure in corneal scrapings.
    - In Vivo Confocal Microscopy: Slit-lamp-like optical device enabling real-time, non-invasive visualization of polygonal double-walled cysts in corneal stroma with 88.3% sensitivity and 91.1% specificity.
*   **Concentration & Stool O&P Techniques:**
    - Stool O&P: Stool examination is generally N/A for primary diagnosis (though amebae may rarely be recovered in feces).
    - Specimen Concentration & Filtration: Filtration of fluid specimens (water, transport saline, CSF) through 0.22 µm or 5.0 µm cellulose acetate/nitrate membrane filters yields significantly higher amebic recovery (100% for 10 parasites, 65% for 2 parasites) compared to centrifugation. Centrifugation of CSF or fluid aspirates should be performed at low speeds (250 to 500 × g for 5 to 10 min) to avoid destroying trophozoite morphology. Cytospin preparations of CSF or BAL fluid fixed and stained with trichrome or H&E facilitate identification.
    - Culture Methods:
      - Non-Nutrient Agar (NNA) with Page's Ameba Saline (or PBS) coated with a live or killed bacterial lawn ([[Escherichia coli]] or Enterobacter aerogenes). Inoculate corneal scrapings, tissue fragments, or CSF sediment onto agar; amebae feed on bacteria, multiply, and form clear "tracks" or trails on the lawn observable under 10x objective. Incubate CNS/tissue cultures at 35 to 37°C and corneal cultures at 30°C; observe daily for 10 to 14 days. Encystment occurs after 4 to 5 days.
      - Commercial Media: Growth achieved on Buffered Charcoal-Yeast Extract (BCYE) agar and Tryptic Soy Agar (TSA) supplemented with 5% horse, rabbit, or sheep blood.
      - Axenic Liquid Media: Grown in cell-free Peptone-Yeast Extract-Glucose (PYG) liquid medium or serum-casein-glucose-yeast extract medium.
      - Tissue Culture: Inoculation onto mammalian cell culture monolayers (monkey kidney cells, rat glioma, human lung fibroblasts) produces cytopathic effects.
*   **Serology & Rapid Antigen Tests:**
    - Serum Antibody Detection: Indirect Immunofluorescence Assay (IFA), Indirect Immunoperoxidase, ELISA, and Immunoblotting using monoclonal or polyclonal antibodies (available at reference centers such as the CDC). Usefulness is limited due to high background seroprevalence in healthy populations and delayed/absent antibody responses in immunocompromised hosts.
    - Rapid Antigen Tests: N/A. No commercial rapid antigen test kits are available.
*   **Molecular Diagnostics:**
    - Triplex Real-Time TaqMan PCR (CDC Assay): Simultaneously detects Acanthamoeba spp., [[Balamuthia mandrillaris]], and [[Naegleria fowleri]] in CSF, brain tissue, corneal scrapings, and formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissues with high specificity and rapid turnaround. Capable of detecting down to 0.1 cyst/µL or a single ameba.
    - Genotyping PCR: Sequencing of the 18S rRNA gene (Rns gene), Acanthamoeba-specific amplimer (ASA.S1), and Diagnostic Fragment 3 (DF3) classifies isolates into genotypes T1 through T20 (T4 being the predominant human pathogen).
    - Loop-Mediated Isothermal Amplification (LAMP): Rapid, highly specific isothermal DNA amplification test developed for detecting Acanthamoeba DNA from corneal scrapings.
    - Other Molecular Tools: Restriction Fragment Length Polymorphism (RFLP), Randomly Amplified Polymorphic DNA (RAPD), Metagenomic Next-Generation Sequencing (mNGS), and MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Anti-Protozoal Agents:**
    - Acanthamoeba Keratitis (AK):
      - Topical Biguanides: Polyhexamethylene biguanide (PHMB) 0.02% or Chlorhexidine gluconate 0.02% drops applied topically to the affected eye (hourly intervals initially).
      - Combined with Topical Diamidines: Propamidine isethionate 0.1% (Brolene) or Hexamidine 0.1% (Desomodine).
      - Oral [[Miltefosine]]: Approved by the FDA for the treatment of Acanthamoeba keratitis.
    - Granulomatous Amebic Encephalitis (GAE) & Disseminated Infections:
      - Combination antimicrobial therapy (CDC recommendations): [[Pentamidine]] isethionate, [[Sulfadiazine]], [[Flucytosine]] (5-fluorocytosine), [[Fluconazole]] (or [[Voriconazole]] / [[Itraconazole]]), [[Azithromycin]] (or [[Clarithromycin]]), and [[Miltefosine]].
*   **Alternative Treatments:**
    - Topical Agents for AK: [[Clotrimazole]] 1% cream/drops, [[Miconazole]], [[Ketoconazole]] 2% cream, [[Neomycin]], [[Polymyxin|Polymyxin B]], [[Paromomycin]].
    - Systemic Agents for GAE/Disseminated Disease: [[Ketoconazole]], [[Clotrimazole]], [[Rifampin]], [[Cotrimoxazole|Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole]], Hydroxystilbamidine, [[Paromomycin]], [[Amikacin]], [[Caspofungin]], and Liposomal [[Amphotericin B]].
    - Surgical Interventions: Ocular epithelial debridement (removes amebae and enhances topical drug penetration), Penetrating Keratoplasty (corneal transplantation for deep stromal disease/perforation), Surgical excision/resection of localized brain lesions or skin ulcers.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Dosing Considerations:**
    - AK Treatment Duration: Requires prolonged, aggressive topical therapy for many months to a year or longer due to the extreme resistance of encysted amebae. Recurrent corneal/scleral inflammation occurs in ~5% of patients post-treatment due to reactivation of dormant cysts in situ.
    - Corticosteroid Warnings: Systemic corticosteroids exacerbate CNS Acanthamoeba infections and must be avoided in GAE. Topical steroids in AK must be introduced with extreme caution only after amebic infection is controlled by biguanides/diamidines, as steroids can induce cyst reactivation and worsen infection.
    - Disinfectant Efficacy in Contact Lens Solutions: Hydrogen peroxide-based disinfection systems (minimum contact time: 3 min for trophozoites, 9 h for cysts) and chlorhexidine-thimerosal are effective against both cysts and trophozoites. Benzalkonium chloride kills trophozoites only. Polyaminopropyl biguanide and polyquaternium-1 solutions are ineffective against cysts.
    - CNS Bioavailability: [[Miltefosine]] (oral dosing: weight <45 kg = 50 mg BID; weight >45 kg = 50 mg TID for 28 days) and [[Voriconazole]] effectively cross the blood-brain barrier and penetrate CNS tissues.
    - Cutaneous Ulcer Protocol: Successful resolution achieved using 1 month of systemic IV [[Pentamidine]] isethionate plus 8 months of oral [[Itraconazole]] (200 mg BID), combined with twice-daily chlorhexidine gluconate skin washing and topical 2% [[Ketoconazole]] cream.
*   **Prophylaxis, Water Treatment & Vector Control:**
    - Contact Lens Hygiene: Thorough handwashing with soap and drying before handling lenses; strict elimination of tap water, homemade saline, or saliva for lens rinsing/storage; complete removal of lenses before showering, bathing, using hot tubs, or swimming; daily mechanical scrubbing and air-drying of lens storage cases; periodic thermal/heat disinfection of cases (at least 70°C); frequent replacement of storage cases.
    - Sinus Hygiene: Use only sterile, distilled, or boiled water for nasal irrigation with neti pots; avoid untreated tap water.
    - Water Treatment: Boiling water for 10 minutes inactivates cysts. Swimming pool chlorination at routine municipal levels does NOT reliably destroy Acanthamoeba cysts.
    - Chemoprophylaxis: N/A
    - Vector Control: N/A

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "Acanthapodia" (fine, spiky, thorn-like hyaline pseudopodial projections).
    - "Double-walled cyst" with "wrinkled outer ectocyst" and "polygonal/stellate inner endocyst".
    - "360-degree or paracentral stromal ring infiltrate" in corneal keratitis.
    - "Radial keratoneuritis" (perineural corneal infiltrates causing severe ocular pain disproportionate to early physical signs).
    - "Amphizoic ameba" (capable of existing both as a free-living environmental organism and as an opportunistic pathogen).
    - "T4 genotype" (the predominant 18S rRNA genotype responsible for human AK and GAE).
    - "Crib" or "Trojan horse" (acts as an environmental reservoir and vehicle for bacterial pathogens such as Legionella).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - Unlike [[Naegleria fowleri]] (which possesses a transient flagellate stage and shows ONLY ameboid trophozoites in brain tissue during acute PAM), Acanthamoeba has NO flagellate stage and displays BOTH trophozoites AND double-walled cysts simultaneously in brain biopsy and tissue sections during GAE.
    - Unlike [[Naegleria fowleri]] PAM (which affects healthy young swimmers via nasal entry causing rapid death in 3 to 5 days), Acanthamoeba GAE primarily affects immunocompromised hosts via skin/lung hematogenous spread with a subacute or chronic clinical course lasting weeks to months.
    - Unlike Naegleria and Acanthamoeba, [[Balamuthia mandrillaris]] CANNOT be grown on non-nutrient agar plates seeded with an E. coli bacterial overlay.
    - Calcofluor white chemofluorescent stain binds specifically to cellulose in the endocyst wall, causing cysts to fluoresce bright green/blue under UV light, but does NOT stain or induce fluorescence in amebic trophozoites.
    - Standard Gram stain of CSF or tissue may yield false-positive impressions of bacterial infection because cellular background debris mimics Gram-positive or Gram-negative bacteria.
