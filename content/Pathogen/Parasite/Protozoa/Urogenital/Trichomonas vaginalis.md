### Trichomonas vaginalis

#### Protozoan Biology & Microscopic Morphology
*   **Trophozoite Stage Features:** Pear-shaped (pyriform) or teardrop-shaped to ovoid flagellated ameboflagellate measuring 7 to 23 µm in length (average 13 to 15 µm or ~20 µm) by 5 to 15 µm in width (average 6 to 8 µm or ~10 µm). Under varying microenvironmental physicochemical conditions (pH, temperature, oxygen tension, ionic strength, or direct contact with host epithelial cells/McCoy cells/erythrocytes), the parasite undergoes morphologic transformation into a flattened ameboid form with prominent pseudopodial extensions or a round non-motile pseudocyst form (lacking a true thick cyst wall). Contains a single, anteriorly located, large oval nucleus with a prominent central karyosome and fine, delicate peripheral chromatin lining the inner nuclear membrane; the nucleus is usually not visible in unstained wet mount preparations. Possesses FIVE flagella total: FOUR free anterior flagella that arise from a single anterior stalk/blepharoplast, and a FIFTH flagellum that extends posteriorly along the outer edge of a short undulating membrane. The undulating membrane extends only about HALF the length of the cell body and does NOT project beyond the body as a free posterior trailing flagellum. C ontains a flexible, hyaline, rod-like supporting structure called an axostyle that extends through the center of the organism and protrudes prominently beyond the posterior tip of the body. Contains a small, primitive anterior cytostome (oral groove) located opposite the undulating membrane, and dark-staining chromatic granules lining the axostyle and costa. The cytoplasm is finely granular, containing food vacuoles packed with ingested bacteria, cellular debris, and host erythrocytes (actively phagocytoses RBCs). Primitive amitochondriate protist: lacks classical mitochondria, peroxisomes, and a golgi apparatus, possessing specialized anaerobic hydrogenosomes for energy metabolism and ATP generation. Has the largest protozoan genome known, potentially encoding ~60,000 proteins. Expresses surface adhesins and receptors for host extracellular matrix proteins (laminin, fibronectin) regulated dynamically by host iron availability. Frequently harbors an endosymbiotic double-stranded RNA virus called *Trichomonas vaginalis virus* (TVV) that modulates parasite virulence and host inflammatory responses, as well as an intracellular bacterial endosymbiont, *[[Mycoplasma hominis]]*, which replicates inside *T. vaginalis* cells and can be co-transmitted to human host tissues.
*   **Cyst / Oocyst Stage Features:** N/A. *Trichomonas vaginalis* COMPLETELY LACKS a true, environmentally resistant cyst stage; it exists exclusively as flagellated/ameboid trophozoites and round pseudocysts (non-motile round forms lacking a true thick cyst wall, observed in growth-phase cultures or cervical neoplasia specimens).
*   **Intracellular Morphologies:** N/A. *Trichomonas vaginalis* is an extracellular, surface-attaching parasite residing on mucosal epithelial surfaces; it does not form obligate intracellular developmental stages in host human cells (though it harbors intracellular endosymbiotic *[[Mycoplasma hominis]]* bacteria and TVV viruses).
*   **Locomotion Mechanics:** Active motility in fresh wet mount preparations of vaginal/urethral discharge or urine is characteristically rapid, jerky, tumbling, or wobbly, propelled by the coordinated whipping of the 4 free anterior flagella and the wave-like movement of the short undulating membrane. Converts to non-motile ameboid forms upon attachment to host epithelial cells.

#### Lifecycle & Transmission
*   **Definitive Host:** Humans serve as the sole natural host and primary reservoir. No animal reservoirs exist.
*   **Intermediate Host & Vector Dynamics:** None required (monoxenous life cycle; direct human-to-human transmission). No arthropod vector.
*   **Transmission Route:**
    - Sexually Transmitted Infection (STI): Primary mode of transmission is direct person-to-person transfer through sexual intercourse (venereal route).
    - Vertical Transmission: Neonatal acquisition occurs during passage of the fetus through an infected mother's birth canal at delivery (causing neonatal pneumonia or conjunctivitis).
    - Fomite Transmission: Rare non-venereal transmission via moist, contaminated fomites (shared toilet articles, clothing, washcloths, underwear, towels), although limited by the environmental lability of the trophozoite stage.
*   **Infective Stage & Portal of Entry:** Direct mucosal inoculation of active trophozoites into the urogenital tract during sexual contact (vagina, vulva, and cervix in females; urethra, prostate gland, and seminal vesicles in males) or into the neonate's respiratory tract/eyes during birth.
*   **Diagnostic Stage & Specimen Source:** Trophozoites. Specimen sources include vaginal discharge/swabs collected from the posterior fornix, endocervical swabs, urethral discharge/swabs, prostatic secretions, semen, first-void morning urine sediment, and PreservCyt solution (Pap test transport medium); rarely, neonatal respiratory secretions or gastric washings.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Tissue Tropism & Cellular Invasion:** Demonstrates strict tropism for the mucosal epithelial lining of the human urogenital tract (vagina, vulva, cervix, female urethra; male urethra, prostate, seminal vesicles). Non-invasive under baseline conditions; adheres tightly to squamous epithelial cells via microfilaments and surface adhesin proteins.
*   **Cytopathic Effects & Tissue Destruction:**
    - Adhesion & Transformation: Parasite surface adhesins bind to host epithelial cell surface receptors and extracellular matrix proteins (laminin, fibronectin) in a time-, temperature-, and pH-dependent manner. Upon attachment, the parasite transforms from a pear-shaped flagellate into a flattened ameboid trophozoite with prominent pseudopodia, maximizing contact area.
    - Cytotoxicity & Cell Detachment: Releases cytotoxic enzymes, including soluble and membrane-associated Phospholipase A1 and A2, cysteine proteinases, and Cell Detaching Factor (CDF). CDF causes extensive cytopathic cell detachment and microvillar destruction of host epithelial cells (CDF levels correlate directly with clinical disease severity; absent in non-pathogenic *[[Pentatrichomonas hominis]]*).
    - Hemolysis: Cysteine proteinases and pore-forming proteins create pores in host red blood cell membranes, inducing cell lysis to acquire essential iron and lipids.
    - Alteration of Vaginal Microenvironment: Disrupts normal protective *Lactobacillus* flora, elevating vaginal pH from normal acidic (pH 4.5) to neutral or alkaline (pH 5.0–7.0). Induces intense acute mucosal inflammation with heavy recruitment of polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNs), producing a profuse, frothy, yellow-green mucopurulent discharge and punctate mucosal micro-hemorrhages ("strawberry cervix").
    - STI & Viral Amplification: Enhances host susceptibility to [[HIV]] acquisition and transmission (>3-fold increased risk) due to mucosal disruption, local inflammatory cell recruitment, and micro-hemorrhages. Serves as a vector for intracellular *[[Mycoplasma hominis]]*.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:**
    - Host Protein Binding: Binds host plasma proteins to its cell surface, masking parasite surface antigens and preventing recognition and lysis by the alternative complement pathway and host proteinases.
    - Phenotypic / Antigenic Variation: Undergoes surface antigenic variation controlled by parasite iron status and environmental microenvironmental cues.
    - Proteolytic Cleavage: Secretes cysteine proteinases that degrade host secretory IgA (sIgA), IgG, and complement factor C3.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Host defense depends primarily on polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNs / neutrophils), which are recruited by parasite chemotactic factors and kill trichomonads via antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and phagocytosis. Local secretory IgA and complement activation assist in clearance. In [[HIV]]-infected patients, *T. vaginalis* infections are often more persistent, severe, and refractory to standard therapy, promoting reciprocal [[HIV]] shedding.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Complications
*   **Acute Clinical Presentation:**
    - Incubation Period: Typically 4 to 28 days (average 5–28 days).
    - Acute Female Trichomoniasis: Symptomatic in ~50% to 75% of infected women. Onset is often sudden, frequently occurring during or immediately after menstruation (due to altered vaginal acidity). Characterized by profuse, frothy, yellow-to-green, foul-smelling mucopurulent vaginal discharge (leukorrhea); intense vulvovaginal pruritus (itching); burning; dysuria; dyspareunia (painful intercourse); vulvovaginal erythema and excoriation; and elevated vaginal pH >4.5 (typically 5.0–7.0).
    - Acute Male Trichomoniasis: Asymptomatic in 50% to 90% of infected men. When symptomatic, presents as acute non-gonococcal urethritis (NGU) with a thin, white/clear urethral discharge, urethral pruritus, dysuria, or dyspareunia.
*   **Chronic & Extra-Intestinal Disease:**
    - Chronic Female Disease: Persistent low-grade vaginal irritation, reduced discharge volume, pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), endometritis, salpingitis, and tubal factor infertility.
    - Adverse Pregnancy Outcomes: In pregnant women, *T. vaginalis* causes premature rupture of membranes (PROM), preterm labor/delivery, and low birth weight infants.
    - Chronic Male Disease: Chronic NGU, prostatitis, epididymitis, orchitis, and impaired sperm motility/viability leading to potential infertility.
    - Neonatal Infection: Infants born through an infected birth canal can acquire neonatal pneumonia, respiratory distress, or neonatal conjunctivitis.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:**
    - "Strawberry Cervix" (Colpitis Macularis): Punctate, diffusely erythematous mucosal micro-hemorrhages on the cervix visible during colposcopic or speculum examination (present in ~2% to 5% of clinical cases).
    - Microscopic identification of motile, flagellated, pear-shaped trophozoites (slightly larger than PMNs) with jerky/tumbling motility and an undulating membrane extending half the body length in fresh wet mounts of vaginal/urethral discharge or urine.
*   **Host Immune Response & Laboratory Findings:**
    - Vaginal Fluid Analysis: High vaginal pH (>4.5; usually 5.0–7.0). Amine "whiff test" may be positive.
    - Microscopy: Direct wet mount reveals numerous PMNs (leukocytes) and active, motile trichomonads.
    - Serology: Anti-*T. vaginalis* antibodies are detectable in serum, but serologic testing is not routinely used or available for individual clinical diagnosis.

#### Epidemiology & Exposures
*   **Geographic Endemicity:** Cosmopolitan / worldwide distribution. It is the most common curable sexually transmitted infection (STI) globally. WHO estimated 156 million incident cases and 110.4 million prevalent cases annually among adults aged 15–49 worldwide (global prevalence 5.3% in females, 0.6% in males). Highest prevalence in the WHO African Region and Region of the Americas. In North America / United States, annual incidence is estimated at 7.4 to 8+ million new cases (~5 million women and 1 million men infected). Prevalence in STI clinics ranges from 5% to 20% in females and 2% to 10% in males.
*   **Environmental Reservoir & Water Systems:** Primary reservoir is the human urogenital tract (vagina, prostate). Organisms survive poorly in the environment outside human body fluids, though short-term survival in moist towels or warm bathwater can occur.
*   **High-Risk Activities & Dietary Exposures:**
    - Sexual Exposure: Unprotected sexual intercourse; multiple sexual partners; history of prior or concurrent STIs (*[[Neisseria gonorrhoeae]]*, *[[Chlamydia trachomatis]]*, *[[Mycoplasma genitalium]]*, or *[[Gardnerella vaginalis]]* / Bacterial Vaginosis); commercial sex work.
    - Perinatal Exposure: Infants born vaginally to infected mothers.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:**
    - Biosafety: Standard BSL-2 containment when handling clinical genital discharges, urine, or tissue culture isolates (ATCC 30001 reference strain).
    - Partner Management: Mandatory partner notification and simultaneous treatment of all sexual partners to prevent "ping-pong" reinfection cycles. Abstain from sexual intercourse until both partners complete therapy and symptoms resolve.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Microscopy & Stains:**
    - Direct Saline Wet Mount (Historical Baseline Method): Direct microscopic examination of fresh vaginal secretions, urethral discharge, or spun urine sediment under 10x and 40x objectives (preferably using phase-contrast microscopy). Visualizes pear-shaped trophozoites (7–23 µm) with rapid, jerky motility and short undulating membrane. MANDATORY IMMEDIATE EXAMINATION (<15 to 30 minutes post-collection) because motility declines rapidly as the specimen cools, leading to loss of motion. Sensitivity is POOR (only 51% to 65% in women; lower in men).
    - Stained Smears: Methanol-fixed smears stained with Giemsa stain or Papanicolaou (Pap) stain visualize trophozoites (pale blue cytoplasm, red nucleus, prominent axostyle). Pap smears frequently produce false-positive and false-negative misidentifications. Gram stain is NOT RECOMMENDED because organisms stain poorly and are routinely mistaken for polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNs).
    - Pediatric / Urine Specimen Artifact Warning: In urine or pediatric specimens, *[[Pentatrichomonas hominis]]* (from fecal contamination) must be carefully distinguished from *T. vaginalis*. Diagnostic misidentification of *P. hominis* as *T. vaginalis* in an infant/child can lead to inappropriate allegations of child sexual abuse. Differentiate by undulating membrane length (full-body length in *P. hominis* vs. half-body length in *T. vaginalis*) and anterior flagella count (5 in *P. hominis* vs. 4 in *T. vaginalis*).
*   **Concentration & Stool O&P Techniques:**
    - Centrifugation: Spun urine sediment (first-void morning urine) enhances microscopic detection sensitivity in adolescent females and men.
    - In Vitro Culture (Gold Standard Historical Method): Cultivation in liquid media (CPLM (Cysteine-Peptone-Liver-Maltose), Diamond's TYI-S-33, YI-S, Trichosel) or commercial culture pouch systems (InPouch TV (Biomed Diagnostics)). InPouch TV allows direct microscopic inspection of the sealed plastic pouch holder without opening. Culture achieves >80–95% sensitivity, detecting low parasite burdens 48–72 hours before wet mounts become positive. Incubate at 37.5°C for up to 4 days.
*   **Serology & Rapid Antigen Tests:**
    - Point-of-Care (POC) Rapid Antigen Detection Kits:
      - OSOM Trichomonas Rapid Test (Genzyme / Sekisui): Rapid immunochromatographic dipstick assay targeting *T. vaginalis* protein antigens from vaginal swabs (sensitivity 83.3–90%, specificity 98.8–100%; results in 10–15 minutes).
      - XenoStrip-Tv (Xenotope): Rapid immunochromatographic dipstick (sensitivity 76.7%, specificity 99.8%).
      - Quik-Trich (PanBio): Latex agglutination test (sensitivity 95%, specificity 99%).
      - Direct Fluorescent Antibody (DFA) Assays: (e.g., Light Diagnostics Chemicon DFA). Fluorescein-labeled monoclonal antibodies visualize fluorescent trophozoites under fluorescence microscopy (sensitivity 97%, specificity 99%).
*   **Molecular Diagnostics (DIAGNOSTIC METHOD OF CHOICE):**
    - Nucleic Acid Amplification Tests (NAATs - Current Gold Standard Standard of Care): Superior sensitivity (>95–100%) and specificity (>99–100%), detecting 3 to 5 times more infections than wet mount microscopy. Does not require viable or motile organisms.
      - APTIMA Trichomonas vaginalis Assay (Hologic / Gen-Probe): FDA-approved Transcription-Mediated Amplification (TMA) assay targeting ribosomal RNA (rRNA). Approved for female clinician-collected or self-collected vaginal swabs, endocervical swabs, female urine, and PreservCyt solution (Pap test transport).
      - Affirm VPIII Microbial Identification Test (Becton Dickinson): Direct DNA probe assay that simultaneously detects *Trichomonas vaginalis*, *Candida* species, and *[[Gardnerella vaginalis]]* from a single vaginal swab within 45 minutes (100% sensitive compared to wet mount, ~80% compared to culture).
      - Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR): Commercial and laboratory-developed PCR and real-time PCR assays targeting 18S rRNA or TVK3/TVK7 repetitive DNA sequences directly from urine (male NGU screening), vaginal swabs, or semen.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Anti-Protozoal Agents:**
    - 5-Nitroimidazole Class (Nitroheterocyclic Agents): [[Metronidazole]] and [[Tinidazole]] are the only class of drugs approved for treating trichomoniasis. [[Metronidazole]] is activated intracellularly within parasite hydrogenosomes via reduction by pyruvate:ferredoxin oxidoreductase (PFOR), generating cytotoxic nitro-radical intermediates that damage parasite DNA.
      - [[Metronidazole]] (Flagyl):
        - Standard Adult Regimen: 500 mg PO BID for 7 days (preferred multi-dose regimen; superior cure rates and lower recurrence compared to single dose).
        - Alternative Adult Regimen: 2 g PO as a single oral dose.
        - Male Sexual Partners: 2 g PO as a single dose OR 500 mg PO BID for 7 days.
      - [[Tinidazole]] (Tindamax):
        - Adult Regimen: 2 g PO as a single dose with food (superior tissue penetration, longer half-life, and better tolerability than [[metronidazole]]).
*   **Alternative Treatments:**
    - [[Metronidazole]]-Resistant *T. vaginalis* (Refractory Infections): High-dose oral [[Tinidazole]] (2 g PO daily for 14 days) combined with high-dose oral [[Metronidazole]] (2 g daily) or intravaginal [[Paromomycin]] cream or [[Tinidazole]].
    - [[Nitazoxanide]] (Alinia): Evaluated off-label as an alternative agent for nitroimidazole-resistant strains.
    - Historical / Topical Adjuncts: Povidone-iodine douches, [[Clotrimazole]] (topical symptom relief only, does not cure).
*   **Treatment Nuances & Dosing Considerations:**
    - MANDATORY SIMULTANEOUS PARTNER TREATMENT: All sexual partners of infected patients MUST be treated concurrently, regardless of symptoms or initial test results, to prevent rapid reinfection ("ping-ponging"). Patients and partners must abstain from sexual intercourse until therapy is complete and symptoms have completely resolved.
    - Alcohol Interaction Caution: Patients taking [[metronidazole]] or [[tinidazole]] MUST strictly avoid ethanol/alcohol consumption during treatment and for at least 24 hours ([[metronidazole]]) to 72 hours ([[tinidazole]]) after the final dose to prevent a severe disulfiram-like reaction (flushing, severe nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, tachycardia).
    - Pregnancy Considerations: Trichomoniasis during pregnancy is associated with adverse birth outcomes (preterm birth, PROM, low birth weight). [[Metronidazole]] 500 mg PO BID for 7 days is recommended for symptomatic pregnant women.
    - Treatment Failure & Resistance Monitoring: Refractory cases are usually caused by non-adherence or partner reinfection rather than true anaerobic drug resistance. If treatment fails, retreat with [[Metronidazole]] 500 mg BID for 7 days; if second failure occurs, treat with [[Metronidazole]] or [[Tinidazole]] 2 g PO daily for 7 days.
*   **Prophylaxis, Water Treatment & Vector Control:**
    - Safe Sexual Practices: Consistent and correct use of latex condoms during sexual activity reduces transmission risk.
    - Screening Protocols: Routine STI screening using NAATs in high-risk populations (women attending STI clinics, women with new or multiple sexual partners, [[HIV]]-infected individuals at initial care and annually).
    - Personal Hygiene: Avoid sharing washcloths, towels, or douches.
    - Chemoprophylaxis: N/A. No prophylactic drug or vaccine exists.

*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - *Trichomonas vaginalis* completely LACKS a true, thick-walled environmental cyst stage, relying entirely on direct person-to-person sexual transmission for survival.
    - Unlike *[[Pentatrichomonas hominis]]* (where the undulating membrane extends the ENTIRE length of the body) and *Trichomonas tenax* (oral flagellate), the undulating membrane of *Trichomonas vaginalis* extends only about HALF the length of the cell body.
    - Standard Gram stain of genital discharge routinely FAILS to identify *T. vaginalis*, as trophozoites stain poorly and closely resemble human polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNs).
    - Finding a flagellate in an infant's or young child's urine specimen must be evaluated with extreme caution: *[[Pentatrichomonas hominis]]* (from fecal contamination of urine) can mimic *T. vaginalis*, and misidentification can lead to erroneous allegations of child sexual abuse.
    - Direct wet mount microscopy, although traditionally used for point-of-care diagnosis, has poor sensitivity (51–65%) and loses accuracy rapidly if specimen evaluation is delayed by more than 15–30 minutes due to loss of trophozoite motility.
