### Entamoeba histolytica

#### Protozoan Biology & Microscopic Morphology
*   **Trophozoite Stage Features:** Uninucleate, actively motile amebic form measuring 10 to 60 µm in diameter (usual range 15 to 20 µm or 15 to 30 µm; wet mount measurements 12 to 60 µm; invasive forms from dysenteric stool may exceed 20 µm). In permanent stained smears (trichrome or iron hematoxylin), artificial shrinkage due to dehydration causes the organism to measure 1 to 2 µm smaller (at least 10.5 to 11 µm). Contains a single, round, delicate nucleus characterized by an even distribution of fine, uniform chromatin granules lined along the inner nuclear membrane and a small, sharp, compact, centrally located karyosome (dot). The cytoplasm is finely granular, differentiated into a clean, clear outer ectoplasm and a granular inner endoplasm containing small food vacuoles. In active dysenteric stools, trophozoites characteristically contain ingested human red blood cells (erythrocytes/RBCs) within cytoplasmic vacuoles—a phenomenon known as erythrophagocytosis or haematophagous trophozoites, which serves as the pathognomonic morphologic marker distinguishing *E. histolytica* from morphologically identical nonpathogenic species (*[[Entamoeba dispar|E. dispar]]*, *[[Entamoeba moshkovskii|E. moshkovskii]]*, *[[Entamoeba bangladeshi|E. bangladeshi]]*, and *E. nuttalli*). Trophozoites in non-invasive or asymptomatic states do not contain RBCs and ingest few or no bacteria or cellular debris. *E. histolytica* is an amitochondrial protist pathogen that lacks functional mitochondria and shares unique metabolic adaptations with *Giardia* and *Trichomonas*, including the utilization of bacterial-type oxidative stress enzymes and anaerobic glycolytic pathways.
*   **Cyst / Oocyst Stage Features:** Spherical to ovoid hyaline bodies measuring 10 to 20 µm in diameter (usual range 12 to 15 µm or 15 to 20 µm; on permanent stained smears shrinks to at least 8.5 to 9 µm). Enclosed by a smooth, refractive, non-staining, protective cyst wall approximately 0.5 µm thick. Uninucleate and binucleate immature cysts (precysts) contain a dense glycogen mass (staining dark reddish-brown with iodine) and elongated, highly refractile masses of ribonucleoprotein called chromatoid bodies or chromatoidal bars. Characteristically, these chromatoid bars possess smooth, rounded, or blunt ends (in sharp contrast to the splintered, jagged, or frayed ends seen in *[[Entamoeba coli]]* cysts). As nuclear division proceeds within the developing cyst, a mature metacyst containing four small, identical nuclei (quadrinucleated cyst; rarely 8 nuclei) is produced. In mature cysts, the glycogen mass and chromatoidal bars usually disappear completely. Cysts are produced exclusively inside the lumen of the large intestine and are never formed after the stool specimen has been evacuated from the host. Cysts resist gastric acidity, ambient environmental conditions, and routine municipal drinking water chlorination levels.
*   **Intracellular Morphologies:** N/A
*   **Locomotion Mechanics:** Motility in fresh, unpreserved stool preparations is rapid, progressive, active, and unidirectional (explosive). Movement is driven by the rapid, sudden extrusion of clear, finger-like hyaline pseudopodia from the cellular ectoplasm, into which the inner granular endoplasm flows, drawing the rest of the cell forward in a snail-like motion. In fresh clinical isolates, *E. histolytica* trophozoites move at speeds up to 5 µm per second. Motility may appear sporadic depending on ambient temperature and conditions.

#### Lifecycle & Transmission
*   **Definitive Host:** Humans serve as the principal natural host and primary reservoir. Non-human primates, dogs, cats, and pigs can occasionally become infected.
*   **Intermediate Host & Vector Dynamics:** None required. Filth flies (*[[Houseflies|Musca domestica]]*) and cockroaches act as mechanical transport vectors by transferring infective cysts from exposed fecal deposits onto human food or water supplies.
*   **Transmission Route:** Fecal-oral route through ingestion of infective cysts present in human feces-contaminated water, ice, raw vegetables, or food prepared by unhygienic asymptomatic food handlers ("cyst passers"). Direct person-to-person transmission occurs via oral-anal or oral-genital sexual activity (particularly prevalent among men who have sex with men [MSM], historically contributing to "gay bowel syndrome"). Direct contact with fecally contaminated fomites. Nosocomial transmission has been documented through contaminated colonic irrigation equipment and improperly disinfected endoscopes/sigmoidoscopes.
*   **Infective Stage & Portal of Entry:** Ingestion of mature quadrinucleated cysts (as well as 1- and 2-nucleated cysts) via the mouth into the gastrointestinal tract. As few as 10 to 100 cysts are sufficient to initiate human infection. Cysts pass unaltered through the acidic stomach; exposure to neutral or slightly alkaline conditions in the duodenum stimulates excystation, where the cyst wall is digested to release a single 4-nucleated metacystic ameba. Each nucleus undergoes one division, producing 8 small metacystic trophozoites that mature as they migrate to the cecum and colon.
*   **Diagnostic Stage & Specimen Source:** Both trophozoites and cysts are passed in stool and recovered in diagnostic clinical specimens. Trophozoites predominate in liquid or diarrheic/dysenteric stools, while cysts predominate in formed or mushy stools. Diagnostic specimen sources include fresh stool, preserved stool, rectal/colonic mucosal scrapings or biopsy material obtained during sigmoidoscopy/colonoscopy (at least 6 distinct areas sampled), amebic liver abscess aspirates (specifically the final reddish portion taken from the outer margin/wall of the lesion), pulmonary/lung aspirates or sputum (in bronchohepatic fistulae), skin biopsy or scrapings from cutaneous ulcer borders, and serum for antibody testing.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Tissue Tropism & Cellular Invasion:** Demonstrates profound tropism for the large intestine (cecum, ascending colon, rectosigmoid junction, appendix), liver (predominantly the right lobe via the portal venous circulation), lungs, brain, skin, and pericardium. Colonization begins when trophozoites bind to colonic mucins and target enterocytes via a surface membrane lectin specifically inhibitable by galactose and N-acetyl-D-galactosamine (Gal/GalNAc adherence lectin). The Gal/GalNAc lectin is a 260-kDa heterodimer composed of a 170-kDa heavy subunit (which shares structural and immunological similarity with human β2 integrins) and a 35/31-kDa light subunit. Additional parasite adhesins include a 220-kDa lectin, a 112-kDa adhesin complex (EhCP112 cysteine proteinase and EhADH112 adhesin), a distinct amebic β2 [[integrin]]-like molecule, fibronectin-binding protein (37-kDa), [[integrin]]-like receptor (140-kDa), and cell-surface lipophosphoglycan / lipopeptidophosphophosphoglycan.
*   **Cytopathic Effects & Tissue Destruction:** Tissue damage occurs through contact-dependent host cell lysis, extracellular matrix degradation, and phagocytosis. Host cell lysis proceeds through 4 steps: (1) Gal/GalNAc lectin-mediated attachment; (2) sustained intracellular calcium influx in target cells; (3) protein tyrosine dephosphorylation; and (4) caspase-3-dependent apoptosis and membrane disruption. Amebae insert 5-kDa pore-forming peptides called amebapores (isotypes A, B, and C in a 35:10:1 ratio) into target host cell lipid bilayers, forming ion channels that induce cell swelling, lysis, and necrosis. Trophozoites overexpress potent cysteine proteinases (releasing 10 to 1,000 times more than nonpathogenic *[[Entamoeba dispar|E. dispar]]*), including EhCP1, EhCP2, and EhCP5, which degrade extracellular matrix components (type I collagen, laminin, fibronectin, elastin, fibrinogen), break down basement membranes, and digest mucus. Ingestion of human erythrocytes (erythrophagocytosis) is mediated by myosin IB. Invasive trophozoites lyse host neutrophils, [[Monocyte|monocytes]], and [[Macrophage|macrophages]], releasing toxic lysosomal granules that accelerate tissue destruction. In the intestine, this produces superficial mucosal erosion followed by lateral subepithelial spread, creating classic "flask-shaped" amebic ulcers with narrow mucosal entry necks and broad necrotic submucosal bases. In the liver, massive cytolysis forms large fluid-filled cavities containing sterile, non-suppurative, necrotic "anchovy paste" or chocolate-brown material.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** Cysteine proteinases actively degrade host secretory IgA, systemic IgG, antimicrobial peptides (human cathelicidin LL-37), and cleave complement component C3 to prevent opsonization. Displays high resistance to complement-mediated cellular lysis via Gal/GalNAc lectin structural similarity to human CD59 (MAC-inhibitory protein). Undergoes "capping"—the surface redistribution of surface-bound host antibodies to one pole of the ameba, followed by membrane exocytosis/shedding. Modulates host immunity by lysing infiltrating neutrophils and [[Macrophage|macrophages]] at the site of invasion.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Host protection relies primarily on cell-mediated immunity (T lymphocytes and [[Macrophage|macrophages]] activated by IFN-γ, TNF-α, and interleukin-8 via Toll-like receptors TLR2/6, TLR4, and TLR9, producing nitric oxide (NO) which inhibits cysteine proteases and alcohol dehydrogenase 2). Secretory anti-Gal/GalNAc IgA antibodies in gut mucus block primary adherence. Host genetic background influences susceptibility: specific HLA class II alleles confer resistance, whereas a single amino acid polymorphism (Q223R) in the extracellular domain of the leptin receptor increases susceptibility to *E. histolytica* infection nearly 4-fold. Risk of invasive amebiasis, severe colitis, bowel perforation, and extraintestinal spread is increased in patients receiving systemic corticosteroid therapy, pregnant women, malnourished individuals, young infants, and organ transplant recipients. Administering corticosteroids to patients misdiagnosed with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) can precipitate fatal toxic megacolon or intestinal perforation. [[HIV|HIV-1]] infection does not appear to increase susceptibility to intestinal colonization, but invasive amebic liver abscesses are documented in HIV-positive patients.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Complications
*   **Acute Clinical Presentation:**
    - Invasive Intestinal Amebiasis / Amebic Dysentery: Incubation period ranges from a few days to 1 to 4 weeks (or up to a year). Onset is typically gradual, presenting with colicky lower abdominal pain, cramping, tenesmus, nausea, vomiting, malaise, weight loss, and frequent passage of blood-tinged, mucoid stools (up to 10 to 12 bowel movements per day). Severe, acute-onset cases feature sudden high fever, profuse bloody diarrhea (>10 stools/day), dehydration, and severe electrolyte imbalances.
    - Amebic Appendicitis: Acute inflammation presenting with severe right lower quadrant pain, mimicking acute bacterial appendicitis.
*   **Chronic & Extra-Intestinal Disease:**
    - Chronic Amebic Colitis: Recurrent episodes of diarrhea alternating with constipation, persistent abdominal cramps, general discomfort, anorexia, fatigue, and weight loss lasting for months or years.
    - Ameboma (Amebic Granuloma): Inflammatory tumor-like mass lesion resulting from chronic amebic invasion and secondary bacterial infection, located most commonly in the cecum (40%) and rectosigmoid junction (20%). May cause intestinal obstruction and mimics colonic carcinoma.
    - Toxic Megacolon & Bowel Perforation: Complication of severe necrotizing amebic colitis leading to peritonitis, abdominal distention, systemic toxicity, and high mortality.
    - Amebic Liver Abscess (ALA) / Amebic Hepatitis: Most common extraintestinal manifestation (occurs in 4% or more of clinical cases; 10 times more frequent in adult males than females). Characterized by abrupt onset of intense right upper quadrant pain radiating to the right scapula and shoulder, high spiking fevers (38–40°C) with rigors and profuse sweating, painful hepatomegaly, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, and right diaphragm elevation on imaging. Jaundice is extremely rare. More than half of ALA patients give no prior history of intestinal amebiasis or dysentery.
    - Pleuropulmonary Amebiasis: Occurs via direct transdiaphragmatic extension or rupture of a right lobe liver abscess into the pleural cavity, bronchial tree, or pericardium. Symptoms include cough, chest pain, dyspnea, and expectoration of thick, chocolate-brown or "anchovy paste-like" sputum (bronchohepatic fistula).
    - Amebic Brain Abscess: Rare, highly lethal metastatic complication arising secondary to hepatic or pulmonary disease, presenting with rapid-onset headache, focal neurological deficits, seizures, altered mental status, and signs of increased intracranial pressure.
    - Cutaneous Amebiasis: Extremely painful, rapidly progressive, indurated skin ulcers with raised, hyperemic, overhanging edges and purulent exudate containing trophozoites. Occurs in the perianal area, abdominal wall surrounding surgical drainage sites or fistulae, or external genitalia.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:**
    - Microscopic: Demonstration of motile amebic trophozoites containing phagocytosed host red blood cells (erythrophagocytosis / haematophagous trophozoites) in fresh stool, mucosal scrapings, or tissue aspirates.
    - Pathologic / Gross: "Flask-shaped" mucosal ulcers in histological sections of the colon and "anchovy paste" or dark chocolate-brown sterile necrotic fluid obtained from liver abscess drainage.
*   **Host Immune Response & Laboratory Findings:**
    - Stool Analysis: Positive for occult or gross blood, mucus, polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNs), [[Macrophage|macrophages]], and Charcot-Leyden crystals (diamond-shaped protein crystals derived from disintegrating eosinophils).
    - Hematology & Chemistry: Marked leukocytosis without prominent eosinophilia in acute dysentery and liver abscess; elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) and C-reactive protein (CRP). Alkaline phosphatase and liver enzymes are mildly elevated; jaundice is uncommon.
    - Serology: Serum anti-amebic antibodies (IgG) detected by Indirect Hemagglutination (IHA; titers ≥1:256 considered diagnostic), ELISA, or IFA. Positive in >85% to 95% of patients with invasive extraintestinal amebiasis (ALA) and >80% with amebic colitis. Serology may be negative in early acute presentation or asymptomatic cyst carriage. Coproantibodies (intestinal IgA) present in ~80% of active amebic dysentery cases.

#### Epidemiology & Exposures
*   **Geographic Endemicity:** Worldwide distribution, with the highest burden in developing countries with poor environmental sanitation and hygiene. Causes an estimated 50 million cases of invasive disease and 40,000 to 110,000 deaths annually, ranking third among parasitic diseases as a cause of death globally (surpassed only by [[Plasmodium sp.|malaria]] and schistosomiasis). High-prevalence regions include Mexico, South America, Western Africa, South Africa, the Middle East, and South/Southeast Asia, where local population prevalence ranges from 10% to 50%. Overall United States prevalence is <5%. Note: Historical epidemiologic data over-reported *E. histolytica* because >90% of asymptomatic "cyst passers" are actually colonized with the nonpathogenic species *[[Entamoeba dispar|E. dispar]]*.
*   **Environmental Reservoir & Water Systems:** Humans are the main reservoir. Cysts contaminate water systems, municipal tap water, shallow wells, springs, and agricultural soils fertilized with untreated human feces ("night soil"). Sewage cross-contamination of drinking water causes major waterborne outbreaks. Cysts remain viable and infective in soil/water for 8 days at 28–34°C, 40 days at 2–6°C, and 60 days at 0°C. Cysts resist standard swimming pool chlorine levels, but are inactivated by boiling or sand filtration.
*   **High-Risk Activities & Dietary Exposures:**
    - Food & Water Consumption: Ingestion of untreated drinking water, ice cubes made from tap water, and unpeeled raw fruits or uncooked vegetables washed in contaminated water.
    - Food Handling: Consuming food prepared by asymptomatic food handlers who are cyst carriers ("cyst passers").
    - Travel & Migration: Recent travel to tropical endemic countries, immigrants, refugees, and migrant workers.
    - Institutional Living: Residents of institutions for the mentally ill, geriatric facilities, prisons, and crowded day-care centers.
    - Sexual Practices: Oral-anal sex (anilingus) and oral-genital sex, particularly among homosexual males.
    - Medical Exposures: Receiving colonic irrigation therapy with contaminated machinery.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:**
    - Biosafety: Standard BSL-2 precautions when processing unpreserved fresh fecal specimens, cyst suspensions, or tissue cultures. Lab personnel must wear gloves and coats. Trophozoites in acute dysenteric stools are fragile and non-infective via ingestion because they disintegrate in stomach acid; infectivity is linked to cyst ingestion.
    - Public Health Measures: Exclusion of asymptomatic cyst carriers from commercial food handling until cleared; mandatory reporting of confirmed cases to public health authorities; enforcement of municipal water filtration and proper human waste disposal.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Microscopy & Stains:**
    - Direct Saline / Iodine Wet Mount: Microscopic examination of fresh, unpreserved stool sediment under 10x and 40x objectives. Insensitive (<10–60% sensitivity). Visualizes motile trophozoites and cysts. Morphologically indistinguishable from *[[Entamoeba dispar|E. dispar]]*, *[[Entamoeba moshkovskii|E. moshkovskii]]*, and *[[Entamoeba bangladeshi|E. bangladeshi]]* UNLESS trophozoites contain ingested red blood cells.
    - Permanent Stained Smears (Mandatory Gold Standard for Morphology):
      - Wheatley's Trichrome Stain: Trophozoite and cyst cytoplasm stain blue-green to green; nuclear chromatin and karyosomes stain dark red/purple; ingested RBCs stain dark red/purple.
      - Iron Hematoxylin Stain (incorporating Carbol Fuchsin): Delineates delicate nuclear membrane, fine peripheral chromatin, and central karyosome.
      - Periodic Acid-Schiff (PAS) Stain: Stains amebic trophozoites bright pink/magenta in histologic tissue sections (H&E stain displays trophozoites with clear cytoplasm and central karyosomes against blue/green background).
*   **Concentration & Stool O&P Techniques:**
    - Formalin-Ethyl Acetate Sedimentation Concentration: Standard method for concentrating cysts from formed or soft stools. Zinc sulfate flotation is not recommended because high specific gravity solutions collapse and distort delicate amebic cysts.
    - Specimen Collection Protocols: Minimum of three separate stool specimens collected on alternate days within a 10-day period. Fresh specimens for motile trophozoite detection must be examined or fixed within 30 minutes of passage.
    - Sigmoidoscopy Material: Scrapings or aspirates (collected using tightly wound cotton sticks or plastic instruments; avoid loose cotton swabs) placed in warm 0.85% saline or fixed immediately in Schaudinn, PVA, or Universal Fixative (TOTAL-FIX).
    - Liver Abscess Aspirate Processing: Aspirate collected from the outer advancing margin of the abscess (the final reddish fraction contains amebae, whereas the early yellow/white pus is necrotic and sterile). Exudate treated with streptodornase/streptokinase or low-speed centrifugation (500 × g for 10 min) prior to wet mounts and iron hematoxylin/trichrome permanent stains.
    - Culture Systems: Xenic media (TYSGM-9, Boeck and Drbohlav's LES, Balamuth's aqueous egg yolk infusion with rice starch) maintained at 35°C. Axenic media (TYI-S-33, YI-S, PACSR serum replacement) support growth without bacteria. Culture is reserved for research and reference labs.
*   **Serology & Rapid Antigen Tests:**
    - Fecal Antigen Detection Immunoassays: Commercial ELISA, EIA, and rapid immunochromatographic test strips (e.g., TechLab *E. histolytica* II, *E. histolytica* Quik Chek, ProSpecT *Entamoeba*, CELISA-PATH, Wampole *E. histolytica* test, Triage Parasite Panel). Assays targeting the Gal/GalNAc lectin specifically differentiate pathogenic *E. histolytica* from nonpathogenic *[[Entamoeba dispar|E. dispar]]*. Sensitivity ranges from 83% to 100% and specificity 95% to 100%. Requires fresh, frozen, or Cary-Blair stool specimens (formalin or PVA preserved specimens are unacceptable for most antigen kits, except TOTAL-FIX).
    - Serum Antibody Detection: Indirect Hemagglutination (IHA), IFA, ELISA (detecting anti-170 kDa Gal-lectin antibodies), and Immunodiffusion (ID). Serum antibodies develop in >85% to 95% of patients with amebic liver abscess and >80% with active amebic colitis. Highly valuable for extraintestinal disease when stool microscopy is negative.
*   **Molecular Diagnostics:**
    - Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) Assays: Real-time TaqMan PCR and nested PCR targeting small subunit (18S) rRNA, 16S rRNA, or SREHP gene sequences. Represents the definitive reference standard for distinguishing *E. histolytica* from *[[Entamoeba dispar|E. dispar]]*, *[[Entamoeba moshkovskii|E. moshkovskii]]*, *[[Entamoeba bangladeshi|E. bangladeshi]]*, and *E. nuttalli*.
    - Commercial Multiplex Molecular Panels: Automated FDA-cleared syndromic PCR panels (e.g., BioFire FilmArray Gastrointestinal Panel, BD MAX Enteric Parasite Panel) simultaneously detect *E. histolytica*, *Giardia*, *[[Cryptosporidium]]*, and *Cyclospora* directly from stool.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Anti-Protozoal Agents:**
    - Dual-Stage Treatment Strategy: Invasive amebiasis ALWAYS requires sequential administration of a tissue amebicide followed immediately by a luminal amebicide.
    - Tissue Amebicides (for invasive intestinal colitis or amebic liver abscess):
      - [[Metronidazole]]: 750 mg PO TID (or 35–50 mg/kg/day in 3 divided doses) for 7 to 10 days.
      - [[Tinidazole]]: 2 g PO once daily for 3 to 5 days.
    - Luminal Amebicides (administered following tissue amebicidal therapy to eliminate intraluminal cysts, or used alone for asymptomatic cyst carriers):
      - [[Paromomycin]]: 25 to 35 mg/kg/day PO in 3 divided doses for 7 days.
      - Iodoquinol: 650 mg PO TID for 20 days.
      - Diloxanide Furoate: 500 mg PO TID for 10 days.
*   **Alternative Treatments:**
    - Alternative Tissue Amebicides: [[Nitazoxanide]], [[Chloroquine]] phosphate (used as an adjunct to [[metronidazole]] in severe liver abscess), Dehydroemetine / Emetine hydrochloride (rarely used due to cardiotoxicity).
    - Surgical / Interventional Management: Percutaneous needle aspiration or catheter drainage of amebic liver abscess indicated if: (1) large abscess (>5 cm diameter), (2) abscess in left lobe (high risk of rupture into pericardium), (3) failure to respond clinically to [[metronidazole]] within 48 to 72 hours, (4) threat of imminent rupture, or (5) secondary bacterial infection. Surgical bowel resection required for toxic megacolon or intestinal perforation.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Dosing Considerations:**
    - Absolute Requirement for Luminal Agent: [[Metronidazole]] and [[tinidazole]] are rapidly and almost completely absorbed in the upper small intestine, failing to reach therapeutic amebicidal levels in the colon lumen. Treating invasive amebiasis with [[metronidazole]] alone leaves surviving luminal amebae/cysts in up to 40% to 60% of patients, resulting in disease relapse and continued cyst shedding. Follow-up treatment with a luminal amebicide ([[Paromomycin]] or Iodoquinol) is mandatory.
    - Asymptomatic Cyst Carriers: Should be treated with a luminal amebicide alone ([[Paromomycin]] or Iodoquinol or Diloxanide furoate) to eradicate carriage, prevent potential future invasion, and eliminate public health risk.
    - Corticosteroid Contraindication: Systemic corticosteroids must NEVER be administered in suspected amebic colitis, as they depress cell-mediated immunity and precipitate fulminant necrotizing colitis, bowel gangrene, toxic megacolon, and perforation.
*   **Prophylaxis, Water Treatment & Vector Control:**
    - Water Purification: Boiling water vigorously for 10 minutes inactivates all cysts. Water filtration using sand filters or NSF 53/58 submicron filters removes cysts. Routine municipal tap water chlorination does NOT destroy amebic cysts. Chemical disinfection requires 200 ppm iodine or 5% to 10% acetic acid.
    - Dietary Hygiene: In endemic regions, boil drinking water (and ice), avoid unpeeled fruits and raw vegetables, and wash produce thoroughly.
    - Chemoprophylaxis: N/A. Individual drug prophylaxis for travelers is not recommended due to low acquisition rates (~0.3%).
    - Vaccine Development: No commercial human vaccine available. Recombinant candidate antigens showing protective immunity in animal models include the serine-rich *E. histolytica* protein (SREHP), the 170-kDa subunit of the Gal/GalNAc lectin (LecA), and the 29-kDa cysteine-rich protein.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "Flask-shaped ulcer" (narrow mucosal entry point expanding laterally into a broad necrotic submucosal base).
    - "Anchovy paste" / "chocolate-brown pus" (thick, sterile, reddish-brown necrotic fluid aspirated from an amebic liver abscess).
    - "Erythrophagocytosis" / "haematophagous trophozoites" (trophozoites containing ingested host RBCs; diagnostic visual marker of true *E. histolytica*).
    - "Gal/GalNAc lectin" (260-kDa adherence protein mediating host attachment, neutrophil destruction, and cytolysis).
    - "Amebapore" (channel-forming peptides A, B, and C that insert into target cell lipid membranes).
    - "Zymodeme analysis" (historical electrophoretic isoenzyme typing of hexokinase and phosphoglucomutase that originally differentiated *E. histolytica* from *[[Entamoeba dispar|E. dispar]]*).
    - "Charcot-Leyden crystals" (diamond-shaped protein crystals derived from eosinophil breakdown in dysenteric stool).
    - "Ameboma" (granulomatous mass in the cecum or rectosigmoid junction mimicking colonic adenocarcinoma).
    - "Gay bowel syndrome" (sexually transmitted amebic colitis among MSM).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - Light microscopy CANNOT distinguish *E. histolytica* from four other species (*[[Entamoeba dispar|E. dispar]]*, *[[Entamoeba moshkovskii|E. moshkovskii]]*, *[[Entamoeba bangladeshi|E. bangladeshi]]*, and *E. nuttalli*); only the visualization of ingested RBCs inside trophozoites permits definitive microscopic identification of *E. histolytica*.
    - Unlike *[[Entamoeba coli]]* cysts (which contain 1 to 8 or 16 nuclei and splintered, jagged chromatoid bars), *E. histolytica* cysts contain 1 to 4 nuclei and smooth, rounded, blunt-ended chromatoid bars.
    - Unlike *[[Entamoeba gingivalis]]* (which inhabits the oral cavity, ingests host PMN leukocytes, and forms no cysts), *E. histolytica* inhabits the colon, ingests RBCs (never PMNs), and forms 4-nucleated cysts.
    - Stool O&P examination is NEGATIVE for amebae in over 50% of patients presenting with amebic liver abscess.
    - In amebic liver abscess aspiration, the first portion of fluid removed is yellowish-white and completely devoid of amebae; active trophozoites are located exclusively at the outer structural margin/wall of the lesion and appear only in the final reddish fraction drawn.
    - Trophozoites passed in liquid dysenteric stools are NON-INFECTIOUS to other humans if ingested, because trophozoites rapidly disintegrate in the environment and are destroyed by stomach acid; only encysted forms passed in formed stool transmit infection.
    - Misdiagnosing amebic colitis as ulcerative colitis/IBD and administering corticosteroids can trigger catastrophic, fatal fulminant amebiasis, toxic megacolon, or intestinal perforation.
