### Cyclospora cayetanensis

#### Protozoan Biology & Microscopic Morphology
*   **Trophozoite Stage Features:** Intracellular vegetative developmental form residing within parasitophorous vacuoles located at the apical/luminal cytoplasm of human jejunal and duodenal enterocytes. Uninucleate, undergoing asexual multiplication (schizogony/merogony) within host intestinal cells to yield Type I and Type II meronts. Historically described in the 1980s as a "cyanobacterium-like body" (CLB) or "coccidian-like body" due to preliminary transmission electron microscopy (TEM) displaying internal photosynthetic organelle-like structures that resembled cyanobacteria. Formally named *Cyclospora cayetanensis* in 1993 by Ortega et al., honoring the Universidad Peruana Cayetano Heredia in Peru where pivotal clinical studies were conducted.
*   **Cyst / Oocyst Stage Features:** Spherical to sub-spherical, environmentally resistant oocysts measuring 7.7 to 10 µm (typically 8 to 10 µm) in diameter. Characteristically passed in fresh human stool as UNSPORULATED oocysts containing an undifferentiated central cytoplasmic morula mass (non-refractile spheres in clean wet mounts). Unsporulated oocysts are non-infectious when passed. Requires an environmental maturation/sporulation period of days to weeks (typically 5 to 10–14 days) at ambient temperatures to reach infectivity. Fully sporulated mature oocysts contain TWO oval sporocysts (each measuring ~4 × 6 µm), and each sporocyst contains TWO slender, bow-shaped/crescentic sporozoites (totaling 4 sporozoites per mature oocyst). Enclosed by a rigid, multilayered oocyst wall that resists environmental desiccation and chemical destruction. Mechanical or enzymatic action in vitro or in the gut lumen causes the oocyst wall to rupture, releasing free sporocysts and infective sporozoites.
*   **Intracellular Morphologies:** Obligate intracellular development inside parasitophorous vacuoles located at the apical microvillous surface of jejunal epithelial cells. Asexual and sexual stages include:
    - Type I Meronts: Contain multiple asexual merozoites that break out of host cells to invade adjacent enterocytes and perpetuate the asexual cycle.
    - Type II Meronts: Contain merozoites destined to initiate sexual development (gametogony).
    - Sexual Stages (Gametocytes): Unicellular Macrogametocytes (female gametocytes) and Microgametocytes (male gametocytes). Fertilization of a macrogametocyte by a microgamete produces a diploid zygote that encysts to form an unsporulated oocyst, which is shed into the gut lumen and excreted in feces.
*   **Locomotion Mechanics:** Free sporozoites and merozoites lack cilia, flagella, or pseudopodia, traversing host enterocyte membranes via substrate-dependent gliding motility. Unsporulated and sporulated oocysts passed in stool are completely non-motile in direct fecal wet mount preparations.

#### Lifecycle & Transmission
*   **Definitive Host:** Humans serve as the sole confirmed natural definitive host and primary reservoir.
*   **Intermediate Host & Vector Dynamics:** Monoxenous parasite (completes its entire life cycle within a single host); no animal intermediate hosts are required. Filth flies and cockroaches can act as mechanical transport vectors by transferring environmental oocysts to food or water.
*   **Transmission Route:** Fecal-oral route through ingestion of mature, sporulated oocysts present in fecally contaminated drinking water, recreational water, or fresh agricultural produce (raspberries, basil, snow peas, baby lettuce leaves / mesclun, pesto). Direct person-to-person or direct fecal-oral transmission is extremely unlikely because oocysts are passed UNSPORULATED and require days to weeks in the external environment to mature and become infectious.
*   **Infective Stage & Portal of Entry:** Ingestion of mature, fully sporulated oocysts (containing 2 sporocysts with 4 sporozoites) via the mouth into the gastrointestinal tract. Following ingestion, excystation occurs in the small intestine, releasing sporozoites that invade enterocytes. The infective dose is estimated to be low (between 10 and 100 oocysts).
*   **Diagnostic Stage & Specimen Source:** Unsporulated oocysts (8–10 µm) containing undifferentiated morula-like cytoplasm passed in stool. Specimen sources include fresh or preserved human stool (feces), duodenal/jejunal aspirates or mucosal biopsy material, and in rare extraintestinal cases, purulent sputum.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Tissue Tropism & Cellular Invasion:** Primary tropism is for the mucosal epithelial cells (enterocytes) of the upper small bowel, specifically the jejunum and distal portion of the duodenum. In severely immunocompromised/AIDS patients, extraintestinal dissemination occurs to the epithelial lining of the biliary tree (gallbladder) and respiratory tract.
*   **Cytopathic Effects & Tissue Destruction:** Intracellular replication causes marked histopathologic changes: blunting and shortening of intestinal villi, crypt hyperplasia, diffuse mucosal edema, vacuolization of surface epithelial cells at villous tips, loss of the microvillous brush border, and transformation of normal columnar enterocytes into flattened cuboidal cells. Induces heavy inflammatory cell infiltration of the lamina propria (composed of [[Plasma Cell|plasma cells]], lymphocytes, PMN neutrophils, and eosinophils). Causes upper GI distress, watery diarrhea, and marked malabsorption of D-xylose.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:**
    - Environmental Maturation Strategy: Shedding unsporulated oocysts into the external environment prevents immediate autoinfection or rapid immune clearance within the same host cycle.
    - Oocyst Wall Chemical Resistance: The multilayered oocyst wall provides extreme resistance to physical drying, environmental stress, and chemical disinfectants, including routine food industry sanitizers, standard commercial swimming pool chlorine, and municipal water chlorination.
    - Strong Agricultural Adhesion: Oocysts attach with high affinity to the surface of fresh produce (raspberries, basil, snow peas) via sticky, undefined surface adhesins that resist routine washing with plain water.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Host protection relies primarily on cell-mediated immunity (CD4+ [[T Cell|T-cell]] responses). In immunocompetent hosts, infection is usually self-limiting (1–2 weeks, though relapses lasting weeks to months occur). In immunocompromised individuals (patients with [[HIV]]/AIDS, CD4+ [[T Cell|T-cell]] depletion), *Cyclospora* causes severe, intractable watery diarrhea lasting >12 weeks, high rates of relapse, and extraintestinal complications (acalculous cholecystitis, respiratory infection).

#### Clinical Syndromes & Complications
*   **Acute Clinical Presentation:**
    - Acute Watery Diarrhea / Cyclosporiasis: Incubation period averages ~1 week (range 1 to 12 days). Begins with a 1-day prodrome of malaise and low-grade fever, followed by abrupt onset of profuse watery, non-bloody diarrhea (up to 6 to 7 stools per day), crampy abdominal pain, flatulence, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, fatigue, myalgia, and significant weight loss. Self-limiting in 3 to 4 days or 1 to 2 weeks in healthy hosts, but characterized by a remitting-relapsing pattern that can recur for 4 to 7 weeks.
*   **Chronic & Extra-Intestinal Disease:**
    - Chronic Relapsing Enteritis: Persistent loose stools, abdominal discomfort, and weight loss lasting for months in untreated non-immune individuals.
    - AIDS-Associated Persistent Cyclosporiasis: Intractable, high-volume watery diarrhea persisting for >12 weeks in patients with advanced [[HIV]]/AIDS.
    - Biliary Cyclosporiasis / Acalculous Cholecystitis: Opportunistic infection of the gallbladder in AIDS patients, presenting with severe right upper quadrant abdominal pain, fever, and elevated liver enzymes, resolving post-treatment.
    - Respiratory Cyclosporiasis: Documented in patients presenting with fever, chronic cough, purulent sputum, and dyspnea mimicking pulmonary tuberculosis (confirmed by nested PCR).
    - Celiac-like Presentation: Infection masquerading clinically and histologically as celiac sprue.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** Microscopic identification of spherical 8 to 10 µm oocysts in stool that are modified acid-fast variable (deep red to pale pink or clear, glassy, wrinkled "ghosts") AND exhibit intense autofluorescence (bright green or intense blue) under UV epifluorescence microscopy.
*   **Host Immune Response & Laboratory Findings:**
    - Stool Examination: Watery, non-bloody stool containing unsporulated oocysts. Fecal leukocytes/PMNs may be present.
    - Hematology: Peripheral blood eosinophilia is identified in many patients.
    - Serology: Commercial serologic antibody tests are unavailable for routine clinical diagnosis (Western blot utilized in research settings).

#### Epidemiology & Exposures
*   **Geographic Endemicity:** Worldwide distribution. Endemic in tropical and subtropical regions, including Peru, Haiti, Guatemala, Mexico, Nepal, India, Indonesia, Nigeria, Egypt, and Turkey. Demonstrates marked seasonal prevalence, peaking during warm, rainy summer months and late spring.
*   **Environmental Reservoir & Water Systems:** Primary reservoir is human feces contaminating surface water, shallow wells, and agricultural soil. Oocysts survive in moist soil and water for extended periods. Extremely resistant to standard municipal drinking water chlorination.
*   **High-Risk Activities & Dietary Exposures:**
    - Foodborne Outbreaks: Consuming imported contaminated fresh produce, including raspberries, basil, baby lettuce leaves (mesclun), snow peas, and pesto.
    - Waterborne Outbreaks: Ingestion of untreated drinking water, surface water, or swimming in contaminated water bodies.
    - Travel: International travel to endemic developing nations.
    - Age / Immune Status: Children in endemic regions (5 times higher risk in Guatemala) and AIDS patients.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:**
    - Biosafety: Standard BSL-2 containment when handling fresh fecal specimens.
    - Outbreak Surveillance: Foodborne and waterborne outbreaks require rapid notification of public health agencies and trace-back investigations of fresh produce imports.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Microscopy & Stains:**
    - Direct Saline / Iodine Wet Mount: Unstained examination under 10x and 40x objectives reveals nonrefractile, spherical oocysts (8–10 µm) containing an undifferentiated central morula mass.
    - Autofluorescence Microscopy (Epifluorescence): Oocysts autofluoresce brilliant green under a 450–490 nm excitation filter or intense blue under a 365 nm excitation filter (calcofluor white filter set). Fast, highly sensitive screening tool.
    - Modified Acid-Fast Stain (Kinyoun / Modified Ziehl-Neelsen): Standard diagnostic stain using 1% to 3% acid-alcohol decolorizer. Oocysts display variable acid-fastness (ranging from deep red/purple to pale pink, mottled, or completely unstained wrinkled/glassy "ghost" spheres measuring 8–10 µm).
    - Routine Permanent Stains (Trichrome / Iron Hematoxylin): DO NOT stain *Cyclospora* oocysts! Oocysts appear as unstained, clear "ghost" cells and are easily missed.
    - Modified Safranin Stain: Heated in a microwave oven; results in uniform, brilliant reddish-orange staining of *Cyclospora* oocysts.
    - Histopathology (H&E): Intracellular meronts, gametocytes, and unsporulated oocysts visible inside parasitophorous vacuoles in jejunal enterocyte brush border/cytoplasm.
*   **Concentration & Stool O&P Techniques:**
    - Formalin-Ethyl Acetate Sedimentation Concentration: Centrifugation at 500 × g for 10 minutes concentrates oocysts from stool. Modified zinc sulfate centrifugal flotation or Sheather's sugar flotation can also be used.
    - Flow Cytometry: High-throughput automated screening option for large numbers of clinical specimens during outbreak investigations.
*   **Serology & Rapid Antigen Tests:**
    - Antibody / Antigen Detection: Commercial fecal antigen detection kits (EIA/DFA/rapid cartridges) do NOT exist for *Cyclospora*. Commercial serum antibody tests are unavailable for clinical diagnosis.
*   **Molecular Diagnostics:**
    - PCR & Nested PCR: DNA amplification targeting small subunit (18S) rRNA or 70-kDa heat shock protein (HSP70) genes. Digestion of amplicons with *MnlI* restriction enzyme (PCR-RFLP) discriminates *Cyclospora* from *Eimeria* species.
    - Commercial Multiplex Molecular Panels: Automated FDA-cleared multiplex PCR panels (e.g., BioFire FilmArray Gastrointestinal Panel) simultaneously detect *Cyclospora cayetanensis* along with other enteric pathogens directly from stool or Cary-Blair media.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Anti-Protozoal Agents:**
    - [[Cotrimoxazole|Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole]]: Drug of choice.
      - Adult Dosage: 1 Double Strength (DS) tablet (160 mg TMP / 800 mg SMX) PO BID for 7 to 10 days. Eradicates infection in >90% of cases; extending therapy for an additional 3 days clears most remaining infections.
      - Pediatric Dosage: TMP 10 mg/kg/day + SMX 50 mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses for 7 to 10 days.
*   **Alternative Treatments:**
    - [[Ciprofloxacin]]: 500 mg PO BID for 7 days (second-line option for sulfa-allergic patients; less effective than [[Cotrimoxazole|TMP-SMX]]).
    - [[Nitazoxanide]]: Used as an alternative agent in some clinical settings.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Dosing Considerations:**
    - Secondary Prophylaxis in [[HIV]]/AIDS: [[HIV]]-infected patients with severe CD4 depletion suffer high relapse rates following standard 7- to 10-day treatment. Secondary chemoprophylaxis with [[Cotrimoxazole|TMP-SMX]] (1 DS tablet PO 3 times weekly or 1 DS BID) is recommended until immune reconstitution is achieved via HAART.
*   **Prophylaxis, Water Treatment & Vector Control:**
    - Water Safety: Boil drinking water vigorously for 1 minute (10 minutes ensures full destruction). Routine chemical chlorination of water does NOT destroy *Cyclospora* oocysts.
    - Food Safety: Wash raw fruits and vegetables thoroughly prior to consumption; however, oocysts adhere strongly to produce surfaces (raspberries, lettuce, basil, snow peas), so washing alone may not completely eliminate risk.
    - Chemoprophylaxis: [[Cotrimoxazole|TMP-SMX]] is effective for chemoprophylaxis but is generally reserved for high-risk immunocompromised individuals.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "Autofluoresces strong green or intense blue under UV light".
    - "Modified acid-fast variable oocysts (8–10 µm)" (larger than *[[Cryptosporidium]]* 4–6 µm).
    - "Unsporulated oocyst passed in fresh stool" (requires days to weeks in environment to mature).
    - "2 sporocysts, each containing 2 sporozoites" (total 4 sporozoites per mature oocyst).
    - "Fresh produce outbreaks" (imported raspberries, basil, snow peas, mesclun).
    - "Cyanobacterium-like body (CLB)" / "Coccidian-like body" (historical descriptor).
    - "Hot safranin microwave stain" (yields uniform reddish-orange oocysts).
    - "[[Cotrimoxazole|Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole]] is first-line treatment".
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - Unlike *[[Cryptosporidium]]* oocysts (which are fully sporulated and immediately infectious when shed in stool), *Cyclospora cayetanensis* oocysts are passed UNSPORULATED and require 5 to 14 days in the environment to mature, making direct person-to-person transmission virtually impossible.
    - Routine stool O&P permanent stains (Wheatley's trichrome or iron hematoxylin) FAIL to stain *Cyclospora* oocysts, which appear as clear, unstained "ghost" cells; special modified acid-fast stains, autofluorescence, or multiplex PCR must be used.
    - Standard water chlorination levels fail to destroy *Cyclospora* oocysts; water must be boiled for complete inactivation.
    - Unlike *[[Cryptosporidium]]* (where [[Nitazoxanide]] is first-line in non-immunocompromised hosts and no drug is completely curative in AIDS), *Cyclospora* infection is highly treatable and rapidly eradicated with [[Cotrimoxazole|Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole]].
