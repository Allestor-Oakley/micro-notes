### Leishmania tropica

#### Protozoan Biology & Microscopic Morphology
*   **Trophozoite Stage Features:** N/A (Extracellular flagellated stage is the promastigote). Belongs to the subgenus *Leishmania*, complex *Leishmania tropica*. Promastigotes are elongated, slender, spindle-shaped, motile extracellular stages measuring 15 to 30 µm in length by 1.5 to 3.5 µm (or 2 to 3 µm) in width. Possesses a single, long, free anterior flagellum measuring 15 to 28 µm in length. Contains a central spherical nucleus and an anterior, rod-shaped, dark-staining kinetoplast (a specialized DNA-containing structure within the single large mitochondrion) located near the base of the flagellum. Promastigote development in the [[Sand flies|sand fly]] vector is restricted to the anterior portion of the alimentary tract (suprapylarian development pattern characteristic of the subgenus *Leishmania*).
*   **Cyst / Oocyst Stage Features:** N/A. *Leishmania tropica* produces no cyst or oocyst stage in its life cycle.
*   **Intracellular Morphologies:** Amastigote stage (Leishman-Donovan body). Small, ovoid or spherical non-motile intracellular forms measuring 2 to 6 µm in length (typically 2 to 3 µm or 3 to 5 µm; 1 to 3 µm in paraffin-embedded tissue sections). Resides and multiplies inside phagolysosomes / parasitophorous vacuoles of host mononuclear phagocytes and tissue dermal [[Macrophage|macrophages]]. Microscopic structure features two distinct dark-staining intracellular bodies: a large, round, dark-staining nucleus ("dot") and a small, distinct, rod-like or bar-shaped kinetoplast ("dash") located adjacent to the nucleus, representing the mitochondrial kinetoplast DNA and flagellar basal axoneme.
*   **Locomotion Mechanics:** Promastigotes exhibit active, directional flagellar motility in culture or vector gut, propelled by the whip-like beating of the single free anterior flagellum. Amastigotes are non-motile intracellular forms.

#### Lifecycle & Transmission
*   **Definitive Host:** Mammals (primarily humans in urban anthroponotic cycles). Note: Mammals act as vertebrate hosts for the asexual amastigote stage, while female phlebotomine [[sand flies]] serve as insect vectors / intermediate hosts for developmental promastigotes. Humans are presumably the main or sole reservoir host for urban *L. tropica*.
*   **Intermediate Host & Vector Dynamics:** Transmitted by blood-feeding adult female Old World phlebotomine [[sand flies]] belonging to the genus *Phlebotomus* (specifically *Phlebotomus sergenti*, *Phlebotomus papatasi*, and *Phlebotomus chabaudi*). Ingested amastigotes transform in the [[Sand flies|sand fly]] anterior gut (suprapylarian development) into promastigotes, which multiply by binary/longitudinal fission, undergo metacyclogenesis, and migrate forward to the fly pharynx and proboscis to be inoculated during subsequent blood meals.
*   **Transmission Route:**
    - Vector-borne: Inoculation of metacyclic promastigotes into human skin during a blood meal taken by an infected female sand fly.
    - Anthroponotic Transmission: Direct human-vector-human transmission cycle predominant in urban centers (e.g., Kabul, Afghanistan; Baghdad, Iraq; Aleppo, Syria), where humans serve as the primary infection reservoir.
    - Non-Vector Transmission: Direct inoculation of material from active sores ("leishmanization"); rare parenteral routes include vertical/congenital transmission, blood transfusion, organ transplantation, needle sharing among intravenous drug users, and accidental laboratory needle-stick or skin microabrasion exposures.
*   **Infective Stage & Portal of Entry:** Inoculation of motile metacyclic promastigotes into human skin via the proboscis of a feeding female [[Sand flies|sand fly]]. Injected promastigotes bind to host dermal macrophages, undergo phagocytosis, lose their flagella, transform into amastigotes within parasitophorous vacuoles, and replicate until host cells rupture to infect surrounding histiocytes.
*   **Diagnostic Stage & Specimen Source:** Intracellular amastigotes (Leishman-Donovan bodies) inside [[Macrophage|macrophages]]/[[Monocyte|monocytes]], or promastigotes in culture. Specimen sources include full-thickness skin punch biopsies (2–4 mm) taken from the active, raised, indurated margin or base of skin ulcers; tissue scrapings/slits from ulcer margins; fine-needle aspirates beneath uninvolved skin surrounding ulcers; and fine-needle aspirates/biopsies of [[bone marrow]], liver, or [[spleen]] and peripheral blood buffy coat in rare viscerotropic syndrome cases.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Tissue Tropism & Cellular Invasion:** Predominantly dermotropic, demonstrating high tropism for dermal [[Macrophage|macrophages]] and histiocytes in the cutaneous layers of exposed skin. In rare viscerotropic syndrome cases, exhibits viscerotropic dissemination to reticuloendothelial cells of visceral organs (spleen, liver, [[bone marrow]], and [[lymph nodes]]).
*   **Cytopathic Effects & Tissue Destruction:**
    - Localized Urban Cutaneous Disease ("Dry" Sore): Intracellular replication of amastigotes in dermal [[Macrophage|macrophages]] triggers localized inflammatory infiltration (lymphocytes, [[Plasma Cell|plasma cells]], histiocytes) and granuloma formation. Lesions develop slowly over weeks to months, becoming swollen, moderately hyperkeratotic, less necrotic, and less exudative than *L. major* ("dry" type). Epidermal penetration results in central ulceration with a thick, adhering crust.
    - Leishmaniasis Recidivans (LR): Chronic, persistent relapsing form where central healing of the primary ulcer occurs alongside active, satellite, papular, hyperkeratotic, or lupus-like lesions erupting along the indurated peripheral margins. Driven by a hypersensitive, hyper-reactive cellular immune response with dense granulomatous inflammation and minimal/scarce demonstrable parasites.
    - Viscerotropic Leishmaniasis: Mild, non-fatal systemic dissemination of amastigotes to visceral reticuloendothelial organs ([[spleen]], [[bone marrow]], liver) without causing classic fulminant visceral destruction (kala-azar).
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:**
    - Intracellular [[Macrophage]] Survival: Adapts to reside inside host [[macrophage]] phagolysosomes, resisting lysosomal digestive enzymes and oxidative burst products.
    - Surface Coat Modifications: Promastigote-to-amastigote transformation downregulates surface flagellar antigens and alters glycoconjugates to evade extracellular host antibody and complement-mediated lysis.
    - Host Immune Modulation: Induces specific cell-mediated immune alterations or localized delayed-type hypersensitivity states (e.g., Leishmaniasis Recidivans).
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Protection against *L. tropica* relies on robust Th1 cell-mediated immunity (Interferon-gamma, Interleukin-12, TNF-alpha) and [[macrophage]] activation. In immunocompromised hosts (e.g., [[HIV]]/AIDS co-infection, organ transplant recipients receiving immunosuppressive agents), *L. tropica* infection can present with diffuse or disseminated cutaneous lesions (DCL), multiple atypical ulcerations, refractory non-healing lesions, or increased propensity for viscerotropic spread and chronic relapses.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Complications
*   **Acute Clinical Presentation:**
    - Acute Urban Cutaneous Leishmaniasis ("Dry" Cutaneous Sore / Oriental Sore / Baghdad Boil / Delhi Boil / Aleppo Evil): Incubation period is prolonged, ranging from weeks to several months (often 2 to 8+ months, longer and slower to develop than *L. major*). Begins as a small, red, pruritic, erythematous papule at the [[Sand flies|sand fly]] bite site on exposed skin (face, arms, legs). The papule slowly enlarges over months into a swollen, indurated plaque or nodule that crusts over. Ulcers tend to be single or few (1–2 lesions), dry, less necrotic, with little exudate and a thick, adherent crust surrounded by a raised margin.
*   **Chronic & Extra-Intestinal Disease:**
    - Leishmaniasis Recidivans (LR / Chronic Relapsing Cutaneous Leishmaniasis): A chronic, slowly progressive, non-healing or relapsing cutaneous manifestation that can persist for years to decades (up to 40 years). Occurs months to years after initial primary lesion healing. Characterized by a central healed, hypopigmented scar surrounded by an active, hyperkeratotic, erythematous ring of papules or nodules (lupus vulgaris-like appearance). Causes extensive facial disfigurement and severe scarring. Highly refractory to standard pentavalent antimonial therapy.
    - Viscerotropic Leishmaniasis (Desert Storm Viscerotropic Syndrome): Recognized in a group of US military personnel deployed to Saudi Arabia/Persian Gulf during Operation Desert Storm (1990–1991). Presents with unexplained systemic symptoms: chronic fatigue, low-grade irregular fever, abdominal pain, malaise, lymphadenopathy, and subtle liver/[[spleen]] involvement without classic signs of severe visceral leishmaniasis (kala-azar).
    - Diffuse / Disseminated Cutaneous Leishmaniasis (rare): Non-ulcerating nodular lesions across the body in anergic or immunocompromised patients.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:**
    - Dry, slow-healing, single or double cutaneous ulcer with thick crusting and indurated margins on exposed skin in urban Middle Eastern settings.
    - Leishmaniasis Recidivans: Active papular nodular ring erupting along the scar borders of a previously healed primary cutaneous ulcer.
    - Microscopic identification of Leishman-Donovan bodies (intracellular amastigotes displaying a characteristic "dot-and-dash" nucleus and kinetoplast) inside dermal [[Macrophage|macrophages]].
*   **Host Immune Response & Laboratory Findings:**
    - Leishmanin Skin Test (LST / Montenegro Test): Strongly POSITIVE in active *L. tropica* CL and exceptionally strong in Leishmaniasis Recidivans (LR) due to intense delayed-type hypersensitivity. Negative in rare anergic diffuse forms.
    - Serology: Anti-*Leishmania* humoral antibodies are variable, low, or undetectable in simple CL and Desert Storm viscerotropic syndrome (making serology unreliable for diagnosis).
    - Histopathology: Dense granulomatous cellular infiltrate in the dermis composed of histiocytes, lymphocytes, and [[Plasma Cell|plasma cells]]. Parasites are present in moderate numbers in early active CL, but are EXTREMELY SCARCE or absent in chronic Leishmaniasis Recidivans (LR) tissue biopsy sections.

#### Epidemiology & Exposures
*   **Geographic Endemicity:** Old World distribution. High prevalence in the Middle East (Afghanistan (major urban focus in Kabul), Iraq (Baghdad), Iran, Syria (Aleppo), Turkey), North Africa, the Mediterranean basin, India, Pakistan, Central Asia, and former USSR countries.
*   **Environmental Reservoir & Water Systems:** Urban environments. Unlike *L. major* (which is rural and rodent-borne), *L. tropica* causes URBAN cutaneous leishmaniasis where humans are presumbly the main or sole reservoir host (anthroponotic cycle). Vector [[sand flies]] breed in urban cracks in brick/mud walls, rubbish heaps, animal shelters, and stone crevices near human dwellings.
*   **High-Risk Activities & Dietary Exposures:**
    - Occupational & Environmental Exposures: Residence or travel in overcrowded urban centers in endemic Middle Eastern/South Asian regions (e.g., Kabul, Baghdad, Aleppo); military deployment to desert/urban combat zones (e.g., US military personnel in Operation Desert Storm / Operation Iraqi Freedom); living in dwellings with mud walls or dirt floors.
    - [[Sand flies|Sand Fly]] Biting Habits: Vector sand flies (*Phlebotomus sergenti*) feed primarily during evening, night, or dusk hours outdoors or inside un-screened homes.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:**
    - Biosafety: Standard BSL-2 precautions when handling clinical tissue scrapings, biopsy material, aspirates, or cultures. Laboratory-acquired infection can occur via accidental needle-stick or skin microabrasion contact with infective culture promastigotes or animal tissues.
    - Public Health & Vector Control: Active case detection and prompt treatment of human cases (eliminating the primary human transmission reservoir); indoor residual spraying (IRS with insecticides such as DDT or pyrethroids); use of insecticide-treated bed nets (ITNs / LLINs); urban sanitation and repair of building wall cracks.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Microscopy & Stains:**
    - Direct Stained Smears (GOLD STANDARD FOR PARASITOLOGICAL CONFIRMATION): Giemsa or Wright's stain of air-dried, methanol-fixed touch preparations, impression smears, skin scrapings, or fine-needle aspirates taken from the active, raised border or base of skin ulcers. Visualizes intracellular and extracellular amastigotes (2–4 µm) displaying pale blue cytoplasm, a round red/purple nucleus, and a distinct, dark, rod-like kinetoplast ("dot and dash").
    - Histopathology: Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E), Gomori's methenamine silver (GMS), or Brown-Hopps tissue Gram stain of 2–4 mm skin punch biopsies. Reveals dermal granulomatous inflammation. Note: Amastigotes are visible inside histiocytes in early lesions, but are EXTREMELY FEW / SCANT in Leishmaniasis Recidivans (LR) tissue sections.
*   **Concentration & Stool O&P Techniques:**
    - Fecal O&P Warning: Stool O&P examination is NOT applicable (*L. tropica* does not infect the gastrointestinal tract).
    - In Vitro Culture (Primary Isolation): Inoculation of triturated skin biopsy material or needle aspirates into biphasic media (Novy-MacNeal-Nicolle (NNN) medium with rabbit blood overlay, Evan's modified Tobie's medium, 4N medium) or liquid media (Schneider's *Drosophila* medium supplemented with 30% fetal bovine serum). Promastigotes develop in the fluid overlay at 20°C to 25°C within 2 to 7 days (or up to 4 weeks before declaring negative). Motile promastigotes often arrange in characteristic "rosette" clusters under 40x microscopy. Microculture methods enhance detection sensitivity.
    - Animal Inoculation: Intraperitoneal or footpad inoculation into young Syrian golden hamsters (2–4 months old).
*   **Serology & Rapid Antigen Tests:**
    - Leishmanin Skin Test (LST / Montenegro Test): Intradermal injection of 0.1 mL of standardized killed *Leishmania* promastigote antigen. Read at 48 hours; induration ≥5 mm is positive. Strongly positive in *L. tropica* CL and LR, reflecting intact cell-mediated immunity.
    - Serum Antibody Assays: Indirect Immunofluorescence Assays (IFA) and ELISAs. Anti-*Leishmania* antibody titers in *L. tropica* CL and Desert Storm viscerotropic syndrome are typically LOW or UNDETECTABLE, limiting the utility of serology for individual diagnosis.
*   **Molecular Diagnostics (METHOD OF CHOICE FOR SPECIES IDENTIFICATION):**
    - Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR): Real-time PCR and PCR-RFLP targeting kinetoplast DNA (kDNA) minicircle sequences (sensitivity 87–100%), small subunit (18S) rRNA, internal transcribed spacer (ITS) regions, or spliced-leader RNA directly from skin scrapings, biopsy tissue, or filter paper blood spots.
    - Diagnostic Importance: Highly sensitive (>90–98%), detecting parasites even in Leishmaniasis Recidivans or viscerotropic cases where amastigotes are extremely scarce. Uniquely capable of identifying *L. tropica* to the species level and distinguishing it from co-endemic Old World species (*L. major*, *L. donovani*, *L. aethiopica*).

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Anti-Protozoal Agents:**
    - Systemic Pentavalent Antimonials: [[Sodium Stibogluconate]] (Pentostam) or Meglumine Antimonate (Glucantime).
      - Dosage: 20 mg Sb5+/kg/day IV or IM for 10 to 20 days (maximum daily dose capped at 1,215 mg Sb5+/day to reduce toxicity). Standard systemic treatment for extensive, multiple, or facial *L. tropica* CL lesions.
    - Intralesional Pentavalent Antimonials PLUS Cryotherapy (Local Combined Therapy):
      - Infiltration of 1 to 5 mL of [[sodium stibogluconate]] / meglumine antimonate directly into the base of the lesion combined with liquid nitrogen cryotherapy every 3 to 7 days for up to 5 sessions. Preferred for simple, low-risk, single lesions.
    - Oral Miltefosine (Impavido):
      - Dose: 2.5 mg/kg/day PO (for adults ≥45 kg: 50 mg PO BID; for 30–44 kg: 50 mg PO once daily) for 28 days. Efficacious oral option for Old World CL. Contraindicated in pregnancy (teratogenic; strict contraception required during treatment and for 5 months post-therapy).
*   **Alternative Treatments:**
    - Topical [[Paromomycin]] Ointment: 15% [[Paromomycin]] plus 12% methylbenzethonium chloride ointment applied topically BID for 20 days.
    - Oral [[Fluconazole]]: 200 mg PO daily for 6 weeks (promotes accelerated healing in Old World CL).
    - Thermotherapy: Localized current radiofrequency heat therapy applied directly to skin lesions.
    - [[Liposomal Amphotericin B]] (AmBisome): 2 to 3 mg/kg/day IV infusion (total cumulative dose 20–40 mg/kg). Preferred for pregnant/breastfeeding women or severe antimonial-refractory cases.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Dosing Considerations:**
    - Slow Healing & Treatment Failure: *L. tropica* lesions develop and heal more slowly than *L. major* (can take a year or more for self-cure). Leishmaniasis Recidivans (LR) is notoriously refractory to standard antimonial monotherapy, often requiring prolonged systemic combination regimens or surgical scar revision.
    - Self-Cure Immunity: Spontaneous healing confers long-term homologous strain immunity; historical "leishmanization" (inoculating live *L. tropica* from active sores onto covered body sites) was practiced in the Middle East to confer lifelong immunity and prevent disfiguring facial scars.
*   **Prophylaxis, Water Treatment & Vector Control:**
    - Personal Protective Measures: Use insect repellents containing DEET or Picaridin on exposed skin; wear long-sleeved shirts and full-length trousers; sleep under [[permethrin]]-impregnated fine-mesh bed nets ([[sand flies]] are tiny (1.5–3.5 mm) and easily penetrate standard mosquito netting).
    - Environmental & Vector Control: Indoor residual spraying (IRS) with insecticides (pyrethroids, DDT) on urban wall surfaces; repair plaster and wall cracks in homes to eliminate *Phlebotomus sergenti* resting and breeding sites.
    - Chemoprophylaxis: N/A. No prophylactic drug regimen exists.
    - Vaccine Status: No approved human vaccine currently available for *L. tropica*.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "Urban dry cutaneous leishmaniasis" (slower-developing, less necrotic lesion than *L. major*).
    - "Leishmaniasis recidivans (LR)" (chronic relapsing lupus-like papular ring around an old healed scar).
    - "Phlebotomus sergenti" (primary urban [[Sand flies|sand fly]] vector).
    - "Desert Storm viscerotropic syndrome" (systemic non-fatal *L. tropica* infection in Persian Gulf War veterans).
    - "Anthroponotic Old World CL" (humans are the primary reservoir).
    - "Kabul, Afghanistan" (largest global urban focus of *L. tropica* CL).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - Unlike *Leishmania major* (which causes "wet" rural zoonotic CL with desert rodents as reservoirs), *Leishmania tropica* causes "dry" URBAN anthroponotic CL with humans as the primary reservoir host.
    - Although *L. tropica* is fundamentally a dermotropic cutaneous parasite, it was discovered to cause a non-fatal, systemic "viscerotropic syndrome" without classic skin ulcers in US military veterans deployed during the Persian Gulf War (Operation Desert Storm).
    - Parasites are EXTREMELY SCARCE or absent in tissue biopsy sections of Leishmaniasis Recidivans (LR) due to intense host cellular hypersensitivity, making microscopic diagnosis on H&E or Giemsa smears difficult and necessitating PCR.
    - Serologic antibody detection is generally negative or shows low/undetectable antibody titers in patients with *L. tropica* cutaneous leishmaniasis and viscerotropic syndrome, unlike visceral leishmaniasis (*L. donovani*) which presents with abundant polyclonal hypergammaglobulinemia.
