### Leishmania braziliensis

#### Protozoan Biology & Microscopic Morphology
*   **Trophozoite Stage Features:** N/A (Extracellular flagellated stage is the promastigote). Belongs to the subgenus *Viannia* (complex *Leishmania braziliensis*). Promastigotes are elongated, slender, spindle-shaped, motile extracellular stages measuring 15 to 30 µm in length by 1.5 to 3.5 µm (or 2 to 3 µm) in width. Possesses a single, long, free anterior flagellum measuring 15 to 28 µm in length. Contains a central spherical nucleus and an anterior, rod-shaped, dark-staining kinetoplast (a specialized DNA-containing structure within the single large mitochondrion) located near the base of the flagellum. Promastigotes develop within the midgut and hindgut (peripylarian development pattern characteristic of the subgenus *Viannia*) of phlebotomine [[sand flies]] before migrating forward to the mouthparts/proboscis.
*   **Cyst / Oocyst Stage Features:** N/A. *Leishmania braziliensis* produces no cyst or oocyst stage in its life cycle.
*   **Intracellular Morphologies:** Amastigote stage (Leishman-Donovan body). Small, ovoid or spherical non-motile intracellular forms measuring 2 to 6 µm in length (typically 2 to 3 µm or 3 to 5 µm; 1 to 3 µm in paraffin-embedded tissue sections). Resides and multiplies inside phagolysosomes / parasitophorous vacuoles of host mononuclear phagocytes and tissue [[Macrophage|macrophages]]. Microscopic structure features two distinct dark-staining intracellular bodies: a large, round, dark-staining nucleus ("dot") and a small, distinct, rod-like or bar-shaped kinetoplast ("dash") located adjacent to the nucleus, representing the mitochondrial kinetoplast DNA and flagellar basal axoneme.
*   **Locomotion Mechanics:** Promastigotes exhibit active, directional flagellar motility in culture or vector gut, propelled by the whip-like beating of the single free anterior flagellum. Amastigotes are non-motile intracellular forms.

#### Lifecycle & Transmission
*   **Definitive Host:** Mammals (primarily humans; wild forest mammals including rodents (e.g., *Agouti paca*), sloths, anteaters, tapirs, opossums, and wild carnivores act as natural vertebrate reservoir hosts). Note: Mammals serve as vertebrate hosts for the asexual amastigote stage, while female phlebotomine [[sand flies]] serve as insect vectors / intermediate hosts for developmental promastigotes. No domestic animal reservoirs are established.
*   **Intermediate Host & Vector Dynamics:** Transmitted by blood-feeding adult female New World phlebotomine [[sand flies]] belonging to the genus *Lutzomyia* (e.g., *Lutzomyia wellcomei*, *Lutzomyia complexa*, *Lutzomyia whitmani*, *Lutzomyia intermedia*) and *Psychodopygus* (e.g., *Psychodopygus ayrozai*). Ingested amastigotes transform in the [[Sand flies|sand fly]] midgut and hindgut (peripylarian development) into promastigotes, which multiply by binary/longitudinal fission, undergo metacyclogenesis, and migrate forward to the fly pharynx and proboscis to be inoculated during subsequent blood meals.
*   **Transmission Route:** Vector-borne transmission via inoculation of metacyclic promastigotes into human skin during a blood meal taken by an infected female sand fly. Rare non-vector transmission routes include vertical/congenital transmission, blood transfusion, organ transplantation, needle sharing among intravenous drug users, and accidental laboratory needle-stick or microabrasion exposures. Direct contact with unbroken skin of active lesions is non-infectious (requires mucosal/epidermal inoculation).
*   **Infective Stage & Portal of Entry:** Inoculation of motile metacyclic promastigotes into human skin via the proboscis of a feeding female sand fly. Injected promastigotes bind to host dermal [[Macrophage|macrophages]], undergo phagocytosis, lose their flagella, transform into amastigotes within parasitophorous vacuoles, and replicate until host macrophages rupture to infect surrounding histiocytes.
*   **Diagnostic Stage & Specimen Source:** Intracellular amastigotes (Leishman-Donovan bodies) inside tissue [[Macrophage|macrophages]]/[[Monocyte|monocytes]], or promastigotes in culture. Specimen sources include full-thickness skin punch biopsies (2–4 mm) taken from the active, raised, indurated margin or base of skin ulcers; tissue scrapings/slits from ulcer margins; fine-needle aspirates beneath uninvolved skin surrounding ulcers; mucosal biopsies/scrapings of nasal or oral lesions (for mucocutaneous leishmaniasis); and regional lymph node aspirates.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Tissue Tropism & Cellular Invasion:** Demonstrates dual tropism: dermotropic (dermal macrophages of the skin) and mucotropic (mucosal epithelial and submucosal [[Macrophage|macrophages]] of the upper respiratory and oral cavities, including the nasal septum, hard/soft palate, pharynx, larynx, vocal cords, and lips). Also invades regional lymphatic drainage channels and lymph nodes.
*   **Cytopathic Effects & Tissue Destruction:** Initial intracellular multiplication in dermal [[Macrophage|macrophages]] produces a localized skin papule that ulcerates. In 1% to 3% (up to 5%) of infected individuals, parasites disseminate via blood or lymphatics to the upper respiratory mucosa (mucocutaneous leishmaniasis / espundia).
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:**
    - *Leishmaniavirus 1* (LRV1): Many *L. braziliensis* strains are persistently infected with an endosymbiotic double-stranded RNA virus (LRV1). When parasitized [[Macrophage|macrophages]] lyse, viral dsRNA released from LRV1 acts as a potent ligand for host endosomal Toll-like Receptor 3 (TLR3), triggering massive pro-inflammatory cytokine production (TLR3-driven cascade), inhibiting [[macrophage]] microbicidal activity, enhancing parasite survival and persistence, and driving metastatic dissemination to mucosal tissues.
    - Subgenus *Viannia* adaptations and macrophage parasitophorous vacuole survival.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:**
    - Immunopathogenesis of Espundia (MCL): Unlike visceral leishmaniasis (which features immunosuppression/anergy), MCL is driven by an EXUBERANT, HYPER-REACTIVE Th1 pro-inflammatory cellular immune response with high production of Interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) and Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha (TNF-α), combined with defective suppression by Interleukin-10 (IL-10) and Transforming Growth Factor-beta (TGF-β). This hyperactive immune cascade causes massive [[T Cell|T-cell]] lymphoproliferation, localized tissue necrosis, and destructive erosion of cartilaginous structures (nasal septum, soft palate, larynx), even though amastigotes are extremely scarce/difficult to find in mucosal lesions.
    - In immunocompromised hosts (e.g., [[HIV]]/AIDS co-infection, organ transplant recipients receiving immunosuppressants), *L. braziliensis* can present with disseminated anergic cutaneous lesions, severe visceralization, atypical ocular involvement, or refractory mucosal ulcerations with high treatment failure and relapse rates.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Complications
*   **Acute Clinical Presentation:**
    - Localized New World Cutaneous Leishmaniasis (CL / American Cutaneous Leishmaniasis): Incubation period ranges from weeks to months (typically 2 to 8 weeks) following a [[Sand flies|sand fly]] bite. Begins as an erythematous, pruritic papule at the bite site on exposed skin (arms, legs, face). The papule enlarges, becomes scaly, and develops a central ulcer with elevated, indurated, "volcano-like" or "rolled" margins. Lesions may be single or multiple, often accompanied by satellite papules or regional lymphadenopathy. Cutaneous ulcers heal very slowly (requiring 6 to 18+ months for self-cure), leaving prominent, hypopigmented or hyperpigmented depressed scars.
*   **Chronic & Extra-Intestinal Disease:**
    - Mucocutaneous Leishmaniasis (MCL / Espundia / Uta / Utu): A chronic, destructive metastatic complication occurring in ~1% to 3% (up to 5%) of patients infected with *L. braziliensis*. Occurs months, years, or even decades after the primary cutaneous ulcer has partially or completely healed (or rarely while the skin lesion is still active).
      - Clinical Evolution: Initial presentation begins with chronic nasal stuffiness, erythema, rhinorrhea, crusting, epistaxis, and nodular swelling of the nasal septum.
      - Progressive Destruction: Ulceration rapidly destroys the cartilaginous nasal septum (sparing the bony septum), resulting in collapse of the nasal bridge and the classic facial deformity known as "tapir nose" (*nariz de tapir*). Disease extends to the anterior nasal mucosa, columnella, upper lip, hard and soft palate, pharynx, larynx, and vocal cords.
      - Complications: Severe pain, extensive facial disfigurement, mutilation, difficulty chewing/swallowing (dysphagia), loss of speech/hoarseness (dysphonia), upper airway obstruction, microaspiration, secondary bacterial infection, and death from asphyxiation or bacterial aspiration pneumonia.
    - Disseminated Cutaneous Leishmaniasis (DCL) / Anergic Forms: Multiple non-healing ulcerated or nodular lesions across the body in severely immunocompromised patients ([[HIV]]/AIDS).
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:**
    - "Tapir nose" (*nariz de tapir*) deformity resulting from subtotal destruction and perforation of the cartilaginous nasal septum and swelling of the upper lip.
    - Painless, indurated cutaneous ulcer with raised, rolled borders and central crater on exposed limbs, accompanied by satellite daughter lesions.
    - Microscopic identification of intracellular amastigotes with a "dot-and-dash" nucleus and kinetoplast inside dermal [[Macrophage|macrophages]], or promastigotes in culture/PCR.
*   **Host Immune Response & Laboratory Findings:**
    - Leishmanin Skin Test (LST / Montenegro Test): Strongly POSITIVE in active *L. braziliensis* CL and MCL due to intense delayed-type hypersensitivity (induration ≥5 mm after 48 hours).
    - Serology: Anti-*Leishmania* IgG antibodies are consistently elevated and detectable in serum of patients with MCL (due to hyperactive immune state), but variable/low in localized CL.
    - Tissue Histopathology: Dense, granulomatous cellular infiltrate in the dermis and mucosal submucosa (lymphocytes, [[Plasma Cell|plasma cells]], histiocytes, epithelioid cells, and giant cells) with extensive tissue necrosis. Amastigotes are present in moderate numbers in early skin ulcers, but are characteristically EXTREMELY SCARCE or absent in chronic mucosal (MCL) tissue biopsy sections.

#### Epidemiology & Exposures
*   **Geographic Endemicity:** Endemic throughout Neotropical regions of Central and South America (New World / American Leishmaniasis). High prevalence in Brazil (especially Amazon basin and northeastern Brazil), Peru (Andean valleys and Amazonian lowlands), Bolivia, Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, Guatemala, Belize, Costa Rica, Panama, Paraguay, Argentina, and French Guiana.
*   **Environmental Reservoir & Water Systems:** Zoonotic cycle maintained in humid tropical rainforests, forested river valleys, and agricultural jungle fringes. Reservoirs include wild forest mammals (forest rodents like *Agouti paca*, sloths, anteaters, tapirs, opossums, and wild carnivores). [[Sand flies|Sand fly]] vector species breed in humid forest leaf litter, tree hollows, animal burrows, and dense vegetation.
*   **High-Risk Activities & Dietary Exposures:**
    - Occupational & Environmental Exposures: Timber cutters, road construction workers, rubber/chicle harvesters (*chicleros*), agricultural field laborers, oil explorers, military personnel operating in jungle environments, eco-tourists, hikers, and researchers in Amazonian rainforests.
    - [[Sand flies|Sand Fly]] Biting Habits: Vector [[sand flies]] (*Lutzomyia* / *Psychodopygus*) feed primarily during evening, night, or dusk hours, but will bite aggressively during the day if disturbed in shaded forest understory.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:**
    - Biosafety: Standard BSL-2 precautions when handling clinical tissue biopsy specimens, aspirates, or cultures. Laboratory-acquired infection can occur via accidental needle-sticks or skin microabrasion contact with infective culture promastigotes or animal tissues.
    - Public Health Significance: Reportable infectious disease in endemic Latin American nations (e.g., PAHO leishmaniasis surveillance). Patients do not require isolation or contact precautions (direct skin-to-skin contact is harmless).

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Microscopy & Stains:**
    - Direct Stained Smears (GOLD STANDARD FOR PARASITOLOGICAL CONFIRMATION): Giemsa or Wright's stain of air-dried, methanol-fixed impression smears, skin scrapings, or fine-needle aspirates taken from the active, raised border or base of skin ulcers. Visualizes intracellular and extracellular amastigotes (2–4 µm) displaying pale blue cytoplasm, a round red/purple nucleus, and a distinct, dark, rod-like kinetoplast ("dot and dash").
    - Histopathology: Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E), Gomori's methenamine silver (GMS), or Brown-Hopps tissue Gram stain of 2–4 mm skin or mucosal punch biopsies. Reveals dermal granulomatous inflammation. Note: Amastigotes are easier to spot on touch preps/impression smears than in paraffin tissue sections where they appear smaller (1–3 µm) and cut at varied angles. In mucosal (MCL) biopsies, amastigotes are extremely scarce due to intense local immune destruction.
*   **Concentration & Stool O&P Techniques:**
    - Fecal O&P Warning: Stool O&P examination is NOT applicable (*L. braziliensis* does not infect the gastrointestinal tract).
    - In Vitro Culture (Primary Isolation): Inoculation of triturated tissue biopsy material or needle aspirates into biphasic media (Novy-MacNeal-Nicolle (NNN) medium with rabbit blood overlay, Evan's modified Tobie's medium, 4N medium) or liquid media (Schneider's *Drosophila* medium supplemented with 30% fetal bovine serum). Promastigotes develop in the fluid overlay at 20°C to 25°C within 2 to 7 days (or up to 4 weeks before declaring negative). Motile promastigotes often arrange in characteristic "rosette" clusters under 40x microscopy.
    - SPECIAL CULTURE NOTE: The **4N medium** formulation was specifically adapted from original formulas for the primary isolation of the *Leishmania braziliensis* complex in Latin America.
    - Animal Inoculation: Intraperitoneal, footpad, or nose inoculation into young Syrian golden hamsters (2–4 months old); slow development (requires weeks to months for swelling/amastigotes to appear).
*   **Serology & Rapid Antigen Tests:**
    - Leishmanin Skin Test (LST / Montenegro Test): Intradermal injection of 0.1 mL of standardized killed *Leishmania* promastigote antigen. Read at 48 hours; induration ≥5 mm is positive. Highly sensitive (>90–95%) in active CL and MCL, reflecting intact cell-mediated immunity.
    - Serum Antibody Assays: Enzyme Immunoassays (EIA / ELISA using *L. braziliensis* membrane antigens or recombinant *L. infantum* Hsp83/Hsp70), Indirect Immunofluorescence Assays (IFA), and Immunoblotting. Serum antibodies are consistently positive in high titers in MCL (aiding diagnosis when mucosal biopsies lack visible amastigotes), but low/inconstant in simple localized CL.
*   **Molecular Diagnostics (METHOD OF CHOICE FOR SPECIES IDENTIFICATION):**
    - Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR): Real-time PCR and nested PCR targeting kinetoplast DNA (kDNA) minicircle sequences, small subunit (18S) rRNA, internal transcribed spacer (ITS) regions, or spliced-leader RNA directly from skin scrapings, biopsy tissue, or filter paper blood spots.
    - Diagnostic Importance: Highly sensitive (>90–98%), especially in mucosal lesions where amastigote counts are extremely low. Uniquely capable of identifying *L. braziliensis* to the species level and distinguishing it from other New World species (*L. mexicana*, *L. amazonensis*, *L. guyanensis*), which is CRITICAL because *L. braziliensis* infection requires aggressive systemic therapy to prevent mucosal destruction (MCL).

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Anti-Protozoal Agents:**
    - Pentavalent Antimonials (Systemic Therapy): [[Sodium stibogluconate]] (Pentostam) or Meglumine Antimonate (Glucantime).
      - Regimen for Localized Cutaneous Leishmaniasis (CL): 20 mg Sb5+/kg/day IV or IM for 20 days (maximum daily dose capped at 1,215 mg Sb5+/day to reduce cardiotoxicity, pancreatitis, and chemical hepatitis).
      - Regimen for Mucocutaneous Leishmaniasis (MCL): 20 mg Sb5+/kg/day IV or IM for 30 days.
    - Pentavalent Antimonials PLUS Oral Pentoxifylline (Combination Regimen for MCL):
      - Antimonial (20 mg Sb5+/kg/day IV/IM for 30 days) PLUS Oral Pentoxifylline 400 mg PO TID for 30 days. Pentoxifylline inhibits TNF-alpha synthesis, markedly accelerating lesion healing and raising cure rates in severe/refractory MCL.
    - Liposomal [[Amphotericin B]] (L-AmB / AmBisome):
      - Dose: 2 to 3 mg/kg/day IV infusion up to a total cumulative dose of 20 to 40 mg/kg (e.g., 3 mg/kg/day on days 1–5, 10, 14, 21). Preferred front-line agent for pregnant or breastfeeding women with MCL or complex CL, and for antimonial-refractory cases.
    - Oral [[Miltefosine]] (Impavido):
      - Dose: 2.5 mg/kg/day PO (for adults ≥45 kg: 50 mg PO BID; for 30–44 kg: 50 mg PO once daily) for 28 days. FDA-approved oral agent for *L. braziliensis* CL and MCL. Contraindicated in pregnancy (teratogenic; strict contraception required during treatment and for 5 months post-therapy).
*   **Alternative Treatments:**
    - [[Amphotericin B Deoxycholate]] (Fungizone): 0.5 to 1.0 mg/kg IV infusion on alternate days for 20 to 30 doses (high cure rate, but requires hospital monitoring due to nephrotoxicity and hypokalemia).
    - [[Pentamidine]] Isethionate: 4 to 7 mg/kg/day IM given in 3 doses applied every 72 hours (or 2–4 mg/kg IV/IM every other day for 4 to 7 doses). Secondary alternative for CL/MCL.
    - Localized Therapies (FOR SIMPLE, SINGLE, LOW-RISK CL LESIONS ONLY): Topical 15% [[Paromomycin]] cream once daily for 20 days, thermotherapy (local radiofrequency heat), or cryotherapy (liquid nitrogen). WARNING: Local/topical therapies are generally NOT recommended for proven *L. braziliensis* infections in Latin America unless systemic risk is ruled out, due to the inherent risk of subsequent mucosal dissemination (MCL).
*   **Treatment Nuances & Dosing Considerations:**
    - Species-Specific Treatment Mandate: Because *L. braziliensis* carries a unique 1% to 5% risk of progressing to catastrophic mucocutaneous destruction (espundia), systemically active therapy is MANDATORY for all confirmed or suspected *L. braziliensis* infections (local topical therapy alone is insufficient).
    - Pregnancy Protocol: Systemic antimonials and [[Miltefosine]] are contraindicated during pregnancy due to fetal toxicity/teratogenicity. Liposomal [[Amphotericin B]] (2–3 mg/kg/day IV up to 20–40 mg/kg total dose) is the safe drug of choice for pregnant women with *L. braziliensis* infection.
    - Adverse Drug Reactions: Antimonials cause frequent side effects: myalgias, arthralgias, EKG changes (T-wave inversion, QT prolongation), elevated pancreatic enzymes (amylase/lipase), and liver transaminase elevation. Monitor EKG, CBC, renal and hepatic function weekly during 30-day MCL courses.
*   **Prophylaxis, Water Treatment & Vector Control:**
    - Personal Protective Measures: Use insect repellents containing DEET or Picaridin on exposed skin; wear long-sleeved shirts and full-length trousers when entering tropical forests/jungles; sleep under [[permethrin]]-impregnated fine-mesh bed nets ([[sand flies]] are tiny (1.5–3.5 mm) and easily penetrate standard [[Mosquitoes|mosquito]] netting).
    - Environmental Control: Screen living quarters with fine mesh; avoid outdoor activities in forest zones during evening and nighttime peak biting hours; remove brush/vegetation near dwellings.
    - Chemoprophylaxis: N/A. No prophylactic drug regimen exists.
    - Vaccine Status: No human vaccine currently approved for *L. braziliensis*.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "Espundia" (historic term for severe, destructive mucocutaneous leishmaniasis).
    - "Uta" / "Utu" (Andean term for cutaneous leishmaniasis in Peru).
    - "Tapir nose" (*nariz de tapir*) (facial deformity from nasal septum perforation and lip edema).
    - "Subgenus *Viannia*" (peripylarian development in [[Sand flies|sand fly]] midgut and hindgut).
    - "4N Medium" (specialized culture medium adapted for *L. braziliensis* primary isolation).
    - "Leishmaniavirus 1 (LRV1)" (endosymbiotic dsRNA virus triggering TLR3 hyper-inflammation and mucosal metastasis).
    - "Montenegro Skin Test / Leishmanin Skin Test (LST)" (strongly positive in CL and MCL).
    - "Pentoxifylline co-therapy" (TNF-alpha inhibitor combined with antimonials for refractory MCL).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - Parasites are characteristically EXTREMELY SCARCE or ABSENT in histologic tissue sections of mucosal (MCL) lesions due to intense host Th1 cell-mediated tissue destruction; diagnosis relies on molecular PCR, serology, or Montenegro skin testing rather than finding amastigotes.
    - Unlike Old World cutaneous species (*L. major*, *L. tropica*) which rarely metastasize and often self-cure without systemic sequelae, *Leishmania braziliensis* carries a unique capacity to metastasize to mucosal cartilaginous tissues years or decades AFTER primary skin lesions have completely healed.
    - Localized topical or physical therapies (cryotherapy, topical [[Paromomycin]]) that are acceptable for Old World CL are STRONGLY DISCOURAGED as sole treatment for *L. braziliensis* CL because they do not eradicate systemic parasites, leaving the host vulnerable to late mucocutaneous relapse (espundia).
    - While visceral leishmaniasis (*[[Leishmania donovani|L. donovani]]*) is associated with [[T Cell|T-cell]] anergy and negative skin tests, mucocutaneous leishmaniasis (*L. braziliensis*) represents the opposite immunopathologic spectrum: a hyperactive Th1 cell-mediated response with strongly positive Montenegro skin tests and massive production of IFN-gamma and TNF-alpha.
