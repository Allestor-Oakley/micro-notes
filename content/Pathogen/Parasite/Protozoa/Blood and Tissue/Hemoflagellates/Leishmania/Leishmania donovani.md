### Leishmania donovani

#### Protozoan Biology & Microscopic Morphology
*   **Trophozoite Stage Features:** N/A (Extracellular flagellated stage is the promastigote). Promastigotes are elongated, slender, motile, spindle-shaped extracellular forms measuring 15 to 30 µm (typically 15 to 20 µm long by 1.5 to 3.5 µm wide). Possesses a single, long free anterior flagellum measuring 15 to 28 µm in length. Contains a central spherical nucleus and an anterior, rod-shaped, dark-staining kinetoplast (a specialized DNA-containing mass within the single large mitochondrion) located near the base of the flagellar basal body.
*   **Cyst / Oocyst Stage Features:** N/A. *Leishmania donovani* produces no cyst or oocyst stage in its life cycle.
*   **Intracellular Morphologies:** Amastigote stage (historically termed Leishman-Donovan body). Small, ovoid, or spherical non-motile intracellular forms measuring 2 to 6 µm in length (typically 2 to 3 µm or 3 to 5 µm; 1 to 3 µm in paraffin tissue sections). Resides and multiplies within phagolysosomes / parasitophorous vacuoles of host [[Macrophage|macrophages]] and mononuclear phagocytes. Characterized microscopically by two distinct dark-staining intracellular structures: a large, round, dark-staining nucleus ("dot") and a small, distinct, rod-like or bar-shaped kinetoplast ("dash") located adjacent to the nucleus, representing the mitochondrial kinetoplast DNA and primitive flagellar axoneme base.
*   **Locomotion Mechanics:** Promastigotes exhibit active, directional flagellar motility in culture or insect vector gut, propelled by the whip-like beating of the single free anterior flagellum. Amastigotes are non-motile intracellular forms.

#### Lifecycle & Transmission
*   **Definitive Host:** Mammals (primarily humans in anthroponotic cycles; wild rodents, carnivores, and domestic dogs in zoonotic cycles). Note: Mammals act as the vertebrate host for the asexual amastigote stage, while female phlebotomine sand flies serve as insect vectors / intermediate hosts for the sexual/developmental promastigote stage.
*   **Intermediate Host & Vector Dynamics:** Transmitted by blood-feeding adult female phlebotomine [[sand flies]] belonging to the genus *Phlebotomus* in the Old World (specifically *Phlebotomus argentipes* in the Indian subcontinent, and *Phlebotomus orientalis*, *Phlebotomus martini*, and *Phlebotomus duboscqi* in East Africa) and *Lutzomyia* in the New World. Ingested amastigotes transform inside the [[Sand flies|sand fly]] midgut into promastigotes, which multiply by binary/longitudinal fission, undergo metacyclogenesis, and migrate to the fly pharynx and proboscis to be inoculated during subsequent blood meals.
*   **Transmission Route:**
    - Vector-borne: Inoculation of metacyclic promastigotes into human skin during a blood meal taken by an infected female [[Sand flies|sand fly]].====
    - Anthroponotic Transmission: Direct human-vector-human transmission cycle predominant in South Asia (India, Bangladesh, Nepal) and East Africa, where humans (including PKDL patients) serve as the primary infection reservoir.
    - Non-Vector Transmission: Congenital / transplacental transmission, blood transfusion, solid organ transplantation, needle sharing among intravenous drug users, and accidental laboratory inoculation (needlesticks or skin microabrasions).
*   **Infective Stage & Portal of Entry:** Inoculation of motile metacyclic promastigotes into human host skin via the proboscis of a feeding female [[Sand flies|sand fly]]. Injected promastigotes bind to host [[Macrophage|macrophages]], undergo phagocytosis, lose their flagella, transform into amastigotes inside parasitophorous vacuoles, and replicate until the host [[macrophage]] ruptures to release progeny that invade adjacent reticuloendothelial cells.
*   **Diagnostic Stage & Specimen Source:** Intracellular amastigotes (Leishman-Donovan bodies) inside [[Macrophage|macrophages]] and [[Monocyte|monocytes]]. Specimen sources include splenic aspirates/punctures (highest sensitivity >95%), [[bone marrow]] aspirates (sternal or iliac crest, 80–85% sensitivity), lymph node aspirates, liver biopsy specimens, peripheral blood buffy coat, skin scrapings/punch biopsies (for PKDL or primary cutaneous lesions), and mucosal biopsies (in severe [[HIV]]/AIDS co-infection).

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Tissue Tropism & Cellular Invasion:** Demonstrates strict tropism for mononuclear phagocytes ([[Macrophage|macrophages]], [[Monocyte|monocytes]], histiocytes) of the reticuloendothelial system throughout visceral organs: [[spleen]], liver (Kupffer cells), [[bone marrow]], [[lymph nodes]], and intestinal mucosa. Also invades dermal [[Macrophage|macrophages]] in post-kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis (PKDL).
*   **Cytopathic Effects & Tissue Destruction:** Uncontrolled intracellular amastigote replication inside [[Macrophage|macrophages]] leads to host cell lysis, releasing parasites that infect surrounding mononuclear cells. Causes severe pathologic changes: marked splenic hyperplasia and massive splenomegaly, hepatomegaly with Kupffer cell enlargement and sinusoid dilation, [[bone marrow]] replacement/suppression resulting in pancytopenia (anemia, leukopenia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia), hypergammaglobulinemia (polyclonal IgG and IgM elevation), severe muscle wasting, emaciation, cachexia, and circulating immune complex-mediated tissue pathology.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:**
    - Intracellular Phagolysosomal Niche: Adapts to survive and multiply within the acidic, proteolytic environment of host [[macrophage]] phagolysosomes.
    - Surface Lipophosphoglycan (LPG) & GP63 (Leishmanolysin): Parasite surface LPG and zinc-metalloprotease GP63 inhibit phagosome-lysosome maturation, block complement-mediated membrane attack complex assembly, modulate host cell signaling pathways, and scavenge reactive oxygen species.
    - Downregulation of Cell-Mediated Immunity: Induces specific [[T Cell|T-cell]] anergy, repressing Th1 pro-inflammatory responses and delayed-type hypersensitivity (leishmanin skin test negativity during active disease); upregulates immunosuppressive cytokines (IL-10 and TGF-beta), inhibiting [[macrophage]] microbicidal activity and nitric oxide production.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Protection against *L. donovani* relies on robust cell-mediated immunity (Th1 response, Interleukin-12, Interferon-gamma, TNF-alpha, and [[macrophage]] nitric oxide production). In immunocompromised hosts (advanced [[HIV]]/AIDS with CD4+ [[T Cell|T-cell]] depletion, organ transplant recipients receiving immunosuppression, or patients on cancer chemotherapy), latent *L. donovani* reactivates or primary infection rapidly disseminates, presenting with severe, refractory visceral leishmaniasis, atypical cutaneous/mucosal lesions, poor treatment response, and high relapse rates.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Complications
*   **Acute Clinical Presentation:**
    - Incubation Period: Typically 2 to 6 months (range 10 days to 2 years, or up to several years).
    - Acute / Subacute Visceral Leishmaniasis (Kala-azar / Dumdum Fever): Onset may be abrupt or insidious. Characterized by prolonged, irregular, hectic fever (frequently exhibiting a double daily peak / dromedary fever spike), chills, profuse night sweats, progressive weakness, fatigue, loss of appetite, and upper abdominal pain/fullness caused by rapid enlargement of the [[spleen]] and liver.
*   **Chronic & Extra-Intestinal Disease:**
    - Advanced Visceral Leishmaniasis: Massive, non-tender splenomegaly ([[spleen]] extending down to the pelvic brim), hepatomegaly, progressive emaciation, extreme cachexia, dark hyperpigmentation of the skin (hence "kala-azar" or black fever in Hindi, particularly notable on the hands, feet, abdomen, and face), severe pancytopenia (anemia causing profound fatigue and pallor, leukopenia/neutropenia predisposing to secondary bacterial infections, thrombocytopenia causing epistaxis, mucosal bleeding, or petechiae), polyclonal hypergammaglobulinemia, and pedal edema. Untreated active visceral leishmaniasis is nearly 100% fatal (case-fatality rate ~10% even with treatment).
    - Post-Kala-Azar Dermal Leishmaniasis (PKDL): Late cutaneous manifestation developing 1 to 3 years (in India) or 0 to 12 months (in East Africa/Sudan) following completion of treatment or clinical cure of visceral leishmaniasis. Characterized by hypopigmented macules, papules, nodules, or plaques on the face, trunk, and extremities containing abundant amastigotes; serves as a major infectious reservoir for [[Sand flies|sand fly]] transmission.
    - [[HIV]]/VL Co-Infection: Presents with high parasite burdens, atypical involvement of the gastrointestinal tract, lungs, or skin, high relapse rates, and elevated mortality.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** Microscopic identification of Leishman-Donovan bodies (intracellular amastigotes displaying a characteristic "dot-and-dash" nucleus and kinetoplast) inside [[Macrophage|macrophages]] on tissue impression smears or [[bone marrow]]/splenic aspirates; massive splenomegaly paired with hypergammaglobulinemia and pancytopenia in an endemic region.
*   **Host Immune Response & Laboratory Findings:**
    - Hematology: Pancytopenia (anemia, severe leukopenia/neutropenia, thrombocytopenia). Eosinophils are characteristically absent. Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) is markedly elevated.
    - Serum Chemistry & Immunology: Polyclonal hypergammaglobulinemia (dramatic elevation of IgG and IgM antibodies; total serum proteins elevated with a reversed albumin-globulin ratio). Circulating immune complexes containing IgA, IgG, and IgM.
    - Skin Testing: Leishmanin Skin Test (LST / Montenegro test) is negative during active visceral leishmaniasis due to cellular anergy, but converts to positive following successful cure.

#### Epidemiology & Exposures
*   **Geographic Endemicity:** High prevalence in East Africa (Sudan, South Sudan, Ethiopia, Kenya), the Indian subcontinent (India, Bangladesh, Nepal), China, the Middle East, and South America. Over 90% of global *L. donovani* VL cases occur in Bangladesh, India, Nepal, Sudan, South Sudan, Ethiopia, and Brazil.
*   **Environmental Reservoir & Water Systems:** In South Asia and East Africa, *L. donovani* is primarily anthroponotic (humans, including patients with active VL and PKDL, are the main reservoir). [[Sand flies|Sand fly]] vector species breed in dark, humid environments with rich organic soil, mud wall cracks, animal shelters, and termitaria.
*   **High-Risk Activities & Dietary Exposures:** Residence or travel in endemic rural agricultural villages; outdoor exposure to [[Sand flies|sand fly]] bites during evening and nighttime hours (night-biting behavior of *[[Sand flies|Phlebotomus]]* vectors); living in dwellings with mud walls, dirt floors, or thatched roofs; poverty and malnutrition (poor nutritional status exacerbates infection severity); occupational risk among field laborers, timber workers, and deployed military personnel.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:**
    - Biosafety: Standard BSL-2 containment when handling clinical blood, tissue aspirates, and cultures. Laboratory-acquired infection can occur via accidental needle-stick injuries or microabrasions of the skin.
    - Public Health & Vector Control: WHO Kala-azar Elimination Program in South Asia; indoor residual spraying (IRS with insecticides such as DDT or pyrethroids); active case detection and treatment of PKDL cases to eliminate human transmission reservoirs; distribution of insecticide-treated bed nets (ITNs / LLINs).

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Microscopy & Stains:**
    - Direct Stained Smears (GOLD STANDARD FOR PARASITOLOGICAL CONFIRMATION): Giemsa, Wright's, or Leishman stain of tissue aspirates/impression smears (splenic aspirate (sensitivity >95%), [[bone marrow]] aspirate (sensitivity 80–85%), lymph node aspirate (sensitivity 50–60%), liver biopsy, or peripheral blood buffy coat). Visualizes intracellular and extracellular amastigotes (2–4 µm) containing a pale blue cytoplasm, a round red/purple nucleus, and a distinct, dark, rod-like kinetoplast ("dot and dash" appearance).
    - Histopathology (H&E, Brown-Hopps tissue Gram stain, Wilder's reticulum stain): Histologic sections of [[spleen]], liver (dilated sinusoids and greatly enlarged Kupffer cells filled with amastigotes), [[bone marrow]], or skin biopsies (PKDL). Amastigotes appear smaller in paraffin sections than in touch preps.
*   **Concentration & Stool O&P Techniques:**
    - Fecal O&P Warning: Stool O&P examination is NOT applicable (*L. donovani* does not infect the intestinal lumen under standard conditions).
    - Buffy Coat Concentration: Centrifugation of anticoagulated venous blood to isolate the buffy coat layer enhances microscopic detection of amastigotes inside [[Monocyte|monocytes]].
    - In Vitro Culture: Inoculation of aspirates or triturated biopsy tissue into biphasic media (Novy-MacNeal-Nicolle (NNN) medium with rabbit blood overlay, Evan's modified Tobie's medium, 4N medium) or liquid media (Schneider's *Drosophila* medium supplemented with 30% fetal bovine serum). Promastigotes develop in the fluid overlay at 20°C to 25°C within 2 to 7 days (or up to 3 weeks). Promastigotes often arrange in characteristic "rosette" formations. Microcapillary culture method (MCM) provides rapid isolation.
    - Animal Inoculation: Intraperitoneal inoculation into young Syrian golden hamsters (2–4 months old). Hamsters develop generalized visceral infection; [[spleen]] impression smears are examined weeks later.
*   **Serology & Rapid Antigen Tests:**
    - Rapid Diagnostic Test (rK39 ICT - Immuno-Chromatographic Test): Field-adapted, rapid (10–20 min) lateral-flow dipstick detecting antibodies against recombinant K39 antigen (a 39-amino-acid repeat of a kinesin-related protein conserved in the *L. donovani* complex). High sensitivity (>95%) and specificity in the Indian subcontinent. (Note: Antibodies persist for months/years post-cure, so rK39 cannot distinguish active infection from past cured infection or be used as a test-of-cure).
    - Direct Agglutination Test (DAT): Semi-quantitative field assay using freeze-dried or liquid suspension of stained, killed *L. donovani* promastigotes mixed with diluted patient serum/blood on microtiter plates. Visible agglutination after 18 hours. High sensitivity and specificity.
    - ELISA & Immunoblotting: Detection of anti-*Leishmania* IgG/IgM antibodies or antibodies against Hsp70 and Hsp83.
    - Urine Antigen Detection (KAtex): Latex agglutination test for detecting heat-stable leishmanial low-molecular-weight carbohydrate antigen in patient urine.
*   **Molecular Diagnostics:**
    - Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR): Real-time PCR and nested PCR targeting kinetoplast DNA (kDNA) minicircle sequences (sensitivity 87–100%), small subunit (18S) rRNA, or telomeric sequences directly from peripheral blood, [[bone marrow]], or tissue aspirates. Allows precise species identification and parasite load quantification.
    - Loop-Mediated Isothermal Amplification (LAMP): SYBR Green or direct-blood-lysis (DBL)-LAMP assays for rapid, sensitive field diagnosis of VL and PKDL.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Anti-Protozoal Agents:**
    - Liposomal [[Amphotericin B]] (L-AmB / AmBisome):
      - Indian Subcontinent Regimen: Single intravenous dose of 10 mg/kg IV infusion (preferred front-line regimen with >95–98% cure rate); OR 3 to 5 mg/kg/day IV on days 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 14, 21 (total cumulative dose 15 to 30 mg/kg).
      - East Africa Regimen: 3 to 5 mg/kg/day IV given over 6–10 days up to a total cumulative dose of 30 mg/kg.
    - Oral [[Miltefosine]] (Impavido):
      - First approved oral antileishmanial drug. Dose: 2.5 mg/kg/day PO (for adults weighing ≥45 kg: 50 mg PO BID; for 30–44 kg: 50 mg PO once daily) for 28 days. Highly effective for *L. donovani* VL and PKDL in India. Contraindicated in pregnancy (teratogenic; strict contraception required during treatment and for 5 months post-treatment).
    - [[Paromomycin]] Sulfate (Aminosidine):
      - 11 mg/kg/day base (15 mg/kg/day sulfate) IM daily for 21 days. Extremely low cost (~$15/course). Frequently used in combination regimens in East Africa and South Asia.
    - Combination Regimens (Preferred to prevent resistance and shorten duration):
      - Single-dose L-AmB (5 mg/kg IV) + [[Miltefosine]] (14 days PO) OR Single-dose L-AmB (5 mg/kg IV) + [[Paromomycin]] (10 days IM).
*   **Alternative Treatments:**
    - Pentavalent Antimonials ([[Sodium stibogluconate]] (Pentostam) / Meglumine Antimonate (Glucantime)): Historical drug of choice. Dose: 20 mg Sb5+/kg/day IV or IM for 30 days. High therapeutic failure rates (>50–65% resistance) in Bihar, India due to antimonial resistance; still utilized in parts of East Africa where sensitivity remains intact, though limited by cardiotoxicity (fatal arrhythmias), pancreatitis, and hepatotoxicity.
    - [[Amphotericin B Deoxycholate]] (Fungizone): 0.5 to 1.0 mg/kg IV infusion daily or every other day for 15 to 20 doses (cure rate ~97%, but causes high fever, chills, nephrotoxicity, hypokalemia).
    - [[Pentamidine]] Isethionate: 2 to 4 mg/kg IV/IM every other day for 15 doses (secondary alternative; limited by diabetes mellitus induction and severe hypotension).
*   **Treatment Nuances & Dosing Considerations:**
    - Regional Efficacy Variation: *L. donovani* exhibits distinct drug susceptibility profiles by region (e.g., high antimonial resistance in India vs. sensitivity in East Africa; [[Paromomycin]] less effective as monotherapy in Sudan).
    - [[HIV]]/VL Co-infection Regimen: Higher total doses of Liposomal [[Amphotericin B]] (30 to 40 mg/kg total cumulative dose, e.g., 5 mg/kg on days 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11) PLUS [[Miltefosine]] (100 mg/day for 14 to 28 days). Secondary prophylaxis with [[Pentamidine]] or L-AmB monthly until CD4+ count exceeds 200 cells/mm³.
    - PKDL Treatment: Prolonged treatment courses required ([[Miltefosine]] 50 mg PO BID for 12 weeks in India; L-AmB or antimonials for 60 to 90 days in East Africa).
*   **Prophylaxis, Water Treatment & Vector Control:**
    - Vector Control: Indoor residual spraying (IRS) with insecticides (DDT, pyrethroids) on indoor wall surfaces of homes and animal shelters to kill resting *[[Sand flies|Phlebotomus]]*.
    - Personal Protection: Use of [[permethrin]]-treated bed nets (ITNs / LLINs), fine-mesh screening ([[sand flies]] are small, 1.5–3.5 mm, requiring smaller mesh sizes than [[mosquitoes]]), insect repellents (DEET) on exposed skin, and wearing long-sleeved clothing during evening hours.
    - Reservoir Control: Active case detection and prompt treatment of active VL and PKDL patients to eliminate human infection reservoirs.
    - Vaccine Status: No human vaccine currently approved for *L. donovani* (candidate subunit vaccines like LEISH-F3 / GLA-SE and ChAd63-KH in clinical trials).

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "Leishman-Donovan bodies" (amastigotes with dot-and-dash nucleus and kinetoplast).
    - "Kala-azar" (Hindi for "black fever", referring to skin hyperpigmentation).
    - "rK39 immunochromatographic rapid test" (kinesin-related recombinant antigen strip).
    - "Double daily dromedary fever peak".
    - "Massive, pelvic-reaching splenomegaly + hypergammaglobulinemia + pancytopenia".
    - "Post-kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis (PKDL)" (hypopigmented macular/nodular rash post-treatment).
    - "[[Miltefosine]]" (first oral antileishmanial drug; teratogenic).
    - "[[Sand flies|Phlebotomus]] argentipes" (primary [[Sand flies|sand fly]] vector in South Asia).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - Although *Leishmania donovani* is fundamentally a viscerotropic pathogen causing systemic fatal infection (kala-azar), it can produce florid cutaneous disease years AFTER successful visceral cure, manifesting as Post-Kala-Azar Dermal Leishmaniasis (PKDL).
    - Unlike *L. infantum* / *L. chagasi* (which are predominantly zoonotic with dogs as major reservoirs), *L. donovani* in the Indian subcontinent and East Africa is strictly ANTHROPONOTIC (humans are the primary reservoir).
    - High-level drug resistance to pentavalent antimonials ([[Sodium stibogluconate]]) exceeds 60% in Bihar, India, rendering antimonials obsolete in that region, whereas they remain active in parts of Africa.
    - The Leishmanin Skin Test (LST) is strictly NEGATIVE during active visceral leishmaniasis due to specific cell-mediated immune anergy, but converts to POSITIVE after effective cure.
    - Stool O&P examination is entirely useless for *L. donovani* diagnosis, as organisms reside exclusively within reticuloendothelial [[Macrophage|macrophages]] of deep visceral organs and blood.
