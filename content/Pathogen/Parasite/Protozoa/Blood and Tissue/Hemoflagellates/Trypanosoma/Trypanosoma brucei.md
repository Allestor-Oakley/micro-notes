### Trypanosoma brucei

#### Protozoan Biology & Microscopic Morphology
*   **Trophozoite Stage Features:** N/A (Extracellular flagellated stage in mammalian hosts is the trypomastigote). Trypomastigotes are elongated, spindle-shaped/leaf-like motile extracellular hemoflagellates measuring 14 to 33 µm in length by 1.5 to 3.5 µm in width. Demonstrates marked pleomorphism in mammalian peripheral blood:
    - Long Slender Forms: Actively dividing forms measuring 23 to 33 µm in length with a long free anterior flagellum, adapted for rapid multiplication in host blood and lymph.
    - Short Stumpy Forms: Non-dividing, broad forms measuring 14 to 20 µm in length with a short or absent free flagellum, abundant mitochondrial development, and metabolic adaptation for survival inside the [[Tsetse flies|tsetse fly]] vector midgut.
    - Intermediate Forms: Transitional morphological stages between long slender and short stumpy forms.
    - Subcellular Anatomy: Contains a single, prominent, central oval nucleus. At the posterior end, possesses a dark-staining, subterminal, rod-like or dot-like kinetoplast (a specialized, circular DNA-containing mass within the single, elongated, branching mitochondrion) lying adjacent to the basal body. A single flagellum originates from the basal body near the kinetoplast, runs anteriorly along the outer edge of a longitudinal lateral undulating membrane (a fold of the pellicle attached to the cell body), and emerges at the anterior tip as a free, whip-like extension. Covered by a dense, homogeneous outer surface coat (12 to 15 nm thick) composed of approximately 10^7 molecules of Variant Surface Glycoproteins (VSGs). Lacks cytoadherence structures; relies entirely on antigenic switching to survive in extracellular plasma.
    - Subspecies Differentiation: The three subspecies belonging to the *Trypanosoma brucei* complex—*Trypanosoma brucei gambiense*, *Trypanosoma brucei rhodesiense*, and *Trypanosoma brucei brucei* (which causes livestock nagana)—are morphologically completely indistinguishable under light or electron microscopy, but differ significantly in genetics, host resistance genes, vector ecology, geographic distribution, clinical virulence, and epidemiology.
*   **Cyst / Oocyst Stage Features:** N/A. *Trypanosoma brucei* COMPLETELY LACKS a cyst or oocyst stage in its life cycle.
*   **Intracellular Morphologies:** N/A. *Trypanosoma brucei* is classically considered a strictly extracellular parasite in humans and mammalian hosts, living freely in blood plasma, lymph, interstitial tissue fluids, and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF).
*   **Locomotion Mechanics:** Active, rapid, wriggling, serpentine, or directional motility in fresh unpreserved blood wet mounts, propelled by the whipping action of the free anterior flagellum and wave propagation along the attached undulating membrane.

#### Lifecycle & Transmission
*   **Definitive Host:** Mammals (primarily humans in *T. b. gambiense* cycles; wild game animals (antelopes, bushbuck) and domestic livestock (cattle) in *T. b. rhodesiense* cycles; wild and domestic animals in *T. b. brucei* cycles). Note: Mammals act as vertebrate hosts for the asexual trypomastigote stage, while [[tsetse flies]] serve as insect vectors / intermediate hosts for developmental epimastigotes and metacyclic trypomastigotes.
*   **Intermediate Host & Vector Dynamics:** Transmitted by blood-feeding adult male and female [[tsetse flies]] (*Glossina* species):
    - *T. b. gambiense* Vector: *Glossina palpalis* group (riverine tsetse flies: *Glossina palpalis*, *Glossina fuscipes*, *Glossina tachinoides*) that inhabit shaded vegetation along riverbanks, lakeshores, and stream crossings.
    - *T. b. rhodesiense* Vector: *Glossina morsitans* group (savannah/bush [[tsetse flies]]: *Glossina morsitans*, *Glossina pallidipes*, *Glossina swynnertoni*) that inhabit dry woodland, savannah thickets, and game reserves.
    - Vector Lifecycle (Salivarian / Anterior Station Development): When a [[Tsetse flies|tsetse fly]] ingests a blood meal containing short stumpy trypomastigotes, the parasites enter the fly midgut and transform into long, slender procyclic trypomastigotes. Procyclic forms multiply by binary fission in the midgut and hindgut lumen. After approximately 2 weeks, parasites migrate forward through the proventriculus, hypopharynx, and salivary ducts into the fly salivary glands, transforming into unattached epimastigotes. Epimastigotes multiply and attach to salivary gland microvilli, eventually undergoing meiotic recombination and differentiation into infectious, free-floating, coat-covered metacyclic trypomastigotes (~18 to 35 days total cycle inside the fly). Metacyclic forms are injected into the mammalian host during subsequent blood meals.
*   **Transmission Route:**
    - Vector-Borne Transmission: Inoculation of metacyclic trypomastigotes into human skin/subcutaneous tissue through the painful bite (laceration) of an infected tsetse fly (*Glossina* spp.).
    - Non-Vector Transmission: Transplacental / congenital transmission from mother to fetus; blood transfusion from asymptomatic early-stage blood donors; and accidental laboratory parenteral inoculation (needle-sticks, microabrasions, or mucous membrane splashes).
*   **Infective Stage & Portal of Entry:** Inoculation of motile metacyclic trypomastigotes via the proboscis of a feeding female or male [[Tsetse flies|tsetse fly]] into human subcutaneous tissue and microvasculature. Parasites proliferate locally at the bite site to form an inflammatory nodule (trypanosomal chancre), then enter regional lymphatics and the bloodstream to establish systemic parasitemia.
*   **Diagnostic Stage & Specimen Source:** Trypomastigotes (slender, stumpy, or intermediate forms). Specimen sources include chancre fluid/aspirates, enlarged lymph node aspirates (especially posterior cervical [[lymph nodes]]), peripheral anticoagulated venous blood (buffy coat, thick/thin smears), and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF).

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Tissue Tropism & Cellular Invasion:** Tropism for subcutaneous connective tissue, blood plasma, lymphatic vessels and nodes, reticuloendothelial organs ([[spleen]], liver), myocardium/heart, and the central nervous system (subarachnoid space, choroid plexus, brain parenchyma, and cerebrospinal fluid).
*   **Cytopathic Effects & Tissue Destruction:**
    - Local & Hemolymphatic Stage: Local proliferation produces an indurated, painful, necrotizing subcutaneous chancre with localized lymphangitis. Hematogenous and lymphatic dissemination causes endothelial damage, vascular permeability, tissue edema, hypergammaglobulinemia, generalized lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, and lymphocytic myocarditis (leading to heart failure and fatal conduction arrhythmias in acute East African disease).
    - Neurological Stage (CNS Invasion): Trypanosomes cross the blood-brain barrier via transcellular or paracellular migration across brain microvascular endothelial cells. Organisms invade the choroid plexus, subarachnoid space, cerebral cortex, and brainstem, provoking severe panencephalitis, leptomeningitis, perivascular mononuclear cuffing (lymphocytes, [[Plasma Cell|plasma cells]], [[Macrophage|macrophages]]), cerebral edema, microglial proliferation, astrogliosis, and progressive neuronal degeneration.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:**
    - Antigenic Variation: Expresses a dense surface coat composed of ~10^7 molecules of Variant Surface Glycoproteins (VSGs). The parasite genome harbors >1,000 distinct VSG genes and pseudogenes. Through genomic recombination, gene conversion, and telomeric expression site switching, *T. brucei* randomly switches its VSG coat expression approximately once every 100 to 1,000 cell divisions. Specific host antibody destroys the predominant VSG population, but a minor mutant clone expressing a new VSG coat survives, replicates, and creates a new wave of parasitemia. This continuous cycle leads to characteristic cyclic waves of parasitemia and fever, causing severe [[B Cell|B-cell]] exhaustion and chronic immune evasion.
    - Polyclonal [[B Cell|B-Cell]] Activation: Secretes toxic catabolites and endotoxin-like molecules that induce massive, non-specific polyclonal [[B Cell|B-cell]] activation, elevating serum and CSF Immunoglobulin M (IgM) to extraordinary levels and forming circulating immune complexes that cause vasculitis and tissue injury.
    - Molecular Mechanisms of Human Serum Resistance: Normal human serum contains innate Trypanosome Lytic Factors (TLF-1 and TLF-2) containing Apolipoprotein L1 (ApoL1), which forms fatal pores in trypanosomal lysosomal membranes (explaining why *T. b. brucei* cannot infect humans):
      - *T. b. rhodesiense* Evasion: Contains the SRA gene (Serum Resistance-Associated gene) encoding a specialized SRA protein that directly binds and neutralizes human ApoL1 inside lysosomes.
      - *T. b. gambiense* Evasion: Expresses the TgsGP gene (*T. b. gambiense*-specific glycoprotein) and modifies membrane lipid composition/haptoglobin-hemoglobin receptors, preventing ApoL1 uptake or degrading ApoL1.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Host defense against bloodstream forms requires specific IgG and IgM neutralizing antibodies, complement-mediated lysis, and [[macrophage]] clearance in the liver and [[spleen]]. However, antigenic variation routinely overwhelms adaptive immunity. In advanced disease, host immunosuppression, suppressor cell generation, and elevated somnogenic immunosuppressive factors (e.g., Prostaglandin D2 (PGD2) in CSF) suppress host cellular immunity and disrupt sleep-wake cycles.

#### Clinical Syndromes & Complications
*   **Acute Clinical Presentation:**
    - Trypanosomal Chancre: Painful, erythematous, indurated, swollen nodule or red tender ulcer appearing 5 to 15 days after a tsetse bite at the inoculation site (present in ~50% of non-African travelers and *T. b. rhodesiense* cases; uncommon in native *T. b. gambiense* patients). Accompanied by local swelling and painful regional lymphadenopathy; desquamates and heals spontaneously over 2 to 3 weeks.
    - First Stage (Stage 1 / Hemolymphatic Stage): Marked by systemic parasite dissemination in blood and lymph.
      - *T. b. gambiense* (West African Sleeping Sickness - Chronic Form): Incubation period lasts months to years (slow, insidious onset). Presents with irregular, recurrent, low-grade fevers matching parasitemic waves; severe headache; joint pain; myalgias; intense generalized pruritus; periorbital/facial edema; hepatosplenomegaly; and circular, transient, erythematous, ring-like rashes (trypanids).
      - *T. b. rhodesiense* (East African Sleeping Sickness - Acute Form): Incubation period is short (days to 3 weeks). Rapid, fulminant progression with high parasitemia, high spiking hectic fevers, rigors, early pancytopenia, severe acute myocarditis, cardiac arrhythmias, pulmonary edema, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), and renal failure. Patients frequently succumb to acute heart failure or toxemia within weeks to months (<9 months) before full CNS signs develop.
*   **Chronic & Extra-Intestinal Disease:**
    - Second Stage (Stage 2 / Neurological / CNS Stage / Sleeping Sickness): Occurs when trypanosomes cross the blood-brain barrier into the central nervous system (after months to years in *T. b. gambiense*; after weeks to months in *T. b. rhodesiense*).
      - Progressive Neurological Signs: Severe, unremitting frontal or diffuse headache; personality changes; irritability; emotional lability; apathy; logorrhea; sensory disturbances; hyperesthesia; extrapyramidal tremors of the tongue, hands, and lips; ataxia; abnormal gait; muscle weakness; slurred speech; paralysis; choreiform movements; nocturnal insomnia; daytime somnolence (progressive apathy and somnolence where the patient falls asleep while eating or standing, hence "sleeping sickness"); coma; and death if untreated.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:**
    - Winterbottom's Sign: Conspicuous, painless, rubbery, soft, enlarged [[lymph nodes]] located in the posterior cervical triangle (along the posterior border of the sternocleidomastoid muscle); classic hallmark of *T. b. gambiense* Stage 1 disease.
    - Kerandel's Sign: Severe, delayed pain and hyperesthesia experienced upon deep muscle compression or squeezing (e.g., squeezing the palm or calf).
    - Sleep Inversion: Complete reversal of circadian sleep rhythm (daytime somnolence and nighttime severe insomnia) driven by elevated levels of Prostaglandin D2 (PGD2) in the CSF.
    - Morular Cells of Mott (Mott Cells): Highly characteristic, altered [[Plasma Cell|plasma cells]] with large spherical, berry-like cytoplasmic inclusions containing condensed Immunoglobulin M (Russell bodies), found in the CSF during Stage 2 disease.
*   **Host Immune Response & Laboratory Findings:**
    - Blood & CSF Chemistry: Extreme elevation of serum and CSF IgM levels (10 to 16 times normal levels; diagnostic marker). Elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), thrombocytopenia, anemia, and elevated CSF protein.
    - CSF Staging Examination: Lumbar puncture is MANDATORY in all confirmed HAT cases. Stage 2 (CNS disease) is defined by CSF pleocytosis (>5 white blood cells [WBCs]/µL) and/or microscopic demonstration of trypomastigotes or Mott cells in CSF sediment.

#### Epidemiology & Exposures
*   **Geographic Endemicity:** Strictly focal distribution across 36 countries in Sub-Saharan Africa bounded by 15°N and 20°S latitude (the "[[Tsetse flies|tsetse fly]] belt").
    - *T. b. gambiense*: Endemic in 24 countries of West and Central Africa (e.g., Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC, which accounts for >70% of global cases), Central African Republic, Chad, Guinea, Côte d'Ivoire, South Sudan, northern Uganda). Accounts for >85% to 95% of all reported human sleeping sickness cases globally.
    - *T. b. rhodesiense*: Endemic in 13 countries of East and Southern Africa (e.g., Uganda (where both forms overlap), Tanzania, Kenya, Malawi, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Ethiopia). Accounts for <5% to 15% of human cases.
*   **Environmental Reservoir & Water Systems:**
    - *T. b. gambiense* Reservoir: Strictly ANTHROPONOTIC cycle. Humans serve as the primary reservoir host; domestic pigs and dogs serve as minor animal reservoirs. Vector sand/fly habitats are riverine (shaded riverbanks, forest streams, water collection points).
    - *T. b. rhodesiense* Reservoir: ZOONOTIC / ANTHROPOZOONOSIS cycle. Primary reservoirs are wild game animals (bushbuck, antelopes, hartebeests) and domestic livestock (cattle). Humans are accidental hosts. Vector habitats are dry savannah woodlands, brushlands, and national game parks.
*   **High-Risk Activities & Dietary Exposures:**
    - *T. b. gambiense* Exposure: Rural agricultural populations, riverine fishermen, wood collectors, and individuals fetching water or washing clothes at shaded riverbanks in West/Central Africa.
    - *T. b. rhodesiense* Exposure: Safari tourists, game park rangers, hunters, wilderness trekkers, cattle herders, and woodcutters entering East African savannah woodlands or game reserves.
    - Fly Biting Behavior: [[Tsetse flies]] (*Glossina* spp.) are day-biting insects attracted to movement, dark colors (especially dark blue and black), and vehicle dust/exhaust. Both male and female [[tsetse flies]] take blood meals and transmit infection.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:**
    - Biosafety: High-risk BSL-2 / BSL-3 laboratory pathogen. Live trypomastigotes in blood, tissue aspirates, CSF, or cultures represent a severe laboratory infection hazard via accidental needle-stick or mucous membrane contamination. Blood-borne pathogen precautions, closed centrifuge tubes, gloves, and safety needles are required.
	    - Public Health Controls: WHO HAT Elimination Strategy; mobile field survey units utilizing CATT screening; systematic active case detection and treatment; tsetse vector control using insecticide-impregnated blue/black cloth traps, targets, ground/aerial spraying, and the Sterile Insect Technique (SIT).

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Microscopy & Stains:**
    - Direct Saline Wet Mount: Direct microscopic examination of fresh, unpreserved anticoagulated blood, chancre aspirate, lymph node aspirate, or centrifuged CSF sediment under 10x and 40x objectives. Visualizes rapidly motile, wriggling trypomastigotes moving between blood cells.
    - Permanent Stained Smears (GOLD STANDARD PARASITOLOGICAL METHOD): Air-dried thin and thick blood films, or tissue/CSF sediment smears fixed in methanol and stained with Giemsa stain, Wright's stain, or Field's stain. Visualizes trypomastigotes (14–33 µm long) displaying pale blue cytoplasm, a central red/purple nucleus, a subterminal posterior dark kinetoplast, a longitudinal undulating membrane, and a whip-like anterior flagellum.
*   **Concentration & Stool O&P Techniques:**
    - Fecal O&P Warning: Stool O&P examination is NOT applicable (*T. brucei* does not infect the gastrointestinal tract).
    - Specialized Blood Concentration Methods (CRITICAL FOR LOW PARASITEMIA IN GAMBIENSE HAT):
      1. Microhematocrit Centrifugation / Buffy Coat Examination: Centrifugation of blood in capillary tubes; organisms concentrate in the buffy coat layer directly above red cells.
      2. Quantitative Buffy Coat (QBC): Acridine orange-coated fluorescent capillary tube system; visualizes glowing fluorescent nuclei and kinetoplasts under UV microscopy.
      3. Mini-Anion-Exchange Centrifugation Technique (mAECT - GOLD STANDARD SENSITIVITY METHOD): Anticoagulated venous blood is passed through an anion-exchange chromatography column packed with DEAE-cellulose. Positively charged trypanosomes pass freely through the column while negatively charged blood cells (RBCs, WBCs, platelets) are retained. The eluate is centrifuged into a sealed glass capillary tip and inspected under 10x and 40x microscopy. Exquisite sensitivity (<100 parasites/mL of blood). Produced in Belgium for HAT field control.
*   **Serology & Rapid Antigen Tests:**
    - Card Agglutination Test for Trypanosomiasis (CATT): Standard mass-screening field test for *T. b. gambiense*. Freeze-dried, stained, antigenically intact *T. b. gambiense* trypanosomes expressing the variable antigen type LiTat 1.3 are mixed with diluted patient blood or serum on plastic cards. Visible blue agglutination within 5 minutes indicates positive anti-trypanosomal antibodies (high field sensitivity >90–95%). Note: CATT is completely USELESS for *T. b. rhodesiense* because *rhodesiense* strains lack the LiTat 1.3 VSG gene.
    - Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs - Point-of-Care Cartridges): Recombinant antigen-based lateral-flow immunochromatographic dipsticks (e.g., SD BIOLINE HAT, HAT Sero-K-SeT) for rapid field screening of *T. b. gambiense* using fingerstick blood.
    - Immune Trypanolysis Test: Reference laboratory bioassay; measures antibody-mediated complement-dependent lysis of cloned trypanosomes expressing specific VSGs. Used to monitor HAT elimination and resolve discordant field serology.
*   **Molecular Diagnostics:**
    - Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR): Real-time PCR and loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) targeting repetitive 18S rRNA genes, satellite DNA (177-bp repeats), or subspecies-specific marker genes:
      - *T. b. rhodesiense* Specific PCR: Targets the Serum Resistance-Associated (SRA) gene.
      - *T. b. gambiense* Specific PCR: Targets the *T. b. gambiense*-specific glycoprotein (TgsGP) gene.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Anti-Protozoal Agents:**
    - *T. b. gambiense* Regimens:
      - Oral First-Line Agent (For Both First-Stage & Non-Severe Second-Stage *T. b. gambiense* HAT in patients ≥6 years old and weighing ≥20 kg):
        - Fexinidazole: First all-oral treatment for gambiense HAT. Adult and pediatric (≥20 kg) dosage: Loading dose of 1,800 mg (3 tablets) PO once daily with a full meal on Days 1 to 4, followed by a maintenance dose of 600 mg (1 tablet) PO once daily with food on Days 5 to 10 (10-day total oral course). (Eliminates the mandatory requirement for diagnostic lumbar puncture in patients with no clinical suspicion of severe Stage 2 HAT).
      - First Stage (Hemolymphatic Stage, CSF WBC ≤5/µL and no trypanosomes in CSF):
        - [[Pentamidine]] Isethionate: 4 mg/kg/day IM (or IV) once daily for 7 to 10 days. (Preferred first-line choice for young children <6 years or <20 kg, or when fexinidazole is contraindicated).
      - Second Stage (Severe Neurological / CNS Stage, CSF WBC ≥100/µL or severe neurological symptoms or failed fexinidazole):
        - NECT ([[Nifurtimox]]-[[Eflornithine]] Combination Therapy): Preferred front-line combination regimen. [[Eflornithine]] (DFMO): 400 mg/kg/day IV divided into two 2-hour infusions (every 12 hours) for 7 days PLUS Oral [[Nifurtimox]]: 15 mg/kg/day PO in 3 divided doses (every 8 hours) with food for 10 days.
    - *T. b. rhodesiense* Regimens:
      - First Stage (Hemolymphatic Stage, CSF normal):
        - [[Suramin]] Sodium: Initial test dose of 100 to 200 mg IV (to check for idiosyncratic anaphylaxis/renal toxicity), followed by 20 mg/kg IV (maximum single dose 1.0 g) given on Days 1, 3, 7, 14, and 21 (total 5 full doses).
      - Second Stage (Neurological / CNS Stage, CSF pleocytosis or trypanosomes in CSF):
        - [[Melarsoprol]] (Arsobal): Organic trivalent arsenical compound. Drug of choice for rhodesiense CNS disease. Dosage: 2.2 mg/kg/day IV once daily for 10 consecutive days (or historical 3-series regimens).
*   **Alternative Treatments:**
    - [[Eflornithine]] (DFMO) Monotherapy: 400 mg/kg/day IV divided into 4 infusions (every 6 hours) for 14 days (alternative for gambiense Stage 2 if [[Nifurtimox]] is unavailable).
    - [[Melarsoprol]]: Trivalent arsenical used as rescue therapy for refractory Stage 2 *T. b. gambiense* cases failing NECT, or as primary therapy for *T. b. rhodesiense* Stage 2. (WARNING: Highly toxic; causes severe reactive arsenical encephalopathy in 5% to 10% of treated patients, with a 50% case-fatality rate among those who develop encephalopathy).
    - Acoziborole: Single-dose oral oxaborole derivative currently in clinical trials as a breakthrough single-dose cure for all stages of *T. b. gambiense*.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Dosing Considerations:**
    - Mandatory Staging Rule: Lumbar puncture and CSF analysis are MANDATORY prior to initiating therapy with stage-specific intravenous/intramuscular drugs ([[Pentamidine]], [[Suramin]], NECT, [[Melarsoprol]]) because Stage 1 drugs ([[Pentamidine]], [[Suramin]]) CANNOT cross the blood-brain barrier and fail completely if CNS invasion has occurred.
    - Arsenical Encephalopathy Prophylaxis: Concurrent systemic corticosteroid administration (e.g., Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day PO) is routinely administered alongside [[Melarsoprol]] infusions to suppress dangerous arsenical neurotoxicity and reactive brain edema.
    - Fexinidazole Food Requirement: Fexinidazole MUST be administered with a full meal to ensure proper GI absorption and systemic therapeutic drug levels.
*   **Prophylaxis, Water Treatment & Vector Control:**
    - Personal Protection: Wear thick, neutral-colored (khaki, olive, tan), long-sleeved shirts and full-length trousers. Strictly AVOID wearing dark blue or black clothing, as [[tsetse flies]] are strongly attracted to blue/black visual spectrums. [[Tsetse flies]] can bite through thin fabric. Apply insect repellents (DEET), though repellents are less effective against [[tsetse flies]] than [[mosquitoes]]. Inspect vehicles before entering.
    - Vector Control: Deploy blue/black insecticide-impregnated cloth traps and screens; perform selective bush clearing near riverbanks and dwellings; apply ground/aerial insecticide spraying; utilize the Sterile Insect Technique (SIT) to eradicate isolated [[Tsetse flies|tsetse fly]] populations.
    - Chemoprophylaxis: N/A. Individual drug chemoprophylaxis is NOT recommended for travelers or residents, as low prophylactic drug doses mask early infection without preventing fatal CNS invasion.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "Winterbottom's sign" (painless posterior cervical lymphadenopathy in gambiense HAT).
    - "Kerandel's sign" (delayed severe deep muscle pain upon compression).
    - "Morular cells of Mott / Mott cells" (CSF [[Plasma Cell|plasma cells]] filled with Ig-containing Russell bodies).
    - "Antigenaemia & VSG Antigenic Variation" (switching among >1,000 VSG genes).
    - "SRA Gene" (*T. b. rhodesiense* Serum Resistance-Associated gene overriding human ApoL1 lysis).
    - "TgsGP Gene" (*T. b. gambiense* resistance marker).
    - "[[Tsetse flies|Tsetse fly]] (*Glossina* spp.) with hatchet cell in wing venation".
    - "CATT (Card Agglutination Test for Trypanosomiasis)" (gambiense screening).
    - "NECT ([[Nifurtimox]]-[[Eflornithine]] Combination Therapy) & Fexinidazole".
    - "Arsenical encephalopathy" (fatal complication of [[Melarsoprol]] therapy).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - Unlike *[[Trypanosoma cruzi]]* (which forms intracellular amastigote nests in cardiac/brain tissues and is transmitted via triatomine bug feces rubbed into wounds), *Trypanosoma brucei* is strictly EXTRACELLULAR in mammalian hosts and is transmitted directly by [[Tsetse flies|tsetse fly]] BITES through salivary gland injection.
    - *Trypanosoma brucei brucei* infects domestic and wild animals (nagana) but CANNOT infect humans because normal human serum contains Trypanosome Lytic Factors (TLF-1/2 containing ApoL1) that lyse the parasite; human-pathogenic subspecies (*T. b. gambiense* and *T. b. rhodesiense*) evolved specific molecular resistance mechanisms (TgsGP and SRA genes) to survive human ApoL1.
    - First-stage HAT drugs ([[Pentamidine]] and [[Suramin]]) DO NOT cross the blood-brain barrier and completely fail to cure second-stage disease; administering Stage 1 drugs to a patient with unrecognized CNS involvement results in 100% relapse and death.
    - CATT (Card Agglutination Test) is highly sensitive for *T. b. gambiense* screening but is completely USELESS and NEGATIVE for *T. b. rhodesiense* because *rhodesiense* strains lack the LiTat 1.3 VSG gene.
