### SARS-CoV-2

#### Molecular Virology & Structure
*   **Genomic Architecture:** The viral genome consists of a single-stranded, positive-sense, unsegmented RNA molecule. It is the largest genome among RNA viruses, measuring approximately 27,000 to 32,000 bases. The genome is polyadenylated at the 3' end. The infecting positive-sense RNA genome translates into proteins from two open reading frames (ORF1a, ORF1b) utilizing a ribosomal frameshift. The genome encodes 16 nonstructural proteins (nsp1-16) that provide replicative and accessory functions, four structural proteins (S, E, M, and N), and nine accessory proteins (3a, 3b, 6, 7a, 7b, 8, 9b, 9c, 10).
*   **Capsid & Nucleocapsid:** The virus possesses a long, flexible helical nucleocapsid (9–11 nm in diameter) formed by the positive-strand genomic RNA associated with many molecules of the phosphorylated nucleocapsid (N) protein.
*   **Envelope Properties:** The nucleocapsid is enclosed within a pleomorphic lipid envelope. The envelope is relatively impervious and stable to drying, detergents, and acids due to the presence of its glycoprotein corona.
*   **Virion Size & Physical Morphology:** The virion is medium-sized, enveloped, and measures approximately 120 to 160 nm in diameter. It displays widely spaced, 20-nm-long club- or petal-shaped projections on the outer surface, giving it a solar corona-like appearance under an electron microscope.
*   **Structural & Non-Structural Proteins:** The virus contains four main structural proteins: the Spike (S) glycoprotein (180–220 kDa) which forms the petal-shaped peplomers as a trimer and functions in viral attachment and fusion (cleaved into S1 and S2 subunits); the Membrane (M) protein (20–35 kDa) which serves as a matrix protein defining viral shape and organizing assembly; the Envelope (E) protein which facilitates envelopment, release, and alteration of cellular membranes; and the Nucleocapsid (N) protein (50–60 kDa). Nonstructural proteins (nsp1-16) include nsp1 (inhibits host translation/degrades host mRNA), nsp3 and nsp5 (proteases for polyprotein cleavage), nsp4 (membrane interaction), nsp6 (escapes antiviral defenses), nsp12 (RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, complexed with nsp7, 8, and 13), nsp13 (helicase), nsp14 (exoribonuclease with proofreading capacity), and nsp16 (RNA-cap methyltransferase). Accessory proteins include 3a (induces apoptosis, virus release, inflammasome activation), 6 (inhibits host protein synthesis and Type I interferon), 8b (immune evasion, inhibits interferon), and 9c (acts on mitochondria).

#### Viral Replication Cycle
*   **Attachment, Host Receptors & Tropism:** The Spike (S) glycoprotein S1 subunit binds primarily to the angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE-2) receptor. This receptor is widely distributed and expressed on multi-ciliated nasal and nasopharyngeal cells, lower airway ciliary epithelial cells, type II pneumocytes, mucoepithelial cells of the gastrointestinal tract, vascular endothelium, neurons, and lymphocytes. The tight binding affinity of SARS-CoV-2 to ACE-2 facilitates highly efficient infection of the upper respiratory tract.
*   **Entry Mechanism:** Following attachment to ACE-2, the Spike protein is cleaved by cell surface proteases, specifically the transmembrane serine protease 2 (TMPRSS2). This cleavage brings the S2 fusion subunit close to the cell membrane, facilitating direct fusion of the viral envelope with the plasma membrane or endosomal membranes, thereby releasing the genome into the host cell cytoplasm.
*   **Uncoating & Intracellular Trafficking:** The virus enters the cytoplasm via fusion at the cell membrane or following absorptive endocytosis and fusion within an endosome, thereby releasing the uncoated positive-sense RNA genome directly into the cytoplasm.
*   **Transcription & Translation Strategy:** Replication and transcription occur entirely in the cytoplasm. The positive-sense RNA genome acts directly as mRNA and is translated to produce two large polyproteins (ORF1a and ORF1b). Viral proteases (nsp3, nsp5) cleave these polyproteins to yield the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp) and other enzymes. These proteins create membrane vesicles associated with the rough endoplasmic reticulum where sequestered replication and transcription take place. The RdRp synthesizes a full-length negative-sense RNA intermediate template, which is used to transcribe both new positive-sense genomic RNAs and a nested set of subgenomic negative-sense templates. These subgenomic templates generate individual mRNAs for the late structural and accessory proteins.
*   **Assembly, Maturation & Egress:** The newly synthesized positive-sense genomic RNA associates with the Nucleocapsid (N) protein to form helical nucleocapsids. The S, E, and M glycoproteins are synthesized and inserted into the endoplasmic reticulum. The nucleocapsid binds to the M protein at the budding compartment, assembling and budding into the endoplasmic reticulum-Golgi intermediate compartment (ERGIC) membranes. Mature virions are released from the host cell via exocytosis-like fusion of vesicles with the plasma membrane.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Direct Cytopathic Effects:** The virus specifically targets and kills ciliated lower airway cells and alveolar type 2 (AT2) cells. Damage to AT2 cells (which produce surfactant and serve as progenitors for AT1 cells) incites diffuse alveolar damage, characterized by an initial exudative phase with hyaline membrane formation, followed by a proliferative phase with alveolar septal fibrosis. Direct infection of endothelial cells causes vascular injury, endothelialitis, and microthrombi formation.
*   **Latent Infection & Reactivation Triggers:** N/A
*   **Oncogenesis Mechanisms:** N/A
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** SARS-CoV-2 evades early recognition by the innate immune system (specifically the double-stranded RNA sensor MDA5), delaying the induction of Type I and Type III interferons. This allows the virus to spread rapidly before typical flu-like symptoms manifest. Viral nsp1 degrades host mRNA and inhibits translation, while accessory proteins 6 and 8b inhibit interferon production and signaling. The viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase is error-prone despite some proofreading capability from nsp14, generating mutations primarily in the Spike protein that allow emerging variants (Variants of Concern: Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Delta, Omicron) to escape pre-existing neutralizing antibodies from vaccination or prior infection.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Early production of Type I and III interferons is critical to halt viral progression; inborn defects in interferon signaling or the presence of autoantibodies against type I IFNs predispose patients to severe, life-threatening COVID-19. Uncontrolled viral replication leads to a dysregulated hyperinflammatory response (cytokine storm), characterized by massive release of IL-1β, IL-6, TNF, and D-dimer primarily from pro-inflammatory [[Macrophage|macrophages]]. This induces neutrophil influx and neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation, contributing to immunothrombosis and acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS). A paucity of CD8+ [[T-Cell|T cells]] in the peripheral blood is associated with higher viral loads and poor outcomes. Severe disease is highly correlated with advanced age (>65 years), obesity, hypertension, diabetes, chronic cardiovascular or lung disease, cancer, organ transplantation, and other immunosuppressive conditions.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** COVID-19 severity ranges from asymptomatic infection (up to 40% of cases) to mild upper respiratory disease (common cold-like symptoms including dry cough, fever >99.6°F, sore throat, congestion, headache, myalgias, and fatigue), to severe lower respiratory tract illness including progressive pneumonia, hypoxia, and acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS).
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Gastrointestinal symptoms (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea) occur in 2% to 40% of patients. Cutaneous manifestations include morbilliform, vesicular, urticarial, and chilblain-like eruptions. Smell and taste abnormalities (anosmia and dysgeusia) are prominent, particularly with the Delta variant. COVID-19 induces a hypercoagulable state (COVID-19-associated coagulopathy) leading to deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary infarction, ischemic stroke, and myocardial infarction. Neurological complications include encephalopathy and Guillain-Barré syndrome. A rare Multisystem Inflammatory Syndrome occurs in children (MIS-C) and adults (MIS-A) weeks after infection, mimicking Kawasaki disease or toxic shock syndrome with cardiogenic shock and coronary aneurysms. Long-COVID (Post-Acute Sequelae of SARS-CoV-2) involves persistent fatigue, dyspnea, "brain fog," and multiorgan dysfunction lasting months to years. Secondary fungal infections, such as COVID-19-associated pulmonary aspergillosis (CAPA) and COVID-19-associated mucormycosis (CAM), occur in critically ill or immunosuppressed patients.
*   **Incubation Period:** Less than 14 days; typically 2 to 10 days, with a median of 5 days (shorter for Omicron variants, median 3 days).
*   **Classic Prodrome:** Mild constitutional symptoms including malaise, headache, dry cough, myalgias, and low-grade fever, often preceding severe shortness of breath by approximately 5 to 7 days.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** N/A
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Progression to ARDS requiring mechanical ventilation, systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS)/shock, cardiac failure, multiorgan failure, and death (mortality 0.2–5.3%). Long-COVID sequelae and permanent lung or cardiovascular damage.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Bats are the likely evolutionary reservoir, with possible intermediate amplification in small mammals (e.g., civets, pangolins). Humans are the primary reservoir for ongoing global transmission.
*   **Transmission Route:** Spread predominantly from person-to-person via inhalation of respiratory droplets and short-range or long-distance aerosols (<5 μm) expelled during coughing, sneezing, talking, or singing. Transmission also occurs through direct contact and indirectly via fomites. The virus is highly transmissible during the asymptomatic and presymptomatic phases (peak shedding occurs 1 to 2 days before symptom onset and continues for 5 to 7 days post-onset). Enteric transmission is also theoretically possible.
*   **Vectors:** N/A
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Designated a global pandemic. Control requires a combination of high-impact interventions: universal continuous masking (N-95/FFP-2 for healthcare), physical and social distancing, de-densification, environmental ventilation improvements, contact tracing, and supported quarantine of high-risk contacts (5 to 10 days). Confirmed cases should isolate for at least 10 days from symptom onset plus 3 days after symptom cessation (or minimum 5 days depending on local guidelines). Epidemic surveillance utilizes wastewater/sewage monitoring and global genomic sequencing (GISAID, Pango) to track Variants of Concern.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Histopathology & Inclusion Bodies:** Lung histopathology displays an initial exudative phase with diffuse alveolar damage, excessive inflammation, endothelial injury, fibrin deposition, microthrombi in small vessels, and hyaline membrane formation, progressing to a proliferative phase with alveolar septal fibrosis.
*   **Molecular Testing:** Nucleic Acid Amplification Tests (NAATs), typically real-time reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR), are the gold standard for detecting viral RNA in nasopharyngeal, nasal, oropharyngeal swabs, or saliva. Results can take 1-8 hours. Variant surveillance requires genomic sequencing; specific variants (e.g., Omicron B.1.1.529) may demonstrate an "S gene drop out" (target failure) on certain multiplex PCR platforms.
*   **Serology & Seroconversion Windows:** Rapid antigen lateral flow tests (detecting the Nucleocapsid protein) yield results in 15 minutes but have lower sensitivity, performing best 3-5 days after symptom onset when viral load is highest. Antibody testing (detecting IgM, IgG, or neutralizing antibodies) is unhelpful for acute diagnosis as seroconversion typically takes 12 to 21 days (peaking 3-4 weeks post-onset).
*   **Cell Culture & Assays:** Virus isolation requires specialized Biosafety Level 3 (BSL-3) facilities using human airway epithelial cells or Vero monkey kidney cells and is not routinely performed for diagnostic purposes.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **Antiviral Targets & First-Line Agents:** Nirmatrelvir co-administered with [[ritonavir]] (Paxlovid) targets the viral protease. Remdesivir (Veklury) is a broad-spectrum adenosine nucleoside triphosphate analog that targets the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, causing premature RNA chain termination. Molnupiravir is a cytidine nucleoside analogue that induces viral hypermutation.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Mutations:** Due to high global transmission, the virus continuously evolves, selecting for mutations primarily in the Spike glycoprotein (e.g., the D614G mutation). These mutations generate Variants of Concern (Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Delta, Omicron) that exhibit increased transmissibility, altered virulence, and the ability to evade vaccine-induced neutralizing antibodies or monoclonal antibody therapies.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Timing Constraints:** Antiviral drugs must be administered as early as possible in the disease course to be effective. For severe, progressive COVID-19, anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive therapies (e.g., dexamethasone, IL-6 inhibitors) are required to combat the cytokine storm, alongside antithrombotic therapy (heparin) to manage coagulopathy, and oxygen/ventilatory support. Convalescent plasma is not recommended.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** Vaccination is the primary mode of prevention. Available platforms include mRNA vaccines, adenovirus-vectored vaccines, and subunit vaccines encoding stabilized forms of the Spike protein. Vaccines significantly reduce severe disease, hospitalization, and death, though effectiveness against symptomatic infection wanes over time and is reduced against heavily mutated variants (e.g., Omicron), necessitating booster doses. Post-exposure prophylaxis with oral antivirals (e.g., Paxlovid) is used in high-risk contacts.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** COVID-19, SARS-CoV-2, pandemic, Spike glycoprotein trimer, ACE-2 receptor, TMPRSS2 cleavage, cytokine storm, anosmia (loss of smell), dysgeusia (loss of taste), Long-COVID (PASC), MIS-C/MIS-A, ground-glass opacities, D614G mutation, mRNA vaccines.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike most RNA viruses, which have high mutation rates due to lacking proofreading enzymes, coronaviruses possess an exoribonuclease (nsp14) with proofreading capacity, allowing them to stably maintain the largest RNA genome. Furthermore, unlike most enveloped viruses that are highly fragile, the "corona" of glycoproteins protects the SARS-CoV-2 virion, allowing it to withstand drying and gastrointestinal environments to some degree. Unlike earlier zoonotic coronaviruses ([[SARS-CoV|SARS-CoV-1]] and [[MERS|MERS-CoV]]), SARS-CoV-2 efficiently infects the upper respiratory tract and transmits heavily during the presymptomatic and asymptomatic phases, facilitating uncontrollable pandemic spread.
