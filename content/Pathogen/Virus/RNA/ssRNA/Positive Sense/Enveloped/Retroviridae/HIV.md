### Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)

#### Molecular Virology & Structure
*   **Genomic Architecture:**
    - Linear, positive-sense, single-stranded RNA (+ssRNA) genome.
    - Non-segmented but diploid; each virion contains two identical copies of the single-stranded positive-sense RNA genome.
    - Approximately 9 to 10 kb (kilobases) in overall size (some sources *mention* 7-11 kb for retroviruses in general).
    - Contains a 5' methylated guanosine cap and a 3' polyadenylated tail.
    - Composed of nine open reading frames (ORFs) encoding three structural precursor polyproteins (Gag, Gag-Pol, and Env) and six regulatory/accessory proteins.
    - Bordered at each end of the proviral DNA by long terminal repeat (LTR) sequences (flanked by LTRs generated during reverse transcription). The LTRs contain promoters, enhancers, and transcriptional control sequences for cellular transcription factors (such as NF-κB) to regulate and stimulate transcription of the integrated genome.
    - Possesses an extremely high mutation rate. The viral reverse transcriptase is highly error-prone (lacking 3'-5' proofreading activity), with an estimated error rate of approximately 1 error per 2000 bases (equivalent to about 5 errors per genome per replication cycle). This high genetic instability leads to the generation of heterogeneous swarms of closely related viral variants called quasispecies within an infected individual.
    - Classified into two distinct epidemics: HIV-1 (globally distributed, divided into major group M, outlier group O, group N, and group P; group M contains clades or subtypes A–L and circulating recombinant forms (CRFs)) and HIV-2 (prevalent and largely confined to West Africa, divided into groups A–H, slower progression of disease and less cytopathic than HIV-1).
*   **Capsid & Nucleocapsid:**
    - The viral core displays complex symmetry, forming an electron-dense, cylindrical, or bar-shaped nucleocapsid resembling a truncated cone.
    - The major structural core antigen is the Capsid (CA) p24 protein, which encapsulates the diploid RNA genome.
    - Within the capsid core, the genomic RNA is complexed with the Nucleocapsid (NC) p7 and p9 proteins (nucleic acid-binding proteins) to form the ribonucleoprotein complex.
    - The capsid core also contains 10 to 50 copies of essential viral replication enzymes (Reverse Transcriptase, Integrase, and Protease) and two cellular transfer RNA (tRNA) molecules (specifically host tRNA-Lys) that serve as primers for reverse transcription.
*   **Envelope Properties:**
    - Surrounded by a lipid bilayer membrane envelope acquired by budding through modified host cell plasma membranes.
    - The envelope is environmentally fragile, highly labile, and readily inactivated by heat, drying, detergents, acid, and lipid solvents (such as ether). It requires moisture to persist and transmit, and does not survive long on dry surfaces or fomites.
    - Embedded in the lipid bilayer are host cellular proteins (including MHC class I and II molecules picked up during egress) and viral glycoprotein spikes.
    - The viral glycoprotein spikes are lollipop-like trimer complexes of gp160, cleaved by host cellular proteases into the outer surface component (gp120) and the transmembrane component (gp41).
*   **Virion Size & Physical Morphology:**
    - Roughly spherical, pleomorphic enveloped particles.
    - Approximately 80 to 120 nm in diameter (some sources specify 80-100 nm).
    - Exhibits a characteristic cylindrical or truncated-cone nucleoid core visible on electron micrographs sectioned at appropriate angles.
*   **Structural & Non-Structural Proteins:**
    - **Structural Proteins:**
        - **Gag (Group-Specific Antigen, encoded by gag):** Translated as a Pr55 precursor polyprotein, cleaved by the viral protease to yield:
            - **MA (Matrix) p17:** Lines the inner surface of the lipid envelope; facilitates viral assembly and anchors the envelope spikes.
            - **CA (Capsid) p24:** Forms the cylindrical/truncated-cone core capsule surrounding the genomic RNA.
            - **NC (Nucleocapsid) p7/p9:** Binds to and packages the diploid genomic RNA.
        - **Env (Envelope, encoded by env):** Synthesized as a gp160 precursor, glycosylated in the rough endoplasmic reticulum, and cleaved by host cellular signal peptidases/proteases in the Golgi apparatus into:
            - **gp120 (Surface component, SU):** Functions as the viral attachment protein (VAP). Tightly glycosylated to shield neutralizing epitopes; contains binding domains for the host CD4 receptor and chemokine co-receptors (CCR5 or CXCR4). Divides into five hypervariable (V1–V5) loops that mutate rapidly, with the V3 loop being highly immunogenic and a primary determinant of neutralizing antibody responses and coreceptor tropism.
            - **gp41 (Transmembrane component, TM):** Forms the anchor stick of the glycoprotein spike; mediates membrane fusion between the viral envelope and the host cell plasma membrane.
    - **Non-Structural Proteins (Enzymes, encoded by pol and pro):**
        - **Protease (PR, p11):** Cleaves the Gag (Pr55) and Gag-Pol (Pr160) precursor polyproteins to release mature enzymes and core structural proteins during and after budding. This proteolytic processing is mandatory to transform immature virions into mature, infectious particles. Represents a major target for [[protease inhibitor]] drugs.
        - **Reverse Transcriptase (RT, p66/p51):** An RNA-dependent DNA polymerase that also possesses DNA-dependent DNA polymerase and Ribonuclease H (RNase H) activities. Translates the genomic +ssRNA into complementary linear double-stranded DNA (cDNA). It carries no proofreading function, driving genetic diversity and drug resistance. Highly targeted by nucleoside and non-nucleoside RT inhibitors.
        - **Integrase (IN, p32):** Directs transport of the linear cDNA into the host nucleus and catalyzes its insertion into host chromosomal DNA to establish the permanent "provirus" state.
    - **Regulatory & Accessory Proteins:**
        - **Tat (Transactivator of Transcription):** Essential early regulatory protein; binds to the viral transactivation response (TAR) element on the nascent viral RNA, greatly augmenting transcriptional elongation and transactivating viral and cellular gene expression up to several hundred-fold.
        - **Rev (Regulator of Expression of Virion Proteins):** Binds to the Rev response element (RRE) on unspliced and singly spliced viral mRNAs, facilitating their nuclear export to the cytoplasm for translation of structural proteins (Gag, Pol, Env) late in infection.
        - **Nef (Negative Factor):** Multifunctional accessory protein that downregulates cell-surface expression of CD4 (preventing superinfection and facilitating viral egress) and MHC class I molecules (shielding infected cells from CD8+ CTL-mediated lysis). It alters [[T-Cell|T-cell]] signaling pathways to promote activation, enhances viral infectivity, and is crucial for high viral loads and progression of SIV/HIV infection to AIDS.
        - **Vif (Viral Infectivity Factor):** Promotes assembly and infectivity by binding to and targeting the cellular cytidine deaminase APOBEC3G for proteasomal degradation, preventing APOBEC3G-mediated hypermutation (C-to-U deamination) of retroviral cDNA.
        - **Vpu (Viral Protein U):** Exclusive to HIV-1. Promotes the degradation of newly synthesized CD4 in the rough endoplasmic reticulum. Inhibits host tetherin (an interferon-induced restriction factor), thereby preventing the retention/tethering of budding virions and promoting egress from the host cell.
        - **Vpr (Viral Protein R) / Vpx (Viral Protein X):** Vpr (present in HIV-1 and HIV-2) arrests host cells in the G2 phase of the cell cycle (an optimal state for transcription of the LTR promoter) and facilitates transport of the viral preintegration complex into the nucleus of non-dividing, terminally differentiated cells (such as [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]]). Vpx (exclusive to HIV-2) targets the host restriction factor SAMHD1 for degradation, facilitating replication in [[Dendritic Cell|dendritic cells]] and [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]], which promotes antigen presentation on MHC-I and CD8+ [[T-Cell|T-cell]] responses, contributing to slower progression of HIV-2.

#### Viral Replication Cycle
*   **Attachment, Host Receptors & Tropism:**
    - **Attachment:** The viral gp120 surface protein binds with high affinity to the primary host receptor, the CD4 molecule, expressed on helper T lymphocytes, [[Monocyte and Macrophage|monocytes]], [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]], [[Dendritic Cell|dendritic cells]], and microglial cells of the central nervous system.
    - **Co-receptors:** Binding of gp120 to CD4 induces a conformational shift that exposes a coreceptor-binding site. The virus must then bind to a seven-transmembrane G-protein-coupled chemokine co-receptor:
        - **CCR5 (R5-tropic, [[Monocyte and Macrophage]]/M-tropic):** The primary co-receptor used during initial infection and transmission. Expressed on [[Monocyte and Macrophage|monocytes]], [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]], [[Dendritic Cell|dendritic cells]], and subsets of CD4+ [[T-Cell|T cells]] (such as memory and Th17 cells). Mutational deletion of this coreceptor (homozygous CCR5-delta 32 mutation, present in approximately 1% of Northern Europeans) protects against HIV-1 acquisition; heterozygous mutation delays disease progression.
        - **CXCR4 (X4-tropic, lymphocyte/T-tropic):** Late in chronic disease, mutations in the env gene of some strains shift co-receptor preference to CXCR4, expanding target cells to include almost all CD4+ [[T-Cell|T cells]], resulting in a precipitous decline in CD4 count and rapid progression to AIDS.
    - **Additional Receptors:** HIV can bind to α-4 β-7 [[Integrin]] (the gut-homing receptor for [[T-Cell|T cells]], promoting rapid depletion of intestinal lymphoid tissue) and DC-SIGN (a [[Dendritic Cell]]-specific lectin). DC-SIGN binds HIV on the surface of [[Dendritic Cell|dendritic cells]] without mediating entry, facilitating transport of the virus to lymphoid organs where it infects CD4+ [[T-Cell|T cells]] ("trans-infection").
*   **Entry Mechanism:**
    - After binding both CD4 and the chemokine co-receptor (CCR5 or CXCR4), a final conformational change is triggered in gp41.
    - The hydrophobic N-terminal fusion peptide of gp41 is inserted into the host cell plasma membrane, promoting fusion of the viral envelope with the cellular membrane and releasing the viral nucleocapsid directly into the host cytoplasm.
*   **Uncoating & Intracellular Trafficking:**
    - **Uncoating:** The viral nucleocapsid enters the cytoplasm and undergoes partial disassembly (uncoating) to release the viral genomic RNA and enzymatic machinery into the host cytosol.
    - **Reverse Transcription:** During uncoating, the RT enzyme synthesizes a complementary negative-sense DNA (cDNA) copy using host tRNA-Lys as a primer. It then degrades the RNA template via its RNase H activity and synthesizes the positive-sense DNA strand, creating a linear double-stranded cDNA copy (proviral DNA). LTR sequences are duplicated at both ends of this cDNA copy.
    - **Nuclear Import:** Unlike most other retroviruses, the preintegration complex of lentiviruses (cDNA complexed with Vpr, integrase, and matrix proteins) is actively imported through intact nuclear pores of resting, non-dividing cells without requiring nuclear envelope breakdown during cell division.
*   **Transcription & Translation Strategy:**
    - **Integration:** The viral integrase catalyzes insertion of the double-stranded cDNA randomly into host chromosomal DNA, creating a permanent "provirus". The provirus behaves as a cellular gene, and replication is permanently maintained.
    - **Transcription:** Host cell RNA polymerase II transcribes the integrated proviral DNA from the promoter/enhancer elements in the 5' LTR. Transcription is highly dependent on host cell activation; cytokines (such as TNF-α, IL-2) and mitogens generate cellular transcription factors (such as NF-κB) that bind to LTR elements to initiate transcription.
    - **Splicing Phases:** Transcription proceeds in two phases:
        - **Early Phase:** Transcripts are heavily spliced (multiply spliced) by host machinery to produce short mRNAs that are exported to the cytoplasm to translate the regulatory proteins Tat, Rev, and Nef.
        - **Late Phase:** As Rev accumulates, it binds to the RRE on unspliced (genomic) or singly spliced mRNAs, facilitating their export from the nucleus. These longer transcripts are translated to produce Env, Gag, and Gag-Pol polyproteins, or packaged as genomic RNA.
    - **Translation:**
        - Env is translated as a gp160 precursor on the rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER) and processed through the secretory pathway.
        - Gag and Gag-Pol are translated on free cytoplasmic ribosomes. Gag-Pol is translated via a ribosomal frameshift (a ribosomal shift of -1 nucleotide occurs about 5% of the time, allowing translation of the Pol gene).
*   **Assembly, Maturation & Egress:**
    - **Assembly:** Newly synthesized viral glycoproteins (gp120/gp41) are transported to the host cell plasma membrane. Gag and Gag-Pol precursor polyproteins are acylated (myristoylated) and target the modified plasma membrane. Two copies of genomic positive-sense RNA and host tRNA primers are recruited to the membrane complex to initiate assembly.
    - **Egress (Budding):** The immature capsid associates with gp120/gp41-rich regions of the cell membrane. The virion buds out of the host cell plasma membrane, acquiring its envelope containing cellular membrane proteins (such as HLA antigens).
    - **Maturation:** Concomitant with or immediately after budding and release, the viral protease is activated within the extracellular particle. The protease cleaves the Gag and Gag-Pol precursor polyproteins to release the mature capsid (p24), matrix (p17), nucleocapsid (p7), and active enzymes (RT, IN, PR). This morphological reorganization (forming the characteristic condensed cylindrical/truncated-cone nucleoid core) is mandatory for the virion to become infectious.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Direct Cytopathic Effects:**
    - Depletion of CD4+ T lymphocytes is the primary pathogenic driver, caused by multiple direct and indirect mechanisms:
        - **Direct Viral Lysis:** Highly productive replication of T-tropic (X4) viruses disrupts membrane integrity and leads to cytolytic lysis of infected cells.
        - **Syncytia Formation:** Multinucleated giant cell formation occurs when gp120 on productively infected cells binds CD4 on adjacent uninfected cells, promoting cell-to-cell fusion and subsequent cytolysis of the syncytia.
        - **Pyroptosis:** The major driver of bystander CD4+ [[T-Cell|T-cell]] depletion in lymphoid tissue. Non-permissive, resting CD4+ [[T-Cell|T cells]] undergo abortive infection (the virus enters but reverse transcription fails to complete, leading to accumulation of incomplete DNA transcripts). This is detected by host sensors, triggering an inflammatory form of programmed cell death (pyroptosis) that releases inflammatory cytokines and recruits more CD4+ [[T-Cell|T cells]] to the infection site.
        - **Immune-Mediated Destruction:** Cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CD8+ CTLs) and [[NK Cell|NK cells]] target and kill productively infected CD4+ [[T-Cell|T cells]].
        - **Decreased Production:** Chronic infection damages lymphoid microenvironments and [[Bone Marrow]] progenitors, leading to lymphoid fibrosis, atrophy of lymph node architecture, and decreased thymic output of naive CD4+ [[T-Cell|T cells]].
*   **Latent Infection & Reactivation Triggers:**
    - Establish a stable, long-lived latent reservoir within a small fraction of resting memory CD4+ T lymphocytes harboring integrated, transcriptionally silent proviral DNA.
    - This latent reservoir decays extremely slowly (with a half-life of many years, estimated to take over 70 years to naturally decay). It is immune-privileged because the cells do not express viral proteins, shielding them from host CTLs and ART.
    - **Reactivation Triggers:** Exposure to specific antigens, superinfection, or inflammatory cytokines (such as TNF-α, IL-2) activates the memory [[T-Cell|T cells]], inducing cellular transcription factors (such as NF-κB) to bind LTR promoters and trigger transcription of the integrated provirus, initiating active viral replication and rebound of viremia within weeks if ART is interrupted.
*   **Oncogenesis Mechanisms:**
    - HIV is non-oncogenic and does not directly carry or integrate cellular oncogenes. Instead, oncogenesis is driven by indirect mechanisms:
        - Profound depletion of CD4+ [[T-Cell|T cells]] impairs immune surveillance of malignant cells.
        - Widespread immune activation and cytokine dysregulation promote clonal [[B Cell|B-cell]] proliferation.
        - Facilitates oncogenesis of co-infecting oncogenic DNA viruses:
            - **[[HHV-8]] (KSHV):** Causes Kaposi sarcoma (characterized by vascular endothelial tumors, presenting with purpuric, necrotic cutaneous plaques/nodules or visceral lesions).
            - **[[EBV]]:** Drives [[B Cell|B-cell]] non-Hodgkin lymphomas (such as Burkitt lymphoma, diffuse large [[B Cell|B-cell]] lymphoma, and primary CNS lymphoma).
            - **[[HPV]]:** Drives cervical dysplasia, invasive cervical carcinoma in women, and anal squamous cell carcinoma in men.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:**
    - **Antigenic Drift:** High error rate of RT and rapid replication drive extreme mutation rates, particularly in the gp120 env gene, allowing the virus to constantly mutate neutralizing epitopes (gp120 variable loops) and escape host antibody clearance.
    - **Glycan Shielding:** The outer surface of gp120 is heavily glycosylated, physically masking neutralizing protein epitopes from antibody access.
    - **MHC Class I Downregulation:** The accessory protein Nef downregulates cell-surface expression of HLA-A and HLA-B, preventing antigen presentation to and evasion from CD8+ CTL-mediated killing.
    - **CD4 Downregulation:** Nef and Vpu downregulate cell-surface CD4, preventing superinfection and gp160-mediated intracellular retention, which facilitates viral egress.
    - **APOBEC3G Degradation:** Vif targets the host antiviral restriction factor APOBEC3G for proteasomal degradation, preventing lethal hypermutation of the cDNA intermediate.
    - **Tetherin Antagonism:** Vpu binds to and degrades host tetherin, preventing the tethering and retention of budding virions.
    - **Cell-to-Cell Transmission:** Spread directly through virological synapses, nanotubes, or phagocytosis of infected cells bypasses standard extracellular spaces, evading neutralizing antibodies.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:**
    - **Host Defense:** Type I/III Interferons, cellular restriction factors (APOBEC3G, TRIM5α, Tetherin), and CD8+ CTLs (directed against Gag and Pol proteins) attempt to restrict replication. Broadly neutralizing antibodies against gp120 develop in a minority of patients only after several years of chronic infection.
    - **Immunodeficiency Links:** Depletion of CD4+ T lymphocytes (the coordinators of cell-mediated and humoral immunity) results in loss of cytokines (IL-2, IFN-γ, IL-17). Th17 cell loss in GALT compromises mucosal barriers, leading to microbial translocation (leakage of lipopolysaccharide and gut bacteria into circulation). This drives chronic, systemic immune activation and hyper-inflammatory responses.
    - When CD4+ counts decline below 200 cells/µL, cell-mediated immunity is completely compromised, leaving patients vulnerable to a predictable spectrum of opportunistic infections and malignancies.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:**
    - **Acute Retroviral Syndrome (Acute Seroconversion Illness):** Occurs 2 to 4 weeks post-infection in 40–90% of individuals. Characterized by sudden onset of a "mononucleosis-like" illness lasting 2 to 6 weeks. Features include high fever (97%), generalized tender lymphadenopathy (77%), [[pharyngitis]] (73%), myalgia/arthralgia, fatigue, and headache. A distinctive, non-pruritic erythematous maculopapular rash (consisting of 5-10 mm slightly raised lesions) occurs in 70% of patients, primarily on the face or trunk, but can involve palms and soles. Painful oral and genital ulcers are a distinctive and classic feature. Highly viremic phase with plasma viral loads often exceeding 10 million copies/mL.
    - **Clinical Latency (Chronic Phase):** Follows resolution of acute syndrome. Asymptomatic or mild, non-specific symptoms. Persistent generalized lymphadenopathy (enlarged [[Lymph Nodes]] in two or more non-contiguous extra-inguinal sites) occurs in 50-70% of individuals. Gradually declining CD4 counts.
    - **Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (AIDS):** Defined when the CD4 count falls below 200 cells/µL (or a CD4 percentage <14%) or by the presentation of an AIDS-defining indicator disease regardless of the CD4 count. Characterized by severe, life-threatening opportunistic infections, secondary malignancies, and systemic syndromes:
        - **HIV Wasting Syndrome ("Slim Disease" in Africa):** Marked by >10% unexplained weight loss combined with chronic [[diarrhea]] (for >1 month) or chronic weakness and documented fever (for >1 month) in the absence of an explanatory concurrent illness.
        - **AIDS Dementia Complex (HIV-Associated Dementia, HAD):** Subcortical dementia presenting in patients with CD4 counts <200 cells/µL. Manifests as progressive cognitive decline, short-term memory loss, poor concentration, apathy, psychomotor retardation, behavioral changes, and motor slowing, eventually progressing to a vegetative state and death. Brain imaging reveals generalized cerebral atrophy.
        - **Lymphoid Interstitial Pneumonitis (LIP):** An HIV-related chronic lung disease occurring primarily in perinatally infected children. Characterized by persistent cough, progressive dyspnea, digital clubbing, parotid gland swelling, and marked generalized lymphadenopathy. Chest radiograph reveals diffuse, bilateral reticulonodular infiltrates with hilar lymphadenopathy.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:**
    - **Pediatric HIV Disease:** Manifests rapidly in untreated infants (50-70% mortality by age 2). Presents with severe oral thrush, failure to thrive/severe malnutrition, recurrent severe bacterial infections ([[sepsis]], [[meningitis]], pneumonia), encephalopathy (developmental delays, loss of developmental milestones, microcephaly, increased tone, hyperreflexia), hepatosplenomegaly, and chronic [[diarrhea]].
    - **Neurological Presentations:** Subacute [[encephalitis]], vacuolar myelopathy (mimicking subacute combined degeneration), peripheral sensory neuropathies, and opportunistic CNS infections (toxoplasmosis, cryptococcal [[meningitis]], PML, primary CNS lymphoma).
    - **Renal Presentations (HIV-Associated Nephropathy, HIVAN):** Highly prevalent in Black patients. A collapsing form of focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) characterized by heavy proteinuria, rapid progression to end-stage renal disease (ESRD), and typical hyaline droplet tubulopathy. Also presents as immune complex-mediated glomerulonephritis (IgA nephropathy, lupus-like GN).
    - **Cardiovascular Presentations:** HIV-associated dilated cardiomyopathy.
    - **Rheumatological Presentations:** HIV-associated arthritis (acute, short-lived, asymmetric non-erosive oligoarthritis), reactive arthritis, painful articular syndrome (excruciating, asymmetric bone/joint pain lasting <24 hours), psoriatic arthritis, and diffuse infiltrative lymphocytosis syndrome (DILS; dry eyes and dry mouth Sjögren-like syndrome, but anti-Ro/La negative, characterized by CD8+ [[T-Cell|T-cell]] infiltration of salivary glands).
*   **Incubation Period:**
    - The incubation period from exposure to the onset of the acute retroviral syndrome is typically 2 to 4 weeks.
    - The clinical latency period between primary infection and progression to full-blown AIDS averages approximately 8 to 10 years in untreated adults.
*   **Classic Prodrome:**
    - Constitutional symptoms referable to "[[diarrhea]] and dwindling" (fatigue, night sweats, low-grade fever, progressive weight loss, chronic [[diarrhea]], and mild lymphadenopathy) often precede the development of severe opportunistic infections.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:**
    - While there are no strictly pathognomonic signs, the combination of a mononucleosis-like illness with painful oral/mucosal ulcers and a macular rash on palms/soles is highly suggestive of acute HIV.
    - For AIDS: The triad of progressive weight loss, chronic [[diarrhea]], and oral candidiasis (thrush) or oral hairy leukoplakia (white, non-scrapable corrugated patches on the lateral borders of the tongue caused by [[EBV]]) is highly suggestive of advanced immunodeficiency.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:**
    - **Severe Opportunistic Infections (OIs):**
        - **Fungal:** *[[Pneumocystis jirovecii]]* pneumonia (PCP; presents with dry cough, dyspnea, hypoxia, bilateral ground-glass infiltrates); Cryptococcal meningitis (raised ICP, visual loss); esophageal candidiasis; disseminated histoplasmosis or coccidioidomycosis.
        - **Bacterial:** Tuberculosis (the leading cause of death in HIV-positive individuals; often atypical, smear-negative, or extrapulmonary/miliary in advanced immunosuppression); disseminated *[[Mycobacterium avium]]* complex (MAC; occurs at CD4 <50 cells/µL, presenting with persistent fever, weight loss, [[diarrhea]], bacteremia, and tissue infiltration); recurrent *Salmonella* bacteremia.
        - **Protozoal:** Cerebral toxoplasmosis (multifocal ring-enhancing lesions on brain imaging, presenting with focal deficits, seizures, altered mental status); chronic cryptosporidiosis or isosporiasis.
        - **Viral:** [[CMV]] retinitis (Pizza fundus, retinal necrosis, blindness), [[CMV]] colitis or [[esophagitis]]; [[JC virus]]-induced PML; severe recurrent [[HSV]].
    - **Opportunistic Malignancies:** Kaposi sarcoma; Burkitt lymphoma, diffuse large [[B Cell|B-cell]] lymphoma, primary CNS lymphoma; invasive cervical or anal carcinoma.
    - **Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome (IRIS):** Paradoxical or unmasking hyper-inflammatory response occurring during the first 3 months of initiating ART. Provoked by recovering CD4+ [[T-Cell|T-cell]] responses driving severe inflammation against underlying treated or untreated pathogens (most commonly TB, cryptococci, and herpesviruses).
    - **Long-term Sequelae of Childhood/Adolescent Infection:** Stunting of growth, delayed puberty, chronic bronchiectasis (complicating LIP or recurrent pneumonias), and cognitive deficits.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:**
    - **Primary Reservoir:** Humans. Chronically infected individuals are the only natural reservoir for HIV-1 and HIV-2.
    - **Sylvatic/Animal Reservoirs:** Non-human primates harbor closely related simian immunodeficiency viruses (SIV), from which HIV-1 (from chimpanzees) and HIV-2 (from sooty mangabeys) were acquired via zoonotic transmission.
*   **Transmission Route:**
    - **Sexual Contact:** Unprotected vaginal, anal, or oral intercourse. Anal intercourse carries the highest per-act transmission risk. Transmission is highly facilitated by concurrent ulcerative STIs (such as syphilis, chancroid, [[HSV]]-2).
    - **Parenteral Exposure:** Transfusion of unscreened blood or blood products, sharing of contaminated needles or syringes among people who inject drugs (PWID), and accidental occupational needlestick injuries (per-act needlestick risk is approximately 0.3%).
    - **Vertical (Mother-to-Child) Transmission (MTCT):** Occurs in utero (transplacental), intrapartum (during labor and delivery via exposure to blood and cervicovaginal secretions), or postpartum through breastfeeding.
*   **Vectors:**
    - None. HIV is not transmitted by [[mosquitoes]], [[bedbugs]], or other biting insect vectors.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:**
    - **PMTCT Regimens (Option B+):** Places all pregnant and breastfeeding women living with HIV on lifelong combination ART immediately, regardless of CD4 count, to suppress viral load below 50 copies/mL. Exposed infants receive prophylactic ARVs (such as [[zidovudine]] or [[nevirapine]]) from birth for 4-6 weeks to prevent transmission. Avoidance of breastfeeding (or exclusive breastfeeding if safe alternatives are unavailable) is recommended.
    - **PrEP & PEP:** Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP; daily oral [[tenofovir]]/[[emtricitabine]] for high-risk individuals) and Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP; 3-drug regimen initiated within 72 hours of occupational or sexual exposure, continued for 28 days) are highly effective.
    - **Treatment as Prevention (TasP):** Suppression of viral load to undetectable levels practically eliminates sexual transmission of HIV ("Undetectable = Untransmittable" or U=U).
    - **Universal Precautions:** Standard barrier precautions (gloves, protective eyewear, gown) for handling blood and body fluids. Needle-exchange programs, screening of the entire blood supply, and public health education are cornerstones of control.
    - **Isolation:** Casual person-to-person contact poses no risk. Patients do not require isolation; however, in healthcare settings, patients with active pulmonary TB should be managed under negative-pressure isolation.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Histopathology & Inclusion Bodies:**
    - **Lymph Node Histopathology:**
        - **Early/Chronic Phase:** Follicular hyperplasia with active germinal centers.
        - **Advanced Phase (AIDS):** Follicular involution, depletion of lymphoid elements, replacement of germinal centers with vascular endothelial proliferation, and diffuse fibrosis of lymph node architecture.
    - **HIVAN (Kidney Biopsy):** Classic collapsing glomerulopathy. Characterized by collapse of the glomerular tuft, hypertrophy and hyperplasia of overlying podocytes with extensive foot process effacement, microcystic dilatation of renal tubules, interstitial inflammation, and hyaline droplets within tubular cells.
    - **Brain Histopathology (HIV [[Encephalitis]]):** Perivascular accumulation of multinucleated giant cells (formed by fusion of infected [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]]/microglia) and myelin loss (demyelination) in the white matter, accompanied by astrocytosis and microglial nodules.
    - **[[CMV]] Co-infection:** Pathognomonic large cytomegalic cells with prominent, intranuclear acidophilic inclusion bodies surrounded by a clear halo ("owl's-eye" appearance).
    - **Inclusion Bodies:** HIV itself does not produce distinct pathognomonic inclusion bodies.
*   **Molecular Testing:**
    - **Qualitative & Quantitative [[PCR]] (RT-[[PCR]] and real-time RT-[[PCR]]):** Gold standard for detecting and quantifying viral RNA (viral load) or integrated proviral DNA in whole blood or plasma.
    - **Early Infant Diagnosis (EID):** NAT is mandatory to diagnose infants and children under 18–24 months because maternal anti-HIV IgG antibodies cross the placenta and persist in the infant's circulation for up to 18-24 months, rendering antibody-based assays uninformative. Points-of-care [[PCR]] tests are increasingly utilized.
    - **Genotypic Resistance Testing:** Done by sequencing the viral reverse transcriptase, protease, and integrase genes to identify known mutations conferring resistance to specific ARVs. Recommended at diagnosis and during treatment failure.
*   **Serology & Seroconversion Windows:**
    - **Assay Evolution ([[Lab/Molecular and Immunological/ELISA]]):**
        - **1st Generation (1985):** Indirect [[Lab/Molecular and Immunological/ELISA]] using virus-infected cell lysates. Detects IgG antibodies against HIV-1 (window period of 8–10 weeks).
        - **2nd Generation (1987):** Recombinant/synthetic peptide-based [[Lab/Molecular and Immunological/ELISA]]. Detects IgG antibodies against HIV-1 and HIV-2 (window period of 4–6 weeks).
        - **3rd Generation (1991):** Antigen-sandwich [[Lab/Molecular and Immunological/ELISA]]. Detects both IgG and IgM antibodies against HIV-1, HIV-2, and Group O (window period of 2–3 weeks).
        - **4th Generation (1997):** Combined assay. Detects IgG and IgM anti-HIV-1/2 and the HIV-1 p24 antigen (window period reduced to 2 weeks).
        - **5th Generation (2015):** Multiplexed assay. Simultaneously detects and differentiates HIV-1 antibodies, HIV-2 antibodies, and the p24 antigen (window period of 2 weeks).
    - **Western Blot (Confirmation Test):** Electrophoretic separation of viral proteins blotted onto nitrocellulose. Detects antibodies to envelope glycoproteins (gp120/gp160, gp41), polymerase enzymes (p66, p51, p32), and core structural proteins (p24, p17, p55). A positive Western blot requires the presence of any two bands corresponding to: p24, gp41, and gp120/160. Indeterminate or negative results can occur during early seroconversion. (WB is not necessary in modern testing algorithms utilizing 5th-generation multiplexed [[Lab/Molecular and Immunological/ELISA]]).
    - **Point-of-Care / Rapid Tests:** Immunochromatographic or particle agglutination tests performed on whole blood or oral mucosal transudates. Detect antibodies or combined antibody/p24 antigen. Useful for rapid clinic results and self-testing.
*   **Cell Culture & Assays:**
    - **Viral Isolation:** Co-cultivating patient peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) with mitogen-stimulated, uninfected donor PBMCs. Viral growth is detected after 7–14 days by measuring RT activity or p24 antigen in the culture supernatant. Slow, complex, and restricted to research settings.
    - **Phenotypic Resistance Assays:** Recombinant virus assays. The reverse transcriptase, protease, or integrase genes are cloned from the patient's virus into a reference laboratory strain, and the IC50 (concentration of drug required to inhibit 50% of replication) is determined and compared against a wild-type reference strain to calculate fold-resistance.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **Antiviral Targets & First-Line Agents:**
    - **First-Line Regimens (WHO Guidelines):** Combination Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) using three active drugs. The standard first-line regimen consists of a preferred backbone of two Nucleoside/Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs) plus a potent Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitor (InSTI), typically [[Dolutegravir]] (DTG). Standard first-line single-tablet combination: **[[Tenofovir]] (TDF) + [[Lamivudine]] (3TC) + [[Dolutegravir]] (DTG)** (or [[Emtricitabine]] (FTC) instead of 3TC).
    - **Drug Classes & Mechanism of Action:**
        - **NRTIs:** Structural analogs of native deoxynucleotides; phosphorylated by host kinases and incorporated into the growing viral DNA chain, acting as DNA chain terminators due to the lack of a 3'-hydroxyl group. Examples: [[Tenofovir]] (TDF or TAF), [[Emtricitabine]] (FTC), [[Lamivudine]] (3TC), [[Abacavir]] (ABC), [[Zidovudine]] (AZT), [[Stavudine]] (d4T), [[Didanosine]] (ddI).
        - **[[HIV NNRTI|NNRTIs]]:** Bind to an allosteric pocket near the active site of RT, causing a conformational change that inhibits polymerase activity. Examples: [[Efavirenz]] (EFV), [[Nevirapine]] (NVP), [[Rilpivirine]] (RPV), [[Etravirine]] (ETR), [[Delavirdine]] (DLV).
        - **[[HIV Integrase Inhibitor|InSTIs]]:** Bind to the active site of viral integrase and block the strand transfer step of integration, preventing provirus formation. Examples: [[Dolutegravir]] (DTG), [[Raltegravir]] (RAL), [[Cabotegravir]] (CAB), Bictegravir (BIC).
        - **[[Protease Inhibitor|Protease Inhibitors]] (PIs):** Competitively inhibit the active site of the viral aspartyl protease, preventing cleavage of Gag and Gag-Pol precursor polyproteins. Budding virions remain immature and non-infectious. Examples: [[Darunavir]] (DRV), [[Atazanavir]] (ATV), Lopinavir (LPV), [[Saquinavir]] (SQV), Indinavir (IDV), [[Ritonavir]] (RTV).
        - **Pharmacokinetic Boosters:** [[Ritonavir]] (RTV) or Cobicistat (COBI). Strongly inhibit cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4), prolonging the half-life and exposure of co-administered [[Protease Inhibitor|PIs]] (e.g., [[Darunavir]]/[[ritonavir]]).
        - **Fusion Inhibitors:** [[Enfuvirtide]] (T-20). A biomimetic peptide that binds the HR1 region of gp41, preventing the conformational change required for membrane fusion.
        - **CCR5 Antagonists (Entry Inhibitors):** [[Maraviroc]]. Binds host CCR5, blocking gp120 binding. Requires tropism testing to confirm R5-only virus before initiation.
        - **Capsid Inhibitors:** Lenacapavir. Binds the capsid, disrupting capsid-mediated nuclear import, virion assembly, and egress.
        - **Attachment Inhibitors:** Fostemsavir. Binds gp120, preventing attachment.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Mutations:**
    - Driven by sub-optimal adherence in the presence of replication.
    - **Low Genetic Barrier (Single mutation confers high-level resistance):**
        - **[[HIV NNRTI|NNRTIs]] ([[Efavirenz]], [[Nevirapine]]):** Single K103N mutation in the RT gene confers high-level cross-resistance.
        - **NRTIs ([[Lamivudine]], [[Emtricitabine]]):** Single M184V/I mutation in the RT gene confers high-level resistance (though it hypersensitizes RT to AZT and reduces viral replicative fitness).
    - **High Genetic Barrier (Requires accumulation of multiple mutations):** Boosted [[Protease Inhibitor|PIs]] (such as [[Darunavir]]/[[ritonavir]]) and [[Dolutegravir]] (DTG) have high genetic barriers, making de novo resistance rare.
    - **NRTI Resistance ([[Tenofovir]]):** The K65R mutation in the RT gene.
    - **InSTI Resistance ([[Raltegravir]], Elvitegravir):** Mutations such as Q148H/R/K or N155H in the integrase gene.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Timing Constraints:**
    - **Supportive Care & Timing:** ART should be initiated immediately upon diagnosis ("test and treat" strategy) regardless of CD4 count, to preserve immune function and prevent transmission.
    - **Important Exceptions in Acute Opportunistic Infections:** Immediate ART can provoke life-threatening IRIS in certain CNS infections due to swelling and intracranial pressure:
        - **Cryptococcal Meningitis:** Defer ART for 4 to 6 weeks after starting induction antifungal therapy ([[amphotericin B]] + [[flucytosine]]/[[fluconazole]]) to prevent fatal neuro-IRIS.
        - **Tuberculous Meningitis / Tuberculoma:** Defer ART for 4 to 8 weeks after starting anti-TB therapy.
        - **Pulmonary/Extrapulmonary TB (Non-CNS):** If CD4 count is ≤50 cells/µL, start ART within 2 weeks of initiating anti-TB therapy; if CD4 count is >50 cells/µL, ART can be deferred for up to 8 weeks.
    - **Drug Co-treatment Complexities (TB/HIV):** Co-treatment of TB and HIV is complicated by drug-drug interactions. [[Rifampin|Rifampicin]] is a potent inducer of CYP3A4 and UGT1A1, which drastically reduces concentrations of [[Dolutegravir]] (DTG) and [[Protease Inhibitor|Protease Inhibitors]].
        - **DTG Adjustment:** When co-administered with [[Rifampin|Rifampicin]], the dose of [[Dolutegravir]] must be doubled to 50 mg twice daily (instead of once daily).
        - **PI Adjustment:** Standard boosted [[Protease Inhibitor|PIs]] are contraindicated with [[Rifampin|Rifampicin]]; double-dose boosting or alternative agents must be used.
    - **Prophylaxis Protocols for Advanced Disease (CD4 <200 or stage 3/4):**
        - **[[Pneumocystis jirovecii|PCP]] & Toxoplasmosis:** Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole ([[Cotrimoxazole|Co-trimoxazole]]) daily.
        - **TB preventive therapy:** [[Isoniazid]] preventive therapy (IPT) for at least 6 months (reduces TB risk by half).
        - **Pre-emptive Cryptococcal Therapy:** Serum CrAg screening in all patients with CD4 <100 (or <200) cells/µL. If CrAg positive without [[meningitis]] on LP, pre-emptive oral [[fluconazole]] is initiated.
        - **Disseminated MAC (CD4 <50 cells/µL):** Primary prophylaxis with oral [[Clarithromycin]] or [[Azithromycin]].
    - **Side Effects and Toxicity Nuances:**
        - **[[Tenofovir]] (TDF):** Renal tubulopathy (Fanconi syndrome) and progressive reduction in bone mineral density. Managed by switching to [[Tenofovir]] Alafenamide (TAF) or [[Abacavir]].
        - **[[Zidovudine]] (AZT):** Life-threatening [[Bone Marrow]] suppression (manifesting as macrocytic anemia and neutropenia).
        - **[[Efavirenz]] (EFV):** Neuropsychiatric side effects (severe vivid dreams, insomnia, dizziness, depression).
        - **[[Nevirapine]] (NVP):** Severe, life-threatening hepatotoxicity and Stevens-Johnson syndrome.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:**
    - **Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP):** [[Tenofovir]] disoproxil fumarate (TDF) or [[tenofovir]] alafenamide (TAF) formulated with [[emtricitabine]] (FTC) daily or long-acting [[cabotegravir]] injections every 2 months. High efficacy in preventing infection.
    - **Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP):** Standard 3-drug regimen (e.g., TDF + FTC + DTG) initiated as soon as possible (strictly within 72 hours of exposure), continued for 28 days.
    - **Vaccines:** There are currently no approved vaccines available. Vaccine development is severely hampered by extreme viral genetic diversity, glycan shielding of neutralizing epitopes, rapid establishment of latency, and lack of clear correlates of protective immunity. (An efficacy trial, RV144, showed modest, transient 31% protection using a prime-boost vector strategy, but no reproducible protective vaccine exists).

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "Slim Disease" (Africa, describing the wasting syndrome).
    - "Trojan Horse" (describing how [[Monocyte and Macrophage|monocytes]]/[[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]] and [[Dendritic Cell|dendritic cells]] carry the virus silently across tissue barriers and seed target organs).
    - "Collapsing FSGS" (characteristic pathognomonic renal lesion of HIVAN).
    - "Collapsing glomeruli" or collapsing podocytopathy.
    - "Collapsing podocytopathy" with extensive foot process effacement.
    - "Owl's-eye" inclusion bodies (indicating co-infecting [[CMV]]).
    - "Pizza fundus" (classic [[CMV]] retinitis on ophthalmoscopy).
    - "Thrush" (oral pseudomembranous candidiasis).
    - "Hairy Leukoplakia" ([[EBV]]-driven corrugated white tongue patches).
    - "Multinucleated giant cells" or syncytia in the brain (HIV [[encephalitis]]).
    - "APOBEC3G deamination" and Vif degradation.
    - "Collapsing glomerulopathy".
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - **The Diploid Exception:** Unlike almost all other RNA viruses which are haploid (carrying a single copy of their genome), retroviruses are diploid, carrying two identical linear positive-sense RNA strands in their nucleocapsid.
    - **Enveloped but not infectious:** The genomic RNA of retroviruses is positive-sense, which in other positive-strand RNA viruses (like picornaviruses or coronaviruses) is infectious on its own when injected into a cell. However, retroviral genomic RNA is *not* infectious on its own because replication strictly requires the active reverse transcriptase and integrase enzymes packaged within the mature virion to convert the RNA into proviral DNA.
    - **Lentivirus Nuclear Import:** Most retroviruses can only infect actively dividing cells because they require nuclear envelope breakdown during mitosis to access the host genome. Lentiviruses (including HIV) are unique exceptions; their preintegration complex is actively imported through intact nuclear membranes, allowing them to infect resting, non-dividing cells (such as terminally differentiated [[Monocyte and Macrophage|macrophages]]).
    - **The Berlin Patient / CCR5 delta-32 Cure:** Standard ART cannot cure HIV because of the latent reservoir of memory CD4+ [[T-Cell|T cells]]. Rare cases of HIV cure (first documented in the "Berlin Patient" in 2007) have been achieved following [[Bone Marrow]] transplantation for leukemia using donor stem cells homozygous for the CCR5-delta 32 mutation, replacing the host's immune cells with cells completely resistant to R5-tropic HIV entry.
