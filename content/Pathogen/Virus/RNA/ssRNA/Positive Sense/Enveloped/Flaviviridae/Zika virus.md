### Zika Virus

#### Molecular Virology & Structure
*   **Genomic Architecture:** The viral genome consists of a linear, positive-sense, single-stranded RNA of approximately 11 kilobases in length. The genomic RNA is infectious. It is a member of the genus Flavivirus within the family Flaviviridae and is closely related to the Spondweni virus (SPOV).
*   **Capsid & Nucleocapsid:** The virus possesses an icosahedral nucleocapsid.
*   **Envelope Properties:** The nucleocapsid is surrounded by a lipid envelope. The virus is inactivated by acid pH, heat, lipid solvents, detergents, bleach, phenol, 70% alcohol, and formaldehyde.
*   **Virion Size & Physical Morphology:** Virions are spherical in shape and measure approximately 40 to 60 nm in diameter.
*   **Structural & Non-Structural Proteins:** The virion contains three structural polypeptides, two of which are glycosylated.

#### Viral Replication Cycle
*   **Attachment, Host Receptors & Tropism:** The virus exhibits a neuro-specific tropism, particularly for fetal neuronal tissue.
*   **Entry Mechanism:** N/A
*   **Uncoating & Intracellular Trafficking:** N/A
*   **Transcription & Translation Strategy:** Viral replication occurs entirely in the host cell cytoplasm. The positive-sense RNA genome acts as messenger RNA and encodes an RNA-dependent RNA polymerase.
*   **Assembly, Maturation & Egress:** Virion assembly occurs within the endoplasmic reticulum.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Direct Cytopathic Effects:** N/A
*   **Latent Infection & Reactivation Triggers:** N/A
*   **Oncogenesis Mechanisms:** N/A
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** Cross-reactive antibodies produced against different flaviviruses can occur. Nonneutralizing antibodies may promote [[dengue virus]] infection, a related flavivirus, but specific evasion strategies for Zika are not fully detailed; neutralizing antibodies can block disease.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** The prodrome of flulike symptoms is caused by the host's interferon and cytokine response. The virus can trigger a post-infectious autoimmune response (Guillain-Barre syndrome) in which the immune system attacks the host's nervous system.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Most infections in adults are asymptomatic or result in a mild systemic febrile illness. When symptomatic, patients typically develop fever, headache, a maculopapular rash, arthralgia, and conjunctivitis.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** The virus is associated with severe neurological inflammatory syndromes including Guillain-Barre syndrome (GBS, which may present as axonal or demyelinating varieties frequently with prominent dysautonomia), acute disseminated encephalomyelitis (ADEM), encephalitis, myelitis, and radiculopathy.
*   **Incubation Period:** N/A (Neurological inflammatory syndromes may appear within 4 months of exposure).
*   **Classic Prodrome:** Mild constitutional flulike symptoms.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** N/A
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** The most severe complications occur when pregnant women are infected, particularly during the first trimester. The virus crosses the placenta to infect fetal neuronal tissue, leading to microcephaly, severe congenital brain abnormalities, and other congenital syndromes.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Natural vertebrate hosts include humans, monkeys, and rodents.
*   **Transmission Route:** The primary mode of transmission is zoonotic via the bite of infected mosquitoes. However, Zika virus uniquely exhibits extensive non-vector transmission, including transplacental (maternal-neonatal) transmission, sexual transmission, and transmission via blood and oral routes. The virus can be found in blood, urine, and other body fluids, including semen, where it can remain transmissible by an asymptomatic male for 1 to 4 months.
*   **Vectors:** Mosquitoes, specifically *Aedes* species.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Zika virus was discovered in Uganda in 1947, spread to southeast Asia in the 1950s, and caused an outbreak in Micronesia in 2007. A 2014 outbreak in French Polynesia yielded the first evidence of transplacental transmission. In 2015, the virus emerged in northeastern Brazil, causing a massive epidemic with thousands of cases of newborn microcephaly, leading to the declaration of a national public health emergency regarding birth defects. The virus has since expanded rapidly throughout tropical South America, Central America, the Caribbean, and globally in tropical and subtropical regions.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Histopathology & Inclusion Bodies:** N/A
*   **Molecular Testing:** Detection of viral nucleic acid via reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) from specimens including blood, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) is the standard for diagnosis.
*   **Serology & Seroconversion Windows:** Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) is used for serological diagnosis, though cross-reactivity with other flaviviruses can occur. Screening is available and recommended for pregnant women who may have been exposed.
*   **Cell Culture & Assays:** N/A

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **Antiviral Targets & First-Line Agents:** There are no specific antiviral treatments available for Zika virus. Clinical management is generally supportive.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Mutations:** N/A
*   **Treatment Nuances & Timing Constraints:** N/A
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** There is no currently approved vaccine. Prevention relies heavily on avoiding mosquito exposure in endemic regions (e.g., using repellents, wearing protective clothing) and monitoring pregnant women.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Fetal microcephaly, *Aedes* mosquito, congenital Zika syndrome, Guillain-Barre syndrome, transplacental transmission, prolonged sexual transmission in semen, Brazil 2015 epidemic.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike most arthropod-borne flaviviruses which are exclusively transmitted by insect vectors, Zika virus demonstrates significant rates of sexual transmission (persisting in semen for months) and direct transplacental transmission leading to severe teratogenic effects.
