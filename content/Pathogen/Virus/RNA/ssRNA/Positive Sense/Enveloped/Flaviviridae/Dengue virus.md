### Dengue Virus

#### Molecular Virology & Structure
*   **Genomic Architecture:**
    - Single-stranded, positive-sense RNA (+ssRNA) genome.
    - Approximately 11 kb (kilobases) in overall size.
    - Contains a single open reading frame (ORF) translated as a single large precursor polyprotein, which is processed by co- and post-translational proteolytic cleavage.
    - The genomic RNA is infectious on its own.
    - Subtle genomic variations and subgenomic RNA can significantly impact transmission and pathogenesis. Specifically, subgenomic flaviviral RNA (sfRNA) binds to host TRIM25 to inhibit interferon expression, promoting epidemiological fitness and virus transmission in [[Mosquitoes|mosquito]] salivary glands.
*   **Capsid & Nucleocapsid:**
    - The positive-sense genomic RNA is complexed with the core nucleoprotein (C) to form a symmetrical helical or spherical nucleocapsid core.
    - The capsid (C) protein has a high affinity for genomic RNA and is translated first.
    - The nucleocapsid displays icosahedral symmetry.
*   **Envelope Properties:**
    - Enwrapped in a lipid-containing membrane envelope acquired by budding through host cellular membranes.
    - The envelope is environmentally labile and readily disrupted by acid, detergents, drying, heat, and solvents such as ether, which inactivates the virus. Therefore, the virion must remain wet to endure and transmit.
    - Structurally, the envelope protein tightly surrounds the membrane envelope, closely conforming to the icosahedral symmetry of the inner nucleocapsid.
*   **Virion Size & Physical Morphology:**
    - Spherical, pleomorphic morphology.
    - Ranges from approximately 40 to 60 nm in diameter.
*   **Structural & Non-Structural Proteins:**
    - **Structural Proteins:**
        - **C (Capsid) Protein:** Structural core protein; binds to and packages the genomic RNA to form the nucleocapsid.
        - **prM (Pre-membrane) Protein:** Glycoprotein precursor cleaved by host furin to produce the mature M (membrane) protein. In secondary infections, anti-prM antibodies facilitate the binding and uptake of immature, non-infectious virions into [[Macrophage|macrophages]] via antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE); once internalized, host furin processes prM to M, rendering the virion mature and fully infectious.
        - **E (Envelope) Glycoprotein:** The primary structural envelope glycoprotein. Functions as the Viral Attachment Protein (VAP), binding to host cell surface receptors and promoting envelope-membrane fusion. It is the principal target for neutralizing, protective antibodies.
    - **Non-Structural Proteins (NS1, NS2A, NS2B, NS3, NS4A, NS4B, NS5) :**
        - **NS1:** A secreted and membrane-bound glycoprotein that is highly significant in pathogenesis. It protects the virus from complement- and lectin-mediated neutralization by binding to C4b-binding protein (C4bp). Soluble NS1 directly binds to heparan sulfate and chondroitin sulfate E in the host endothelial glycocalyx, inducing host sialidases and heparinase to disrupt the glycocalyx, triggering cytokine-independent capillary leakage and vascular hyperpermeability.
        - **NS2A:** Small hydrophobic protein involved in viral RNA replication and assembly (N/A for other specific functions in sources).
        - **NS2B:** Hydrophobic protein that acts as an essential cofactor for the NS3 protease.
        - **NS3:** Multifunctional protein possessing serine protease activity (with NS2B), RNA helicase, and NTPase activities. It is an immunodominant target for cell-mediated immunity; CD8+ [[T-Cell|T-cell]] responses in DHF are most marked against NS3 (characterized by high cytokine release and low CD107a degranulation).
        - **NS4A:** Non-structural protein involved in replication complex assembly and ER membrane remodeling (N/A for other details in sources).
        - **NS4B:** Membrane-anchored protein involved in replication and immune escape ; represents a promising target for novel therapeutic small molecule inhibitors.
        - **NS5:** The largest non-structural protein; functions as the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp) to replicate the genome, and possesses methyltransferase activity for applying 5' caps.

#### Viral Replication Cycle
*   **Attachment, Host Receptors & Tropism:**
    - **Attachment:** The E glycoprotein binds to receptors on the host cell surface.
    - **Host Receptors:** Primary attachment is to receptors expressed on myeloid cells. In the presence of sub-neutralizing concentrations of cross-reactive antibodies, entry is mediated via host cell Fcγ receptors (FcγR), which enhances viral uptake 200- to 1000-fold. The E protein also interacts with cell-surface glycosaminoglycans like heparan sulfate.
    - **Tropism:** Strictly tropic for cells of the [[Monocyte]]-[[Macrophage]] lineage, including [[Dendritic Cell|dendritic cells]], [[Monocyte|monocytes]], and [[Macrophage|macrophages]]. Also exhibits tropism for capillary endothelial cells.
*   **Entry Mechanism:**
    - Enters the host cell via receptor-mediated endocytosis into clathrin-coated vesicles.
    - Employs Antibody-Dependent Enhancement (ADE) in secondary infections, utilizing pre-existing heterologous non-neutralizing antibodies to bind Fcγ receptors on macrophages, bypassing standard extracellular neutralization and dramatically increasing internalizing efficiency.
*   **Uncoating & Intracellular Trafficking:**
    - **Uncoating:** Upon endosomal acidification, the low pH triggers a conformational shift in the E glycoprotein, exposing its hydrophobic fusion peptide. This promotes fusion of the viral envelope with the endosomal membrane, releasing the nucleocapsid and genomic RNA into the cytoplasm.
    - **Intracellular Trafficking:** The replication cycle occurs entirely in the cytoplasm. The (+)ssRNA genome is targeted to ribosomes on the rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER) for translation.
*   **Transcription & Translation Strategy:**
    - **Translation:** The positive-sense genomic ssRNA is directly translated by host ribosomes to synthesize a single large precursor polyprotein.
    - **Proteolytic Processing:** Host signalases and the viral NS2B-NS3 serine protease cleave the polyprotein co- and post-translationally to yield mature structural and non-structural proteins.
    - **Transcription/Replication:** Non-structural proteins assemble onto cellular membrane scaffolds derived from the endoplasmic reticulum to form replication complexes/organelles. The viral RdRp (NS5) transcribes the positive-sense genomic RNA into a full-length negative-sense (-) RNA template.
    - This negative-sense intermediate serves as a template to transcribe multiple positive-sense (+) genomic RNA progeny and mRNAs. Unlike alphaviruses, there is no subgenomic RNA transcribed for structural proteins; the entire genome is represented by a single polyprotein.
*   **Assembly, Maturation & Egress:**
    - **Assembly:** Newly synthesized genomic RNA molecules interact with the C protein to assemble into icosahedral capsids. The capsid then associates with prM and E proteins on the membranes of the endoplasmic reticulum (RER).
    - **Maturation:** Immature, non-infectious virions containing prM-E complexes bud into the lumen of the endoplasmic reticulum and travel through the Golgi apparatus. During egress/trafficking, host furin proteases cleave prM to M in mature particles (though in secondary infections, immature particles can egress and be matured after ADE uptake in secondary hosts).
    - **Egress:** Mature virions are transported in secretory vesicles to the plasma membrane and are released into the extracellular space via exocytosis, or released upon cell lysis.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Direct Cytopathic Effects:**
    - Direct lytic infection, cytolysis, and necrosis occur in [[Monocyte|monocytes]], [[Macrophage|macrophages]], [[Dendritic Cell|dendritic cells]], and capillary endothelial cells.
    - Bone marrow suppression during the early phase of illness leads to depression of all marrow elements and a reversible maturation arrest of megakaryocytes.
*   **Latent Infection & Reactivation Triggers:**
    - N/A.
*   **Oncogenesis Mechanisms:**
    - N/A.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:**
    - **Secreted NS1:** NS1 binds to C4b-binding protein (C4bp) to downregulate and modulate classical and lectin complement pathway activation, protecting the virus from complement-mediated lysis.
    - **sfRNA (Subgenomic Flaviviral RNA):** Disrupts host innate immunity. The sfRNA binds to TRIM25, a host ubiquitin ligase, which prevents RIG-I ubiquitination and subsequently inhibits type I interferon expression and signaling. sfRNA in [[Mosquitoes|mosquito]] saliva also suppresses vector salivary gland immunity to increase transmission efficiency.
    - **ADE (Antibody-Dependent Enhancement):** Uses sub-neutralizing heterologous antibodies from a prior infection to facilitate entry into FcγR-bearing host cells ([[Macrophage|macrophages]]/[[Monocyte|monocytes]]), effectively shielding the virus from extracellular neutralization and increasing intracellular replication.
    - **Intracellular Replication Compartments:** Replication occurs within ER-derived membrane vesicles ("replication organelles") that sequester viral dsRNA intermediates, shielding them from cytoplasmic pattern recognition receptors (PRRs like RIG-I and MDA5) and avoiding interferon induction.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:**
    - **Host Defense:** Type I Interferons (IFN-α/β) and [[T-Cell|T-cell]]-mediated cellular immunity are critical for primary viral clearance. Neutralizing antibodies (directed against E glycoprotein) provide lifelong serotype-specific protection.
    - **ADE-associated Immunopathology:** A secondary infection with a heterologous serotype in the presence of sub-neutralizing, cross-reactive IgG antibodies drives ADE, leading to a massive "cytokine storm". Pre-sensitized cross-reactive memory [[T-Cell|T cells]] with low avidity for the current serotype (Original Antigenic Sin) proliferate rapidly and release massive quantities of TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-6, and soluble IL-2 receptors.
    - This hyper-inflammatory response activates complement (releasing C3a and C5a anaphylatoxins and forming terminal SC5b-9 complexes). High levels of inflammatory cytokines and complement activation productively disrupt endothelial cells and vascular integrity.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:**
    - **Dengue Fever (DF) / Classic Dengue:** A self-limiting, acute febrile illness characterized by sudden onset of high fever (up to 40°C), severe retro-orbital pain (pain behind the eyes, worse with eye movement), severe frontal headache, myalgia, arthralgia, backache, deep bone pain ("breakbone fever"), anorexia, vomiting, and generalized lymphadenopathy. Fever typically lasts 2–7 days and may have a biphasic or "saddleback" course (subsiding on day 3 and rising again on day 5–8). Diffuse macular/maculopapular rash appears on day 3–4, starting on the trunk/chest before spreading to face and extremities, often accompanied by pruritus and hyperesthesia.
    - **Severe Dengue / Dengue Hemorrhagic Fever (DHF) & Dengue Shock Syndrome (DSS):** Formerly categorized into four grades (DHF I-IV, with III and IV representing DSS). Characterized by the acute onset of classic dengue symptoms, which deteriorate rapidly around the time of defervescence (the critical phase, around day 5). Hallmarks include:
        - **Plasma Leakage:** Caused by functional vascular hyperpermeability and disruption of the endothelial glycocalyx. Leads to plasma extravasation, manifesting as pleural effusions, ascites, hypoproteinemia, and severe hemoconcentration (manifested by a ≥20% rise in hematocrit).
        - **Hypovolemic Shock (DSS):** Occurs when plasma loss crosses a critical threshold, presenting as cold, clammy extremities, tachycardia, hypotension, and a narrowed pulse pressure (<20 mmHg).
        - **Abnormal Hemostasis / Hemorrhage:** Thrombocytopenia (platelets <100 x 10^9/L, often dropping rapidly below 100,000/µL before the onset of shock). Hemorrhagic manifestations include petechiae (especially on extremities), ecchymoses, purpura, epistaxis, gum bleeding, gastrointestinal hemorrhage (hematemesis, melena, hematochezia), menorrhagia, and uncontrolled bleeding at venipuncture/injection sites.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:**
    - **Atypical Severe Dengue / Organ Impairment:** Can present as severe organ dysfunction even in the absence of classic plasma leakage or shock:
        - **Hepatic Impairment:** Fulminant hepatitis with tender hepatomegaly, jaundice, and transaminitis with AST/ALT elevated >1000 U/L.
        - **Neurological Manifestations (Dengue [[Encephalitis]]):** Presents as acute [[encephalitis]] syndrome with altered consciousness, convulsions, sensory changes, and potentially long-term neurological sequelae.
        - **Cardiac Manifestations:** Acute [[myocarditis]], myocardial depression, and conduction abnormalities (such as prolongation of PR and QT intervals on ECG).
        - **Renal Manifestations (Dengue Nephropathy):** Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) caused by hypovolemia or rhabdomyolysis-induced myoglobinuria. Immune complex-mediated Glomerulonephritis (GN) manifesting as hematuria, proteinuria, and transient renal insufficiency due to immune complexes depositing in the glomerular basement membrane.
*   **Incubation Period:**
    - Typically 5–8 days, with an overall clinical range of 3–14 days.
*   **Classic Prodrome:**
    - Mild prodromal symptoms of general malaise, chills, and moderate headache may precede the sudden onset of high fever.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:**
    - **Islands of White in a Sea of Red:** A classic, late erythematous macular recovery rash that appears 3–6 days after fever onset, featuring patches of completely normal, spared skin (islands of white) within the confluent erythematous rash.
    - **Positive Tourniquet Test:** The appearance of 10 or more petechiae per square inch under an inflated blood pressure cuff (held midway between systolic and diastolic pressure) indicating capillary fragility.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:**
    - **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC):** Occurs in severe DHF/DSS, leading to consumption coagulopathy and massive internal bleeding.
    - **Fluid Overload:** A critical complication of over-aggressive fluid resuscitation during the critical phase, leading to pulmonary edema and respiratory distress as capillary permeability resolves and fluid is reabsorbed in the recovery phase.
    - **Neurological Sequelae:** Long-term cognitive or motor deficits following dengue [[encephalitis]].
    - **Convalescent Asthenia:** Prolonged weakness, depression, and fatigue lasting several weeks to months during convalescence.
    - **Obstetric Complications:** In pregnant women, severe dengue carries a high risk of maternal and fetal mortality (especially in the third trimester), spontaneous abortion, preterm birth, and low birth weight.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:**
    - **Primary Reservoir:** Humans in urban/peridomestic transmission cycles.
    - **Sylvatic Reservoir:** Non-human primates (monkeys) in jungle/sylvatic cycles in Southeast Asia and West Africa.
*   **Transmission Route:**
    - **Vector-Borne:** Bites of infected female [[mosquitoes]].
    - **Blood-Borne (Rare):** Person-to-person transmission via blood transfusion or organ transplantation.
    - **Vertical Transmission:** In utero or peripartum vertical transmission from viraemic mothers to their newborns at the time of delivery.
    - **Breast Milk:** Detected in breast milk of viraemic mothers, though not yet confirmed as a definitive transmission route.
*   **Vectors:**
    - **Primary Vector:** *Aedes aegypti*. A highly efficient peridomestic, daytime-biting [[Mosquitoes|mosquito]] that breeds in clean domestic water containers.
    - **Secondary Vector:** *Aedes albopictus*. Competent vector capable of surviving at lower temperatures, enabling transmission in temperate regions.
    - **Other Vectors:** *Aedes polynesiensis* and species of the *Aedes scutellaris* complex.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:**
    - **Vector Control:** The primary means of prevention. Involves eliminating vector breeding sites (emptying standing water in domestic containers), larvicide treatment, using insecticides/spraying, wearing protective clothing, and using insecticide-treated bed nets.
    - **Blood Supply Screening:** Dengue virus is screened in endemic regions or during outbreaks to prevent transfusion-transmitted cases.
    - **Isolation:** Universal precautions are sufficient for managing patients; dengue is not transmitted directly person-to-person via casual contact or aerosols. Patients should be protected from [[Mosquitoes|mosquito]] bites (using bed nets/indoor isolation) during the viraemic febrile phase to prevent infecting local vector populations.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Histopathology & Inclusion Bodies:**
    - **Liver Pathology:** Focal necrosis of hepatocytes and Kupffer cells with the formation of Councilman-like bodies (acidophilic, apoptotic hepatocytes). Eosinophilic degeneration of hepatocytes.
    - **Lymphoid Tissues:** Proliferation of reticuloendothelial cells with accelerated phagocytosis (erythrophagocytosis) ; active proliferation of [[Plasma Cell & Memory B Cell|plasma cells]] and lymphoblastoid cells in [[Spleen]], [[Thymus]], and [[Lymph Nodes]].
    - **Vascular Changes:** Vasodilation, microvascular congestion, perivascular hemorrhage, and arterial wall edema.
    - **Antigen Localization:** Dengue viral antigen detectable by immunohistochemistry predominantly in splenic [[Macrophage|macrophages]], thymic cells, Kupffer cells, and lymph node sinusoidal lining cells.
    - **Inclusion Bodies:** N/A (Unlike herpesviruses or [[rabies]], dengue does not produce pathognomonic intracellular inclusion bodies like Negri bodies or Cowdry A bodies).
*   **Molecular Testing:**
    - **RT-PCR (Reverse Transcriptase [[PCR]]):** The gold standard for rapid, highly sensitive identification and serotyping (DENV1–4) of viral RNA in acute-phase serum during the febrile/viraemic phase (typically days 1–5 after onset).
*   **Serology & Seroconversion Windows:**
    - **NS1 Antigen Capture [[Lab/Molecular and Immunological/ELISA]]:** Detects circulating non-structural protein 1 in serum. Highly useful for early diagnosis, as NS1 is detectable from day 1 of fever and remains positive for up to 9 days in both primary and secondary infections.
    - **IgM Capture [[Lab/Molecular and Immunological/ELISA]] (MAC-ELISA):** Detects dengue-specific IgM antibodies, which develop within 3–5 days of illness onset, peak at 2 weeks, and remain detectable for up to 2–3 months.
    - **IgG [[Lab/Molecular and Immunological/ELISA]]:** IgG antibodies appear around day 7 in primary infection and persist for life. In secondary infections, high-titer IgG is detectable rapidly in the acute phase (cross-reactive with other serotypes).
    - **Hemagglutination Inhibition (HI) Test:** Measures titers of neutralizing/cross-reactive antibodies; a four-fold or greater rise in antibody titer between paired acute and convalescent sera is diagnostic.
    - **Cross-Reactivity Challenges:** Serological assays show significant cross-reactivity with other flaviviruses (e.g., [[Zika virus|Zika]], [[Yellow fever virus|Yellow Fever]], [[West nile virus|West Nile]], [[Japanese encephalitis virus|Japanese Encephalitis]]). Epitope-blocking ELISAs or Neutralization (N) tests are required to confirm species-specific antibodies.
*   **Cell Culture & Assays:**
    - **Viral Isolation:** Achieved by inoculating patient serum onto [[Mosquitoes|mosquito]] cell lines (such as C6/36) followed by RT-[[PCR]] or immunofluorescence to identify the recovered virus. Difficult to perform routinely.
    - **Plaque Reduction Neutralization Test (PRNT):** The gold standard assay to measure type-specific neutralizing antibody titers and determine previous exposure history.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **Antiviral Targets & First-Line Agents:**
    - **First-Line Agents:** Presently, there are no approved specific antiviral drugs or targeted therapeutics available for Dengue Virus. Treatment is strictly supportive.
    - **Experimental Targets:** Active research is focusing on developing monoclonal antibodies to block E glycoprotein-mediated entry, and small-molecule inhibitors targeting the viral NS4B protein  or the NS3-NS2B protease.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Mutations:**
    - N/A (Because there are no approved antivirals, clinical resistance mechanisms are not documented; however, genetic drift and mutations occur spontaneously ).
*   **Treatment Nuances & Timing Constraints:**
    - **Supportive Care (Fluid Management):** The absolute cornerstone of therapy.
        - **In Outpatients (Febrile Phase):** Standard oral rehydration therapy and antipyretics.
        - **Contraindicated Medications:** Aspirin, ibuprofen, and other non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are strictly contraindicated due to their antiplatelet effects, which exacerbate hemorrhage, and the risk of Reye's syndrome in children. Acetaminophen (paracetamol) is the preferred antipyretic. Corticosteroids (e.g., [[dexamethasone]]) have shown no clinical benefit.
        - **Critical Phase Monitoring:** Close monitoring for "warning signs" (Box 21.1) is vital as the patient enters defervescence.
        - **Intravenous Fluid Resuscitation:** For severe dengue with shock, immediate IV infusion of crystalloids (e.g., 5% dextrose in normal saline or Ringer's lactate) at 5–10 mL/kg/hr, or colloids (e.g., dextran, starch, plasma) for hypotensive shock.
        - **Fluid Overload Risk:** Clinicians must carefully titrate and reduce IV fluids as hemodynamics stabilize and capillary leakage resolves, to prevent pulmonary edema and acute congestive heart failure during the recovery phase.
        - **Hemorrhage Management:** If the hematocrit falls without hemodynamic improvement, severe internal hemorrhage must be suspected. Treat immediately with packed red blood cell transfusion (5–10 mL/kg) or fresh whole blood (10–20 mL/kg). Platelet transfusions are indicated only for massive bleeding; prophylactic platelet transfusions are not clinically supported and should be avoided.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:**
    - **Dengvaxia (CYD-TDV):** The first licensed dengue vaccine.
        - **Vaccine Composition:** A recombinant, live attenuated, tetravalent vaccine consisting of the pre-membrane (prM) and envelope (E) proteins of DENV1–4 on a [[Yellow Fever virus]] 17D vaccine vector backbone.
        - **Protocols & Safety Constraints:** Highly serostatus-dependent. It is only indicated for individuals with documented prior laboratory-confirmed dengue infection (seropositive individuals).
        - **Critical Safety Exception:** In dengue-naive (seronegative) individuals, vaccination acts as an immunologically silent primary infection. When these vaccinated naive individuals later encounter wild-type dengue, they experience a waning heterologous antibody response that triggers ADE, dramatically increasing the cumulative 5-year incidence of severe dengue, DHF/DSS, and hospitalization. Therefore, pre-vaccination screening is mandatory, and the vaccine is contraindicated in seronegative individuals.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "Breakbone Fever" or "Break back fever".
    - "Islands of white in a sea of red" (characteristic recovery rash).
    - "Tourniquet test" (clinical test for capillary fragility).
    - "ADE (Antibody-Dependent Enhancement)".
    - "Original Antigenic Sin" ([[T-Cell|T-cell]] skewing in secondary infection).
    - "NS1 Antigen" (key diagnostic marker and pathogenic trigger).
    - "Councilman bodies" (apoptotic hepatocytes).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - **Detergent-labile but vector-borne:** Unlike most enveloped RNA viruses, which are extremely fragile and cannot survive environmental conditions (limiting transmission to direct contact or secretions), dengue survives and replicates persistently inside mosquito vectors, using day-biting [[mosquitoes]] to traverse skin barriers directly into the bloodstream.
    - **The ADE paradox:** Typically, pre-existing antibodies provide protective immunity against viral re-infection. For dengue, pre-existing cross-reactive antibodies to a different serotype are instead the primary driver of severe, life-threatening immunopathology (DHF/DSS) via ADE.
    - **Vaccine that causes severe disease:** Dengvaxia is one of the very few vaccines where administration to a naive (seronegative) recipient is contraindicated because it increases the risk of severe, life-threatening disease upon subsequent natural infection.
