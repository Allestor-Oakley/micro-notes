### Hantavirus

#### Molecular Virology & Structure
*   **Genomic Architecture:** Contains a triple-segmented, negative-sense (and ambisense), single-stranded RNA genome. The overall genome size ranges from 11 to 21 kb. The genome is divided into Large (L), Medium (M), and Small (S) segments that associate with protein to form nucleocapsids. The nucleocapsids form circles.
*   **Capsid & Nucleocapsid:** Features a helical nucleocapsid within a roughly spherical virion particle measuring 90 to 120 nm in diameter.
*   **Envelope Properties:** The virion is enveloped. The lipid envelope contains two viral glycoproteins, Gc (G1) and Gn (G2). Uniquely, the virus lacks a matrix protein entirely.
*   **Virion Size & Physical Morphology:** Roughly spherical and pleomorphic, ranging from 80 to 120 nm in diameter.
*   **Structural & Non-Structural Proteins:** The virus encodes its own RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (L protein, ~170 kDa) on the L segment. The M segment encodes the Gc glycoprotein (~75 kDa), Gn glycoprotein (~65 kDa), and NSm nonstructural protein (15–17 kDa). The S segment encodes the N structural nucleocapsid protein (~25 kDa) and NSs nonstructural protein (~10 kDa).

#### Viral Replication Cycle
*   **Attachment, Host Receptors & Tropism:** Exhibits specific tropism for the vasculature (endothelial cells), kidneys, and lungs.
*   **Entry Mechanism:** N/A
*   **Uncoating & Intracellular Trafficking:** N/A
*   **Transcription & Translation Strategy:** Replicates entirely in the host cell cytoplasm. The negative-strand RNA genome serves as a template for individual mRNAs, requiring an RNA-dependent RNA polymerase.
*   **Assembly, Maturation & Egress:** Maturation and assembly occur by budding into the Golgi apparatus.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Direct Cytopathic Effects:** Although not overtly cytolytic, the virus disrupts endothelial cell function, causing profound vascular permeability and capillary leak syndrome.
*   **Latent Infection & Reactivation Triggers:** Establishes persistent, lifelong, chronic asymptomatic infections in its natural rodent hosts, which leads to long-term viral shedding in the rodent's urine, feces, and saliva without deleterious effects to the animal.
*   **Oncogenesis Mechanisms:** N/A
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** N/A
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Pathogenesis is largely attributed to immunopathogenesis. Infection induces highly cytolytic host immune responses that cause hemorrhagic tissue destruction and lethal pulmonary disease. The characteristic prodrome of flulike symptoms is directly driven by the host's interferon and cytokine response to the intense viremia.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (HFRS) and Hantavirus pulmonary syndrome (HPS). Over 20 pathogenic hantaviruses are currently recognized, causing estimated 100,000–200,000 cases annually worldwide.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Severe acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), shock, disseminated intravascular coagulation, and severe pulmonary edema.
*   **Incubation Period:** Usually 1 to 2 weeks, up to a maximum of 21 days.
*   **Classic Prodrome:** Abrupt onset of a severe flulike illness characterized by fever, lethargy, severe headache, myalgia, nausea, vomiting, cough, and a "sore chest."
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** In HFRS, rapid progression to microvascular instability, impaired hemostasis, capillary leak, and renal failure. In HPS, rapid progression to severe respiratory distress, hypoxia, and bilateral pulmonary infiltrates.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Progression to severe hemodynamic instability, bleeding, hypovolemic shock, multiorgan system failure, and death. HPS case fatality rates are extremely high, often reaching 30% to 50% (and depending on the specific virus, varying from <1% to 50%).

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Rodents. Each hantavirus species is associated with a specific rodent host (e.g., the deer mouse, *Peromyscus maniculatus*, for Sin Nombre virus; the common Norway rat for Seoul virus).
*   **Transmission Route:** Zoonotic transmission occurs strictly by the inhalation of aerosolized, dried rodent excreta (urine, feces, and saliva). Direct contact with rodents or rodent bites can also transmit the virus. With the notable exception of the Andes virus, human-to-human transmission does not occur.
*   **Vectors:** None.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Disease distribution is entirely determined by the geographic range of the specific rodent reservoirs. Hantaviruses are divided into Old World groups (Asia, Europe, Africa) associated with HFRS (e.g., Hantaan, Seoul, Puumala) and New World groups (the Americas) associated with HPS (e.g., Sin Nombre, Andes, New York, Bayou, Black Creek Canal, Laguna Negra). Sin Nombre virus gained notoriety in 1993 during an outbreak of fatal respiratory disease in the Four Corners region of the USA (Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico, Utah). Seoul virus is found globally because the Norway rat has spread worldwide on ships.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Histopathology & Inclusion Bodies:** N/A
*   **Molecular Testing:** Reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) of blood, tissue, or respiratory specimens is the primary and accepted method for detecting viral RNA. Sequencing of PCR products is utilized to identify the specific infecting hantavirus strain.
*   **Serology & Seroconversion Windows:** Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) is utilized to detect IgM and IgG antibodies or viral antigens directly from clinical specimens during the intense viremic phase.
*   **Cell Culture & Assays:** Hantaviruses are notoriously difficult to isolate in routine cell culture.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **Antiviral Targets & First-Line Agents:** [[Ribavirin]] (a guanosine analog) has broad-spectrum activity against many hemorrhagic fever viruses. Activated protein C has been used experimentally to prevent disseminated intravascular coagulation.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Mutations:** N/A
*   **Treatment Nuances & Timing Constraints:** Treatment is primarily optimized supportive care, often requiring critical care interventions such as prompt intubation, mechanical ventilation for ARDS/HPS, and meticulous fluid management for capillary leak and shock. [[Ribavirin]] has been shown to successfully reduce mortality in HFRS if administered early (within 4 days of symptom onset); however, clinical trials of [[ribavirin]] for HPS have been inconclusive due to limitations in study design.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** No vaccines are currently available. Prevention relies predominantly on aggressive rodent control measures and minimizing human exposure to rodent excreta in rural or infested environments.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Sin Nombre virus, deer mouse (*Peromyscus maniculatus*), Four Corners region outbreak, Hantavirus pulmonary syndrome (HPS), hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (HFRS), rodent urine aerosols, Andes virus.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike all other members of the *Bunyaviridae* family—which are arthropod-borne viruses (arboviruses) transmitted by mosquitoes, ticks, or flies—hantaviruses are a unique exception transmitted strictly by rodents without an insect vector. Furthermore, unlike other negative-strand RNA viruses, members of the *Bunyaviridae* uniquely lack a matrix protein. Finally, while human-to-human transmission is virtually nonexistent among hantaviruses, the Andes virus from South America represents a unique exception where person-to-person spread has been documented.
