### Lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus

#### Molecular Virology & Structure
*   **Genomic Architecture:** Contains a double-segmented (designated L and S), circular, single-stranded RNA genome. The overall genome size is 10 to 14 kb. The S genome segment exhibits an unusual ambisense coding strategy.
*   **Capsid & Nucleocapsid:** The virion uniquely incorporates nonfunctional host cell ribosomes during assembly, which gives the internal core a characteristic "sandy" or grainy appearance under electron microscopy.
*   **Envelope Properties:** Enveloped.
*   **Virion Size & Physical Morphology:** Spherical and pleomorphic, ranging from 50 to 300 nm in diameter (mean diameter of 110 to 130 nm).
*   **Structural & Non-Structural Proteins:** The virus encodes four major polypeptides. The virion packages its own transcriptase (RNA-dependent RNA polymerase).

#### Viral Replication Cycle
*   **Attachment, Host Receptors & Tropism:** Spreads via the blood to target tissues, including the central nervous system and neurons.
*   **Entry Mechanism:** N/A
*   **Uncoating & Intracellular Trafficking:** N/A
*   **Transcription & Translation Strategy:** Replicates entirely in the host cell cytoplasm. Encodes its own RNA-dependent RNA polymerase to facilitate replication.
*   **Assembly, Maturation & Egress:** Assembles by incorporating host ribosomes into the particle and matures by budding from the host cell plasma membrane.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Direct Cytopathic Effects:** N/A
*   **Latent Infection & Reactivation Triggers:** Establishes a persistent, chronic infection in its natural rodent hosts (*Mus musculus*). In newborn mice (established via in utero infection), the virus establishes a chronic infection with circulating virus but little or no apparent disease.
*   **Oncogenesis Mechanisms:** N/A
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** In animal models (e.g., newborn mice), an immunologic tolerance develops in which virus-specific [[T Cell|T cells]] are not activated, preventing cellular immune clearance.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Antibody can block the disease. In the animal model (mice), the host produces antibody against viral proteins that subsequently form antigen-antibody complexes with circulating virus, producing immune complex disease. In humans, the prodrome of flulike symptoms is strictly caused by the host's interferon and cytokine response to the viremia.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Often an acute disease manifested by aseptic meningitis or a mild systemic [[influenza]]-like illness.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Rarely, it can cause a severe encephalomyelitis or a fatal systemic disease in healthy individuals.
*   **Incubation Period:** N/A
*   **Classic Prodrome:** Characterized by systemic interferon-induced flulike symptoms (fever, lethargy, severe headache, myalgia, nausea, and vomiting) driven by the initial viremia.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** N/A
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Progression to meningitis presents with severe headache, stiff neck, photophobia, and cerebrospinal fluid pleocytosis (e.g., elevated white blood cells, typically predominantly lymphocytes, with normal glucose and protein).

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** The natural vector and primary reservoir is the wild house mouse (*Mus musculus*). It is endemic in mice (about 5% of mice throughout the United States carry the virus). It can also chronically infect mouse or hamster colonies and may infect pet rodents.
*   **Transmission Route:** Zoonotic transmission occurs primarily via the inhalation of infectious aerosols generated from rodent urine or feces, or via contact with mouse droppings. There is no evidence of horizontal person-to-person spread.
*   **Vectors:** None.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Discovered in 1933, the virus is widely distributed worldwide, with particular prevalence noted in Europe and the United States.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Histopathology & Inclusion Bodies:** N/A
*   **Molecular Testing:** Reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) genome analysis of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) is highly sensitive for diagnosis.
*   **Serology & Seroconversion Windows:** Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA).
*   **Cell Culture & Assays:** N/A

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **Antiviral Targets & First-Line Agents:** N/A
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Mutations:** N/A
*   **Treatment Nuances & Timing Constraints:** Most cases (such as aseptic meningitis) resolve completely within approximately 1 week. Treatment is typically supportive.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** No vaccine is available. Prevention relies predominantly on rodent control and avoiding exposure to mouse-infested areas.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Sandy" virion appearance, packaged host ribosomes, ambisense RNA, *Mus musculus* (house mouse) reservoir, rodent droppings, aseptic meningitis, Old World arenavirus.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike almost all other virus families, arenaviruses actively incorporate nonfunctional host ribosomes into the mature virus particle during assembly, giving the virion a unique "sandy" appearance (from the Latin *arena*, meaning sand) under electron microscopy.
