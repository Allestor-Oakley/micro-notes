### Hendra Virus

#### Molecular Virology & Structure
*   **Genomic Architecture:** The viral genome consists of one molecule of single-stranded, negative-sense RNA. The genome is unsegmented and encodes an RNA-dependent RNA polymerase.
*   **Capsid & Nucleocapsid:** The virus possesses a helical nucleocapsid that associates with the matrix (M) protein at the inner surface of the envelope membrane. The nucleocapsid is formed by the single-stranded RNA associating with the nucleoprotein (N), phosphoprotein (P), and large protein (L).
*   **Envelope Properties:** The virion is enveloped by a lipid bilayer containing a larger viral attachment glycoprotein and a smaller fusion (F) glycoprotein. Unlike several other paramyxoviruses, members of the Henipavirus genus lack neuraminidase activity.
*   **Virion Size & Physical Morphology:** As a paramyxovirus, the virion is relatively large in size.
*   **Structural & Non-Structural Proteins:** Structural components include the matrix (M) protein, the fusion (F) protein, and a viral attachment glycoprotein. The nucleocapsid contains the nucleoprotein (N), the phosphoprotein (P), and the large protein (L), which serves as the viral polymerase. The virus does not possess a neuraminidase enzyme.

#### Viral Replication Cycle
*   **Attachment, Host Receptors & Tropism:** Viral attachment to the host cell is mediated by the larger envelope glycoprotein, although Hendra virus uniquely lacks the neuraminidase activity used by other paramyxoviruses for receptor interactions.
*   **Entry Mechanism:** The smaller fusion (F) glycoprotein promotes the fusion of the viral envelope directly with the host cell's plasma membrane.
*   **Uncoating & Intracellular Trafficking:** N/A
*   **Transcription & Translation Strategy:** The RNA-dependent RNA polymerase is carried directly into the cell within the nucleocapsid. Transcription and protein synthesis occur entirely within the host cell's cytoplasm. The negative-sense genome is transcribed into individual messenger RNAs (mRNAs) and a full-length positive-sense RNA template.
*   **Assembly, Maturation & Egress:** New viral genomes are replicated from the positive-sense RNA template and associate with the L, N, and P proteins to assemble into helical nucleocapsids. These nucleocapsids subsequently associate with M proteins at the plasma membrane, which has been modified by newly synthesized and processed viral glycoproteins. Mature virions acquire their envelope by budding from the host cell plasma membrane and exit without killing the host cell.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** The natural host and reservoir for the virus is the fruit bat, also known as the flying fox. The virus is endemic in fruit bat species in southern Indonesia and Australia.
*   **Transmission Route:** Humans are accidental hosts who acquire the infection through contact with horses, which serve as intermediate amplifying hosts after acquiring the virus from bats. Direct bat-to-human or human-to-human transmission of Hendra virus is not thought to occur. The emergence of this zoonotic virus is attributed to ecologic changes, habitat loss, and animal husbandry practices that increase contact between bats, humans, and livestock. During a 2008 equine outbreak, there was a 10% attack rate among veterinary clinic staff exposed to infected horses. The virus can affect a broad host range, including pigs, dogs, cats, humans, and other mammals.
