### Nipah Virus

#### Molecular Virology & Structure
*   **Genomic Architecture:** The viral genome is a linear, negative-sense, single-stranded, non-segmented RNA of approximately 15 kilobases in length. The sequence of the viral genome separates into two distinct lineages/genotypes: Nipah-Malaysia (NiV-M) and Nipah-Bangladesh (NiV-B).
*   **Capsid & Nucleocapsid:** The virus possesses a helical nucleocapsid (13 or 18 nm in diameter) that consists of the negative-sense, single-stranded RNA associated with the nucleoprotein (N), phosphoprotein (P), and large protein (L). The L protein serves as the RNA polymerase, the P protein facilitates RNA synthesis, and the N protein maintains genomic structure.
*   **Envelope Properties:** The nucleocapsid is surrounded by a fragile, pleomorphic lipid envelope that measures 150 nm to 300 nm (and occasionally up to 700 nm) in diameter. The viral matrix (M) protein lines the inner surface of the envelope. The envelope is studded with 8- to 12-nm glycoprotein spikes.
*   **Virion Size & Physical Morphology:** The virion is large, pleomorphic, and generally ranges from 150 nm to over 300 nm (up to 700 nm) in diameter.
*   **Structural & Non-Structural Proteins:** The virion contains six main structural proteins. The internal proteins are the nucleocapsid (N), phosphoprotein (P), and large polymerase (L) protein. The envelope contains the matrix (M) protein and two transmembrane glycoproteins: the viral attachment glycoprotein (G) and the fusion (F) protein. Nipah virus specifically uses a G protein for attachment, which uniquely lacks both hemagglutinating and neuraminidase activities found in other paramyxoviruses. The F protein must be activated by proteolytic cleavage into F1 and F2 glycopeptides (held together by a disulfide bond) to express membrane-fusing activity. Additional non-structural proteins (V and C) are generated from alternative transcripts of the P gene.

#### Viral Replication Cycle
*   **Attachment, Host Receptors & Tropism:** Infection is initiated by the binding of the viral G glycoprotein to specific host cell protein receptors. Unlike some other paramyxoviruses, Nipah virus does not bind to sialic acid and does not require neuraminidase activity for entry or egress.
*   **Entry Mechanism:** Following attachment, the F1 cleavage product of the viral fusion (F) glycoprotein undergoes complex refolding to promote direct fusion of the viral envelope with the host cell's plasma membrane at a neutral pH. This allows the virus to bypass internalization through endosomes.
*   **Uncoating & Intracellular Trafficking:** Because the viral envelope fuses directly with the plasma membrane, the viral nucleocapsid is released directly into the host cell cytoplasm.
*   **Transcription & Translation Strategy:** The entire replication cycle takes place in the host cell cytoplasm. The RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (L and P proteins) is carried into the cell within the nucleocapsid. There is no need for exogenous primers and no dependence on cell nuclear functions. The polymerase transcribes the negative-sense genome into individual, monocistronic messenger RNAs (mRNAs) for each gene, governed by transcriptional start and termination sequences. Viral proteins are synthesized in the cytoplasm, and viral glycoproteins (G and F) are synthesized and glycosylated via the cellular secretory pathway (endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi).
*   **Assembly, Maturation & Egress:** The viral polymerase complex copies the full-length genome to synthesize a positive-strand antigenome intermediate, which serves as a template to generate new full-length negative-sense genomes. These new genomes associate with N, P, and L proteins to form progeny nucleocapsids. The nucleocapsids migrate to the cell surface, where they associate with M proteins at sites on the plasma membrane modified with the viral G and F glycoproteins. Mature virions are released by budding from the plasma membrane without lysing the host cell.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Direct Cytopathic Effects:** Nipah virus directly induces cell-to-cell fusion, resulting in the formation of multinucleated giant cells known as syncytia. Infection causes widespread endothelial damage and vasculitis in the brain parenchyma, leading to microinfarcts caused by vasculitis-induced thrombosis. Direct neural invasion also occurs.
*   **Latent Infection & Reactivation Triggers:** The virus can cause late-onset, relapsing encephalitis months to several years after the initial exposure, even in patients who originally had mild or asymptomatic infections. This clinical and immunological presentation is highly reminiscent of subacute sclerosing panencephalitis (SSPE) caused by the [[Measles virus|measles]] virus.
*   **Oncogenesis Mechanisms:** N/A
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** The V and C proteins (derived from the P gene) function as potent antagonists of innate host protections, specifically by interfering with type I interferon induction and action. Furthermore, the ability of the virus to spread directly from cell to cell via syncytia formation allows it to evade neutralizing antibodies.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** N/A

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Nipah virus causes severe, acute encephalitis characterized by fever, reduced level of consciousness, myoclonus (typically involving the diaphragm, arms, and neck), areflexia, hypotonia, tremors, and cerebellar dysfunction. About one-third of patients exhibit meningism, and 20% experience generalized seizures. Severe cases progress to brainstem involvement with pinpoint unreactive pupils, abnormal oculocephalic reflexes, tachycardia, hypertension, and coma. The NiV-B (Bangladesh) lineage frequently presents with additional atypical pneumonia and acute respiratory distress symptoms, with chest radiographs showing diffuse interstitial infiltrates.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Late-onset and relapsing encephalitis can occur months to years after the initial infection. Some infections may present initially as purely respiratory or remain asymptomatic until late neurological relapse occurs.
*   **Incubation Period:** The incubation period ranges from 7 to 40 days.
*   **Classic Prodrome:** N/A
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** Small lesions in the subcortical and deep white matter with surrounding oedema are typically observed on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** The disease has a high case-fatality rate: approximately 35% in the Malaysian outbreaks (NiV-M) and ranging from 60% to 100% in Bangladesh and India outbreaks (NiV-B). Survivors may suffer from persistent neurological deficits.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** The natural reservoir is the "flying fox" fruit bat of the genus *Pteropus* (family Pteropodidae).
*   **Transmission Route:** The virus is excreted in bat urine, saliva, and excreta. Humans can be infected via inhalation of respiratory secretions from infected pigs or humans, direct contact with bat excreta, or ingestion of fruit/raw date palm sap contaminated by bats. In Malaysia (NiV-M), pigs acted as the intermediate amplifying host, infecting pig farmers and abattoir workers. In Bangladesh and India (NiV-B), no intermediate host is identified; transmission occurs directly from bats (via contaminated date palm sap) or through high rates of human-to-human transmission (via respiratory secretions and saliva). The virus affects a broad host range including humans, pigs, dogs, horses, cats, and other mammals.
*   **Vectors:** N/A
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Nipah virus is classified as a Biosafety Level 4 (BSL-4) pathogen requiring stringent safety and biocontainment precautions. Control relies on strict hospital infection control practices, contact tracing, isolation of cases, and the use of personal protective equipment (especially when patients are ventilated, due to nosocomial transmission risks). One-health approaches include animal surveillance, identifying "barking pigs" with respiratory symptoms, culling infected herds, and educating the public to boil raw date palm juice and wash/peel fruits.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Histopathology & Inclusion Bodies:** Post-mortem immunohistochemistry reveals endothelial damage, vasculitis, widespread microinfarcts, and perivascular inflammation in the brain. Nuclear inclusions similar to those seen in [[Measles virus|measles]] virus infections can be observed in the brain parenchyma.
*   **Molecular Testing:** Nucleic acid amplification testing (RT-PCR and sequencing) from throat swabs, nasal swabs, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), urine, and blood collected in the early stages of disease is the recommended and most sensitive method for active diagnosis.
*   **Serology & Seroconversion Windows:** A NiV-specific IgM ELISA or an IgG/IgM/antigen ELISA can be used as an alternative serological approach when PCR is unavailable. CSF analysis usually reveals pleocytosis with a lymphocyte predominance, alongside mild thrombocytopenia and elevated liver function tests in the blood.
*   **Cell Culture & Assays:** Virus isolation and virus neutralization assays can be used for definitive confirmation but must be strictly restricted to BSL-4 maximum containment laboratories.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **Antiviral Targets & First-Line Agents:** There are no proven antiviral therapies specifically approved for Nipah virus. [[Ribavirin]] has been used in some clinical cases, but its efficacy remains unproven.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Mutations:** N/A
*   **Treatment Nuances & Timing Constraints:** Treatment is strictly supportive. Extreme caution must be used to prevent nosocomial spread, particularly when managing airway secretions or mechanically ventilating patients.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** There is no available human vaccine for Nipah virus.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Zoonotic paramyxovirus. Henipavirus. Flying foxes / fruit bats (*Pteropus*). Raw date palm sap. Barking pigs. Malaysia 1998. Bangladesh outbreaks. Relapsing SSPE-like encephalitis. Biosafety Level 4.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Although it is a paramyxovirus, Nipah virus entirely lacks both the hemagglutinin and neuraminidase activities typically associated with this family (it uses a simple G glycoprotein). Additionally, unlike most RNA viruses which replicate strictly in the cytoplasm without leaving nuclear traces, Nipah virus generates distinct nuclear inclusions in the brain similar to the [[Measles virus|measles]] virus. Furthermore, it causes a unique late-onset encephalitis relapse months to years after the initial exposure.
