### BK Virus

#### Molecular Virology & Structure
*   **Genomic Architecture:** Contains a double-stranded, circular DNA genome of approximately 5 kbp in size.
*   **Capsid & Nucleocapsid:** Features a small, naked icosadeltahedral (icosahedral) capsid composed of 72 capsomeres.
*   **Envelope Properties:** Nonenveloped (naked capsid). It is resistant to ether.
*   **Virion Size & Physical Morphology:** Small virion measuring approximately 45 nm in diameter.
*   **Structural & Non-Structural Proteins:** Encodes the Large T antigen.

#### Viral Replication Cycle
*   **Attachment, Host Receptors & Tropism:** Exhibits tropism for the tonsils and lymphocytes initially, and subsequently targets the kidneys and urogenital tract.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Direct Cytopathic Effects:** N/A
*   **Latent Infection & Reactivation Triggers:** The virus is ubiquitous, and primary infections are typically asymptomatic. It establishes a persistent and latent infection in organs such as the kidneys. Reactivation occurs in immunosuppressed individuals, leading to kidney damage and renal disease.
*   **Oncogenesis Mechanisms:** The viral Large T antigen inactivates the host cellular tumor suppressor proteins p53 and RB (retinoblastoma protein) to allow unchecked cell growth. It also binds to other growth-promoting proteins.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** N/A
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** [[T Cell|T cells]] play a critical role in controlling the infection. Immunocompromised people (e.g., renal transplant patients receiving immunosuppressive therapy) are at severe risk for viral reactivation, which can cause significant kidney damage.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Initial infection is typically completely asymptomatic.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Causes renal disease and urogenital tract infections in immunosuppressed patients.

*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Kidney damage and severe renal disease, specifically posing a major threat to renal transplant recipients.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Humans.
*   **Transmission Route:** Transmitted by inhalation or direct contact with contaminated water or saliva. Following initial infection of the tonsils and lymphocytes, the virus spreads by viremia to other body sites (e.g., the kidneys).
*   **Vectors:** None.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** The virus is ubiquitous and found worldwide with no seasonal incidence.
