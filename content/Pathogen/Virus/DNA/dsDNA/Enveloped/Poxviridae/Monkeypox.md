### Monkeypox

#### Molecular Virology & Structure
*   **Genomic Architecture:** Contains a large, linear, double-stranded DNA genome with fused ends.
*   **Capsid & Nucleocapsid:** Complex, oval- to brick-shaped morphology with an internal structure.
*   **Envelope Properties:** Enveloped.
*   **Virion Size & Physical Morphology:** Very large and complex virion.
*   **Structural & Non-Structural Proteins:** Encodes its own DNA-dependent RNA polymerase and DNA-dependent DNA polymerase. It also encodes and carries all proteins necessary for mRNA synthesis, as well as proteins for functions such as DNA synthesis, nucleotide scavenging, and immune escape mechanisms.

#### Viral Replication Cycle
*   **Attachment, Host Receptors & Tropism:** N/A
*   **Entry Mechanism:** N/A
*   **Uncoating & Intracellular Trafficking:** N/A
*   **Transcription & Translation Strategy:** Replicates entirely in the cytoplasm.
*   **Assembly, Maturation & Egress:** Assembled in inclusion bodies (Guarnieri bodies or factories) in the cytoplasm, where the virus acquires its outer membranes. It is a lytic virus.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Direct Cytopathic Effects:** Lytic virus that causes vesicular and pustular lesions.
*   **Latent Infection & Reactivation Triggers:** N/A
*   **Oncogenesis Mechanisms:** N/A
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** N/A
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Cell-mediated immunity is essential for control of the infection.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Monkeypox.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Lesions may be restricted to the site of infection or the infection may become systemic/generalized and progress in a manner similar to smallpox.
*   **Incubation Period:** 6 to 14 days (with a range of 5 to 21 days).
*   **Classic Prodrome:** Abrupt onset of a prodromal illness lasting 2 to 5 days characterized by fever, lethargy, severe headache, myalgias, back pain, prostration, and marked lymphadenopathy (particularly cervical, submandibular, and sublingual).
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** On the third to fifth days, an exanthem appears consisting of a single crop of discrete papules, which are more abundant on the face and extremities than on the trunk. The soles of the feet and palms are usually involved. The papules form pustules which become umbilicated and are covered with crusts. The crusts separate after about 10 days, leaving small scars. Complete resolution takes 2 to 4 weeks. The presence of marked lymphadenopathy differentiates it from smallpox.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Secondary bacterial infections, respiratory and gastrointestinal involvement, encephalitis, and ocular disease (corneal scarring). The mortality rate is higher in children (1% to 10%).

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Rodents and monkeys. (Gambian giant rats and squirrels have also been implicated).
*   **Transmission Route:** Zoonotic transmission via direct contact with the blood, body fluids, or cutaneous lesions of infected animals. Secondary human-to-human transmission occurs through close contact with respiratory secretions (droplets/aerosols in close proximity), direct contact with skin lesions (including sexual contact, wrestling, self-inoculation), or contact with contaminated fomites (e.g., clothing, towels, bedding).
*   **Vectors:** None.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Originally endemic to rainforests in Central and West Africa. A global outbreak occurred in 2022-2023 involving Clade IIb monkeypox, resulting in approximately 90,000 cases across 114 countries. Most reported cases in this outbreak involved men who have sex with men (many co-infected with [[HIV]]). A prior 2003 US outbreak was linked to contact with pet prairie dogs that had been exposed to imported Gambian giant rats. Quarantine is used as a control measure.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Histopathology & Inclusion Bodies:** N/A
*   **Molecular Testing:** Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) genome analysis or restriction enzyme cleavage of viral DNA from vesicle/lesion fluid, blood, or throat swabs.
*   **Serology & Seroconversion Windows:** N/A
*   **Cell Culture & Assays:** Viral isolation is possible. Electron microscopy of vesicle fluid reveals characteristic orthopoxvirus particles. Analysis of polypeptides in infected cells can demonstrate distinct characteristics separating it from other orthopoxviruses.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **Antiviral Targets & First-Line Agents:** [[Cidofovir]] has in vitro activity against the virus and may be used for severe disease, but no antiviral is currently approved for routine treatment.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Mutations:** N/A
*   **Treatment Nuances & Timing Constraints:** Treatment is primarily supportive.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** The live-attenuated vaccinia virus vaccine (smallpox vaccine) is highly cross-reactive and provides approximately 85% cross-protection against monkeypox.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Orthopoxvirus, umbilicated pustules on palms and soles, marked lymphadenopathy, zoonosis, prairie dogs, Gambian giant rats, Clade IIb.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike the vast majority of DNA viruses that must replicate in the host cell nucleus, poxviruses (including monkeypox) are unique exceptions that replicate entirely within the host cell cytoplasm. Additionally, the prominent presence of marked lymphadenopathy is a key clinical feature that distinguishes monkeypox from smallpox.
