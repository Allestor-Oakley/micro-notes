### Viridans streptococci

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Gram-positive cell envelope containing peptidoglycan. They generally do not react with the commonly used antibody preparations for the Lancefield group-specific carbohydrate substances (Groups A, B, C, F, and G), with the exception of the *S. anginosus* group, which can possess Lancefield antigens A, C, F, or G.
*   **Appendages:** N/A
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** N/A
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Nutritionally fastidious organisms requiring complex media supplemented with blood products or tissue fluids. Growth is aided by an incubation atmosphere containing 5% to 10% CO2. They are facultative anaerobes, though some may grow only under anaerobic or microaerophilic conditions.
*   **Primary Agar:** Blood agar (e.g., 5% sheep blood agar), producing α-hemolysis (green pigment from incomplete lysis of erythrocytes) or γ-hemolysis (nonhemolytic).
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** N/A
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Catalase-negative. Unlike *S. pneumoniae*, viridans streptococci are not inhibited by optochin (ethylhydrocupreine dihydrochloride) and their colonies are not soluble in bile (deoxycholate). They are Voges-Proskauer (VP) positive, except for the *S. mitis* group. The *S. bovis* group grows in the presence of bile and hydrolyzes esculin but does not grow in 6.5% NaCl. Carbohydrate fermentation patterns and biochemical batteries are historically used for speciation. The *S. anginosus* group can be detected by a characteristic butterscotch or caramel odor. Certain species (*S. mutans*) synthesize large polysaccharides (dextrans or levans) from sucrose.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Found as normal microbiota, they enter the bloodstream via trauma to mucosal surfaces. Following events like dental extractions (which cause bacteremia in at least 30% of patients) or surgical procedures on the respiratory, GI, or urinary tracts, the organisms accidentally reach the blood. They adhere to thrombotic lesions that develop on injured endothelium and settle on normal or previously deformed heart valves to establish vegetations. Adherence to tooth enamel (*S. mutans*) via the synthesis of large polysaccharides (dextrans/levans) from sucrose initiates dental caries.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Extracellular.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** N/A
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Neutropenic patients are at increased risk for severe septicemia caused by the *S. mitis* group. Patients with congenital heart deformities, rheumatic lesions, or atherosclerotic lesions are highly predisposed to endocarditis.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:**
    *   **Subacute Bacterial Endocarditis:** Most frequent cause; slowly progressive infection of abnormal heart valves.
    *   **Acute Endocarditis:** Can occur on normal or previously deformed valves, leading to rapid destruction.
    *   **Dental Caries:** Mediated by the *S. mutans* group.
    *   **Deep Tissue Abscesses:** Brain, liver, lung, and peritoneal cavity abscesses are frequently associated with the *S. anginosus* group.
    *   **Bacteremia and Septicemia:** Associated with the *S. mitis* group in neutropenic patients, and the *S. bovis* group (specifically *S. gallolyticus* subsp. *gallolyticus*), which is highly correlated with gastrointestinal malignancies/colon cancer.
    *   **Meningitis:** Caused by the *S. mitis* group, *S. gallolyticus* subsp. *pasteurianus*, and *S. suis*.
*   **Incubation Period:** N/A
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** In subacute endocarditis, the clinical picture is gradual, presenting with fever, anemia, weakness, a heart murmur, embolic phenomena, an enlarged spleen, and renal lesions, strongly suggested by repeatedly positive blood cultures.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Without antibiotic treatment, subacute endocarditis is invariably fatal. Vegetations consist of fibrin, platelets, blood cells, and bacteria adherent to the valve leaflets, which can result in fatal cardiac failure, valvular regurgitation, or embolic phenomena.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Permanent, normal resident microbiota of the human upper respiratory tract (oropharynx), gastrointestinal tract, and genitourinary tract. Within 4–12 hours after birth, viridans streptococci become established as the most prominent members of the resident flora and remain so for life (originating from the respiratory tracts of the mother and attendants).
*   **Transmission Route:** Endogenous spread; dissemination via the bloodstream following mucosal trauma (e.g., tooth brushing, dental extractions, or surgical procedures).
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** Ubiquitous; worldwide distribution.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** N/A

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Gram-stained smears of pus or tissue reveal Gram-positive spherical/ovoid cocci in single cells, pairs, or chains. As cultures age, they rapidly lose Gram positivity and may appear Gram-negative. Smears from throat swabs are not diagnostic because these organisms are normal flora.
*   **Molecular Testing:** Because biochemical reactions are labor-intensive and frequently unreliable, laboratories use molecular capabilities like gene sequencing or matrix-assisted laser desorption ionization-time of flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS) to accurately speciate viridans streptococci.
*   **Serology & Immunology:** Most lack Lancefield group-specific antigens, making serologic grouping methods ineffective.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** Penicillin G, often combined with an aminoglycoside (e.g., gentamicin) to enhance the rate of bactericidal action. Treatment duration for endocarditis is typically 2 weeks.
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** Broad-spectrum cephalosporins, vancomycin, or telavancin. Vancomycin is the drug of choice for penicillin-resistant strains.
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Viridans streptococci vary in their susceptibility to antimicrobial agents. Moderately resistant (penicillin MIC 0.2–2 µg/mL) and highly resistant (MIC >2 µg/mL) strains have become increasingly common, particularly within the *S. mitis* group.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** In bacterial endocarditis, antibiotic susceptibility testing is essential to determine optimal therapy due to variable resistance. Surgery and prosthetic valve replacement may be necessary if acute heart failure or unresponsive infection occurs.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** Prophylactic antimicrobial agents are administered prior to dental extractions or surgical procedures involving the respiratory, gastrointestinal, and urinary tracts for patients with known heart valve deformities, prosthetic valves, or prosthetic joints to prevent endocarditis.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Viridans" is derived from the Latin word *viridis* (green), reflecting the green pigment produced by α-hemolysis on blood agar. Butterscotch/caramel odor (*S. anginosus* group). Strongly associated with colon cancer (*S. bovis* group).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** While generally α-hemolytic or non-hemolytic, members of the *S. anginosus* group can be β-hemolytic and may possess Lancefield group A, C, F, or G antigens. While *S. pneumoniae* is technically classified phylogenetically under the *S. mitis* group of viridans streptococci, it is clinically identified and treated as a distinct pathogen due to its unique virulence factors (capsule) and bile solubility.
