### Salmonella non-typhoidal

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Possesses a typical Gram-negative cell wall. The lipopolysaccharide (LPS) consists of an outer somatic O polysaccharide, a core polysaccharide (common antigen), and lipid A (endotoxin).
*   **Appendages:** Most isolates are highly motile with peritrichous flagella (H antigens).
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** N/A
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Facultative anaerobe. Capable of surviving freezing in water for long periods.
*   **Primary Agar:** Grows readily on simple agar media and routine bacteriologic media.
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** Isolation from stool specimens requires selective media such as MacConkey agar, Hektoen enteric agar (where it appears lactose-negative and H2S-positive), or Bismuth sulfite agar. Media containing chemicals like brilliant green, sodium tetrathionate, or sodium deoxycholate selectively inhibit other enteric bacteria to allow recovery.
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Fermentative and oxidase-negative. Forms acid and sometimes gas from the fermentation of glucose and mannose, but almost never ferments lactose or sucrose. Utilizes citrate as a sole carbon source and lysine as a nitrogen source. Usually produces hydrogen sulfide (H2S). Highly tolerant of acidic environments, including the interior of phagocytic vesicles.

#### Virulence Factors & Genetics
*   **Secretion Systems:** Possesses a Type III secretion system (T3SS) encoded by *Salmonella* pathogenicity islands (SPI-1 and SPI-2), which injects effector proteins directly into host cells.
*   **Exotoxins:**
    *   **Toxin Name:** Enterotoxin and cytotoxin.
    *   **Mechanism of Action:** The enterotoxin activates adenylate cyclase (sharing physicochemical characteristics with cholera toxin) and induces secretory mechanisms that cause water and electrolyte transport defects in the gut. The cytotoxin contributes to localized tissue damage.
*   **Endotoxins & Superantigens:** The lipid A component of the LPS functions as a potent endotoxin.
*   **Enzymes:** N/A
*   **Genetic Elements:** *Salmonella* pathogenicity islands (SPI-1 and SPI-2) encode the T3SS and crucial effectors for attachment, host cell invasion, and intracellular survival. Multi-locus sequence typing (MLST) and whole-genome sequencing (WGS) reveal significant genetic adaptations, such as the emergence of the *S. Typhimurium* ST313 pathovariant, which is highly adapted to cause systemic disease in Africa.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Infection is acquired via the fecal-oral route through ingestion of contaminated food or water. The bacteria invade the intestinal mucosa by attaching to and passing through M cells located in Peyer patches, facilitated by SPI-1 effectors. This induces an intense mucosal inflammatory response with polymorphonuclear neutrophil infiltration, which helps the bacteria out-compete the gut microbiome and stimulates diarrhea to enable onward transmission.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Facultative intracellular pathogen. After crossing the intestinal epithelium, the organisms are engulfed by [[Macrophage|macrophages]]. They are able to survive and multiply within these phagocytic cells and can disseminate systemically to the liver, [[spleen]], and [[bone marrow]].
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** The SPI-2 T3SS allows the bacteria to evade the phagocyte NADPH oxidase, ensuring survival and replication within the [[macrophage]].
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** While NTS usually causes localized enterocolitis in immunocompetent hosts, it is a leading cause of severe invasive bacterial disease in immunocompromised individuals. It has emerged as a massive clinical burden in adults with [[HIV]], and in children with concurrent [[Plasmodium sp.]], malnutrition, and [[HIV]] in sub-Saharan Africa. Reduced gastric acid levels also significantly increase the risk of establishing infection.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Acute enterocolitis/gastroenteritis. Symptoms include fever, nausea, vomiting, severe lower abdominal cramps, and watery diarrhea that may become bloody.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Invasive non-typhoidal *Salmonella* (iNTS) disease. This manifests as life-threatening bacteremia (septicemia), meningitis, and metastatic extraintestinal suppurative infections. *Salmonella* Choleraesuis is particularly noted for causing bacteremia.
*   **Incubation Period:** Usually between 12 and 48 hours, though longer incubation periods are reported.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** N/A
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Systemic dissemination leading to overwhelming sepsis and death, notably recrudescent in patients with [[HIV]]/AIDS or other cellular immune defects.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Extremely broad host range, including humans and a wide variety of vertebrate animals (poultry, cattle, pigs, rodents, dogs, cats, reptiles, amphibians, and birds).
*   **Transmission Route:** Zoonotic transmission via the fecal-oral route. Heavily associated with the consumption of contaminated animal products (undercooked poultry, eggs, dairy products, meat) or contaminated fresh fruit, vegetables, and water. Human-to-human transmission can occur from infected individuals or convalescent carriers, especially among young children in nurseries or pediatric wards.
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** Ubiquitous worldwide. Severe invasive non-typhoidal *Salmonella* (iNTS) disease is highly endemic and epidemic in sub-Saharan Africa.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Disease control relies on the proper preparation and thorough cooking of poultry, meats, and eggs, the pasteurization of dairy, and the avoidance of cross-contamination in food preparation areas. Carriers should be excluded from food handling.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Gram stain of positive cultures reveals Gram-negative rods with characteristic intense bipolar staining (staining predominantly at the ends of the bacteria).
*   **Molecular Testing:** Enteric multiplex nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs / PCR) directly from stool specimens are the gold standard diagnostic tool. MLST and WGS are critical for subtyping and epidemiological tracking.
*   **Serology & Immunology:** Strains are typed by the Kaufmann-White scheme using specific antisera against somatic (O) and flagellar (H) antigens.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** For uncomplicated enterocolitis, treatment is symptomatic with fluid and electrolyte replacement; antibiotics are generally withheld to avoid prolonging fecal shedding. For invasive disease or in high-risk immunocompromised patients, third-generation cephalosporins (e.g., [[ceftriaxone]], [[cefotaxime]]) or fluoroquinolones (e.g., [[ciprofloxacin]]) are required.
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** [[Azithromycin]], [[Cotrimoxazole|trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole]], [[ampicillin]], or [[chloramphenicol]].
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Antimicrobial resistance is a rapidly emerging global problem, including high rates of resistance to fluoroquinolones and cephalosporins. Epidemic multiple drug-resistant clones (like *S. Typhimurium* ST313) drive massive mortality in Africa.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Antibiotic therapy in simple diarrheal presentations is contraindicated as it provides no clinical benefit and may prolong the carrier state.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** There is currently no effective vaccine available for non-typhoidal *Salmonella* (unlike the vaccines available for *Salmonella* Typhi), making vaccine development a major global health priority.
#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** iNTS (invasive non-typhoidal *Salmonella*), bipolar staining, poultry and eggs, reptiles, SPI-1 and SPI-2, enterocolitis.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike typhoidal *Salmonella* serovars (Typhi and Paratyphi), which are strictly host-restricted to humans and cause an invasive systemic fever, non-typhoidal *Salmonella* serovars have a vast host range and typically cause localized diarrheal disease—except in patients with immune defects (like [[HIV]] or [[Plasmodium sp.]]), where they exploit an ecological niche to cause severe invasive septicemia.
