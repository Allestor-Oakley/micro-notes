### Escherichia coli

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Possesses a typical Gram-negative cell wall. The lipopolysaccharide (LPS) consists of an outer somatic O polysaccharide, a core polysaccharide (common antigen), and lipid A.
*   **Appendages:** Motile (possessing H or flagellar antigens) or nonmotile. Possesses a diverse array of pili and fimbriae, including Type 1 fimbriae, P pili, bundle-forming pili (BFP), aggregative adherence fimbriae (AAF/I, AAF/II, AAF/III), and Dr fimbriae.
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** Certain strains possess a polysaccharide capsule (K antigen), most notably the K1 capsular antigen which is heavily associated with neonatal meningitis.
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Facultative anaerobe. Grows rapidly on standard laboratory culture media.
*   **Primary Agar:** Readily grows on routine bacteriologic media, including blood agar and MacConkey agar.
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** Sorbitol-MacConkey agar is selectively used to screen for O157:H7 STEC strains (which uniquely do not ferment sorbitol rapidly). Eosin methylene blue (EMB) agar is commonly used, producing a characteristic iridescent green metallic "sheen."
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Oxidase-negative, catalase-positive, and nitrate-positive. It is a rapid lactose fermenter. It yields a positive spot indole test. More than 90% of isolates are positive for beta-glucuronidase using the substrate 4-methylumbelliferyl-beta-glucuronide (MUG).

#### Virulence Factors & Genetics
*   **Secretion Systems:** Possesses a Type III secretion system (especially in EPEC and STEC strains) used to translocate effector proteins (e.g., Tir) directly into host epithelial cells.
*   **Exotoxins:**
    *   **Toxin Name:** Heat-labile toxin (LT-1), Heat-stable toxin (STa), Shiga toxins (Stx1, Stx2), Enteroaggregative heat-stable toxin, and Hemolysin (HlyA).
    *   **Mechanism of Action:**
        *   **LT-1 and STa (ETEC):** LT-1 (an A-B toxin similar to cholera toxin) activates adenylate cyclase, increasing intracellular cAMP. STa activates intestinal guanylyl cyclase, increasing cGMP. Both lead to hypersecretion of fluids and electrolytes, causing watery diarrhea.
        *   **Shiga toxins (STEC):** Cytotoxic A-B toxins that bind to globotriaosylceramide (Gb3) receptors on intestinal villi and renal endothelial cells. The A subunit cleaves 28S rRNA, disrupting protein synthesis and causing cell death (leading to hemorrhagic colitis and hemolytic uremic syndrome).
        *   **Hemolysin (HlyA):** Lyses erythrocytes and other cell types, leading to cytokine release and inflammation (crucial for uropathogenic strains).
*   **Endotoxins & Superantigens:** The lipid A component of the LPS acts as a potent endotoxin.
*   **Enzymes:** N/A
*   **Genetic Elements:** Highly genetically adaptable. Virulence genes are frequently clustered on large chromosomal pathogenicity islands (e.g., the locus of enterocyte effacement [LEE] in EPEC/STEC; PAI I536, II536, and IJ96 in uropathogenic strains). Other virulence factors (toxins, colonization factors, invasion genes) are frequently carried on transmissible plasmids or temperate bacteriophages.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:**
    *   **EPEC/STEC:** Attach to small or large bowel epithelium and utilize the LEE pathogenicity island to inject the translocated intimin receptor (Tir) into the host membrane. The bacterial surface adhesin intimin then binds to Tir, causing actin polymerization, loss of microvilli, and the formation of characteristic cup-like pedestals (attachment/effacement [A/E] histopathology).
    *   **EAEC:** Mediate autoagglutination in a "stacked brick" arrangement over the small intestine epithelium via aggregative adherence fimbriae, causing shortening of microvilli and decreased fluid absorption.
    *   **EIEC:** Plasmid-mediated invasion (pInv genes) and destruction of the colonic epithelium. Bacteria lyse the phagocytic vacuole, replicate in the cytoplasm, and move to adjacent cells via actin tails.
    *   **Uropathogens:** Utilize P pili, AAF, and Dr fimbriae to adhere tightly to the uroepithelium, preventing elimination by voided urine.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Primarily extracellular, with the distinct exception of EIEC which invades and replicates intracellularly within the colonic epithelium.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** The K1 polysaccharide capsule provides antiphagocytic protection and resistance to serum killing, facilitating survival in the bloodstream and penetration into the central nervous system.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Endogenous extraintestinal infections frequently occur when host defenses are compromised (e.g., bowel perforation, urinary obstruction, trauma, or immunosuppression).

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:**
    *   **Gastroenteritis:** Ranges from watery diarrhea (ETEC, EAEC, EPEC) to dysentery (EIEC) and severe hemorrhagic colitis (STEC). ETEC is the main agent of traveler's diarrhea.
    *   **Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs):** The most common cause of uncomplicated cystitis and pyelonephritis (accounting for ~80-90% of community-acquired cases).
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Neonatal meningitis (often K1 strains), sepsis/bacteremia (most common Gram-negative rod isolated from sepsis), intraabdominal infections (e.g., peritonitis following ruptured appendix), and nosocomial pneumonia.
*   **Incubation Period:** For gastroenteritis, typically 1 to 4 days depending on the specific pathotype (e.g., ETEC develops after 1-2 days; STEC develops after 3-4 days).
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** In STEC infections, initial watery diarrhea progresses to grossly bloody diarrhea with severe abdominal cramps but little or no fever.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS) is a severe complication of STEC (particularly O157:H7) occurring in 5-10% of infected children, characterized by acute renal failure, thrombocytopenia, and microangiopathic hemolytic anemia.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** The normal microbial flora of the gastrointestinal tract in humans and many animals. STEC (especially O157:H7) has a prominent reservoir in cattle.
*   **Transmission Route:**
    *   **Extraintestinal infections:** Usually caused by endogenous spread (the patient's own flora migrating to sterile body sites, such as ascending the urethra).
    *   **Gastroenteritis:** Acquired exogenously via the fecal-oral route. ETEC, EPEC, EAEC, and EIEC are typically acquired from fecally contaminated food or water. STEC is heavily associated with consumption of undercooked ground beef, unpasteurized milk/juice, or raw vegetables (e.g., spinach) contaminated by cattle feces. Person-to-person transmission can occur with highly infectious strains like STEC and EIEC.
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** Worldwide. ETEC, EPEC, and EIEC cause massive morbidity and mortality in infants in developing countries. STEC is a major cause of foodborne outbreaks in developed nations (e.g., the 2006 multi-state spinach outbreak in the US).
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Control relies on improved sanitation, clean water supplies, proper cooking of beef products, and hand hygiene.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Gram stain of positive blood cultures, urine, or CSF reveals short, Gram-negative rods (frequently demonstrating bipolar staining).
*   **Molecular Testing:** Enteric multiplex nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs/PCR) from stool specimens have become the gold standard diagnostic tool to rapidly differentiate the specific diarrheagenic pathotypes (detecting genes for LT, ST, Shiga toxins, intimin, etc.).
*   **Serology & Immunology:** Pathotypes are historically subtyped using somatic (O) and flagellar (H) antigens (e.g., O157:H7). Commercial enzyme immunoassays are available for detecting Shiga-like toxins directly in clinical specimens. MALDI-TOF MS is used for rapid species identification.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** Extraintestinal infections require antibiotic therapy guided by in vitro susceptibility testing (often [[Cotrimoxazole|trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole]], fluoroquinolones, or broad-spectrum cephalosporins). Uncomplicated gastroenteritis is generally treated symptomatically with fluid and electrolyte replacement.
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** Carbapenems are used for severe nosocomial infections or isolates producing ESBLs.
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Antimicrobial resistance is a massive global challenge. The emergence of the pandemic clone *E. coli* O25b/ST131 is characterized by plasmid-mediated resistance to fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, and the elaboration of extended-spectrum beta-lactamases (ESBLs, specifically CTX-M15) which destroy penicillins and cephalosporins. Strains acquiring carbapenemase genes are also increasingly reported.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Antibiotics are strictly contraindicated in the treatment of STEC (e.g., O157:H7) because they can induce phage lytic cycles, increase the expression and release of Shiga toxins, and significantly elevate the risk of developing Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** No widely licensed vaccine is currently available, although modified recombinant ETEC vaccines are undergoing clinical trials.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Traveler's diarrhea", "Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS)", "green metallic sheen on EMB agar", "stacked bricks" autoagglutination (EAEC), attachment/effacement (A/E) lesions, "O157:H7", Shiga-like toxin, K1 capsule, sorbitol-negative.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** While *E. coli* is considered a normal, beneficial commensal of the human gut, the acquisition of highly specialized mobile genetic elements (plasmids, phages, pathogenicity islands) easily transforms it into one of the most deadly diarrheagenic and systemic pathogens in existence. Furthermore, *[[Shigella]]* species are technically just highly specialized biogroups within the species *E. coli* from a genetic standpoint, though historical nomenclature keeps them separate.
