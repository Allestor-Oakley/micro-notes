#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Capable of causing severe primary infections of the head and neck and frequently acts as an opportunistic complication following other infectious processes, such as infectious mononucleosis (caused by the [[EBV|Epstein-Barr virus]]).

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Lemierre's disease (a complicated, severe head and neck infection characterized by acute jugular vein septic thrombophlebitis that rapidly progresses to sepsis with metastatic abscesses). Also causes severe pharyngitis.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Can cause meningitis, brain abscesses, and pelvic abscesses (often as part of a polymicrobial anaerobic infection). It is historically associated with the term "necrobacillosis".
*   **Incubation Period:** Can present abruptly, often developing around 2 weeks after an initial viral illness like infectious mononucleosis.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** A worsening, severe sore throat accompanied by an inability to swallow, severe neck and chest pain, and respiratory distress, particularly in an older child or young adult with a recent history of infectious mononucleosis.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Progression to Lemierre's disease leads to overwhelming sepsis with destructive metastatic abscesses seeding the lungs, mediastinum, pleural space, and liver.
