### Pseudomonas aeruginosa

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Possesses a typical Gram-negative cell wall containing lipopolysaccharide (LPS). The outer membrane contains porin proteins.
*   **Appendages:** Motile via a single polar flagellum. Also possesses pili (fimbriae) that extend from the cell surface to promote attachment to host epithelial cells.
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** Produces an exopolysaccharide capsule composed of alginate (a polymer of d-mannuronic acid and l-glucuronic acid). Alginate overproduction is responsible for the highly mucoid colonies frequently seen in cultures from patients with cystic fibrosis (CF), providing the matrix for the organisms to live within a protective biofilm.
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Obligate aerobe, utilizing carbohydrates through aerobic respiration with oxygen as the terminal electron acceptor. However, it can grow anaerobically using nitrate or arginine as an alternate electron acceptor. It has minimal nutritional requirements and tolerates a wide range of temperatures (4°C to 42°C). Growth at 42°C helps differentiate it from other fluorescent Pseudomonas species.
*   **Primary Agar:** Grows readily and rapidly on common laboratory isolation media, including blood agar and MacConkey agar.
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** Because it grows on most routine media, highly specialized selective agar is typically not required for primary isolation, though it thrives on routine Gram-negative differential media.
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Non-fermenter of carbohydrates (including lactose), but oxidizes glucose. Oxidase-positive (detected via a rapid 5-minute test, differentiating it from Enterobacterales) and catalase-positive. Forms flat, smooth, round colonies with a spreading border. Some strains exhibit beta-hemolysis on blood agar. The organism produces a characteristic sweet, grape-like, or corn taco-like odor and secretes diffusible water-soluble pigments: pyocyanin (blue), pyoverdine (yellow-green, fluorescing under UV light), and pyorubin (reddish-brown).

#### Virulence Factors & Genetics
*   **Secretion Systems:** Possesses multiple secretion systems: Type I (Sec-independent, secretes alkaline protease), Type II (Sec-dependent, secretes elastase, exotoxin A, and phospholipase C), Type III (Sec-independent, contact-dependent, injects cytotoxins directly into host cells), and Type VI (Sec-independent, produces pore-forming toxin Hcp1).
*   **Exotoxins:**
    *   **Toxin Name:** Exotoxin A (ETA), Exoenzyme S, Exoenzyme T, Exoenzyme U, Exoenzyme Y, and Pyocyanin.
    *   **Mechanism of Action:** Exotoxin A is an A-B toxin that disrupts protein synthesis by blocking peptide chain elongation (via elongation factor 2) in eukaryotic cells—similar in action, though structurally different, to diphtheria toxin—resulting in tissue necrosis. Exoenzymes S and T (delivered via the Type III secretion system) are bifunctional enzymes with GTPase and ADP-ribosyl transferase activity that cause cytotoxicity via actin rearrangement. Exoenzyme U is a phospholipase, and Exoenzyme Y is an adenylyl cyclase. Pyocyanin catalyzes the production of toxic superoxide and hydrogen peroxide and stimulates the release of interleukin-8 (IL-8), attracting neutrophils.
*   **Endotoxins & Superantigens:** Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) possesses typical endotoxin activity, causing fever, shock, oliguria, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), and adult respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS).
*   **Enzymes:** Produces two synergistic elastases: LasA (a serine protease) and LasB (a zinc metalloprotease) that degrade elastin, causing parenchymal damage and hemorrhagic lesions while inhibiting neutrophil chemotaxis. Also secretes alkaline protease (interferes with host immune responses) and phospholipase C (a heat-labile hemolysin that breaks down lipids and lecithin).
*   **Genetic Elements:** Features one of the largest bacterial genomes (5567 genes, including 468 regulatory genes), making the organism highly adaptable to environmental extremes and antimicrobial pressures. Virulence expression is heavily regulated by complex cell-density signaling (quorum sensing).

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Typically established via exogenous acquisition from moist environmental reservoirs. The organism takes advantage of disrupted mucous membranes or skin (e.g., burn wounds, trauma) or indwelling medical devices (e.g., urinary catheters, intravenous lines, mechanical ventilators). Attachment is mediated by pili, flagella, lipopolysaccharide, and the alginate capsule.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Primarily an extracellular pathogen that establishes localized colonization before invading tissues and disseminating systemically.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** The alginate exopolysaccharide capsule forms a thick biofilm matrix that protects the bacterial colony from host antibodies, phagocytosis, and antimicrobial agents (a hallmark of airway infections in CF patients). Secreted elastases and alkaline protease degrade complement components and inhibit neutrophil chemotaxis and function.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Strongly characterized as an opportunistic pathogen. Infections occur predominantly in patients with compromised host defenses, including those with neutropenia, cystic fibrosis, extensive burns, diabetes mellitus, hematologic malignancies, or those whose normal microbiome has been suppressed by broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Necrotizing bronchopneumonia (especially ventilator-associated), chronic pneumonia in cystic fibrosis, burn wound infections (often yielding blue-green pus), catheter-associated urinary tract infections, and hot tub folliculitis.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Malignant external otitis (an invasive, bone-destroying ear infection in diabetics), simple otitis externa ("swimmer's ear"), chronic otitis media, devastating eye infections (corneal ulcers following abrasions), endophthalmitis, osteomyelitis (e.g., following foot puncture through a sneaker), septic arthritis, meningitis (introduced via lumbar puncture), and endocarditis (primarily involving the tricuspid valve in intravenous drug abusers).
*   **Incubation Period:** N/A
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** Ecthyma gangrenosum (erythematous vesicles that become hemorrhagic, necrotic, and ulcerated, typically devoid of pus) occurring during bacteremia. Blue-green pus with a sweet, grape-like odor in wounds or burns.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Systemic shock, ARDS, DIC, massive soft tissue necrosis, loss of the eye in corneal infections, and high mortality rates in bacteremic neutropenic or burn patients.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Ubiquitous in nature; found in soil, decaying organic matter, vegetation, and water. A highly prominent reservoir in hospitals within moist environments (e.g., sinks, toilets, respiratory therapy equipment, dialysis machines, cut flowers, floor mops).
*   **Transmission Route:** Direct contact with contaminated environmental sources, fomites, or medical equipment. Introduction occurs via breached skin/mucosa or aerosolization from contaminated respiratory devices. Endogenous infection can occasionally occur after transient colonization of the gastrointestinal or respiratory tracts.
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** Found worldwide. Outbreaks are often localized to specific contaminated point sources, such as hot tubs, swimming pools, or specific intensive care units.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** It is a leading cause of nosocomial infections (e.g., the 5th most common pathogen in US hospital-acquired infections). Control relies on rigorous infection control, sterilization of respiratory/medical equipment, and antibiotic stewardship. Complete elimination from hospital environments is considered practically impossible due to its hardiness and simple nutritional requirements.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Gram stain of clinical specimens reveals thin Gram-negative rods, often arranged in pairs. In tissue biopsies of ecthyma gangrenosum lesions, abundant organisms and severe vascular destruction are seen with a notable absence of neutrophils.
*   **Molecular Testing:** Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and nucleic acid amplification tests are used for rapid detection and for identifying specific multidrug resistance genes.
*   **Serology & Immunology:** Matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight (MALDI-TOF) mass spectrometry is highly reliable for species identification. Serology is rarely used for acute diagnosis, though CF patients frequently develop antibodies against LasA and LasB, leading to immune complex deposition.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** Due to intrinsic and acquired resistance, combination therapy with active, synergistic antibiotics is generally required for serious systemic infections. Typical regimens include an antipseudomonal beta-lactam (e.g., [[Piperacillin]]-[[tazobactam]], [[Ceftazidime]], [[Cefepime]], [[Imipenem]], [[Meropenem]], [[Doripenem]], or [[aztreonam]]) combined with an aminoglycoside (e.g., [[Gentamicin]], [[Tobramycin]], [[Amikacin]]).
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** Fluoroquinolones (e.g., [[Ciprofloxacin]], [[Levofloxacin]]). For extensively multidrug-resistant strains, [[Polymyxin|colistin]] (polymyxin) may be the only active therapeutic option.
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Highly notorious for antimicrobial resistance. Intrinsically resistant to many agents due to multiple multidrug efflux pumps and altered outer membrane porin channels. It possesses an inducible chromosomal AmpC beta-lactamase (conferring resistance to [[Ampicillin]] and early cephalosporins) and frequently acquires extended-spectrum beta-lactamases (ESBLs) and carbapenemases (including metallo-enzymes and OXA-family enzymes).
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Monotherapy is generally ineffective for serious infections and rapidly selects for highly resistant subpopulations (heteroresistance). Antimicrobial susceptibility testing is strictly mandatory to guide therapy due to vast geographic and strain-to-strain variations in resistance profiles. It is inherently resistant to [[Cotrimoxazole|trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole]] (differentiating it from *[[Stenotrophomonas maltophilia]]*).
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** No approved vaccine is currently available. Prevention hinges on rigorous infection control, strict catheter/device care, avoiding unnecessary broad-spectrum antibiotics (which select for colonization), and maintaining appropriate chlorination of pools and hot tubs.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Blue-green pus", "pyocyanin", "pyoverdine", "sweet grape-like or corn taco odor", "ecthyma gangrenosum", "cystic fibrosis biofilm", "alginate capsule", "hot tub folliculitis", "swimmer's ear", "exotoxin A", "oxidase-positive non-fermenter", "burn wounds".
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike the Enterobacterales (which are fermentative and oxidase-negative), *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* is a non-fermenter and strictly oxidase-positive. Uniquely, it can grow at 42°C, a trait used in the laboratory to differentiate it from other fluorescent pseudomonads.
