### Burkholderia cepacia

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Possesses a typical Gram-negative cell wall.
*   **Appendages:** N/A
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** N/A
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Aerobic environmental organism. It grows more slowly than enteric Gram-negative rods, often requiring up to 3 days of incubation before colonies become visible.
*   **Primary Agar:** Grows on most routine media used for culturing Gram-negative bacteria.
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** *B. cepacia* selective agar (which contains [[Polymyxin|colistin]]) is highly recommended for selectively culturing the organism from the sputum of patients with cystic fibrosis.
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Oxidase-positive and lysine decarboxylase-positive. It produces acid from glucose. Differentiating it from other nonfermentative pseudomonads requires a complex battery of biochemical tests.

*   **Enzymes:** Produces antimicrobial degradation and modifying enzymes that contribute to its high levels of antibiotic resistance.
*   **Genetic Elements:** The specific identification and classification of the organism is highly complex; it comprises at least 18 closely related genomospecies collectively referred to as the *Burkholderia cepacia* complex.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Infection is acquired via exposure to contaminated environmental sources (e.g., water, soil) or contaminated hospital equipment (intravascular catheters, mechanical ventilation). Person-to-person spread via close contact is also a major transmission route among vulnerable patient populations.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Extracellular pathogen.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** Capable of growing embedded within a protective biofilm, which significantly enhances its resistance to host defenses and antimicrobial clearance.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Highly opportunistic pathogen. It specifically targets patients with cystic fibrosis (CF) and patients with chronic granulomatous disease (CGD).

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Pulmonary infections in CF and CGD patients. The clinical course can range from asymptomatic carriage to progressive respiratory deterioration over a period of months, or rapidly progressive deterioration with necrotizing pneumonia and bacteremia.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Urinary tract infections in catheterized patients and bacteremia/septicemia in immunocompromised patients with contaminated intravascular catheters.
*   **Incubation Period:** N/A
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** N/A
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** In patients with cystic fibrosis, infection with *B. cepacia* complex is associated with progressive, significant destruction of pulmonary tissue and high morbidity. Acquisition of the organism frequently disqualifies a CF patient from eligibility for a life-saving lung transplantation.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Widely distributed in the environment. Able to grow in water, soil, plants, animals, and decaying vegetable materials. In the hospital environment, it thrives in a variety of moist water and environmental sources.
*   **Transmission Route:** Environmental exposure, nosocomial transmission from contaminated water/medical devices, and direct person-to-person spread (particularly from one CF patient to another).
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** Ubiquitous worldwide.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Because it can be transmitted from one CF patient to another by close contact, an infected patient may be strictly isolated and not allowed to associate with other CF patients. Due to the prognostic implications for CF patients, clinical isolates should be submitted to specialized reference laboratories (e.g., those supported by the Cystic Fibrosis Foundation) for definitive phenotypic and genotypic confirmation.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Gram stain reveals Gram-negative rods.
*   **Molecular Testing:** Genotypic methods are frequently required by reference laboratories to accurately confirm the identity of organisms within the *B. cepacia* complex due to the difficulty of definitive biochemical identification.
*   **Serology & Immunology:** N/A

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** [[Cotrimoxazole|Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole]] (TMP-SMX). Other effective treatments include carbapenems (e.g., [[Meropenem]]), [[chloramphenicol]], and [[Minocycline]].
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** [[Ceftazidime]], [[Ciprofloxacin]], or [[Piperacillin]] can demonstrate good activity (especially against planktonic forms or when embedded in a biofilm).
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Known for extensive antimicrobial resistance; isolates recovered from CF patients are often multidrug-resistant or pan-resistant. Resistance is mediated by efflux pumps, antimicrobial degradation or modifying enzymes, and altered membrane functions. It is notably inherently resistant to the glycylcycline antibiotic [[Tigecycline]].
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Antimicrobial susceptibility testing must be performed on all clinical isolates, although the organism's slow growth can make routine in vitro testing difficult.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** N/A

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Cystic fibrosis", "chronic granulomatous disease", *B. cepacia* selective agar, lung transplant contraindication, slow-growing.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** *B. cepacia* derives its name from "cepacia" (meaning "like an onion") because the original strains were isolated as plant pathogens causing rot in onions. Unlike typical rapidly growing enteric Gram-negative rods, it grows quite slowly on routine media, taking up to 3 days to form visible colonies.
