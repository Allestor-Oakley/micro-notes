### Eikenella corrodens

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Possesses a typical Gram-negative cell wall.
*   **Appendages:** Nonmotile.
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** N/A
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Facultative anaerobe with fastidious growth requirements. It requires a carbon dioxide–enriched atmosphere (5% to 10% CO2) and is slow-growing.
*   **Primary Agar:** Readily grows on enriched media such as blood agar or chocolate agar, forming small colonies (0.5- to 1.0-mm) after 48 hours of incubation.
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** It grows poorly or not at all on selective culture media normally used for Gram-negative rods.
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** The organism derives its name from its characteristic ability to pit or "corrode" the surface of agar media, although this feature is only observed in fewer than half of all clinical isolates. Additionally, it characteristically produces a distinct bleach-like odor.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** The organism is a normal, commensal inhabitant of the human upper respiratory tract and oropharynx. Infection typically occurs via traumatic introduction of oral organisms into deep tissues, such as through human bite wounds or fistfight injuries. It can also spread from the oropharynx into the bloodstream, establishing infection on damaged endocardium.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Extracellular pathogen.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** N/A
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Acts as an opportunistic pathogen primarily in patients who have pre-existing diseases or trauma of the oral cavity, as well as in patients who are immunocompromised or have previously damaged heart valves.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Subacute bacterial endocarditis (a defining member of the HACEK group of fastidious organisms causing "culture-negative" endocarditis) and deep tissue infections originating from human bite wounds or fistfight injuries.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Can also cause sinusitis, meningitis, brain abscesses, pneumonia, and lung abscesses. Because infections typically originate from the oropharynx, they frequently manifest as polymicrobial infections involving mixtures of aerobic and anaerobic bacteria.
*   **Incubation Period:** N/A (Endocarditis typically presents with a gradual, subacute onset).
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** In the context of endocarditis, the disease is characterized by the gradual onset of low-grade fevers, night sweats, and chills.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** N/A

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Humans. It is a normal resident of the human oropharynx and upper respiratory tract.
*   **Transmission Route:** Endogenous spread or traumatic inoculation of oral flora into deep tissues (e.g., human bite wounds, fistfights).
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** N/A (Found universally as part of the normal human microbiota).
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** N/A

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** Highly susceptible to [[Penicillin]] (which is highly unusual for a Gram-negative bacterium), [[Ampicillin]], extended-spectrum cephalosporins, tetracyclines, and fluoroquinolones.
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** N/A
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Inherently resistant to [[Oxacillin]], first-generation cephalosporins, [[clindamycin]], [[Erythromycin]], and aminoglycosides.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** The organism's inherent resistance profile is clinically significant because it renders many antibiotics typically selected empirically for the treatment of bite-wound infections completely ineffective.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** N/A

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Human bite wound", "fistfight injury", "pits or corrodes agar", "bleach-like odor", subacute endocarditis, "HACEK group", CDC subgroup HB-1.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike the vast majority of Gram-negative bacteria, it is highly susceptible to [[Penicillin]] but resistant to clindamycin, erythromycin, and aminoglycosides. Additionally, it fails to grow on standard selective media designed for Gram-negative rods.
