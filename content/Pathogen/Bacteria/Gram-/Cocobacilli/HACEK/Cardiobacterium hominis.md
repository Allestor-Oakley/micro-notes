### Cardiobacterium hominis

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Typical Gram-negative cell wall, though the organism can appear Gram-variable when stained.
*   **Appendages:** Nonmotile.
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** N/A
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Facultatively anaerobic. Highly fastidious and slow-growing. Requires enhanced carbon dioxide (5% to 10% CO2) and humidity for growth on agar media. Growth in blood cultures typically requires 1 week or more to be detected.
*   **Primary Agar:** Blood agar or chocolate agar. After 2 or more days of incubation, the organism forms pinpoint (1-mm) colonies.
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** Does not grow on MacConkey agar or other selective media commonly used for Gram-negative rods.
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Fermentative, oxidase-positive, and catalase-negative.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** The organism is a normal resident of the human upper respiratory tract and oropharynx. Bacteria enter the bloodstream from the oropharynx, typically following oral disease or a dental procedure, and adhere to damaged heart tissue where they slowly multiply.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Extracellular pathogen.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** The organism is of relatively low virulence and grows slowly, allowing it to establish an insidious, subacute infection over a prolonged period.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Patients with preexisting heart disease or previously damaged heart valves are at significant risk for opportunistic infection leading to endocarditis.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Subacute bacterial endocarditis.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** N/A
*   **Incubation Period:** Insidious and prolonged; patients typically experience symptoms for months before seeking medical care.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** Fatigue, malaise, anorexia, low-grade fever, lower extremity edema, and a new heart murmur, frequently presenting weeks to months after a dental procedure.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Progression to heart failure, pleural effusions, and severe valvular damage requiring surgical replacement. However, severe complications are rare with proper diagnosis, and complete recovery after appropriate antibiotic therapy is common.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Humans. It is present in the upper respiratory tract of most healthy people.
*   **Transmission Route:** Endogenous spread from the oropharyngeal flora into the bloodstream.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** [[Ceftriaxone]] therapy for 2 to 6 weeks is currently recommended. The organism is also highly susceptible to [[penicillin]] and [[ampicillin]].
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** In cases with severe cardiac damage, surgical intervention (e.g., prosthetic valve replacement) combined with antibiotic therapy is required.
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Commonly resistant to [[erythromycin]].
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** [[Erythromycin]] should not be used due to widespread resistance. Diagnosing the infection requires clinical laboratories to hold blood cultures for extended incubation periods (at least 7 days) because of the organism's slow growth.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** No vaccine is available. Endocarditis in individuals with preexisting heart disease is prevented by maintaining good oral hygiene and administering antibiotic prophylaxis (e.g., long-acting [[penicillin]]) at the time of dental procedures.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "HACEK group", subacute bacterial endocarditis, slow-growing fastidious Gram-negative rod, "recent dental procedure", negative on MacConkey agar.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike most typical Gram-negative rods, it is completely unable to grow on MacConkey agar and frequently stains Gram-variable.
