### Bordetella pertussis

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Possesses a typical Gram-negative cell wall. The cell wall contains lipooligosaccharide (LOS), which may be important in causing damage to the epithelial cells of the upper respiratory tract.
*   **Appendages:** Possesses fimbriae (surface appendages) that mediate adhesion to ciliated epithelial cells. The organism is nonmotile.
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** A capsule is present.
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Strict aerobe. Fastidious and slow-growing. Recovery in vitro requires prolonged incubation at 35–37°C for 3 to 7 days in a moist environment (e.g., a sealed plastic bag). It is extremely sensitive to drying and requires rapid delivery to the laboratory.
*   **Primary Agar:** Primary isolation requires enriched media. Regan-Lowe agar (a charcoal-containing medium supplemented with horse blood, [[cephalexin]], and [[amphotericin B]]) is preferable because of its longer shelf life. Bordet-Gengou medium (potato-blood-glycerol agar) with [[Penicillin G]] can also be used.
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** Media must be supplemented with charcoal, starch, blood, or albumin because the organism is highly susceptible to toxic substances and metabolites present in common laboratory media.
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Oxidase positive and catalase positive, but nitrate, citrate, and urea negative. It is nonfermentative but can oxidize amino acids as an energy source. It does not require X and V factors on subculture.

#### Virulence Factors & Genetics
*   **Secretion Systems:** N/A
*   **Exotoxins:**
    *   **Toxin Name:** Pertussis toxin, Adenylate cyclase toxin (ACT), Dermonecrotic toxin (DNT), and Tracheal cytotoxin.
    *   **Mechanism of Action:** Pertussis toxin (a classic A-B5 structure toxin) activates adenylate cyclase by incapacitating inhibitory G-proteins, causing an increase in intracellular cyclic AMP (cAMP); this promotes lymphocytosis, sensitization to histamine, enhanced insulin secretion, and disrupts cellular signal transduction. Adenylate cyclase toxin (ACT) inhibits phagocyte function. Tracheal cytotoxin kills respiratory epithelial cells in vitro. Dermonecrotic toxin (DNT) mediates localized tissue destruction, though its exact role in pertussis is less understood.
*   **Endotoxins & Superantigens:** Lipooligosaccharide (LOS) contributes to epithelial damage.
*   **Enzymes:** Adenylate cyclase.
*   **Genetic Elements:** The virulence genes are regulated by a central locus containing two operons, bvgA and bvgS. This functions as a two-component regulatory system: bvgS responds to environmental signals, and bvgA acts as a transcriptional activator for virulence genes (including toxins and adhesins).

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Acquired via inhalation of infectious aerosols (respiratory droplets). The organism specifically attaches to ciliated epithelial cells of the respiratory tract. Adherence is mediated by pertactin, filamentous hemagglutinin (a large surface protein), and fimbriae, which are essential for tracheal colonization.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Extracellular pathogen.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** Secretes adenylate cyclase toxin (ACT), which specifically inhibits phagocyte function.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Unvaccinated individuals, particularly infants and children younger than 1 year, are at the greatest risk for infection and mortality.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Pertussis (whooping cough), acute bronchitis, and pharyngitis.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** In vaccinated populations, the disease may present as a milder, persistent cough in older children and young adults rather than classic whooping cough.
*   **Incubation Period:** 7 to 10 days.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** The clinical course is characterized by three distinct stages: the catarrhal stage (resembling the common cold), progressing to the paroxysmal stage (repetitive, severe coughing fits followed by inspiratory "whoops" and frequently vomiting), and finally the convalescent stage (diminishing paroxysms). Profound peripheral lymphocytosis is a hallmark sign.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Extreme exhaustion from coughing episodes and secondary respiratory complications.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Humans are the only known reservoir. There is no animal or environmental reservoir.
*   **Transmission Route:** Spread person-to-person via infectious aerosols (respiratory droplets). It is highly communicable.
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** Worldwide distribution. There is a high prevalence and mortality rate in unvaccinated populations (particularly in developing countries).
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Disease control relies on active vaccination programs and post-exposure contact prophylaxis.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Microscopy (Gram stain) is insensitive and nonspecific for clinical specimens. Immunofluorescence staining can be used to identify organisms from culture and occasionally in direct smears.
*   **Molecular Testing:** Nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs/PCR) performed on nasopharyngeal swabs, washes, or aspirates are the diagnostic methods of choice due to their superior sensitivity and specificity compared to culture.
*   **Serology & Immunology:** Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) testing can detect IgG or IgA antibodies against pertussis toxin, filamentous hemagglutinin, pertactin, and fimbriae, serving as a confirmatory test.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** Macrolide antibiotics (e.g., [[azithromycin]], [[clarithromycin]], or [[erythromycin]]).
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** Supportive therapy is crucial.
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** N/A
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** While antibiotic treatment effectively eradicates the organism and reduces the length of the infectious shedding stage, it often does not significantly alter the clinical course of the disease if initiated after the paroxysmal stage has begun.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** [[Azithromycin]] is recommended for contact prophylaxis. Disease is highly preventable via vaccination with an acellular vaccine (containing inactivated pertussis toxin, filamentous hemagglutinin, and pertactin). The pediatric vaccine (DTaP) is administered in a five-dose series, with booster doses (Tdap) recommended for older children, adults, and pregnant women.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Whooping cough", "inspiratory whoop", profound peripheral lymphocytosis, catarrhal/paroxysmal/convalescent stages, Regan-Lowe agar, Bordet-Gengou agar, A-B5 pertussis toxin, bvgA/bvgS regulatory system.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike many robust Gram-negative rods, it is extremely sensitive to drying and highly susceptible to toxic substances and metabolites in standard laboratory media, making it notoriously difficult to culture without adding charcoal, starch, blood, or albumin.
