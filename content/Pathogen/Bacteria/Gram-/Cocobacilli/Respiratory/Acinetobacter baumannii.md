### Acinetobacter baumannii

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Typical Gram-negative cell wall.
*   **Appendages:** Nonmotile (the genus name *Acinetobacter* is derived from *akinetos*, meaning unable to move).
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** N/A
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Strictly aerobic. While most acinetobacters grow between 20°C and 35°C, medically important species grow best at 35°C to 37°C. *A. baumannii* is uniquely capable of growing at higher temperatures, up to 44°C. It is able to survive on moist surfaces (e.g., mechanical ventilation equipment) as well as dry surfaces (e.g., human skin).
*   **Primary Agar:** Grows readily and well on most types of routine agar media used to culture patient specimens, including blood agar and MacConkey agar.
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** N/A
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Catalase-positive and oxidase-negative. *A. baumannii* belongs to the glucose-oxidizing group of *Acinetobacter* species.

#### Virulence Factors & Genetics
*   **Secretion Systems:** N/A
*   **Exotoxins:**
    *   **Toxin Name:** N/A
    *   **Mechanism of Action:** N/A
*   **Endotoxins & Superantigens:** N/A
*   **Enzymes:** Produces various beta-lactamases, including chromosomally mediated enzymes, Class B metallo-beta-lactamases (which require zinc for activity), and Class D carbapenemases.
*   **Genetic Elements:** Often harbors transmissible resistance genes (e.g., VIM and NDM metallo-beta-lactamases on integrons) that mediate multidrug resistance.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Pathogenicity is closely tied to its ability to create biofilms on human cells and artificial surfaces (e.g., indwelling urinary catheters, endotracheal tubes, and intravascular devices). Environmental contamination can lead to inoculation during traumatic injury.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Extracellular pathogen.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** Biofilm production protects the colony from the host environment and antimicrobial agents.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Primarily an opportunistic pathogen. Infections occur predominantly in immunocompromised individuals, intensive care unit (ICU) patients, patients receiving respiratory therapy/mechanical ventilation, patients recovering from surgery, and those on broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Nosocomial pneumonia (especially ventilator-associated pneumonia), septicemia (accounting for up to 2% of all bloodstream infections), wound infections (such as those from burns or severe trauma), and urinary tract infections.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Meningitis following neurosurgical procedures.
*   **Incubation Period:** N/A
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** N/A
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Infections in hospitalized and ICU patients lead to increased morbidity, extended length of hospital stay, and higher mortality.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Ubiquitous saprophyte in nature, widely distributed in soil and water. Can be found in hospital environments on moist and dry surfaces. It is also part of the normal oropharyngeal and skin microbiota of a small number of healthy people and can proliferate to large numbers during hospitalization.
*   **Transmission Route:** Nosocomial transmission via contaminated medical devices (intravascular catheters, respiratory ventilators) or surfaces, and environmental contamination of traumatic wounds.
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** Found globally. *A. baumannii* was notably responsible for causing an outbreak of serious wound infections among servicemen sustaining traumatic injuries in military conflicts in Iraq.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Widespread dissemination of [[Carbapenem]]-resistant and multidrug-resistant strains poses a serious global public health challenge. Preventative measures emphasize strict environmental decontamination and hand hygiene. Infected patients are often placed on maximum infection control precautions to prevent spread within hospitals.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Gram stain of clinical specimens reveals plump Gram-negative coccobacilli. They may appear as diplococci on smears, mimicking *Neisseria* species or *Haemophilus*, but can be distinguished because *A. baumannii* is oxidase-negative.
*   **Molecular Testing:** Nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs/PCR) are often required to reliably detect the specific genes encoding [[Carbapenem]] resistance (e.g., KPC, NDM), as conventional phenotypic susceptibility tests may fail to detect them accurately.
*   **Serology & Immunology:** Matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight (MALDI-TOF) mass spectrometry provides reliable and rapid identification to the species level.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** Antimicrobial therapy must always be guided by in vitro susceptibility testing due to extensive resistance. More susceptible strains may respond to [[Gentamicin]], [[Amikacin]], or [[Tobramycin]], combined with extended-spectrum penicillins or cephalosporins. For multidrug-resistant isolates, [[Polymyxin|colistin]] may be the only active antimicrobial agent.
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** [[Tigecycline]] has been successfully used to treat infections caused by [[Carbapenem]]-resistant acinetobacters. Combination therapy with multiple active agents is often necessary for serious infections.
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Famous for multidrug resistance (MDR). It possesses Class D carbapenemases (OXA-derived) and Class B metallo-beta-lactamases (VIM, NDM) that encode resistance to virtually all beta-lactam antibiotics, including carbapenems. It can also acquire resistance to fluoroquinolones and aminoglycosides.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Extreme care must be taken when selecting carbapenems or [[Polymyxin|colistin]] because in vitro susceptibility tests may fail to detect "heteroresistant" strains (a highly resistant subpopulation of organisms within the isolate).
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** No vaccines are currently available.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Plump Gram-negative coccobacilli, survives on dry surfaces, glucose-oxidizer, oxidase-negative, ventilator-associated pneumonia, combat trauma wound infections (Iraq).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike the vast majority of Gram-negative rods, *Acinetobacter* species are highly unusual in their ability to survive successfully for extended periods on dry environmental surfaces and human skin. Furthermore, its appearance as a diplococcus on Gram stain frequently causes it to be mistaken for *Neisseria*, but it is decisively separated by a negative oxidase test.
