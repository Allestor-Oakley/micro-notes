### Pasteurella multocida

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Possesses a typical Gram-negative cell wall.
*   **Appendages:** The organism is nonmotile.
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** Produces a prominent polysaccharide capsule composed of hyaluronic acid.
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Aerobe or facultative anaerobe that grows readily at 37°C.
*   **Primary Agar:** Grows well on ordinary bacteriologic media, specifically blood agar and chocolate agar. After overnight incubation on blood agar, it forms large, buttery colonies due to its polysaccharide capsule.
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** Grows poorly on MacConkey agar and other media typically selective for Gram-negative rods.
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Oxidase-positive and catalase-positive. Uniquely, it produces indole, which gives the colonies a characteristic musty odor.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Infection is primarily acquired via the bite, scratch, or lick of a colonized animal (most commonly cats and dogs) introducing the organism into deep tissues or open wounds. It can also be acquired via the respiratory route, leading to chronic pulmonary infections without evident connection to animals.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Extracellular pathogen.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** The hyaluronic acid polysaccharide capsule is a major virulence factor that protects the organism from host phagocytosis.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Opportunistic systemic infections are particularly severe in immunocompromised patients, such as those with underlying hepatic disease, chronic renal insufficiency, gouty arthritis, or those undergoing systemic steroid treatments (e.g., Cushing syndrome).

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Animal bite wound infections (most commonly from cats and dogs). Cat bites are particularly prone to severe infection because the puncture wounds are deep and difficult to disinfect.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Can cause exacerbations of chronic pulmonary disease, bacteremia, and meningitis. In immunocompromised patients, it can cause rapidly progressive, fatal necrotizing fasciitis accompanied by hemorrhagic bullae, shock, and gastrointestinal bleeding.
*   **Incubation Period:** Highly rapid; clinical signs usually present within hours of the animal bite.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** A history of an animal bite followed within hours by an acute, rapid onset of redness, swelling, pain, and variable regional lymphadenopathy.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Systemic shock, massive necrotizing fasciitis, and death in compromised hosts. It is also a leading cause of hemorrhagic septicemia in many animal species.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** The normal oral, respiratory, and gastrointestinal microbiota of many domestic and wild animals, including cats, dogs, rabbits, rats, horses, sheep, fowl, and swine.
*   **Transmission Route:** Direct contact via animal bites, deep scratches, or an animal licking an open wound.
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** Ubiquitous and occurs worldwide.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** Penicillin G is the drug of choice for infections resulting from animal bites.
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** Expanded-spectrum cephalosporins, macrolides, tetracyclines, and fluoroquinolones.
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** The organism demonstrates poor susceptibility or inherent resistance to semisynthetic penicillins (e.g., oxacillin), first-generation cephalosporins, and aminoglycosides.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Aggressive wound care and appropriate prophylactic/therapeutic antibiotics are critical since the organism can rapidly disseminate and cause tissue necrosis, particularly in steroid-dependent or immunocompromised patients.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** N/A

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Cat and dog bites", rapid onset within hours, "musty odor", indole-positive, bipolar staining, buttery colonies, hyaluronic acid capsule.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike many other Gram-negative bacilli, it grows very poorly on MacConkey agar. Furthermore, while most Gram-negative rods are inherently resistant to it, this organism remains highly susceptible to Penicillin G.
