### Moraxella catarrhalis

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Possesses a Gram-negative cell wall.
*   **Appendages:** N/A

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Strictly aerobic.
*   **Primary Agar:** Grows well on sheep blood agar and chocolate agar, producing nonpigmented or pinkish gray opaque colonies.
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** Capable of growing on selective media such as modified Thayer-Martin (MTM) agar.
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Oxidase-positive and catalase-positive. Unlike *Neisseria* species, *M. catarrhalis* is asaccharolytic and does not acidify carbohydrates. It produces DNase and butyrate esterase, the latter of which forms the basis for rapid fluorometric identification tests used to differentiate it from saprophytic *Neisseria* species in respiratory samples.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Typically established through endogenous spread from the normal microbiota of the oropharynx, often following an antecedent viral respiratory infection.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** N/A
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** N/A
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Primarily acts as an opportunistic pathogen. Infections are significantly more common in young children, the elderly, patients on corticosteroid or immunosuppressive therapy, and individuals with a compromised pulmonary system or underlying chronic pulmonary diseases (e.g., chronic obstructive pulmonary disease).

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Tracheobronchitis, bronchopneumonia, acute sinusitis, otitis media, and conjunctivitis. It is one of the three most common bacterial causes of otitis media and sinusitis (alongside *[[Streptococcus pneumoniae]]* and *[[Haemophilus influenzae]]*).
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Rarely associated with pleural effusions and bacteremia.
*   **Incubation Period:** N/A
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** Radiographs of pulmonary infections may show patchy infiltrates with occasional lobar consolidation.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** N/A

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Humans. It is a member of the normal upper respiratory tract (oropharyngeal) microbiota in 40–50% of healthy school children.
*   **Transmission Route:** Endogenous spread from normal respiratory flora to sterile or adjacent sites; person-to-person transmission can occur via respiratory droplets.
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** N/A
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** N/A

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Gram stain of sputum or bronchial aspirate reveals typical Gram-negative diplococci along with polymorphonuclear leukocytes.
*   **Molecular Testing:** Included in commercial multiplex nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs) for respiratory pathogens.
*   **Serology & Immunology:** N/A

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** [[Amoxicillin]]/clavulanic acid or cephalosporins.
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** The organism remains uniformly susceptible to many other broad-spectrum antibiotic classes, such as macrolides and fluoroquinolones.
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** The vast majority of clinically significant isolates produce beta-lactamases, rendering them inherently resistant to penicillins and requiring the use of beta-lactamase inhibitors or alternative antibiotic classes.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Empirical [[penicillin]] monotherapy is generally ineffective due to widespread beta-lactamase production.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** N/A

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Pinkish gray opaque colonies", "DNase positive", "butyrate esterase positive", "Gram-negative diplococci", "COPD exacerbation".
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** It morphologically mimics *Neisseria* species (which are also Gram-negative diplococci and oxidase-positive) but is easily differentiated by its lack of carbohydrate fermentation (asaccharolytic), production of DNase, and production of butyrate esterase.
