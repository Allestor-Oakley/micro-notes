### Neisseria meningitidis

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** The organism possesses a typical Gram-negative cell wall containing a thin peptidoglycan layer between the inner cytoplasmic membrane and the outer membrane. The outer membrane contains lipooligosaccharide (LOS), which lacks long O-antigen side chains, and highly conserved reduction-modifiable proteins (Rmp). The outer membrane also contains porin proteins (Por A and Por B) and opacity proteins (Opa). During rapid cell growth, it spontaneously releases outer membrane blebs containing LOS and surface proteins.
*   **Appendages:** Possesses pili extending from the cytoplasmic membrane through the outer membrane. Pili undergo posttranslational modification and consist of repeating pilin subunits with a highly conserved amino terminal and a highly variable carboxyl terminal.
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** Surrounded by a prominent polysaccharide capsule that determines serogroup classification. The group A polysaccharide is a polymer of N-acetyl-mannosamine-1-phosphate. Serogroups B, C, Y, and W-135 incorporate human sialic acid derivatives (N-acetylneuraminic acid [NANA]) into their capsules.
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** N/A

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Fastidious, strictly aerobic organisms that grow best at 35°C to 37°C in a humid atmosphere supplemented with 5% to 10% CO2. They are rapidly killed by drying, prolonged exposure to sunlight, moist heat, and many disinfectants. They produce autolytic enzymes causing rapid swelling and lysis in vitro at 25°C and at alkaline pH.
*   **Primary Agar:** Readily grows on complex organic media, such as 5% sheep blood agar or heated blood (chocolate) agar. Colonies are gray, convex, glistening, mucoid, transparent or opaque, nonpigmented, and nonhemolytic.
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** Selective media such as modified Thayer-Martin (MTM), Martin-Lewis, GC-Lect, and New York City agars. These contain antibiotics (e.g., [[vancomycin]], [[Polymyxin E|colistin]], [[nystatin]], and [[trimethoprim]]) to suppress normal microbiota. *N. meningitidis* is [[Polymyxin E|colistin]]-resistant and grows well on these media.
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Oxidase-positive and catalase-positive. Produces acid by the oxidation (not fermentation) of glucose and maltose, but does not oxidize lactose or sucrose.

    *   **Mechanism of Action:** N/A
*   **Endotoxins & Superantigens:** Lipooligosaccharide (LOS) in the outer membrane contains a lipid A moiety with extremely potent endotoxin activity. Endotoxin levels measured in meningococcemia are 50- to 100-fold greater than in other Gram-negative sepsis infections. LOS triggers the release of massive amounts of tumor necrosis factor (TNF) and interleukins (IL-1, IL-6), leading to vasculitis, vascular permeability, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), and profound shock.
*   **Enzymes:** Produces IgA1 protease (cleaves the hinge region in IgA1 to create immunologically inactive Fc and Fab fragments) and autolysins.
*   **Genetic Elements:** The genome is approximately 2.27 Mb. *N. meningitidis* rarely carries plasmids. Readily undergoes genetic transformation by acquiring DNA from other nasopharyngeal flora (e.g., leading to altered penicillin-binding proteins).

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Enters via the respiratory tract. Pili mediate initial selective attachment to specific receptors on nonciliated columnar epithelial cells in the nasopharynx. After attachment, meningococci multiply, forming aggregates. Pili then undergo posttranslational modification to destabilize these aggregates, enhancing tissue penetration and host-cell entry.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Both. Bacteria are internalized into phagocytic vacuoles, avoid intracellular death, replicate, and migrate to subepithelial spaces to enter the bloodstream.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** The polysaccharide capsule prevents phagocytosis. Sialylation of the capsule mimics human tissues (molecular mimicry), allowing the organism to evade host immune surveillance. Rmp proteins stimulate antibodies that block serum bactericidal activity. Shedding of outer membrane LOS blebs binds and depletes host antibodies.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Clearance requires specific bactericidal antibodies and complement. Patients with deficiencies in late complement components (C5, C6, C7, C8) have a 6000-fold increased risk of infection. Asplenia and impaired cellular immunity also greatly increase the risk of overwhelming, fulminant sepsis.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Meningitis (intense headache, vomiting, photophobia, confusion, stiff neck) and Meningococcemia (overwhelming septicemia).
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Meningococcal pneumonia (often following viral respiratory infections), purulent or immunologically mediated arthritis, and urethritis.
*   **Incubation Period:** Rapid; illness often develops suddenly and can progress to death in as little as 2 to 12 hours for fulminant meningococcemia.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** Petechial skin lesions on the trunk and lower extremities that rapidly coalesce into large hemorrhagic bullae and purpura. Bilateral hemorrhagic necrosis of the adrenal glands (Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome).
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Septic shock, multiorgan failure, DIC, gangrene of the skin and extremities requiring amputation, cerebral infarctions, hydrocephalus. Neurologic sequelae include hearing deficits, learning disabilities, and arthritis. Mortality is near 100% if untreated.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Humans are the only natural hosts. 5% to 40% of the healthy population may be asymptomatic nasopharyngeal carriers (rates increase to 70-80% during epidemics).
*   **Transmission Route:** Spread person-to-person via respiratory droplets and oral secretions, primarily requiring prolonged close contact (e.g., family members, military barracks, college dorms).
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** Worldwide distribution. Epidemic cycles occur every 2-14 years in the sub-Saharan African "meningitis belt" (primarily serogroup A, W, X, and C). In the Americas and Europe, endemic disease is mostly caused by serogroups B, C, and Y.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Outbreaks trigger mass vaccination campaigns. Droplet and standard precautions are recommended for infected patients for the first 24 hours of antimicrobial therapy. Reducing personal contacts (avoiding crowding) controls spread.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Gram-stain of centrifuged cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) or petechial aspirate reveals characteristic intracellular and extracellular Gram-negative diplococci (coffee-bean shaped) among abundant polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNs).
*   **Molecular Testing:** Multiplex nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs) / PCR on CSF are the diagnostic standard of choice, especially valuable if the patient received prior antibiotics.
*   **Serology & Immunology:** Latex agglutination for capsular antigens in CSF is available but insensitive and largely replaced by NAATs.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** Prompt intravenous administration of a third-generation cephalosporin ([[Ceftriaxone]] or [[Cefotaxime]]). If the isolate is proven susceptible, [[Penicillin G]] or [[Ampicillin]] can be used.
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** [[Chloramphenicol]] (often given as an oily suspension during epidemics in resource-poor settings), [[meropenem]], or fluoroquinolones.
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Widespread resistance to sulfonamides. Resistance to [[Penicillin]] is rising due to genetic transformations yielding altered penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) with decreased affinity for the drug. Resistance to [[rifampin]] has also been reported in up to 27% of isolates.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Antibiotic penetration into the CSF is poor, requiring high, continuous dosing. Dexamethasone is rarely proven effective as an adjunct in resource-poor regions but is standard in high-income countries.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** Chemoprophylaxis for close household/living contacts uses [[Rifampin]], [[Ciprofloxacin]], [[Ceftriaxone]], or [[Azithromycin]] to eradicate nasopharyngeal carriage. Vaccines include multivalent conjugate and polysaccharide vaccines targeting serogroups A, C, Y, and W-135. Recombinant protein-based vaccines (e.g., 4CMenB/Bexsero and Trumenba) are used for serogroup B because its capsule is poorly immunogenic.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome", "meningitis belt", "coffee-bean/kidney-bean shaped diplococci", petechial/hemorrhagic rash, LOS blebbing, Thayer-Martin agar.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike *N. gonorrhoeae*, *N. meningitidis* possesses a prominent polysaccharide capsule, oxidizes maltose in addition to glucose, and only rarely harbors plasmids. Unlike most bacteria that use siderophores to capture iron, *N. meningitidis* steals iron by directly binding host transferrin and lactoferrin via unique outer membrane receptors.
