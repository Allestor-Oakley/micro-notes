### Mycoplasma pneumoniae

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Uniquely lacks a cell wall entirely (classified in the class Mollicutes). The cell membrane is unique among bacteria because it contains sterols.
*   **Appendages:** Possesses specialized polar tip attachment structures consisting of a complex group of interactive proteins, adhesins (most notably the P1 adhesin), and adherence-accessory proteins that are proline-rich.
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** N/A
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Strict aerobe. It exhibits a very slow rate of growth, with a generation time of approximately 6 hours. It is the smallest free-living bacterium and is capable of passing through 0.45-µm pore filters.
*   **Primary Agar:** Cannot be grown on standard media. Requires highly specialized, complex broth or agar supplemented with serum (to provide essential sterols), yeast extract (for nucleic acid precursors), glucose, a pH indicator, and [[Penicillin]] (to selectively inhibit other contaminating bacterial flora).
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** See Primary Agar (serum-supplemented media with [[Penicillin]]).
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** N/A

*   **Secretion Systems:** N/A

*   **Endotoxins & Superantigens:** Functions as a superantigen. It stimulates inflammatory cells to migrate to the site of infection and release cytokines, initially tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-1 (IL-1), and later IL-6.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Acts as an extracellular pathogen that adheres to the respiratory epithelium. The P1 adhesin protein on its specialized polar tip interacts specifically with sialated glycoprotein receptors situated at the base of cilia on host epithelial cells (as well as on the surface of erythrocytes).
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Extracellular pathogen.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** Able to rapidly change the expression of its surface lipoproteins, an important mechanism for evading the host immune response and establishing persistent or chronic infections.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Cytolysis is partly mediated by the host's own antigen-antibody reactions or by chemotaxis and the action of mononuclear cells. Severe disease occurs predominantly in patients with hypogammaglobulinemia or undiagnosed immune defects.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Tracheobronchitis (the most common clinical presentation) and primary atypical pneumonia (also known as walking pneumonia). Also commonly causes pharyngitis.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Can cause significant secondary complications including neurologic abnormalities (meningoencephalitis, paralysis, myelitis), pericarditis, myocarditis, hemolytic anemia, arthritis, and mucocutaneous lesions.
*   **Incubation Period:** 2 to 3 weeks.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** In pneumonia cases, chest radiographs reveal extensive, patchy bronchopneumonia infiltrates (often in the lower lobes) that are characteristically more impressive and severe than the physical examination findings would suggest.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Progression to hemorrhagic pneumonia, bilateral pleural effusions, multiorgan failure, and death in highly susceptible or immunocompromised individuals. Severe hemolytic anemia mediated by cold agglutinins.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Strict human pathogen.
*   **Transmission Route:** Transmitted from person to person by inhalation of aerosolized droplets (infectious respiratory secretions).
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** Worldwide distribution. Unlike many other respiratory pathogens, its infections do not have a specific seasonal incidence and occur throughout the year.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Disease is most common in children between the ages of 5 and 15 years and young adults, but all populations are susceptible.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Microscopy (including Gram stain) is of absolutely no diagnostic value because the organisms lack a cell wall and stain poorly or not at all.
*   **Molecular Testing:** PCR-based nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs) targeting species-specific genes are the diagnostic tests of choice due to high sensitivity and rapid turnaround. These are commonly included in multiplex molecular respiratory panels.
*   **Serology & Immunology:** Enzyme immunoassays detecting IgM and IgG antibodies are available and more sensitive than culture, but they are limited by the need to collect paired acute and convalescent sera 3 to 4 weeks apart to demonstrate a four-fold rise in antibody titer. Historically, nonspecific production of cold agglutinins (IgM antibodies that bind to the human erythrocyte I antigen) was measured, but this test lacks sensitivity and specificity.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** Macrolides (e.g., [[erythromycin]], [[azithromycin]], [[clarithromycin]]), tetracyclines (e.g., [[doxycycline]]), or newer fluoroquinolones (e.g., [[levofloxacin]]).
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** N/A
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Inherently and uniformly resistant to all beta-lactam antibiotics (e.g., penicillins, cephalosporins) and other cell-wall synthesis inhibitors because it completely lacks a peptidoglycan cell wall. Global prevalence of resistance to macrolides is increasingly reported.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Beta-lactam antibiotics are completely ineffective and contraindicated.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** No vaccines are currently available or effective. Immunity to reinfection following a natural infection is not lifelong.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Walking pneumonia", "Eaton agent", "cold agglutinins", "P1 adhesin", "sterols in cell membrane", "smallest free-living bacterium", "fried egg colonies" (typical of Mollicutes, though extremely slow to grow).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Extremely unique among all bacteria because its cell membrane contains sterols and it is completely devoid of a cell wall.
