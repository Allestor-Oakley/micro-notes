### Anaplasma phagocytophilum

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Possesses a Gram-negative cell wall, but uniquely lacks genes for lipid A biosynthesis and instead incorporates cholesterol for its survival. It stains poorly with the standard Gram stain; Giemsa or Gimenez stains are highly preferred.
*   **Appendages:** N/A
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** N/A
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Obligate intracellular bacterium. It infects and multiplies within the phagocytic vacuoles of host cells.
*   **Primary Agar:** N/A
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** N/A
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Unlike chlamydiae, it is able to synthesize its own adenosine triphosphate (ATP).

#### Virulence Factors & Genetics
*   **Secretion Systems:** N/A
*   **Exotoxins:**
    *   **Toxin Name:** N/A
    *   **Mechanism of Action:** N/A
*   **Endotoxins & Superantigens:** Lacks genes for lipid A biosynthesis, meaning it does not possess a traditional Gram-negative endotoxin structure.
*   **Enzymes:** N/A
*   **Genetic Elements:** Taxonomically grouped in the family Anaplasmataceae within the order Rickettsiales. Molecular reorganizations designated *Ehrlichia equi* and the "HGE agent" as subjective synonyms of *Anaplasma phagocytophilum*.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Transmitted to humans via the bite of an infected tick (specifically the nymph stage, which is most commonly associated with human disease).
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Obligate intracellular pathogen. It specifically infects circulating leukocytes, localizing in granulocytes (neutrophils, eosinophils, and basophils). It multiplies within phagocytic vacuoles to form characteristic mulberry-like clusters called morulae.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** Intracellular growth protects the bacteria from immune clearance. It successfully survives by preventing the fusion of the phagosome with lysosomes inside granulocytes.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** The pathology of the disease appears to be related to [[Monocyte and Macrophage]] activation and the host's initiated inflammatory response.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Human granulocytic anaplasmosis (HGA), formerly known as human granulocytic ehrlichiosis (HGE). It predominantly presents as an acute, nonspecific flulike illness characterized by high fever, headache, malaise, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, weight loss, and myalgias.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Asymptomatic infections can frequently occur.
*   **Incubation Period:** Typically 5 to 11 days after exposure to a tick bite.
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** Presence of morulae (intracytoplasmic inclusions) within circulating granulocytes. Unlike many rickettsial diseases, a cutaneous rash is uncommon and is observed in less than 10% of patients.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Most patients exhibit leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and elevated serum transaminases. Over half of infected symptomatic patients require hospitalization. Severe complications can include peripheral neuropathies, such as facial palsy or demyelinating polyneuropathy. Despite the severity, the mortality rate is less than 1%. Residual muscle weakness and pain may persist even after successful treatment.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Small mammals (rodents, white-footed mice, chipmunks, voles), deer, and sheep. Humans act purely as incidental or accidental hosts.
*   **Transmission Route:** Bite of an infected tick (specifically *Ixodes* species, such as the blacklegged tick).
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** North America (highly concentrated in the Upper Midwest, Northeast/East Coast, and West Coast), Europe, and Asia.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Disease is controlled by preventing vector exposure. This involves avoiding tick-infested brushy/wooded areas, wearing protective clothing, using effective insect repellents, and promptly removing embedded ticks.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Peripheral blood smears can be examined for rare intracytoplasmic inclusions (morulae) in granulocytes; however, this diagnostic method is relatively insensitive.
*   **Molecular Testing:** Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) analysis of blood samples targeting specific *Anaplasma* DNA is a highly reliable confirmatory test.
*   **Serology & Immunology:** Indirect fluorescent antibody (IFA) testing is widely used to detect patient antibody responses to the pathogen.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** [[Doxycycline]]. Therapy must be initiated immediately based on clinical suspicion and should not be delayed while waiting for laboratory confirmation.
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** [[Rifampin]] is an acceptable alternative therapy, particularly for patients who are unable to tolerate [[Doxycycline]].
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Inherently resistant to fluoroquinolones, penicillins, cephalosporins, [[chloramphenicol]], aminoglycosides, and macrolides.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Beta-lactam antibiotics and macrolides are completely ineffective against this pathogen.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** No vaccines are currently available.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Morulae" in granulocytes, human granulocytic anaplasmosis (HGA), *Ixodes* tick vector, leukopenia and thrombocytopenia without rash, lack of lipid A.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** The historical derivation of the organism's name translates to "a thing without form found in phagocytes" (*an* = without, *plasma* = form; *phago* = eat, *kytos* = cell, *philein* = to love). Unlike *Rickettsia* species which cause marked vasculitis and maculopapular rashes, *Anaplasma phagocytophilum* rarely produces a rash (<10% of cases). It also uniquely lacks genes for lipid A biosynthesis, an extraordinarily unusual feature for a Gram-negative bacterium.
