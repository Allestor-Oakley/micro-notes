### Mycoplasma genitalium

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Uniquely lacks a cell wall entirely (classified in the class Mollicutes). The cell membrane is unique among bacteria because it contains sterols.
*   **Appendages:** Possesses specialized polar tip attachment structures consisting of a complex group of interactive proteins, adhesins (specifically the MgPa adhesin), and adherence-accessory proteins that are proline-rich.
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** N/A
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Culture of the organism is extremely difficult. It requires much longer incubation periods compared to other urogenital mycoplasmas (like *Ureaplasma* or *M. hominis*).
*   **Primary Agar:** N/A (Does not grow on standard media; specialized culture is too difficult for routine use).
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** N/A

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Transmitted via sexual contact. It adheres to the surfaces of ciliated and nonciliated host epithelial cells utilizing its specialized polar tip structure and the MgPa adhesin protein. The receptors are likely mucosal cell sialoglycoconjugates and sulfated glycolipids.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Extracellular pathogen.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** N/A
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Tissue damage and cytolysis are partly mediated by the host's own antigen-antibody reactions or by chemotaxis and the action of mononuclear cells.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Nongonococcal, nonchlamydial urethritis in men; cervicitis and pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) in women.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Endometritis and salpingitis.
*   **Incubation Period:** N/A
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** N/A
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Can lead to infertility in females.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Strict human pathogen.
*   **Transmission Route:** Sexually transmitted.
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** N/A
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** *M. genitalium* is a significant cause of non-gonococcal, non-chlamydial urethritis and cervicitis, and in most settings, it is more common than *[[Neisseria gonorrhoeae]]*.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Microscopy (including Gram stain) is of absolutely no diagnostic value because the organisms lack a cell wall and do not stain with conventional reagents.
*   **Molecular Testing:** Nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs) are the most sensitive and specific diagnostic method and are considered the standard for diagnosis. First voided urine, urethral, or endocervical specimens can be used.
*   **Serology & Immunology:** While serological data have historically been used to associate the organism with disease, it is generally not used for routine clinical diagnosis today.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** [[Azithromycin]] or fluoroquinolones. ([[Tetracycline|Tetracyclines]] and macrolides are also generally effective against urogenital mycoplasmas).
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** N/A
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Inherently and uniformly resistant to all beta-lactam antibiotics (e.g., penicillins, cephalosporins) and other cell-wall synthesis inhibitors because it completely lacks a peptidoglycan cell wall.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Beta-lactam antibiotics are completely ineffective and contraindicated.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** Safe sexual practices and prompt treatment of the patient and sexual partners help control infections. No vaccine is available.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "MgPa adhesin", "sterols in cell membrane", "lacks cell wall", nongonococcal urethritis (NGU).
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** *M. genitalium* (and other mycoplasmas) are extremely unique among all bacteria because their cell membranes contain sterols and they are completely devoid of a cell wall, rendering them invisible on Gram stain and immune to beta-lactam antibiotics.
