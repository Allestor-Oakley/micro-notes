### Mycoplasma hominis

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Belongs to the class Mollicutes. It entirely lacks a peptidoglycan cell wall. Uniquely among bacteria, its cell membrane contains sterols (unesterified cholesterol) which are required for growth and membrane stability.
*   **Appendages:** Some pathogenic mycoplasmas possess specialized polar tip structures composed of interactive proteins and adhesins that mediate adherence to host cells, while others lack distinct tip structures and use alternative adhesin proteins.
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** N/A
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Cultivation requires an incubation temperature of 37°C. Compared to other *Mycoplasma* species, *M. hominis* exhibits relatively rapid growth (2 to 3 days) and has less stringent nutritional requirements. Reproduction can occur via budding, fragmentation, or binary fission.
*   **Primary Agar:** Requires complex media containing serum (to provide sterols), a metabolic substrate (such as glucose or urea), and growth factors like yeast extract. On solid media, it classically forms minute, characteristic "fried egg" colonies.
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** Media is often supplemented with a [[Penicillin]] antibiotic to inhibit the overgrowth of normal, cell-wall-producing bacterial flora.
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Some human mycoplasmas are known to produce peroxides and hemolyze red blood cells. Antigenically, it is distinct from other *Mycoplasma* species.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Adheres to the surfaces of host cells (both ciliated and nonciliated) via adhesin proteins, likely interacting with mucosal cell sialoglycoconjugates and sulfated glycolipids.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Predominantly observed at the cell surfaces in vivo and in cell cultures, though they are frequently found intracellularly as well.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** Evades the host immune system through extensive antigenic variation of its outer membrane proteins.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Capable of causing severe systemic infections primarily in immunocompromised patients. Pathogenesis partly involves direct cytotoxicity from generated hydrogen peroxide/superoxide radicals, and cytolysis mediated by host mononuclear cells and antigen-antibody reactions.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Genitourinary infections. It is isolated from the uterine tubes of approximately 10% of women with salpingitis, and from the blood of approximately 10% of women presenting with postabortal or postpartum fever. It is also cultured from the upper urinary tract in about 10% of patients with pyelonephritis.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Occasionally isolated from joint fluid in patients with arthritis. Can cause respiratory tract infections and systemic infections in immunocompromised individuals. It is notably more prevalent in women with bacterial vaginosis compared to healthy women.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Humans (genitourinary and respiratory tracts).
*   **Transmission Route:** Endogenous spread or direct contact, including sexual transmission and maternal-fetal/neonatal transmission.
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** Worldwide distribution.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** N/A

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Routine Gram stains are useless because the bacteria lack a cell wall. Giemsa stains of centrifuged sediment from broth cultures can reveal the characteristic pleomorphic structures.
*   **Molecular Testing:** Nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs) / PCR are the most sensitive diagnostic methods and the test of choice.
*   **Serology & Immunology:** Serologic profiling can be performed; the complement fixation (CF) antigens of mycoplasmas are glycolipids, whereas enzyme-linked immunoassay (ELISA) antigens are proteins.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** Tetracyclines, macrolides (e.g., [[azithromycin]], [[clarithromycin]]), and fluoroquinolones are the antimicrobial agents of choice.
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** N/A
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Because *M. hominis* entirely lacks a peptidoglycan cell wall, it is intrinsically and uniformly resistant to all beta-lactam antibiotics (penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems) and glycopeptides ([[vancomycin]]) that target cell wall synthesis.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Beta-lactams are completely ineffective and contraindicated.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** N/A

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Fried egg" colonies, Mollicutes, cell wall-free, sterols/cholesterol in cell membrane, postpartum fever, "filterable forms".
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Extremely unusual among bacteria, *Mycoplasma* species are so uniquely small (down to 0.2 μm) that they can behave as "filterable forms," passing directly through 0.45-μm bacterial filters used for standard liquid sterilization. Furthermore, they are the only bacteria that naturally require and incorporate host-derived sterols (cholesterol) into their cell membranes for structural integrity.
