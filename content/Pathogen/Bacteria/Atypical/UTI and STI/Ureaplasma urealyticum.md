### Ureaplasma urealyticum

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Uniquely lacks a cell wall entirely (classified in the class Mollicutes / cell wall-less eubacteria). The cell membrane is unique among bacteria because it contains sterols.
*   **Appendages:** N/A
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** N/A
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Easily cultivatable due to its rapid growth and less stringent nutritional requirements compared to other pathogenic mycoplasmas (such as [[Mycoplasma pneumoniae]] and [[Mycoplasma genitalium]]).
*   **Primary Agar:** N/A
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** N/A
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Has a strict biochemical requirement for 10% urea for growth (hence the species name *urealyticum*).

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Colonizes the mucosal surfaces of the genitourinary tract. Neonates acquire the organism vertically during passage through the infected birth canal.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** N/A

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:** Nongonococcal, nonchlamydial urethritis in men.
*   **Secondary, Atypical & Alternate Presentations:** Postpartum fever in women. Lung disease (pneumonia) in premature, low-birth-weight infants. (Note: Evidence suggesting that U. urealyticum is associated with involuntary female infertility is considered marginal at best).
*   **Incubation Period:** N/A

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Strict human pathogen. It is a common colonizer of the normal female genital tract, where its association with active disease is weak.
*   **Transmission Route:** Sexually transmitted between adults; vertically transmitted from mother to infant during birth.
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** Ubiquitous worldwide.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** N/A

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Microscopy (including the Gram stain) is of absolutely no diagnostic value because the organisms completely lack a cell wall and do not stain with conventional reagents.
*   **Molecular Testing:** N/A
*   **Serology & Immunology:** Serologic studies, along with culture, have historically been used to associate the organism with various diseases.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** [[Tetracycline|Tetracyclines]], [[Macrolide|macrolides]], and [[Fluoroquinolone|quinolones]] are the agents of choice.
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** N/A
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Inherently and uniformly resistant to all beta-lactam antibiotics (e.g., [[Penicillin|penicillins]], [[Cephalosporin|cephalosporins]]) and other cell-wall synthesis inhibitors because it completely lacks a peptidoglycan cell wall.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Beta-lactam antibiotics are completely ineffective and contraindicated. In a symptomatic neonate with radiographic abnormalities in the lung and the absence of another discernible cause for the pneumonia, empiric treatment directed against Ureaplasma species is warranted.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** No vaccines are available.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Requires 10% urea", cell wall-less eubacteria (Mollicutes), sterols in cell membrane, nongonococcal urethritis, biovar 2, lung disease in premature infants.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Extremely unique among all bacteria because its cell membrane contains sterols and it is completely devoid of a cell wall, rendering it invisible on Gram stain and naturally immune to all beta-lactam antibiotics.
