### Treponema pallidum subsp. pallidum

#### Molecular Microbiology & Morphology
*   **Cell Wall / Membrane Features:** Possesses an outer sheath or glycosaminoglycan coating. Inside the sheath is an outer membrane containing peptidoglycan that maintains structural integrity. It lacks the lipopolysaccharide (LPS) typically found in Gram-negative bacilli. It has a remarkably low density of outer membrane proteins. Membrane proteins contain covalently bound lipids at their amino terminals, anchoring the proteins to the cytoplasmic or outer membranes and keeping them inaccessible to antibodies.
*   **Appendages:** Actively motile via endoflagella (axial filaments) located in the periplasmic space between the inner and outer membranes. The endoflagella begin at each end of the organism, wind around it, and extend to and overlap at the midpoint. They are composed of three core proteins homologous to other bacterial flagellins.
*   **Capsule Composition & Structure:** Outer sheath or glycosaminoglycan coating.
*   **Spore Characteristics & Germination:** Non-spore-forming.

#### Growth & Biochemical Profile
*   **Optimal Growth Conditions:** Microaerophilic or anaerobic. Extremely sensitive to oxygen toxicity and drying. It reproduces by transverse fission and divides slowly with a replication time of approximately 30 hours. It is highly dependent on the host cell for purines, pyrimidines, and most amino acids because it lacks the tricarboxylic acid cycle.
*   **Primary Agar:** Cannot be cultured continuously on artificial media, in fertile eggs, or in tissue culture. Limited, slow growth for only a few generations can be achieved in cultured rabbit epithelial cells.
*   **Selective / Enriched Agar:** N/A
*   **Metabolic Profile & Biochemical Testing:** Lacks genes for catalase or superoxide dismutase, making it extremely sensitive to oxygen.

#### Virulence Factors & Genetics
*   **Secretion Systems:** N/A
*   **Exotoxins:**
    *   **Toxin Name:** N/A
    *   **Mechanism of Action:** N/A
*   **Endotoxins & Superantigens:** N/A (Lacks LPS).
*   **Enzymes:** Hyaluronidase, which breaks down hyaluronic acid in the ground substance of tissue and presumably enhances invasiveness and perivascular infiltration.
*   **Genetic Elements:** The genome is a highly conserved, small circular chromosome of approximately 1,138,000 base pairs. It uniquely lacks transposable elements, which likely explains its continued susceptibility to [[penicillin]]. Its genome differs by less than 0.2% from other closely related endemic treponemes.

#### Pathogenesis & Host Immune Evasion
*   **Entry & Attachment Mechanism:** Infection penetrates intact mucous membranes or enters through microscopic abrasions. Outer membrane proteins promote adherence to host cells, and it is known to bind to host fibronectin.
*   **Intracellular vs Extracellular Survival:** Extracellular.
*   **Immune Evasion Strategies:** The extremely low density of outer membrane proteins, combined with an outer coating of host fibronectin and the burial of membrane proteins away from surface exposure, protects the bacteria from phagocytosis and antibody recognition, allowing it to persist in the host for lengthy periods.
*   **Host Defense & Immunodeficiency Links:** Tissue destruction is primarily the result of the host's immune response to the infection rather than direct bacterial toxicity.

#### Clinical Syndromes
*   **Primary Diseases:**
    *   **Primary Syphilis:** Localized infection at the site of inoculation characterized by a painless papule that ulcerates to form a "hard chancre".
    *   **Secondary Syphilis:** Systemic dissemination of the spirochetes resulting in a maculopapular rash (frequently involving the trunk and extremities), fever, lymphadenopathy, and mucosal lesions.
    *   **Latent Syphilis:** Clinically asymptomatic period characterized by the absence of symptoms but a persistently positive serologic test.
    *   **Tertiary Syphilis:** Late-stage, severe, and destructive disease characterized by central nervous system involvement (neurosyphilis), cardiovascular involvement (e.g., aortitis, aneurysms), and the formation of destructive granulomatous lesions called gummas in skin, bones, and cartilage.
    *   **Congenital Syphilis:** Transplacental transmission causing severe fetal infection, birth defects, or intrauterine death.
*   **Incubation Period:** N/A (Replication time is slow at ~30 hours; primary lesions typically develop weeks after exposure).
*   **Pathognomonic Signs:** The painless "hard chancre" of primary syphilis.
*   **Complications & Sequelae:** Neurosyphilis, cardiac failure, destructive gummas, and congenital malformations. Untreated syphilis in pregnancy causes severe adverse pregnancy outcomes.

#### Epidemiology & Transmission
*   **Reservoir:** Humans are the only natural host.
*   **Transmission Route:** Venereal transmission (direct sexual contact), congenital transmission (transplacental), and rarely through occupational exposure or blood transfusions (the organism can remain viable for 24 hours in whole blood/plasma stored at 4°C).
*   **Endemic Regions & Outbreaks:** Worldwide distribution without seasonal incidence. Highly prevalent with an estimated 6.3 million new cases among adults aged 15-49 worldwide in 2016.
*   **Public Health & Isolation:** Disease control relies on safe sex practices, widespread screening of pregnant women, and the notification and treatment of sexual contacts.

#### Advanced Laboratory Diagnostics
*   **Direct Visualization & Stains:** Too thin to be seen with standard brightfield microscopy, Gram, or Giemsa stains. Organisms from tissue fluid or early surface exudates are best visualized using dark-field microscopy or direct fluorescent antibody (DFA) testing, where they appear as actively motile corkscrews. Tissue sections can be visualized with silver impregnation methods. Dark-field examination should not be used for oral lesions because pathogenic treponemes cannot be morphologically distinguished from normal commensal oral spirochetes.
*   **Molecular Testing:** Real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification of specific treponemal DNA sequences from clinical samples or ulcers.
*   **Serology & Immunology:** The absolute mainstay of diagnosis because the organism cannot be cultured.
    *   **Nontreponemal Tests (Screening/Monitoring):** VDRL (Venereal Disease Research Laboratory) and RPR (Rapid Plasma Reagin). These detect non-specific antibodies directed against lipid antigens (cardiolipin) released from damaged host cells. Used to monitor treatment efficacy. CSF is tested with VDRL for neurosyphilis.
    *   **Treponemal Tests (Confirmatory):** TP-PA (Treponema pallidum particle agglutination), EIA (enzyme immunoassay), or CIA (chemiluminescence immunoassay). These measure specific antibodies against the bacteria and typically remain positive for life. Diagnostic algorithms often utilize a reverse sequence screening protocol starting with an EIA/CIA and confirming with an RPR.

#### Pharmacology, Prevention & Prophylaxis
*   **First-Line Treatment:** [[Penicillin]] (e.g., [[Penicillin G|Benzylpenicillin]], [[Penicillin G]]) is the definitive drug of choice for all stages of syphilis.
*   **Alternative / Second-Line Treatment:** [[Doxycycline]], [[ceftriaxone]], or [[azithromycin]] can be used as alternatives for patients allergic to [[penicillin]] (except in pregnant patients where [[penicillin]] desensitization is often required).
*   **Mechanisms of Resistance & Resistance Genes:** Highly susceptible to [[penicillin]] because its conserved, small genome lacks transposable elements to acquire resistance genes.
*   **Treatment Nuances & Contraindications:** Antibiotic treatment handles the active infection but does not reverse tissue damage sustained in late (tertiary) disease.
*   **Prophylaxis & Vaccine Protocols:** No vaccine is available. Prevention relies on condom use, sexual health education, and prophylactic treatment of exposed partners.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** "Hard chancre", "painless ulcer", "dark-field microscopy", "VDRL/RPR", "TP-PA", "corkscrew-shaped spirochete", "endoflagella/axial filaments".
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Despite physically possessing an outer membrane characteristic of Gram-negative bacteria, it completely lacks LPS. Although structurally a bacterium, it cannot be cultured on artificial media and lacks a tricarboxylic acid cycle, making it entirely dependent on its host for purines, pyrimidines, and amino acids.
