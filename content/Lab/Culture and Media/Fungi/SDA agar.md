### Definition and Classification

- **Classic Mycological Medium**: Sabouraud Dextrose Agar (SDA) is a traditional, enriched nonselective culture medium widely used for the primary isolation, cultivation, and morphological characterization of fungi, including yeasts and molds.
- **Baseline Identification Standard**: Morphologic characteristics of fungi used for taxonomic identification and species-level descriptions are conventionally defined based on growth observed on SDA.

---

### Composition and Biochemical Principles

- **Nutrient Formulation**: Formulated with glucose (dextrose) as a fermentable carbon and energy source, along with modified peptones that supply nitrogenous compounds, amino acids, and peptides required for fungal growth.
- **pH and Bacterial Suppression**: Prepared at a neutral pH (pH 7.0), which supports the growth of fungi while restricting the growth of many bacteria.
- **SABHI Enriched Variant**: SDA can be combined with Brain Heart Infusion (BHI) agar to formulate SABHI (Sabouraud dextrose and BHI) agar, an enriched medium recommended for recovering fastidious fungal pathogens from clinical samples.

---

### Selective Modifications (Selective SDA)

- **Antibacterial Incorporation**: When processing non-sterile clinical specimens contaminated with resident microbiota (such as skin, hair, nail scrapings, or respiratory samples), antibacterial antibiotics (e.g., chloramphenicol, gentamicin, or penicillin and streptomycin) are added to SDA to suppress bacterial overgrowth.
- **Antifungal Incorporation**: Cycloheximide is added alongside antibacterial agents to inhibit rapidly growing saprobic environmental molds, favoring the selective recovery of slower-growing dermatophytes and endemic dimorphic pathogens.
- **Paired Media Requirement**: Because cycloheximide inhibits certain opportunistic fungal pathogens (such as _Candida_ and _Aspergillus_ species), diagnostic protocols require pairing cycloheximide-containing media with complementary media lacking cycloheximide.

---

### Primary Diagnostic Applications

- **Dermatophyte Cultivation and Speciation**: Routinely used for isolating and identifying dermatophytes (_Trichophyton_, _Microsporum_, and _Epidermophyton_) from clinical skin, hair, and nail specimens incubated at room temperature (25°C) for 1 to 4 weeks.
- **Dimorphic and Opportunistic Fungi**: Cultivates the saprobic mold phases of thermally dimorphic pathogens (e.g., _Blastomyces dermatitidis_ and _Sporothrix schenckii_) as well as dematiaceous (melanized) molds and opportunistic yeasts.
- **Morphological Evaluation**: Facilitates the evaluation of diagnostic macroscopic colonial features—such as growth rate, surface texture (cottony, granular, or velvety), and pigmentation—alongside microscopic conidial morphology (macroconidia and microconidia).
