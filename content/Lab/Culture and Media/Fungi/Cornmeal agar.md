### Definition and Diagnostic Classification

- **Nutritionally Deficient Identification Medium**: Cornmeal agar is a simple, starvation-inducing culture medium used in diagnostic mycology primarily for the microscopic identification and differentiation of yeast species, specifically within the genus _Candida_.
- **Morphological Induction**: Unlike enriched nutrient media (such as Sabouraud dextrose agar) that promote rapid vegetative budding, the low nutrient density of cornmeal agar suppresses excessive vegetative growth and stimulates the formation of characteristic fungal structures, including hyphae, pseudohyphae, blastoconidia, and chlamydospores.

---

### Biochemical Principle and Mechanism

- **Nutritional Starvation**: The formulation contains a simple cornmeal infusion and agar without added soluble sugars.
- **Repression of Budding**: The scarcity of carbohydrates limits simple yeast cell replication (blastoconidia formation) and forces the fungal organism to undergo cellular elongation and structural differentiation.
- **Tween 80 Supplementation**: The addition of a non-ionic surfactant (such as polysorbate 80 / Tween 80) lowers surface tension, encouraging submerged growth beneath the agar surface and significantly enhancing the production of pseudohyphae, blastoconidia clusters, and terminal chlamydospores.

---

### Microscopic Diagnostic Features

When inoculated via a deep streak technique and covered with a sterile glass coverslip, cornmeal agar permits direct microscopic examination under low- and high-power magnification to evaluate four primary structures:

1. **True Hyphae**: Continuous, septate fungal filaments with parallel walls lacking constrictions.
2. **Pseudohyphae**: Chains of elongated, budding yeast cells that remain attached, exhibiting distinct pinches or constrictions at the septations between adjacent cells.
3. **Blastoconidia**: Clusters or arrangements of small, oval, budding yeast cells arranged along the length or at the septa of pseudohyphae.
4. **Chlamydospores (Chlamydoconidia)**: Large, thick-walled, spherical, highly resistant resting spores that develop terminally or laterally on pseudohyphae.

---

### Species Differentiation and Interpretation

- **_Candida albicans_ (and _Candida dubliniensis_)**:
    - Form abundant pseudohyphae and true hyphae bearing characteristic clusters of blastoconidia at the nodes.
    - Produce pathognomonic, large, thick-walled, terminal **chlamydospores**.
- _**Candida tropicalis**_:
    - Forms extensive, long, branching pseudohyphae with blastoconidia produced singly or in small groups randomly along the pseudohyphae; does not produce chlamydospores.
- _**Candida parapsilosis**_:
    - Produces short, curved pseudohyphae with a characteristic branched appearance ("sagebrush" or "matchstick" morphology) and small numbers of blastoconidia.
- _**Candida krusei**_:
    - Forms elongated, branched pseudohyphae resembling a "cross-match" pattern with blastoconidia budding laterally.
- _**Candida glabrata**_:
    - Uniquely fails to form pseudohyphae or hyphae on cornmeal agar; produces only small, tightly packed, budding yeast cells (blastoconidia).
