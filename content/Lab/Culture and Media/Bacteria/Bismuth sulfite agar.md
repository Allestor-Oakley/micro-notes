**Bismuth Sulfite Agar** is a highly selective and differential culture medium utilized in clinical microbiology for the isolation and preliminary identification of _Salmonella_ species—particularly _Salmonella enterica_ serovar Typhi (_Salmonella_ Typhi) and non-typhoidal salmonellae—from stool specimens and other clinical samples.

### Selective Function

- **Inhibitory Agents**: The incorporation of **bismuth sulfite** and **brilliant green** suppresses the growth of Gram-positive bacteria as well as most normal Gram-negative intestinal microbiota (such as _Escherichia coli_ and other coliforms).
- **Isolation from Polymicrobial Samples**: By suppressing competing commensal organisms, the medium allows _Salmonella_ pathogens to be recovered even when present in small numbers within fecal specimens.

### Differential Mechanism

- **Hydrogen Sulfide (\(\text{H}_2\text{S}\)) Production**: Most _Salmonella_ species produce \(\text{H}_2\text{S}\) during metabolic growth.
- **Bismuth Reduction**: The produced \(\text{H}_2\text{S}\) reacts with bismuth compounds in the medium, reducing bismuth sulfite to insoluble **bismuth sulfide**. This chemical reaction results in the formation of **black colonies** surrounded by a dark precipitate or metallic sheen.

### Colonial Appearance

- **_Salmonella_ Species**: Form characteristic **jet-black colonies**, frequently accompanied by a surrounding dark zone or metallic black halo caused by \(\text{H}_2\text{S}\) production and bismuth reduction.
- **Non-target Organisms**: Most non-_Salmonella_ species are completely inhibited. Rare surviving coliforms appear as green, brown, or dull colonies lacking the characteristic blackening.

### Clinical Workflow

- **Direct Plating & Subculture**: Used as a differential plate for direct specimen inoculation or following preliminary enrichment in selective liquid media (such as selenite F broth or tetrathionate broth) to enhance recovery rates from fecal specimens.
