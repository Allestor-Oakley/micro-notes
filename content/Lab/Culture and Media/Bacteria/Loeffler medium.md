**Loeffler medium** (or Loeffler's serum medium) is an enriched, nonselective culture medium used in diagnostic microbiology primarily for the rapid cultivation of _Corynebacterium diphtheriae_ and the demonstration of its characteristic cellular morphology.

### History and Composition

- **Historical Derivation**: Originally formulated by Friedrich Loeffler in 1884 to obtain pure cultures of the diphtheria bacillus (_Corynebacterium diphtheriae_).
- **Core Ingredients**: Formulated with animal serum (such as horse, beef, or sheep serum), nutrient broth (containing peptone, dextrose, and sodium chloride), and egg or coagulated serum base.
- **Preparation via Inspissation**: Solidified through inspissation—a process of slow heating at 80°C to 85°C—which coagulates serum proteins into a smooth slant without boiling or destroying heat-sensitive nutrients.

### Biochemical Mechanism and Diagnostic Purpose

- **Rapid Proliferation**: Allows _Corynebacterium_ species to grow significantly faster (yielding visible colonies within 8 to 24 hours) than competing upper respiratory tract organisms.
- **Morphological Development**: Preserves and highlights the pleomorphic cellular structure of _C. diphtheriae_, including its club-shaped ends and characteristic angular ("Chinese letter" or palisading) arrangement.
- **Metachromatic Granule Production**: Promotes the synthesis of intracellular inorganic polyphosphate storage reserves, known as **metachromatic granules** (Babès-Ernst granules).

### Staining and Microscopic Demonstration

- **Loeffler's Methylene Blue**: Bacterial smears prepared from Loeffler medium colonies are routinely stained with **Loeffler's methylene blue** or Albert's stain.
- **Microscopic Appearance**: Metachromatic granules absorb the dye intensely, appearing as deeply stained dark blue or reddish-purple beaded structures against the lighter blue bacterial cytoplasm.

### Diagnostic Utility and Clinical Workflow

- **Primary Specimen Processing**: Functions as a primary enrichment medium for nasopharyngeal and throat swabs collected from patients suspected of having respiratory diphtheria.
- **Nonselective Limitations**: Because Loeffler medium is nonselective, it does not suppress the growth of normal upper respiratory microbiota. Consequently, clinical laboratories inoculate it alongside selective, differential tellurite-based media (such as cystine-tellurite blood agar or Tinsdale medium) to isolate and identify _C. diphtheriae_.
