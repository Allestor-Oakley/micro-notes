**Simmons Citrate Agar** is a defined, differential culture medium used in diagnostic microbiology to determine whether a bacterium can utilize sodium citrate as its sole carbon source and ammonium salts as its sole nitrogen source.

---

### Definition and Diagnostic Purpose

- **Differential Classification**: Functions as a key biochemical test—traditionally included in the IMViC panel (Indole, Methyl Red, Voges-Proskauer, Citrate)—to differentiate Gram-negative rods, particularly within the family _Enterobacteriaceae_.
- **Defined Nutrient Source**: Formulated as a chemically defined medium containing **sodium citrate** as the exclusive carbon and energy source, along with **ammonium phosphate** (or inorganic ammonium salts) as the single nitrogen source.

---

### Biochemical Mechanism and Indicator System

- **Enzymatic Transport**: Organisms that grow on Simmons citrate agar possess the transport protein **citrate permease**, allowing them to import citrate into the cell to be converted into pyruvate and carbon dioxide.
- **Alkalinization**: The uptake of citrate and simultaneous breakdown of ammonium salts yield basic alkaline end-products (such as ammonia and sodium carbonate), elevating the pH of the surrounding medium.
- **pH Indicator (Bromothymol Blue)**:
    - **Uninoculated / Neutral pH (~6.9)**: The medium exhibits a **forest green** color.
    - **Alkaline pH (≥7.6)**: The indicator turns an **intense royal blue** as the medium becomes alkaline.

---

### Interpretation of Results

- **Positive Test Result**: Presence of bacterial growth accompanied by a color change from **green to deep blue** (or visible colony growth along the slant).
- **Negative Test Result**: **No growth** and no color change (medium remains green), indicating the organism cannot utilize citrate as its sole carbon source.

---

### Representative Bacterial Reactions

- **Citrate-Positive Organisms**:
    - _Klebsiella pneumoniae_
    - _Enterobacter_ species (e.g., _Enterobacter cloacae_)
    - _Citrobacter_ species (e.g., _Citrobacter freundii_)
    - _Serratia marcescens_
    - Most _Salmonella_ species
    - _Pseudomonas aeruginosa_
- **Citrate-Negative Organisms**:
    - _Escherichia coli_
    - _Shigella_ species

---

### Technical Considerations

- **Inoculation Technique**: The slant is lightly streaked on the surface using a needle rather than a loop to avoid transferring excess nutrient-rich media from primary culture, which could cause false-positive growth.
