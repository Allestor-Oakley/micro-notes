**Chocolate Agar**

### Definition and Classification

- **Enriched Nonselective Medium**: Chocolate agar is a complex, enriched, nonselective culture medium used routinely in diagnostic microbiology to isolate and cultivate fastidious bacteria.
- **Physical Appearance**: The medium derives its name from its characteristic dark brown, chocolate-like appearance, which results from the heat treatment of added blood or hemoglobin during preparation, not from actual chocolate.

### Preparation and Biochemical Mechanism

- **Lysis of Red Blood Cells**: Chocolate agar is prepared by adding blood (such as sheep or horse blood) or hemoglobin to a basal nutrient agar and gently heating the mixture.
- **Release of Essential Growth Factors**: Thermal lysis of the erythrocytes releases intracellular nutrients into the medium, specifically:
    - **Factor X (Hemin / Heme)**: An iron-containing porphyrin required for essential bacterial metabolic processes.
    - **Factor V (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide - NAD)**: An essential coenzyme required for bacterial oxidation-reduction reactions and cellular growth.
- **Inactivation of Heat-Labile Inhibitors**: Gentle heating destroys heat-labile enzymes present in unheated blood (such as intact sheep blood) that would otherwise degrade Factor V (NAD) and inhibit the growth of V-factor-dependent bacteria.

### Key Target Fastidious Pathogens

- _**Haemophilus influenzae**_:
    - Requires both Factor X (hemin) and Factor V (NAD) for growth.
    - Fails to grow on unheated sheep blood agar alone (unless growing as satellite colonies surrounding _Staphylococcus aureus_ streaks that lyse red blood cells and excrete NAD) because unheated blood contains active Factor V inhibitors.
    - Grows readily on chocolate agar, forming flat, grayish, translucent colonies 1 to 2 mm in diameter after 24 hours of incubation.
- **_Neisseria gonorrhoeae_ and _Neisseria meningitidis_**:
    - Fastidious Gram-negative diplococci that require enriched media containing heated blood or hemin for optimal growth.
    - _N. gonorrhoeae_ is sensitive to toxic fatty acids present in standard media; chocolate agar supplies complex organic nutrients and soluble starch to neutralize these inhibitory substances.
    - Optimal recovery on chocolate agar requires incubation at 35°C–37°C in a humid atmosphere supplemented with 5%–10% \(\text{CO}_2\).
- _**Francisella tularensis**_:
    - A fastidious Gram-negative coccobacillus requiring cysteine and hemin; grows reliably on cysteine-supplemented or standard chocolate agar.
- _**Other Microorganisms**_:
    - Supports the recovery of additional fastidious and non-fastidious organisms, including _Brucella_ species, _Pasteurella multocida_, _Moraxella catarrhalis_, and various streptococci.

### Selective Variations

- **Modified Thayer-Martin (MTM) and Related Agars**: Formulated using a chocolate agar base supplemented with selective antimicrobial agents (such as vancomycin to inhibit Gram-positive bacteria, colistin to inhibit Gram-negative bacteria, nystatin/amphotericin B to inhibit fungi, and trimethoprim). These additions suppress competing normal microbiota, allowing the selective isolation of pathogenic _Neisseria_ species from non-sterile clinical sites.
