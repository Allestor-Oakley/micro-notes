**5% Sheep Blood Agar** is a complex, enriched, nonselective, and differential growth medium routinely used in clinical microbiology laboratories for cultivating and identifying bacteria.

### Composition and Preparation

- **Basal Nutrient Medium**: Contains nutrient-rich agar with peptones, carbon sources, and essential salts to support bacterial metabolic needs.
- **Blood Supplementation**: Contains **5% intact sheep red blood cells (erythrocytes)** by volume.
- **Preparation Method**: The agar base is sterilized and melted at 100°C, then allowed to cool to approximately 45°C before the intact sheep red blood cells are added. Cooling to 45°C allows the blood cells to be uniformly suspended in liquid agar without thermal lysis or cell damage before the medium gels.

### Key Functions and Classification

1. **Enriched Nonselective Medium**: Provides complex nutrients and growth factors required by a broad spectrum of non-fastidious and moderately fastidious bacteria (such as species of _Staphylococcus_, _Streptococcus_, _Enterococcus_, and _Bacillus_).
2. **Differential Medium**: Differentiates bacterial colonies based on their **hemolytic activity**—the ability of bacterial extracellular enzymes (hemolysins) to lyse sheep erythrocytes in the surrounding agar.

### Hemolytic Patterns Observed on 5% Sheep Blood Agar

Bacteria grown on 5% sheep blood agar are categorized into three primary hemolytic patterns:

- **Beta (β)-Hemolysis**: Complete lysis and destruction of erythrocytes, resulting in a distinct, clear, transparent zone around the bacterial colonies.
    - _Examples_: _Streptococcus pyogenes_ (Group A strep), _Streptococcus agalactiae_ (Group B strep), and _Staphylococcus aureus_.
- **Alpha (α)-Hemolysis**: Incomplete or partial lysis of red blood cells accompanied by the reduction of hemoglobin, creating a greenish or brownish zone around the colonies.
    - _Examples_: _Streptococcus pneumoniae_ and viridans group streptococci.
- **Gamma (γ)-Hemolysis (Non-hemolytic)**: Absence of hemolysis; no clearing or color change occurs in the agar surrounding the growth.
    - _Examples_: _Bacillus anthracis_ and most _Enterococcus_ species.

### Diagnostic Applications

- **Routine Primary Isolation**: Serves as a standard agar plate for culturing clinical specimens such as throat swabs, wound aspirates, body fluids, and blood culture subcultures.
- **Species Identification**: Helps distinguish pathogenic organisms from closely related species, such as differentiating non-hemolytic _Bacillus anthracis_ from β-hemolytic _Bacillus cereus_.
- **Satellite Growth Phenomenon**: Fastidious bacteria like _Haemophilus influenzae_ cannot grow on unheated 5% sheep blood agar alone because the intact sheep blood contains heat-labile inhibitors of V-factor (NAD). However, when grown alongside _Staphylococcus aureus_, _H. influenzae_ forms tiny "satellite" colonies surrounding the _S. aureus_ streak because the staphylococci lyse the red blood cells and secrete NAD.
