**Campylobacter Blood Agar** (including formulations such as Campy-Blood agar, Skirrow's medium, Butzler's medium, and Preston medium) is a specialized, selective, and enriched culture medium designed for the primary isolation and recovery of thermophilic _Campylobacter_ species (predominantly _Campylobacter jejuni_ and _Campylobacter coli_) from clinical stool specimens containing dense normal intestinal microbiota.

---

### Composition and Mechanism of Components

- **Nutrient Base and Blood Enrichment**: Uses a nutrient-rich base (such as Columbia Blood Agar base) supplemented with blood (intact, lysed, or defibrinated sheep or horse blood). Blood provides essential growth factors and acts as an oxygen-quenching agent to neutralize toxic oxygen radicals that inhibit fastidious campylobacters.
- **Selective Antimicrobials**: Incorporates antibiotic combinations to suppress competing commensal bacteria (Gram-positive flora, coliforms, and enteric rods) and fungi present in fecal specimens.
    - _Skirrow's Formulation_: Uses vancomycin (inhibits Gram-positive organisms), polymyxin B (inhibits Gram-negative bacteria), and trimethoprim (inhibits enteric rods).
    - _Other Formulations_: May incorporate cephalosporins, colistin, or additional antifungal/antibacterial agents.
    - _Selectivity Limitations_: Antimicrobial agents in standard _Campylobacter_ selective media can inhibit sensitive non-target species, such as _Campylobacter upsaliensis_.

---

### Cultivation and Incubation Parameters

- **Microaerophilic Environment**: Optimal bacterial growth requires a microaerophilic atmosphere containing reduced oxygen and elevated carbon dioxide (**5%–7% O₂ and 5%–10% CO₂**).
- **Elevated Temperature**: Inoculated plates are incubated at an elevated temperature of **42°C**. This thermophilic temperature selects for pathogenic _Campylobacter_ species (_C. jejuni_ and _C. coli_) while suppressing competing intestinal commensals that cannot proliferate at 42°C.
- **Incubation Duration**: Because campylobacters multiply at a slower rate than most enteric bacteria, culture plates generally require **48 hours or longer** of incubation for visible colony development.

---

### Colonial Appearance and Handling Precautions

- **Colonial Morphology**: Colonies are nonhemolytic and typically appear gray or colorless. They may present either as flat, watery, spreading colonies or as round, convex, mucoid colonies.
- **Light Sensitivity**: Uninoculated and inoculated media must be protected from direct light exposure prior to and during processing, as free radicals generated by ultraviolet irradiation rapidly kill _Campylobacter_ organisms.
