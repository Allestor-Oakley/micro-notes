**Bordet-Gengou agar** is an enriched, specialized, and selective culture medium designed for the primary isolation and cultivation of _Bordetella pertussis_ and _Bordetella parapertussis_, the causative agents of whooping cough (pertussis).

### Base Composition and Formulation

- **Potato-Glycerol Agar Base**: Utilizes a potato infusion with glycerol as the basal nutrient medium, supplying starch and carbon sources necessary for bacterial cell growth.
- **Blood Supplementation**: Formulated with a high concentration of fresh blood (typically sheep or horse blood).
- **Selective Antimicrobials**: Frequently incorporates low concentrations of selective antibiotics, such as penicillin G (0.5 µg/mL) or cephalexin, to suppress competing normal respiratory microbiota.

### Primary Function and Mechanism

- **Detoxification of Media Inhibitors**: _Bordetella pertussis_ is exceptionally fastidious and highly sensitive to toxic fatty acids, peroxides, and metabolic byproducts present in standard culture media. The starch (from the potato base) and blood proteins bind and neutralize these inhibitory substances to allow bacterial multiplication.
- **Selective Recovery**: Added antimicrobials inhibit the overgrowth of fast-growing commensal flora present in nasopharyngeal clinical specimens.

### Diagnostic Utility and Cultivation Parameters

- **Specimen Inoculation**: Inoculated using nasopharyngeal (NP) swabs, NP aspirates, or nasal washings collected from patients suspected of having pertussis.
- **Incubation Conditions**: Culture plates are incubated aerobically at 35°C–37°C in a moist, humidified environment.
- **Growth Rate and Identification**: _B. pertussis_ grows slowly, with visible colonies typically developing after 3 to 7 days of incubation. Isolates appear as small, faintly staining Gram-negative coccobacilli that are identified via direct immunofluorescence staining or slide agglutination.
- **Modern Alternatives**: While freshly prepared Bordet-Gengou agar is historically significant, charcoal-based media (such as Regan-Lowe medium containing horse blood and cephalexin) are widely used in contemporary laboratories due to their longer shelf life and improved recovery efficiency.
