**Regan-Lowe Agar** is an enriched, selective culture medium formulated specifically for the primary isolation, cultivation, and transport of _Bordetella pertussis_ and _Bordetella parapertussis_, the causative agents of whooping cough (pertussis).

---

### Composition and Role of Key Ingredients

- **Activated Charcoal Base**: _Bordetella pertussis_ is an extremely fastidious organism that is highly sensitive to toxic metabolites, fatty acids, and trace peroxides commonly present in standard media. Activated charcoal functions as a detoxifying agent that absorbs these inhibitory substances to permit bacterial proliferation.
- **Defibrinated Horse Blood**: Enriches the medium by supplying nutrients, amino acids, and growth-stimulating factors required for the multiplication of fastidious bordetellae.
- **Selective Antimicrobials (Cephalexin & Amphotericin B)**: Incorporates cephalexin to suppress competing normal respiratory microbiota (such as Gram-positive cocci and commensal flora) and amphotericin B to prevent fungal overgrowth in clinical specimens.

---

### Advantages Over Traditional Media

- **Extended Shelf Life**: Regan-Lowe agar offers a significantly longer shelf life and greater chemical stability than traditional potato-glycerol-blood (Bordet-Gengou) agar, making it the preferred culture medium in diagnostic laboratories.
- **Transport Capability**: Functions effectively both as a selective isolation medium and as a transport/holding medium for nasopharyngeal specimens during transit to the laboratory.

---

### Specimen Processing and Cultivation Parameters

- **Specimen Collection**: Inoculated using nasopharyngeal (NP) aspirates, NP swabs, or nasal washings. Swabs must have Dacron or rayon tips; calcium alginate swabs (which inhibit PCR testing) and cotton swabs (which contain toxic fatty acids that kill _B. pertussis_) are strictly contraindicated.
- **Incubation Conditions**: Inoculated plates are incubated aerobically at 35°C–37°C in a moist, humidified environment (such as a sealed plastic bag).
- **Growth Rate and Colonial Appearance**: Due to slow bacterial growth, cultures require prolonged incubation for 3 to 7 days before being reported as negative. Mature colonies of _B. pertussis_ appear small, raised, glistening, and dome-shaped with a characteristic metallic or "mercury droplet" appearance.
