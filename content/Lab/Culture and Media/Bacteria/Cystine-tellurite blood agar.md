**Cystine-tellurite blood agar (CTBA)** is a selective and differential culture medium used for the primary isolation and preliminary identification of _Corynebacterium diphtheriae_ from clinical specimens, such as nasopharyngeal and throat swabs.

### Composition and Mechanisms

- **Base Enrichment**: Formulated with an enriched blood agar base that supplies complex growth factors required by _Corynebacterium_ species.
- **Potassium Tellurite (Selective Agent)**: Inhibits the growth of most normal upper respiratory tract flora, Gram-negative rods, and competing organisms.
- **Tellurite Reduction (Differential Mechanism)**: _Corynebacterium diphtheriae_ intracellularly reduces potassium tellurite to metallic tellurite, producing characteristic gray to black colonies.
- **Cystine Addition**: Serves as an amino acid substrate; the cysteinase activity of _C. diphtheriae_ degrades cysteine, forming a distinctive brown halo surrounding the black colonies.

### Diagnostic Utility and Incubation

- **Specimen Inoculation**: Inoculated alongside a nonselective medium (such as blood agar) when evaluating patients suspected of having respiratory or cutaneous diphtheria.
- **Incubation Parameters**: Culture plates are incubated at 37°C (typically in an atmosphere of 5% \(\text{CO}_2\)).
- **Observation Timeline**: Plates are inspected at 18 to 24 hours; characteristic black colonies surrounded by brown halos become sufficiently definite for recognizing _C. diphtheriae_ within 36 to 48 hours.

### Advantages and Limitations

- **Practical Shelf Life**: CTBA has a long shelf life, making it convenient for clinical laboratories that infrequently perform diphtheria cultures.
- **Inhibitory Potential**: CTBA can inhibit the growth of certain strains of _C. diphtheriae_.
- **Comparison to Tinsdale Medium**: Tinsdale medium provides superior recovery of _C. diphtheriae_, but it has a short shelf life and requires horse serum supplementation.
- **Requirement for Toxigenicity Testing**: Growth and blackening on CTBA provide only presumptive identification; all isolates resembling _C. diphtheriae_ must undergo further biochemical testing and exotoxin confirmation (e.g., Elek immunodiffusion test or PCR for the _tox_ gene) because non-toxigenic strains also grow on tellurite media.
