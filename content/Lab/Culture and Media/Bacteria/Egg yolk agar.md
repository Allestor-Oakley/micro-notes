### Definition and Composition

- **Enriched Complex Medium**: Egg yolk agar is an enriched, complex differential culture medium supplemented with egg yolk, supplying complex organic substances, lipids (such as lecithin), and essential nutrients.
- **Diagnostic Purpose**: It is primarily employed in diagnostic microbiology to detect specific bacterial extracellular enzymes and cultivate fastidious organisms.

---

### Differential Mechanisms and Reactions

1. **Lecithinase Activity**:
    
    - **Biochemical Reaction**: Bacterial lecithinase (phospholipase C) hydrolyzes lecithin present in egg yolk, splitting it into diglyceride and phosphorylcholine.
    - **Visual Result**: Lecithinase-producing bacteria form an insoluble **opaque precipitate** (zone of opacity or clearing) in the agar directly surrounding the colonies.
2. **Proteolytic Activity**:
    
    - **Biochemical Reaction**: Protease-producing bacteria hydrolyze and degrade protein substrates in egg media.
    - **Visual Result**: Proteolysis is indicated by visible zones of clearing or degradation surrounding bacterial growth.

---

### Primary Clinical Applications and Target Microorganisms

- **_Clostridium_ Species**: Evaluation of lecithinase activity on egg yolk media is a key diagnostic test for identifying invasive and toxigenic clostridia. Specifically, _Clostridium perfringens_ produces a major alpha toxin that acts as a potent lecithinase, creating a distinct precipitate around colonies.
- **_Helicobacter_ Species**: Fastidious bacteria such as _Helicobacter pylori_ can be cultured on complex media enriched with egg yolk, blood, serum, charcoal, or starch under microaerophilic conditions.
- **Egg-Based Media Formulations**: Related inspissated egg media (such as Löwenstein-Jensen medium, containing fresh eggs or egg yolks, defined salts, glycerol, and potato flour) are used for cultivating _Mycobacterium_ species.
