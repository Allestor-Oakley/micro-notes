**Cooked Meat Broth** (also referred to as **Robertson's Cooked Meat Medium** or **Chopped Meat Medium**) is an enriched, complex liquid culture medium used for the isolation, cultivation, long-term maintenance, and biochemical differentiation of obligate anaerobic bacteria, particularly species within the genus _Clostridium_.

### Composition and Biochemical Principles

- **Meat/Tissue Granules**: Formulated using cooked beef heart muscle tissue suspended in a nutrient broth base.
- **Anaerobic Microenvironment**: Thermal denaturation of meat proteins during preparation exposes free sulfhydryl (-SH) groups and unsaturated fatty acids. These compounds function as reducing agents that scavenge oxygen and maintain a low oxidation-reduction potential (\(E_h\)) in the deeper portions of the tube, creating an anaerobic environment required for anaerobic proliferation.
- **Nutrient Enrichment**: Supplies tissue proteins, amino acids, peptides, organic carbon, nitrogen, and essential growth factors needed to support fastidious microorganisms.
- **Carbohydrate Supplementation**: Glucose is frequently added (chopped meat–glucose medium) to serve as a fermentable carbon and energy source for saccharolytic bacteria.

### Metabolic and Differential Functions

Cooked meat broth differentiates anaerobic bacteria—specifically _Clostridium_ species—based on their metabolic capabilities:

- **Proteolytic Activity**: Proteolytic species digest tissue proteins, resulting in the **blackening and decomposition** of the meat particles accompanied by the formation of foul-smelling putrefactive by-products.
    - _Examples_: Proteolytic clostridia, including _Clostridium tetani_, _Clostridium sporogenes_, and _Clostridium histolyticum_.
- **Saccharolytic Activity**: Saccharolytic species ferment carbohydrates without decomposing muscle tissue, causing the meat particles to turn **pink or reddened** with active gas production and acid formation, without blackening.
    - _Examples_: Saccharolytic clostridia, including _Clostridium perfringens_ and _Clostridium septicum_.

### Diagnostic Utility

- **Primary Specimen Isolation**: Functions as a primary liquid enrichment broth for deep-tissue biopsies, wound exudates, pus, and body fluids suspected of containing anaerobic pathogens.
- **Enrichment for Subculture**: Supports the multiplication of small bacterial inocula, facilitating subculture onto solid media such as anaerobically incubated blood agar plates.
- **Culture Maintenance**: Maintains viable stock cultures of anaerobic bacteria over extended periods due to its reducing environment and nutrient density.
