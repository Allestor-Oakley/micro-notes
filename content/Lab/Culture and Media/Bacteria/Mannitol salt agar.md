### Definition and Classification

- **Selective and Differential Medium**: Mannitol salt agar is a specialized culture medium used for the selective isolation, recovery, and differentiation of staphylococci, specifically _Staphylococcus aureus_, from clinical specimens containing mixed or contaminating microbiota.

---

### Composition and Biochemical Mechanisms

- **Selective Agent (7.5% Sodium Chloride)**: MSA is formulated with **7.5% sodium chloride (NaCl)**. This high salt concentration serves as a selective agent that inhibits the growth of most other normal microbiota and non-staphylococcal organisms, while allowing salt-tolerant staphylococci to grow.
- **Differential Substrate (Mannitol)**: The medium incorporates the carbohydrate **mannitol** as a differential substrate.

---

### Differential Principles and Identification

- _**Staphylococcus aureus**_: _S. aureus_ ferments mannitol, which differentiates it from most other staphylococcal species.
- **Other Staphylococci**: Other staphylococci (such as coagulase-negative species) generally grow on the high-salt medium but do not ferment mannitol.

---

### Clinical Applications

- **Selective Recovery from Mixed Specimens**: Facilitates the recovery of _S. aureus_ from specimens contaminated with a mixed microbiota, such as wound aspirates or respiratory specimens from patients with cystic fibrosis.
- **Carriage Screening**: Employed in clinical and epidemiological settings to screen individuals for nasal carriage of _S. aureus_.
