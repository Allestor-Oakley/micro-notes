### Levamisole
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class:**
    *   Synthetic imidazothiazole derivative; biological response modifier (specifically, an immunostimulant) and broad-spectrum anthelmintic agent.
*   **Target Cytokine, Receptor, or Immune Cell:**
    *   Nematode nicotinic acetylcholine receptors on muscle cells (parasitic target).
    *   Host T lymphocytes (CD4+ helper T cells and CD8+ cytotoxic T cells), macrophages, and neutrophils (immunological targets).
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    *   **Anthelmintic Action:** Levamisole acts as a selective and highly potent agonist at nematode nicotinic acetylcholine receptors located on parasitic muscle membranes. Binding to these receptors opens nonselective cation channels, promoting a rapid influx of sodium ions and resulting in depolarization. This induces persistent activation and spastic paralysis of the worm. Deprived of motility, the paralyzed helminths detach from the host's gastrointestinal tract and are subsequently expelled alive in the feces through normal intestinal peristaltic movements.
    *   **Immunomodulatory Action:** Levamisole is a thymomimetic agent that restores depressed cell-mediated immune function by mimicking the physiological maturation and activation properties of the thymic hormone thymopoietin. At the cellular level, the imidazole component of levamisole stimulates cyclic GMP (cGMP) production and decreases cyclic AMP (cAMP) levels, bringing leukocyte and lymphocyte reactivity back to normal physiological baselines. Additionally, levamisole is metabolized in vivo into OMPI (dl-2-oxo-3-(2-mercaptoethyl)-5-phenylimidazolidine), a pharmacologically active sulfhydryl compound. OMPI acts as an efficient free radical scavenger and antioxidant, protecting multiplying host lymphocytes from oxidative stress and maintaining leukocyte membrane integrity during active immune responses.
*   **Downstream Immunological Effects:**
    *   **Restoration of Depressed Cellular Immunity:** Restores defective T-lymphocyte and macrophage activity back to normal baselines without hyper-stimulating immune functions beyond normal limits in healthy hosts.
    *   **Phagocytic Activation:** Significantly enhances leukocyte chemotaxis, phagocytosis, and lysosomal enzyme activity in neutrophils and macrophages, boosting host phagocytic clearance of microbial invaders.
    *   **T-Cell Clonal Expansion and Maturation:** Stimulates the proliferation, differentiation, and maturation of naive T cells into active helper T (CD4+) and cytotoxic CD8+ T lymphocytes.
    *   **Th1 Cytokine Polarization:** Promotes the production and release of interleukin-2 (IL-2), interferon-gamma, and other Th1-type cytokines, activating host cell-mediated defenses.
    *   **Adjuvant Activity:** Acts as an immunological adjuvant, enhancing host antibody and cellular responses to concomitant vaccination or tumor antigens.

#### Pharmacokinetics & Administration
*   **Route of Administration:**
    *   Oral (rapidly and almost completely absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract).
*   **Metabolism & Clearance Pathway:**
    *   Extensively metabolized in the liver (first-pass metabolism) to multiple metabolites, including p-hydroxylevamisole and the active sulfhydryl metabolite OMPI. Clearance is primarily renal, with over 60% of the administered dose excreted as metabolites in the urine within 24 hours. A small percentage is eliminated via the feces.
*   **Onset of Action & Half-Life:**
    *   **Onset:** Reaches peak plasma concentrations (Cmax) rapidly within 1 to 2 hours after oral ingestion.
    *   **Half-Life:** Relatively short plasma elimination half-life of approximately 4 hours (ranging between 3 and 5 hours).
*   **Therapeutic Drug Monitoring Required:**
    *   Routine monitoring of serum levamisole levels is not standard. However, strict hematologic monitoring is mandatory:
        1.  **Complete Blood Count (CBC) with Differential:** Must be checked at baseline and weekly during therapy to detect early, severe drug-induced neutropenia.
        2.  **Hepatic and Renal Function Panels:** Assessed at baseline and periodically to monitor clearance capacity and detect hepatotoxicity.

#### Clinical Indications
*   **Primary Autoimmune / Inflammatory Indications:**
    *   **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Historically utilized as a disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD) to normalize cellular immunity and manage joint inflammation, but this use was abandoned due to its severe toxicity profile.
    *   **Pediatric Nephrotic Syndrome:** Used off-label in some international regions as steroid-sparing therapy for children with steroid-dependent or frequently-relapsing nephrotic syndrome to maintain clinical remission.
    *   **Recurrent Aphthous Stomatitis:** Historically used to treat recurrent, severe aphthous ulcers of the mucosal tissue.
*   **Oncology Indications:**
    *   **Adjuvant Therapy for Colon Cancer:** FDA-approved and widely used in the 1990s in combination with 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) as adjuvant chemotherapy for Dukes' C (Stage III) colon cancer after primary surgical resection. This combination significantly improved disease-free and overall survival rates. It has since been replaced by modern, more effective, and less toxic regimens (e.g., FOLFOX).
*   **Transplant Rejection / Prophylaxis:**
    *   N/A (Contraindicated in transplant recipients due to the risk of triggering acute graft rejection through immune stimulation).

#### Adverse Effects & Toxicity
*   **Infection Risks & Opportunistic Pathogens:**
    *   **Neutropenia-Related Sepsis:** The most critical risk is drug-induced, immune-mediated agranulocytosis. Severe neutropenia (absolute neutrophil count < 500/microliter) dramatically increases the host's vulnerability to life-threatening bacterial infections, sepsis, and opportunistic pathogens.
*   **Malignancy Risks:**
    *   N/A
*   **Organ-Specific Toxicity:**
    *   **Hematologic:** Severe, idiosyncratic, and potentially fatal agranulocytosis and thrombocytopenia.
    *   **Neurotoxicity (Multifocal Inflammatory Leukoencephalopathy - MIL):** A serious, delayed inflammatory demyelinating disease of the CNS. MIL is characterized by gait ataxia, dysarthria, dysphagia, confusion, paresthesias, and multiple white matter lesions on brain MRI. It typically occurs several weeks into therapy and requires immediate cessation of the drug and treatment with high-dose corticosteroids or plasmapheresis.
    *   **Dermatologic / Vasculitic (Levamisole-Induced Vasculopathy):** Characterized by retiform purpura, painful skin necrosis (frequently affecting the earlobes, nose, cheeks, and extremities), neutropenia, and positive titers for anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies (p-ANCA and c-ANCA) and antinuclear antibodies (ANA). Endothelial damage, vascular thrombosis, and immune complex deposition drive this pathology.
    *   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea, metallic taste, stomatitis, and chemical pancreatitis.
    *   **Constitutional:** Severe influenza-like syndrome consisting of fever, chills, myalgias, arthralgias, and fatigue, which typically resolve upon drug withdrawal.
    *   **Hepatic:** Mild, reversible elevations in serum transaminases and bilirubin.
*   **Black Box Warnings:**
    *   **Agranulocytosis:** Severe and potentially fatal agranulocytosis can occur abruptly. CBC with differential must be performed at baseline and weekly during treatment. The drug must be discontinued immediately if the leukocyte count falls below 3,000 cells/mm3 or if neutropenia develops.
    *   **Neurotoxicity:** Associated with the development of severe demyelinating multifocal inflammatory leukoencephalopathy. Promptly evaluate any patient presenting with new-onset neurological deficits (ataxia, dysarthria, dysphagia), discontinue levamisole permanently, and initiate supportive corticosteroid therapy.

#### Pre-Screening & Contraindications
*   **Required Pre-Treatment Screening:**
    *   **Complete Blood Count with Differential:** Establish baseline white blood cell and absolute neutrophil counts to rule out pre-existing cytopenias.
    *   **Liver and Renal Panels:** Evaluate baseline liver enzymes, bilirubin, serum creatinine, and BUN.
    *   **Pregnancy Screening:** Negative serum pregnancy test is required within 24 hours of starting therapy for women of childbearing potential.
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    *   Severe baseline cytopenias or bone marrow suppression.
    *   Severe hepatic impairment.
    *   Known immediate hypersensitivity to levamisole or any formulation excipients.
    *   History of levamisole-induced neurotoxicity or vasculopathy.
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:**
    *   **Pregnancy Category:** Category C. Animal models demonstrate embryotoxicity and fetotoxicity at maternal doses. Levamisole is contraindicated in pregnant women, and double-method contraception is required for female patients of childbearing potential.
    *   **Lactation:** Breastfeeding is contraindicated as levamisole is excreted in breast milk and poses a risk of agranulocytosis to nursing infants.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    *   **"Thymomimetic Agent":** Reflects its capacity to restore cellular immunity by mimicking the biological actions of the thymic hormone thymopoietin.
    *   **"Imidazothiazole Derivative":** Chemical descriptor of its unique bicyclic ring structure.
    *   **"Cocaine Adulterant Vasculitis":** In the 2000s and 2010s, levamisole became infamous as a cutting agent (adulterant) in up to 70-80% of illicit cocaine smuggled into North America and Europe. This practice led to a public health epidemic of cocaine-levamisole-induced vasculopathy, characterized by necrotic purpura on the ears and nose, agranulocytosis, and positive p-ANCA titers in cocaine users.
*   **Historical Milestones:**
    *   **Discovery:** Discovered in 1966 by Janssen Pharmaceutica as a veterinary and human anthelmintic agent.
    *   **Oncology Era:** Gained FDA approval in 1990 in combination with 5-fluorouracil as the first successful adjuvant therapy for Dukes' C colon cancer.
    *   **Market Withdrawal:** Withdrawn from the US and Canadian human pharmaceutical markets in 1999-2000 due to the high risk of severe agranulocytosis and the availability of superior alternative therapies.
