### Infliximab
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class:**
    *   Chimeric IgG1-kappa monoclonal antibody; tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-alpha) inhibitor; biologic disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (bDMARD).
*   **Target Cytokine, Receptor, or Immune Cell:**
    *   Directly targets and neutralizes soluble homotrimeric (sTNF-alpha) and transmembrane (mTNF-alpha) forms of Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha (TNF-alpha).
    *   Inhibits downstream signaling mediated by the p55 (TNFR1) and p75 (TNFR2) cell surface TNF receptors.
    *   Targets activated macrophages, T lymphocytes, and other immune cells expressing surface transmembrane TNF-alpha.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    *   **High-Affinity Binding & Neutralization:** Infliximab binds specifically and with high affinity to both soluble (free-floating) and transmembrane (cell-surface) forms of the pro-inflammatory cytokine TNF-alpha. This stable, high-affinity binding sterically blocks the interaction of TNF-alpha with its functional cell-surface receptors (TNFR1 and TNFR2), preventing the initiation of receptor-mediated intracellular signaling.
    *   **Complement-Dependent Cytotoxicity (CDC):** Through its human IgG1 Fc constant region, infliximab activates the classical complement pathway when bound to cells presenting transmembrane TNF-alpha (such as activated macrophages and lamina propria mononuclear T cells). This triggers the assembly of the membrane attack complex (MAC), resulting in rapid complement-mediated osmotic lysis of these pathologic inflammatory cells.
    *   **Antibody-Dependent Cellular Cytotoxicity (ADCC):** The Fc region of bound infliximab engages Fc-gamma receptors (FcγR) on host effector cells, primarily natural killer (NK) cells and macrophages. This interaction stimulates the release of cytolytic perforin and granzymes, causing granzyme-mediated cell apoptosis and receptor-mediated phagocytosis of the target cells.
    *   **Outside-In Reverse Signaling & Apoptosis:** Binding of infliximab to transmembrane TNF-alpha on activated macrophages and T lymphocytes triggers outside-in reverse signaling. This cascade halts cell cycle progression and directly induces apoptosis of these crucial mediators of chronic autoimmune inflammation.
    *   **Selectivity:** Infliximab is highly selective for TNF-alpha and does not bind to or neutralize lymphotoxin-alpha (TNF-beta), preserving lymphotoxin's distinct immunoprotective and tissue-organizing properties.
*   **Downstream Immunological Effects:**
    *   **Suppression of Inflammatory Cascade:** Complete neutralization of TNF-alpha disrupts downstream signaling pathways (specifically the NF-κB and MAPK pathways). This leads to a rapid and sustained downregulation of other critical pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and mediators, including Interleukin-1 (IL-1), Interleukin-6 (IL-6), Interleukin-8 (IL-8), and Interferon-gamma (IFN-γ).
    *   **Endothelial Stabilization & Adhesion Blockade:** Decreases the expression of cellular and vascular adhesion molecules on endothelial cells, including E-selectin (ELAM-1), VCAM-1, and ICAM-1. This disrupts leukocyte-endothelial interactions, preventing the recruitment, rolling, adhesion, and migration of activated lymphocytes, neutrophils, and monocytes into inflamed joints (synovial tissue) and the intestinal mucosa.
    *   **Inhibition of Angiogenesis:** Reduces levels of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), suppressing pathologic angiogenesis and synovial tissue hyperplasia.
    *   **Restoration of Matrix Homeostasis:** Decreases the synthesis and release of matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs, notably MMP-1 and MMP-3) by chondrocytes and synovial fibroblasts. This halts the progressive degradation of cartilage, joint remodeling, and bone destruction in rheumatoid arthritis, and restores the balance between MMPs and tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs).
    *   **Regulatory T Cell (Treg) Induction:** Promotes the survival, expansion, and functional stability of CD4+CD25+FoxP3+ regulatory T cells (Tregs), helping to restore peripheral self-tolerance.
    *   **Mucosal Healing:** Promotes rapid epithelial cell barrier repair and structural healing of deep ulcers in the mucosal lining of the gastrointestinal tract in patients with inflammatory bowel disease.

#### Pharmacokinetics & Administration
*   **Route of Administration:**
    *   Intravenous (IV) infusion (administered slowly over a standard 2-hour period under direct clinical supervision). A subcutaneous (SC) maintenance formulation is also available under specific biosimilars (CT-P13), which provides steadier plasma concentrations.
*   **Metabolism & Clearance Pathway:**
    *   **Reticuloendothelial Catabolism:** As a high-molecular-weight chimeric IgG1 monoclonal antibody (approximately 149 kDa), infliximab is not filtered by the kidneys or metabolized by Cytochrome P450 hepatic enzymes. Instead, it undergoes non-specific endocytosis and is degraded into small peptides and amino acids by lysosomal proteases and peptidases in the reticuloendothelial system and tissues.
    *   **FcRn-Mediated Recycling:** The human IgG1 Fc component binds to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) within vascular endothelial cells, protecting the antibody from intracellular degradation and recycling it back into the systemic circulation, which prolongs its survival in the bloodstream.
    *   **Clearance:** Systemic clearance is extremely low, averaging approximately 11 to 15 mL/hour (or ~0.35 L/day). Clearance is significantly increased in the presence of anti-drug antibodies (antibodies to infliximab, ATI), low serum albumin, high body weight, and severe systemic inflammation (high C-reactive protein, CRP).
*   **Onset of Action & Half-Life:**
    *   **Onset of Action:** Rapid. Significant reductions in systemic acute-phase reactants of inflammation (CRP and ESR) and clinical improvement are typically observed within 24 to 72 hours in Crohn's disease, and within 1 to 2 weeks in rheumatoid arthritis.
    *   **Elimination Half-Life:** The median terminal elimination half-life is approximately 7 to 14 days (averaging ~9.5 days). No significant drug accumulation is typically observed during standard maintenance dosing (every 8 weeks), demonstrating linear pharmacokinetics over the recommended dose range.
*   **Therapeutic Drug Monitoring Required:**
    *   Highly recommended in clinical practice, particularly in cases of secondary loss of response, to guide dose optimization (dose escalation or interval shortening).
    *   **Whole Blood/Serum Assay:** Measured via enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) or radioimmunoassay on serum drawn immediately before the next scheduled infusion (trough level).
    *   **Target Trough Concentrations:**
        - In inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's and UC): Target trough levels of at least 3 to 5 mcg/mL (preferably >5 mcg/mL, and >7.5 mcg/mL in active fistulizing disease) are strongly associated with clinical remission and mucosal healing.
        - In rheumatoid arthritis: Target trough levels of at least 1 mcg/mL are associated with response.
    *   **Anti-Drug Antibodies (ATI):** Patients can develop high-titer antibodies to infliximab (ATI), which neutralize the drug's binding capacity and markedly increase its clearance. Concomitant immunosuppressive therapy (e.g., methotrexate) significantly reduces the incidence of ATI development.

#### Clinical Indications
*   **Primary Autoimmune / Inflammatory Indications:**
    *   **Crohn's Disease (CD):** Reducing signs and symptoms, inducing and maintaining clinical remission, and promoting mucosal healing in adult and pediatric patients (6 years of age and older) with moderately to severely active CD who have had an inadequate response to conventional therapy.
    *   **Fistulizing Crohn's Disease:** Reducing the number of active enterocutaneous or rectovaginal fistulas and maintaining fistula closure in adult patients.
    *   **Ulcerative Colitis (UC):** Reducing signs and symptoms, inducing and maintaining clinical remission and mucosal healing, and eliminating corticosteroid use in adult and pediatric patients (6 years of age and older) with moderately to severely active UC who have had an inadequate response to conventional therapy.
    *   **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):** In combination with methotrexate, reducing signs and symptoms, inhibiting the progression of structural joint damage, and improving physical function in patients with moderately to severely active RA.
    *   **Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS):** Reducing signs and symptoms in patients with active AS.
    *   **Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA):** Reducing signs and symptoms, inhibiting the progression of structural damage of active arthritis, and improving physical function in patients with active PsA.
    *   **Plaque Psoriasis (PsO):** Treatment of adult patients with chronic, severe, plaque psoriasis who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy.
*   **Oncology Indications:**
    *   N/A
*   **Transplant Rejection / Prophylaxis:**
    *   N/A

#### Adverse Effects & Toxicity
*   **Infection Risks & Opportunistic Pathogens:**
    *   **Serious Systemic Infections:** Potent blockade of TNF-alpha compromises host cell-mediated immunity. This dramatically increases susceptibility to serious, life-threatening infections leading to hospitalization or death.
    *   **Opportunistic Pathogens:** Highly elevated risk of:
        - **Tuberculosis:** Reactivation of latent TB, which frequently presents as active, disseminated, or extrapulmonary TB.
        - **Invasive Fungal Infections:** Disseminated histoplasmosis, coccidioidomycosis, blastomycosis, aspergillosis, and candidiasis.
        - **Bacterial Sepsis & Pneumonia:** Rapidly progressive systemic bacterial infections.
        - **Viral Reactivations:** Highly increased risk of Hepatitis B virus (HBV) reactivation in chronic carriers (potentially causing hepatic failure or death), Varicella Zoster Virus (shingles) reactivation, and Herpes Simplex Virus flares.
*   **Malignancy Risks:**
    *   **Pediatric and Adolescent Malignancies:** Malignancies, some fatal, have been reported in children and adolescent patients treated with TNF blockers.
    *   **Lymphoma:** Increased risk of developing Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma compared to the general population.
    *   **Hepatosplenic T-Cell Lymphoma (HSTCL):** A rare, extremely aggressive, and almost universally fatal T-cell lymphoma. It has occurred predominantly in adolescent and young adult male patients with inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's or UC) treated with the combination of infliximab and concomitant thiopurines (azathioprine or 6-mercaptopurine).
    *   **Skin Cancers:** Elevated risk of non-melanoma skin cancer (squamous cell and basal cell carcinoma) and melanoma.
*   **Organ-Specific Toxicity:**
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Exacerbation or new-onset of congestive heart failure (CHF). High-dose regimens (>5 mg/kg) in clinical trials (such as the ATTACH trial) were associated with a significantly increased risk of hospitalization and death in patients with pre-existing moderate-to-severe heart failure.
    *   **Neurological (Demyelinating Disease):** Reactivation or new-onset of central nervous system demyelinating disorders, including multiple sclerosis, optic neuritis, and transverse myelitis, as well as peripheral demyelinating diseases (Guillain-Barré syndrome).
    *   **Infusion Reactions:**
        - *Acute Infusion Reactions:* Occur during or within 2 hours of infusion (in up to 10% of patients), manifesting as fever, chills, pruritus, urticaria, chest pain, dyspnea, and hypotension. Can rarely progress to life-threatening anaphylaxis.
        - *Delayed Hypersensitivity (Serum Sickness-Like) Reactions:* Occur 3 to 12 days after infusion (often in patients after drug-free intervals), presenting as fever, rash, polyarthralgias, myalgias, hand/facial edema, and dysphagia.
    *   **Hepatic:** Severe hepatic reactions, including acute liver failure, hepatitis, and autoimmune hepatitis.
    *   **Hematological:** Rare cases of cytopenias, including leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and pancytopenia/aplastic anemia.
    *   **Immunological (Autoantibody Induction):** Development of antinuclear antibodies (ANA) and double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) antibodies, which can rarely present as a reversible lupus-like syndrome.
*   **Black Box Warnings:**
    *   **Serious Infections:** Increased risk of serious, life-threatening infections leading to hospitalization or death, including active tuberculosis, bacterial sepsis, invasive fungal infections (such as histoplasmosis), and other opportunistic infections. Discontinue treatment if a serious infection or sepsis develops. Screen for latent TB prior to starting and monitor all patients during therapy.
    *   **Malignancy:** Lymphoma and other malignancies, some fatal, have been reported in children and adolescent patients treated with TNF blockers. Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTCL) has occurred in young adults with inflammatory bowel disease treated with TNF blockers and concomitant thiopurines.

#### Pre-Screening & Contraindications
*   **Required Pre-Treatment Screening:**
    *   **Tuberculosis Screening:** Mandatory evaluation for active TB and testing for latent TB (TST or IGRA) prior to starting therapy. If positive, complete latent TB treatment before commencing infliximab. Monitor all patients for active TB during treatment, even if initial latent TB test is negative.
    *   **Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Screening:** Serological screening (HBsAg, anti-HBc) is mandatory prior to initiating therapy to identify chronic carriers.
    *   **Cardiovascular Assessment:** Evaluation for pre-existing congestive heart failure.
    *   **Baseline Labs:** Complete Blood Count (CBC) and liver function tests (LFTs) to monitor for baseline cytopenias and transaminase elevations.
    *   **Immunization Update:** Ensure all routine childhood and adult immunizations are up to date before starting, as live vaccines cannot be given during therapy.
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    *   Moderate-to-severe congestive heart failure (NYHA Class III/IV) at doses greater than 5 mg/kg (due to the ATTACH trial showing increased mortality).
    *   Known severe immediate hypersensitivity to infliximab, other murine proteins, or any excipients.
    *   Active, severe, clinically significant systemic infections, sepsis, or active tuberculosis.
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:**
    *   **Pregnancy Profile:** Infliximab actively crosses the placental barrier, particularly during the third trimester, via FcRn-mediated transport, exposing the fetus to maternal IgG levels. Prospective cohort studies have not demonstrated a clear pattern of congenital malformations or teratogenicity in humans (formerly pregnancy Category B).
    *   **Live Vaccine Restriction in Infants:** Because of in utero exposure, infants born to mothers treated with infliximab during pregnancy may have detectable drug levels in their serum for up to 5 to 6 months after birth. Administration of live vaccines (such as the BCG vaccine or rotavirus vaccine) to these infants is strictly contraindicated for at least 5 to 6 months post-birth due to the risk of severe, vaccine-induced infections.
    *   **Lactation:** Excreted in human breast milk in extremely low concentrations. Systemic absorption in the breastfed infant is minimal because maternal IgG is degraded in the infant's gastrointestinal tract, making it generally compatible with breastfeeding.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    *   **Chimeric cA2 Monoclonal Antibody:** Refers to its molecular construct of 25% mouse-derived variable regions (which bind TNF) and 75% human-derived IgG1-kappa constant regions, a design choice engineered to achieve high binding specificity.
    *   **ATTACH Trial Failure:** The landmark clinical trial that demonstrated that high-dose infliximab (10 mg/kg) caused severe clinical worsening and increased mortality in patients with moderate-to-severe congestive heart failure, establishing heart failure as a key contraindication.
    *   **Remicade Pioneer:** Renowned as the first anti-tumor necrosis factor (anti-TNF) biologic therapy approved by the FDA (in 1998), pioneering the use of biological monoclonal antibodies in rheumatology and gastroenterology.
    *   **Thiopurine Combination Trap:** Highlights the highly fatal development of hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTCL) in young male patients with IBD receiving combination therapy of infliximab and thiopurines.
*   **Naming Conventions:**
    *   **Chimeric Suffix (-ximab):** The "-ximab" suffix denotes a chimeric monoclonal antibody containing both human and non-human (murine) constant and variable regions, in contrast to "-umab" (fully human monoclonal antibodies, like adalimumab) and "-zumab" (humanized antibodies).
    *   **Remicade:** The original commercial trade name developed and marketed by Centocor (now Janssen Biotech, Inc.).
    *   **cA2:** The historic pre-clinical investigational code name assigned to the antibody during its early development.
