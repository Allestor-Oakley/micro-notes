### Rituximab
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class:**
    *   Chimeric mouse-human IgG1-kappa monoclonal antibody; B-cell depleting biologic agent.
*   **Target Cytokine, Receptor, or Immune Cell:**
    *   Target cell surface antigen: CD20 (B-lymphocyte antigen CD20), a 33–37 kDa non-glycosylated hydrophobic transmembrane phosphoprotein.
    *   Target cell populations: Pre-B lymphocytes, immature B lymphocytes, mature B lymphocytes, and memory B lymphocytes. Hematopoietic stem cells, pro-B cells, and long-lived plasma cells do not express CD20 and are spared from depletion.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    *   **Complement-Dependent Cytotoxicity (CDC):** Binding of rituximab to CD20 on the B-cell membrane aggregates and clusters the CD20 molecules into lipid rafts. This conformational change promotes the rapid recruitment and binding of C1q, triggering the classical complement cascade. C3b and C4b are deposited on the B-cell membrane, leading to the assembly of the membrane attack complex (MAC) and direct osmotic lysis of the cell.
    *   **Antibody-Dependent Cellular Cytotoxicity (ADCC):** The human IgG1 Fc constant region of bound rituximab binds with high affinity to Fc-gamma receptors (primarily FcγRIIIa/CD16) expressed on the surface of host effector cells, including Natural Killer (NK) cells, neutrophils, and macrophages. This immune synapse activation triggers the release of cytotoxic granules (perforins and granzymes) from NK cells or coordinates target cell death.
    *   **Antibody-Dependent Cellular Phagocytosis (ADCP):** Rituximab opsonizes B cells, marking them for recognition by professional phagocytic cells (macrophages and monocytes) via Fc-gamma receptors (FcγRI, FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa) in the liver, spleen, and tissue microenvironments, resulting in cellular engulfment and phagocytosis.
    *   **Direct Signaling & Apoptosis:** Binding and cross-linking of CD20 by rituximab initiates multiple intracellular signaling cascades, including the activation of p38 Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase (MAPK), Src family kinases, and caspase-3 pathways. This leads to cell cycle arrest, down-regulation of anti-apoptotic proteins (such as Bcl-2 and Bcl-xL), and direct programmed cell death (apoptosis) independent of host effector mechanisms.
*   **Downstream Immunological Effects:**
    *   **Profound B-Cell Depletion:** Induces rapid, extensive clearance of circulating and tissue-resident B lymphocytes, dropping levels to near-undetectable levels within 2 to 3 weeks of administration. Depletion typically persists for 6 to 12 months (or longer) before slow B-cell reconstitution begins from spared hematopoietic stem cells in the bone marrow.
    *   **Decline in Autoantibody Production:** Suppresses B-cell differentiation into autoantibody-secreting B-lineages, resulting in a gradual and significant reduction in systemic pathogenic autoantibodies (such as Rheumatoid Factor and anti-citrullinated protein antibodies) over several months.
    *   **Sparing of Protective Humoral Memory:** Because long-lived plasma cells lack CD20 expression, they are spared from rituximab-mediated depletion, allowing baseline serum immunoglobulin levels (IgG, IgA, IgM) and pre-existing protective humoral antibodies to be largely maintained (although hypogammaglobulinemia can develop with chronic, long-term therapy).
    *   **Suppressed Antigen Presentation & T-Cell Activation:** Depleting the B-cell pool eliminates B cells as professional Antigen-Presenting Cells (APCs). This blocks B-cell-mediated antigen presentation, costimulatory signaling, and cytokine provision to autoreactive T lymphocytes, dampening cellular immune responses.
    *   **Downregulation of Proinflammatory Cytokines:** Markedly decreases B-cell-derived secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including Interleukin-6 (IL-6), Interleukin-10 (IL-10), and Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha (TNF-α).

#### Pharmacokinetics & Administration
*   **Route of Administration:**
    *   Intravenous (IV) infusion only (administered at a slow, titrated rate under continuous clinical monitoring; must never be administered as an IV bolus or rapid injection).
*   **Metabolism & Clearance Pathway:**
    *   **Lysosomal Proteolysis:** As a high-molecular-weight chimeric IgG1 monoclonal antibody (molecular weight approximately 145 kDa), rituximab is not cleared by glomerular filtration or metabolized by hepatic Cytochrome P450 enzymes. Instead, it is cleared via endocytosis followed by non-specific proteolytic degradation into small peptides and amino acids within lysosomes of host reticuloendothelial cells and circulating leukocytes.
    *   **Target-Mediated Drug Disposition (TMDD):** Exhibits a dual-clearance pharmacokinetic profile. Initially, clearance is accelerated by binding to a high burden of circulating and tissue-resident B lymphocytes (the "B-cell sink" effect). As B-cell targets are depleted, clearance slows significantly, leading to more stable serum concentrations and a prolonged terminal half-life.
*   **Onset of Action & Half-Life:**
    *   **Onset of Action:** Rapid. Complete depletion of peripheral blood B cells occurs within 24 to 48 hours of starting the initial infusion.
    *   **Elimination Half-Life:** Long and highly variable, averaging approximately 22 days (ranging from 18 to 35 days across various autoimmune and oncology indications). The half-life is shorter during the first infusion and in patients with a high tumor/B-cell burden, and increases with repeat dosing as the cellular target is depleted.
*   **Therapeutic Drug Monitoring Required:**
    *   Direct therapeutic drug monitoring of serum rituximab concentrations is not clinically standard. Standard clinical and safety monitoring protocols include:
        1.  **Complete Blood Count (CBC) with Differential:** Monitored frequently (weekly to monthly) to detect severe myelosuppression, neutropenia, or thrombocytopenia.
        2.  **Peripheral B-Lymphocyte Panels (CD19+ / CD20+):** Flow cytometry of peripheral blood is monitored periodically to confirm successful B-cell depletion and track the onset of B-cell reconstitution.
        3.  **Quantitative Serum Immunoglobulins:** Regular measurement of serum IgG, IgA, and IgM levels, particularly in patients undergoing chronic maintenance therapy.
        4.  **Quantitative HBV DNA PCR:** Serial viral load monitoring is required for patients with past HBV exposure (anti-HBc positive) who are receiving prophylactic antiviral therapy.

#### Clinical Indications
*   **Primary Autoimmune / Inflammatory Indications:**
    *   **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):** Indicated in combination with methotrexate to reduce signs and symptoms, slow the progression of structural joint damage, and improve physical function in adult patients with moderately to severely active RA who have had an inadequate response to one or more TNF antagonists.
    *   **Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (GPA / Wegener's) & Microscopic Polyangiitis (MPA):** Indicated for the induction of remission in adult and pediatric patients (2 years of age and older) in combination with glucocorticoids.
    *   **Pemphigus Vulgaris (PV):** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with moderate-to-severe PV.
    *   **Off-Label Autoimmune Indications:** Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorder (NMOSD), Myasthenia Gravis, refractory Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), and chronic refractory Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP).
*   **Oncology Indications:**
    *   **Non-Hodgkin's Lymphoma (NHL):**
        - Relapsed or refractory, low-grade or follicular, CD20-positive B-cell NHL as a single agent.
        - Previously untreated follicular, CD20-positive, B-cell NHL in combination with first-line chemotherapy, and as single-agent maintenance therapy in responders.
        - Non-progressing (including stable disease), low-grade, CD20-positive B-cell NHL as a single agent after first-line CVP chemotherapy.
        - Previously untreated diffuse large B-cell, CD20-positive NHL (DLBCL) in combination with CHOP (R-CHOP) or other anthracycline-based chemotherapy.
        - Previously untreated, advanced-stage, CD20-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), Burkitt lymphoma (BL), Burkitt-like lymphoma (BLL), or mature B-cell acute leukemia (B-AL) in pediatric patients (6 months of age and older) in combination with chemotherapy (LMB scheme).
    *   **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL):** Indicated in combination with fludarabine and cyclophosphamide (FCR) for the treatment of patients with previously untreated and previously treated CD20-positive CLL.
*   **Transplant Rejection / Prophylaxis:**
    *   **Post-Transplant Lymphoproliferative Disorder (PTLD):** Indicated as first-line therapy for CD20-positive B-cell PTLD arising after solid organ or hematopoietic stem cell transplantation.
    *   **ABO-Incompatible & Highly Sensitized Transplants (Off-Label):** Used in desensitization protocols prior to kidney transplantation to reduce pre-formed anti-ABO or anti-HLA antibodies.
    *   **Antibody-Mediated Rejection (AMR) (Off-Label):** Used as rescue therapy to deplete CD20+ antibody-producing lineages in recipients experiencing acute humoral graft rejection.

#### Adverse Effects & Toxicity
*   **Infection Risks & Opportunistic Pathogens:**
    *   **Humoral Immunosuppression:** Profound B-cell depletion impairs adaptive humoral immunity, highly elevating susceptibility to severe, life-threatening, or fatal bacterial, fungal, and viral infections.
    *   **Opportunistic Infections:** Increased risk of Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP), Cytomegalovirus (CMV) tissue-invasive disease, invasive aspergillosis, and disseminated candidiasis.
    *   **JC Virus Reactivation (PML):** JC virus reactivation can occur, leading to Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML) (see Black Box Warnings).
    *   **Viral Reactivation:** Highly increased risk of Hepatitis B virus (HBV) reactivation in chronic carriers, which can cause acute fulminant hepatitis and liver failure. Highly increased risk of Varicella Zoster Virus (shingles) reactivation and Herpes Simplex Virus flares.
*   **Malignancy Risks:**
    *   Rituximab monotherapy is not directly associated with an increased risk of secondary solid tumors or secondary leukemia/myelodysplastic syndromes, though chronic immunosuppressive states can elevate the risk of non-melanoma skin cancers (squamous cell and basal cell carcinomas).
*   **Organ-Specific Toxicity:**
    *   **Infusion-Related Reactions:** Extremely common, especially during the first infusion (occurring in up to 80% of patients). Manifests as fever, chills, rigors, pruritus, urticaria, hypotension, bronchospasm, and angioedema. Can progress to fatal Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS), pulmonary infiltrates, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), and cardiogenic shock.
    *   **Severe Mucocutaneous Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), paraneoplastic pemphigus, and lichenoid dermatitis, which can be fatal.
    *   **Renal Toxicity:** Acute kidney injury and acute renal failure, particularly when co-administered with nephrotoxic chemotherapies or secondary to Tumor Lysis Syndrome (TLS) in oncology patients with high tumor burden.
    *   **Hematological:** Leukopenia, anemia, thrombocytopenia, and delayed-onset neutropenia (occurring up to several months after completing the treatment course).
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Angina, myocardial infarction, atrial fibrillation, ventricular arrhythmias, and worsening of pre-existing congestive heart failure.
*   **Black Box Warnings:**
    *   **Fatal Infusion-Related Reactions:** Severe, potentially fatal infusion-related reactions can occur, with approximately 80% of fatal reactions occurring during the first infusion. Titrate infusion rate, pre-medicate, and monitor closely.
    *   **Severe Mucocutaneous Reactions:** Severe, including fatal, mucocutaneous reactions such as SJS and TEN can occur. Discontinue immediately if cutaneous reactions develop.
    *   **Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Reactivation:** HBV reactivation can occur, in some cases resulting in fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure, and death. Screen all patients prior to treatment and monitor during and after therapy.
    *   **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML):** JC virus reactivation resulting in PML, severe neurological deficit, and death can occur. Discontinue treatment immediately upon suspicion.

#### Pre-Screening & Contraindications
*   **Required Pre-Treatment Screening:**
    *   **Hepatitis B Virus Screening:** Mandatory serological screening (HBsAg and anti-HBc) is required for all patients prior to starting therapy to identify active or latent HBV carriers.
    *   **Baseline Complete Blood Count:** To assess baseline neutrophil (>1500/mcL) and platelet (>100,000/mcL) levels.
    *   **Baseline Renal and Hepatic Function Panel:** Establish baseline serum creatinine, estimated GFR, and transaminases.
    *   **Quantitative Immunoglobulin Levels:** Establish baseline IgG, IgA, and IgM levels.
    *   **Active Infection Screen:** Careful evaluation to ensure the complete absence of active, clinically significant localized or systemic infections.
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    *   Known severe immediate hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis) to rituximab, other murine-derived proteins, or any excipients.
    *   Active, severe, life-threatening systemic infections.
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:**
    *   **Pregnancy Category:** Category C. Rituximab is an IgG1 monoclonal antibody that actively crosses the placental barrier, particularly during the second and third trimesters. It can deplete fetal CD20+ B cells, causing neonatal B-cell depletion, severe lymphopenia, and transient neonatal immunodeficiency.
    *   **Contraceptive Mandates:** Females of childbearing potential must use highly effective contraception during therapy and for at least **12 months** after the final dose of rituximab.
    *   **Live Vaccine Restriction in Infants:** Infants exposed to rituximab in utero must not receive any live-attenuated vaccines (such as rotavirus or BCG) for at least **6 months** after birth due to the high risk of severe, vaccine-induced infections.
    *   **Lactation:** Breastfeeding is not recommended during therapy and for at least 6 months after the last dose, as rituximab is excreted in human breast milk and can potentially cause B-cell depletion and immunodeficiency in the nursing infant.
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:**
    *   **Live-Attenuated Vaccines:** Concomitant administration is strictly contraindicated during therapy and for several months after treatment due to the high risk of severe vaccine-induced infection.
    *   **Additive Immunosuppressive/Myelosuppressive Agents (e.g., Fludarabine, Cyclophosphamide):** Concomitant use increases the risk of severe cytopenias and serious systemic infections.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    *   **Pan-B-Cell Depletion:** Denotes the complete, systemic clearance of B lymphocytes expressing the CD20 marker.
    *   **B-Cell Aplasia:** Describes the complete, prolonged absence of circulating B cells following treatment.
    *   **First-in-Class Blockbuster Biologic:** Refers to its pioneering status and massive commercial success, paving the way for the therapeutic monoclonal antibody revolution.
    *   **Target-Mediated Drug Disposition (TMDD):** The classic PK characteristic where the drug's clearance is dominated by the abundance of its cellular target.
*   **Historical Milestones:**
    *   **First Approved Oncology Monoclonal Antibody (1997):** Synthesized and developed initially by IDEC Pharmaceuticals under the investigational code name **IDEC-C2B8** (led by researchers Ron Levy, Nabil Hanna, and others). On November 26, 1997, it became the first-ever monoclonal antibody approved by the US FDA for the treatment of cancer, specifically for relapsed or refractory low-grade or follicular B-cell Non-Hodgkin's Lymphoma.
    *   **The R-CHOP Revolution:** The landmark addition of rituximab to the standard CHOP chemotherapy regimen (R-CHOP) in the early 2000s revolutionized the treatment of Diffuse Large B-cell Lymphoma (DLBCL), significantly increasing long-term cure rates and transforming it from a highly fatal disease to a highly curable malignancy.
    *   **Pediatric Approval Expansion (2021):** On December 2, 2021, the FDA expanded rituximab's indications to include pediatric patients aged 6 months and older with previously untreated, advanced-stage CD20-positive DLBCL, Burkitt Lymphoma, or mature B-cell acute leukemia in combination with chemotherapy, based on the global Inter-B-NHL Ritux 2010 trial which demonstrated a dramatic improvement in event-free survival.
*   **Naming Conventions:**
    *   **Murine-Human Chimeric Suffix (-ximab):** The "-ximab" suffix indicates a chimeric antibody consisting of mouse-derived variable regions (which bind CD20) combined with human-derived IgG1-kappa constant regions (providing effector functions). This distinguishes it from "-umab" (fully human, like adalimumab) and "-zumab" (humanized).
    *   **Rituxan vs. MabThera:** Rituxan is the primary brand name under which it is marketed in the United States, Canada, and Japan by Genentech/Biogen, whereas MabThera is the brand name used by Roche in Europe and other international markets.
    *   **Cluster of Differentiation 20 (CD20):** Denotes the target antigen, a 33–37 kDa non-glycosylated transmembrane protein that functions as a calcium channel and is uniquely restricted to B-lineage cells.
