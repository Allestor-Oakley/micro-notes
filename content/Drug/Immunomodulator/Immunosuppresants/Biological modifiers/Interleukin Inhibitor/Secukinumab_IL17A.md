### Secukinumab
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class:**
    *   Recombinant fully human IgG1-kappa monoclonal antibody; selective interleukin-17A (IL-17A) inhibitor; biologic disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (bDMARD).
*   **Target Cytokine, Receptor, or Immune Cell:**
    *   Directly targets and neutralizes soluble homodimeric Interleukin-17A (IL-17A) and the heterodimeric IL-17A/F cytokine.
    *   Inhibits downstream signaling mediated by the IL-17 receptor complex, which is composed of the IL-17RA and IL-17RC receptor subunits.
    *   Targets tissue cells expressing IL-17 receptors, primarily keratinocytes, synovial fibroblasts (synoviocytes), osteoclasts, osteoblasts, endothelial cells, and dermal fibroblasts.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    *   **High-Affinity Neutralization:** Secukinumab binds specifically and with high affinity to the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-17A (both as a homodimer) and the IL-17A/F heterodimeric complex. It does not bind to other IL-17 family members, such as IL-17B, IL-17C, IL-17D, IL-17E (IL-25), or IL-17F homodimers.
    *   **Receptor Blockade:** By binding to soluble IL-17A, secukinumab sterically prevents the cytokine from engaging and binding to its cell-surface receptor complex, which is a heterodimer of IL-17RA and IL-17RC subunits.
    *   **Inhibition of the SEFIR-Act1 Pathway:** Under normal conditions, binding of IL-17A to IL-17RA/RC causes the intracellular SEFIR (similar expression to fibroblast growth factor receptor/IL-17R) domains of the receptor subunits to recruit the cytoplasmic adapter protein Act1 (NF-κB activator 1). Secukinumab blocks this interaction, preventing Act1 recruitment.
    *   **Halting of TRAF6 and TAK1 Activation:** Because Act1 is not recruited, its U-box ubiquitin ligase function is not activated. This halts the recruitment and ubiquitination of tumor necrosis factor receptor-associated factor 6 (TRAF6), preventing downstream activation of the transforming growth factor-beta-activated kinase 1 (TAK1) complex.
    *   **Suppression of NF-κB and AP-1 Translocation:** Blockade of the TAK1 pathway prevents activation of the IκB kinase (IKK) complex, thereby blocking the phosphorylation and degradation of IκBα and keeping the transcription factor Nuclear Factor-kappa B (NF-κB) trapped in the cytoplasm. Simultaneously, activation of Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase (MAPK) pathways—specifically p38 MAPK and c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK)—is blocked, preventing activation of the Activator Protein 1 (AP-1) transcription factor.
    *   **Prevention of mRNA Stabilization:** Act1-mediated recruitment of TRAF2 and TRAF5 is blocked, preventing the assembly of RNA-binding protein complexes (including HuR and Arid5a). This disrupts the stabilization of inflammatory mRNAs, allowing rapid degradation of transcripts encoding cytokines, chemokines, and matrix metalloproteinases.
*   **Downstream Immunological Effects:**
    *   **Suppression of Inflammatory Mediators:** Profoundly decreases the expression and secretion of downstream pro-inflammatory cytokines (including TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6) and tissue-remodeling enzymes (including matrix metalloproteinases MMP-1, MMP-3, and MMP-13) by keratinocytes, synovial fibroblasts, and chondrocytes.
    *   **Inhibition of Chemokine-Driven Neutrophil Infiltration:** Markedly downregulates the secretion of potent neutrophil-recruiting chemokines (including CXCL1, CXCL2, CXCL5, and CXCL8/IL-8) and Th17-recruiting chemokines (CCL20). This halts the chemotaxis, rolling, adhesion, and accumulation of neutrophils in psoriatic plaques, synovial joints, and other target tissues.
    *   **Normalization of Keratinocyte Proliferation:** Resolves the IL-17A-driven hyperproliferation, abnormal differentiation, and hyperplasia of epidermal keratinocytes, thereby reducing epidermal thickness, eliminating parakeratosis, and restoring normal skin histology.
    *   **Inhibition of Osteoclastogenesis and Bone Erosion:** Suppresses the induction of receptor activator of nuclear factor-κB ligand (RANKL) on osteoblasts and T cells, which blocks osteoclast maturation and halts pathologic bone resorption and erosion in joint spaces.
    *   **Reduction of Angiogenesis:** Decreases VEGF and CXCL8 expression in inflamed tissue, thereby suppressing pathologic vascular endothelial cell proliferation and cutaneous neovascularization.

#### Pharmacokinetics & Administration
*   **Route of Administration:**
    *   Subcutaneous (SC) injection (available as 150 mg/mL prefilled syringes, 150 mg/mL Sensoready pens, and 300 mg/2mL UnoReady pens).
    *   Intravenous (IV) infusion (reconstituted from a single-use vial containing 150 mg of lyophilized powder for clinical infusion).
*   **Metabolism & Clearance Pathway:**
    *   **Lysosomal Proteolysis:** As a fully human IgG1-kappa monoclonal antibody, secukinumab is not metabolized by hepatic Cytochrome P450 enzymes. It undergoes non-specific endocytosis and is degraded by lysosomal proteases and peptidases into small peptides and individual amino acids.
    *   **FcRn-Mediated Recycling:** Binds to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) in vascular endothelial lysosomes, which rescues the antibody from lysosomal degradation and recycles it back into the systemic circulation, prolonging its half-life.
    *   **Clearance:** Slow systemic clearance, estimated from population PK models at approximately 0.14 to 0.22 L/day in plaque psoriasis patients, and 0.26 L/day in hidradenitis suppurativa patients. Clearance increases with higher body weight in an allometric relationship. It is not cleared by hemodialysis.
*   **Onset of Action & Half-Life:**
    *   **Bioavailability:** Estimated at approximately 73% following subcutaneous administration.
    *   **Peak Concentration (Tmax):** Steady-state peak serum concentrations (Cmax) are achieved at a median Tmax of approximately 6 days after both SC and IV administration.
    *   **Elimination Half-Life:** Extremely long, with a mean elimination half-life ranging from 22 to 31 days (averaging approximately 27 days) in plaque psoriasis patients, and 23 days in hidradenitis suppurativa patients.
    *   **Onset of Action:** Clinical improvement, characterized by reductions in skin scaling, joint pain, and enthesitis, is typically observed within 2 weeks of initiating loading doses.
*   **Therapeutic Drug Monitoring Required:**
    *   Routine therapeutic drug monitoring of serum secukinumab concentrations is not clinically standard. Required safety monitoring includes:
        - **Infection Assessments:** Ongoing clinical evaluations for signs and symptoms of localized or systemic infections.
        - **Gastrointestinal Monitoring:** Close surveillance for symptoms of new-onset or worsening Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD).
        - **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** Periodic monitoring of absolute neutrophil counts (ANC) to detect potential neutropenia.

#### Clinical Indications
*   **Primary Autoimmune / Inflammatory Indications:**
    *   **Plaque Psoriasis (PsO):** Treatment of moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis in adult and pediatric patients (6 years of age and older) who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy.
    *   **Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA):** Treatment of active psoriatic arthritis in adult and pediatric patients (2 years of age and older).
    *   **Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS):** Treatment of active ankylosing spondylitis in adult and pediatric patients (12 years of age and older).
    *   **Non-radiographic Axial Spondyloarthritis (nr-axSpA):** Treatment of active non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis with objective signs of inflammation in adult patients.
    *   **Enthesitis-Related Arthritis (ERA):** Treatment of active enthesitis-related arthritis in pediatric patients (4 years of age and older).
    *   **Hidradenitis Suppurativa (HS):** Treatment of moderate-to-severe active hidradenitis suppurativa in adult and pediatric patients (12 years of age and older).
*   **Oncology Indications:**
    *   N/A
*   **Transplant Rejection / Prophylaxis:**
    *   N/A

#### Adverse Effects & Toxicity
*   **Infection Risks & Opportunistic Pathogens:**
    *   **Serious Infections:** Blockade of IL-17A compromises host defenses, leading to an increased risk of serious systemic infections.
    *   **Mucocutaneous Candidiasis (The Primary Specific Risk):** Highly elevated and selective susceptibility to localized fungal infections, most notably **Candida albicans** (presenting as oral thrush, esophageal candidiasis, and vulvovaginal candidiasis). This occurs because IL-17A is the primary physiological coordinator of mucosal host defense against extracellular fungi by inducing epithelial antimicrobial peptides (such as beta-defensins and S100 proteins) and recruiting neutrophils.
    *   **Upper Respiratory Infections:** Nasopharyngitis, pharyngitis, and sinusitis.
    *   **Opportunistic Pathogens:** Increased risk of localized viral infections, including oral herpes (HSV-1) flares. Tuberculosis reactivation is extremely rare.
*   **Malignancy Risks:**
    *   No established causal relationship with systemic malignancies or secondary leukemias, though chronic immunosuppression requires monitoring for non-melanoma skin cancers.
*   **Organ-Specific Toxicity:**
    *   **Gastrointestinal (Inflammatory Bowel Disease):** Can cause new-onset or severe, life-threatening exacerbations of **Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)**, including both Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis. This occurs because IL-17A plays a critical, protective role in the gastrointestinal tract, where it maintains the integrity of the mucosal epithelial barrier and stimulates the expression of tight-junction proteins (such as claudin-1). Neutralizing IL-17A can compromise gut epithelial barrier function, leading to pathologic mucosal inflammation.
    *   **Hematological:** Transient, mild-to-moderate neutropenia (decreased absolute neutrophil count), which is typically self-limiting.
    *   **Hepatic:** Mild, transient elevations in liver transaminases (AST, ALT).
    *   **Immunological:** Rare cases of anaphylactic reactions, generalized urticaria, and angioedema.
    *   **Local Injection Site Reactions:** Mild erythema, swelling, or pain at the subcutaneous injection site.
*   **Black Box Warnings:**
    *   N/A (Secukinumab does not carry any formal FDA Black Box Warnings, but carries prominent safety warnings regarding serious infections, tuberculosis screening, inflammatory bowel disease, and hypersensitivity).

#### Pre-Screening & Contraindications
*   **Required Pre-Treatment Screening:**
    *   **Tuberculosis Screening:** Mandatory clinical evaluation for active TB and testing for latent TB (TST or IGRA) prior to initiating therapy. Latent TB must be treated before commencing secukinumab.
    *   **Gastrointestinal Screening:** Thorough history-taking to screen for pre-existing Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis.
    *   **Active Infection Assessment:** Ensure the complete absence of active, clinically important localized or systemic infections before starting.
    *   **Latex Sensitivity Check:** The removable needle cap of the 150 mg/mL prefilled syringe and Sensoready pen contains dry natural rubber (a derivative of latex), requiring screening for latex hypersensitivity.
    *   **Immunization Update:** Pediatric and adult patients must be up to date with all age-appropriate vaccines before starting therapy.
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    *   Known severe immediate hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis) to secukinumab or any of its formulation excipients.
    *   Active, clinically important systemic infections.
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:**
    *   **Pregnancy Profile:** Formerly Category B. Animal reproduction studies in cynomolgus monkeys revealed no evidence of embryofetal toxicity or maternal harm at doses up to 30 times the maximum human dose. As an IgG1 monoclonal antibody, secukinumab actively crosses the placental barrier, primarily during the third trimester.
    *   **Live Vaccine Restriction in Infants:** Infants exposed to secukinumab in utero should not receive live-attenuated vaccines (such as rotavirus or BCG) for at least 5 to 6 months after birth due to the potential risk of vaccine-induced infections.
    *   **Lactation:** It is unknown if secukinumab is excreted in human breast milk. Due to its large molecular size, oral absorption in the breastfed infant is expected to be minimal due to gastrointestinal degradation, but caution should be exercised.
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:**
    *   **Biologic Immunomodulators (e.g., Abatacept, Rituximab, TNF Blockers):** Concurrent use is strictly not recommended. Concomitant administration significantly increases the risk of serious infections and neutropenia with no added clinical benefit.
    *   **Live-Attenuated Vaccines:** Concomitant administration is strictly contraindicated during therapy due to the risk of severe, vaccine-induced infections. Non-live or inactivated vaccines can be safely co-administered.
    *   **Cytochrome P450 Substrates (Narrow Therapeutic Index):** Chronic inflammation suppresses CYP450 enzyme expression. Initiating secukinumab can normalize cytokine levels and thus restore CYP450 enzyme levels. This may increase the metabolism of CYP450 substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (such as warfarin or cyclosporine), requiring monitoring.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    *   **Mucocutaneous Candida Indicator:** Highlights the highly selective increased risk of mucosal yeast infections, reflecting IL-17's specific physiological role in barrier defense.
    *   **Gut Epithelial Barrier Paradox:** The phenomenon where blocking IL-17 resolves skin inflammation but can catastrophically disrupt gut barrier integrity, precipitating active inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's or UC).
    *   **First-in-Class IL-17A Blockade:** Pioneered the therapeutic targeting of the Th17 cytokine axis, revolutionizing the treatment of moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis and ankylosing spondylitis.
*   **Historical Milestones:**
    *   **The Discovery of AIN457:** Discovered and engineered by Novartis Pharmaceuticals, originally designated as AIN457.
    *   **FDA Approval (2015):** In January 2015, secukinumab (Cosentyx) became the first-ever interleukin-17A inhibitor to receive FDA approval, establishing a new therapeutic class for plaque psoriasis, later expanded to psoriatic arthritis and ankylosing spondylitis in 2016, and hidradenitis suppurativa in 2023.
*   **Naming Conventions:**
    *   **Fully Human Suffix (-umab):** The "-umab" suffix designates a fully human monoclonal antibody, distinguishing it from chimeric "-ximab" (e.g., infliximab) or humanized "-zumab" antibodies.
    *   **Cosentyx:** The commercial brand name under which it is marketed globally.
    *   **Interleukin-17A (IL-17A):** The target cytokine, originally discovered as CTLA-8, which acts as a key effector molecule of the Th17 cellular lineage.
