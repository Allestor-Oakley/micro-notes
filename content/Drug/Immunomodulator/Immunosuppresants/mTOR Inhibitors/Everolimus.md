### Everolimus
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class:**
    *   Synthetic macrocyclic lactone; mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitor; rapalog; immunosuppressive and antineoplastic agent.
*   **Target Cytokine, Receptor, or Immune Cell:**
    *   Intracellular targets: FK506-binding protein 12 (FKBP12) and the serine/threonine protein kinase mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1).
    *   Target cell populations: CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes, B lymphocytes, dendritic cells, and neoplastic cells.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    *   **FKBP12 Binding:** Everolimus penetrates the cell membrane via passive diffusion and binds with high affinity to its intracellular receptor immunophilin, FKBP12 (FK506-binding protein 12).
    *   **mTORC1 Inhibition:** The resulting everolimus-FKBP12 complex binds with high specificity and affinity to the FRB (FKBP12-rapamycin-binding) domain of the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) within the mTORC1 complex. mTORC1 is a multiprotein kinase complex consisting of mTOR, regulatory-associated protein of mTOR (Raptor), mammalian lethal with SEC13 protein 8 (mLST8), proline-rich Akt substrate of 40 kDa (PRAS40), and DEP domain-containing mTOR-interacting protein (Deptor).
    *   **Allosteric Kinase Blockade:** This binding physically blocks the kinase active site of mTORC1, preventing its ability to phosphorylate downstream substrates.
    *   **Downstream Translation Arrest:** Specifically, mTORC1 inhibition halts the phosphorylation of two key translational regulators:
        1.  **p70S6 Kinase 1 (S6K1):** Inhibits S6K1 activation, preventing the phosphorylation of ribosomal protein S6 and thus blocking the translation of mRNAs containing a 5'-terminal oligopyrimidine (5'TOP) tract, which encode essential ribosomal proteins and translation elongation factors.
        2.  **Eukaryotic Translation Initiation Factor 4E-Binding Protein 1 (4E-BP1):** Inhibits 4E-BP1 phosphorylation, keeping 4E-BP1 tightly bound to eukaryotic initiation factor 4E (eIF4E). This prevents the assembly of the eIF4F cap-binding complex, selectively blocking the translation of mRNAs encoding key proteins necessary for cell cycle progression (Cyclin D1, c-Myc) and angiogenesis (Hypoxia-Inducible Factor 1-alpha, HIF-1α).
    *   **Metabolic & Angiogenic Blockade:** Downregulation of HIF-1α reduces transcription and release of Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF) and basic Fibroblast Growth Factor (bFGF), severely inhibiting angiogenesis. In tumor cells, everolimus suppresses glycolysis by downregulating glucose transporters (such as GLUT1 and GLUT12). It does not directly inhibit mTORC2 acutely, but long-term exposure can disrupt mTORC2 assembly in some cell lines.
*   **Downstream Immunological Effects:**
    *   **Blockade of Lymphocyte Clonal Expansion:** By blocking the downstream signals of growth-promoting cytokines (IL-2, IL-4, and IL-15), everolimus arrests CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, and B cells in the G1 phase of the cell cycle. This inhibits their clonal expansion and cell division.
    *   **Suppressed Antibody Production:** Impairs B-cell differentiation and immunoglobulin (antibody) production, reducing humoral immune responses.
    *   **Impaired Dendritic Cell Function:** Downregulates dendritic cell maturation, decreasing their antigen-presenting capacity, expression of costimulatory molecules (CD80, CD86), and secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-12.
    *   **Regulatory T Cell (Treg) Preservation:** Unlike calcineurin inhibitors, mTOR inhibition preferentially spares or even promotes the survival and differentiation of CD4+CD25+FoxP3+ regulatory T cells (Tregs), which can support the induction of immunological transplant tolerance.

#### Pharmacokinetics & Administration
*   **Route of Administration:**
    *   Oral (administered as tablets, dispersible tablets, or oral suspension). It must be taken consistently either with or without food.
*   **Metabolism & Clearance Pathway:**
    *   **First-Pass and Intestinal Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized in the liver and small intestine, primarily via the Cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4) and 3A5 enzymes. Metabolism occurs via monohydroxylation, demethylation, and ring-opening reactions, producing at least 3 monohydroxylated metabolites, 2 hydroxylated metabolites, and a ring-opened metabolite. These metabolites have less than 10% of the pharmacological activity of the parent drug.
    *   **Efflux Transporter:** Everolimus is a substrate for the active efflux transporter P-glycoprotein (P-gp/ABCB1), which limits its intestinal absorption and tissue distribution.
    *   **Biliary Excretion:** Excreted primarily in the feces (approximately 80% of a dose is recovered in feces), with only a minor fraction excreted in the urine (approximately 5%). No parent drug is detected in the urine or feces. It is not significantly cleared by hemodialysis.
*   **Onset of Action & Half-Life:**
    *   **Absorption & Peak (Tmax):** Rapidly absorbed. Peak whole blood concentrations (Cmax) are reached within 1 to 2 hours (median of 0.75-1.5 h) under fasting conditions. A high-fat meal decreases Cmax by 60% and delays Tmax to 3 hours.
    *   **Protein Binding:** Approximately 74% bound to plasma proteins (mainly albumin) in whole blood, but it extensively partitions into erythrocytes (whole blood-to-plasma ratio is approximately 17:1), meaning most of the drug resides in red blood cells.
    *   **Volume of Distribution (Vd/F):** Highly distributed into tissues, with an apparent volume of distribution of approximately 110 L in renal transplant patients.
    *   **Elimination Half-Life:** Long, terminal elimination half-life is approximately 30 ± 11 hours (ranging from 19 to 53 hours). Steady-state concentrations are achieved within 4 days of daily or twice-daily dosing, with 2- to 3-fold accumulation.
*   **Therapeutic Drug Monitoring Required:**
    *   **Narrow Therapeutic Index:** Routine therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) of whole blood trough concentrations (C0) is mandatory in transplant medicine to optimize efficacy and prevent severe toxicity.
    *   **Whole Blood Assays:** Because of extensive partition into erythrocytes, whole blood (not plasma or serum) must be used. Analytical methods include liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) or specific turbidimetric immunoassays.
    *   **Target Ranges:**
        - **Kidney Transplant (Zortress/Certican):** Target whole blood trough concentration is typically 3 to 8 ng/mL when combined with reduced-dose cyclosporine and corticosteroids.
        - **Liver Transplant:** Target whole blood trough concentration is 3 to 8 ng/mL in combination with reduced-dose tacrolimus.
        - **Oncology (Afinitor):** TDM is not routinely mandated but has been studied; trough levels between 10 to 20 ng/mL are associated with improved progression-free survival in advanced breast cancer but also increased toxicity.
    *   **Sirolimus Cross-reactivity:** Sirolimus cross-reacts significantly with everolimus immunoassays, so concentrations cannot be reliably determined in patients transitioning between these two drugs.

#### Clinical Indications
*   **Primary Autoimmune / Inflammatory Indications:**
    *   N/A
*   **Oncology Indications:**
    *   **Advanced Breast Cancer:** Approved for hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer in postmenopausal women in combination with exemestane after failure of treatment with letrozole or anastrozole.
    *   **Neuroendocrine Tumors (NET):** Approved for progressive, well-differentiated, non-functional neuroendocrine tumors of pancreatic, gastrointestinal, or lung origin that are locally advanced, metastatic, or unresectable.
    *   **Advanced Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC):** Approved for advanced renal cell carcinoma after failure of treatment with VEGF-targeted therapies (sunitinib or sorafenib).
    *   **Tuberous Sclerosis Complex (TSC) Astrocytoma (SEGA):** Approved for adult and pediatric patients with TSC-associated subependymal giant cell astrocytoma (SEGA) requiring therapeutic intervention but not amenable to curative resection.
    *   **TSC-Associated Renal Angiomyolipoma:** Approved for TSC-associated renal angiomyolipoma not requiring immediate surgery.
    *   **TSC-Associated Partial-Onset Seizures:** Approved as adjunctive treatment for adult and pediatric patients (aged 2 years and older) with TSC-associated partial-onset seizures (Afinitor Disperz).
*   **Transplant Rejection / Prophylaxis:**
    *   **Renal Transplantation:** Approved for the prophylaxis of organ rejection in adult kidney transplant recipients at low-to-moderate immunological risk, used in combination with reduced-dose cyclosporine and corticosteroids.
    *   **Hepatic Transplantation:** Approved for the prophylaxis of organ rejection in adult liver transplant recipients, used in combination with reduced-dose tacrolimus and corticosteroids.

#### Adverse Effects & Toxicity
*   **Infection Risks & Opportunistic Pathogens:**
    *   **Susceptibility to Infections:** Potent immunosuppression increases the risk of developing serious bacterial, viral, fungal, and protozoal infections.
    *   **Opportunistic Pathogens:** Increased risk of Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP), cytomegalovirus (CMV) reactivation/viremia, and systemic fungal infections (Aspergillus, Candida).
    *   **Latent Reactivations:** High risk of the reactivation of latent Tuberculosis (TB) and latent Hepatitis B virus (HBV).
*   **Malignancy Risks:**
    *   **Lymphoma & Lymphoproliferative Disorders:** Increased risk of developing lymphoma, post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD, typically EBV-driven).
    *   **Skin Cancers:** Increased risk of non-melanoma skin malignancies, particularly squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) and basal cell carcinoma (BCC) of the skin (patients must limit UV light exposure).
*   **Organ-Specific Toxicity:**
    *   **Non-Infectious Pneumonitis:** Interstitial lung disease, presenting as cough, dyspnea, pleural effusion, and pulmonary infiltrates; can be severe or fatal.
    *   **Stomatitis/Mucositis:** Extremely common (up to 90% of oncology patients), presenting as painful mouth ulcers and mucosal inflammation.
    *   **Renal/Proteinuria:** Proteinuria, increased serum creatinine, and potentiated nephrotoxicity when combined with standard-dose calcineurin inhibitors. Renal graft thrombosis (arterial or venous graft thrombosis resulting in graft loss, usually within the first 30 days post-transplantation).
    *   **Metabolic/Endocrine:** Severe hyperlipidemia (hypercholesterolemia, hypertriglyceridemia), hyperglycemia, insulin resistance, and new-onset diabetes mellitus.
    *   **Hematologic:** Thrombocytopenia, anemia, neutropenia, and leukopenia.
    *   **Impaired Wound Healing:** High incidence of delayed wound healing, wound dehiscence, lymphocele, and incisional hernia.
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, peripheral edema, and angioedema (especially when co-administered with ACE inhibitors).
*   **Black Box Warnings:**
    *   **Malignancies and Serious Infections:** Increased risk of developing serious infections and malignancies (lymphoma and skin cancer) which can lead to hospitalization or death.
    *   **Physician Experience Only:** Should be prescribed only by physicians experienced in immunosuppressive therapy and transplant medicine.
    *   **Renal Graft Thrombosis:** Increased risk of kidney arterial and venous thrombosis resulting in graft loss, mostly occurring within the first 30 days post-transplantation.
    *   **Cyclosporine Nephrotoxicity:** Potentiates cyclosporine-induced nephrotoxicity; cyclosporine doses must be reduced when combined with everolimus.
    *   **Mortality in Heart Transplantation:** Increased mortality was observed in a clinical trial of de novo heart transplant recipients receiving everolimus (Zortress is not approved for heart transplantation).

#### Pre-Screening & Contraindications
*   **Required Pre-Treatment Screening:**
    *   **Baseline Metabolic and Renal Panels:** Serum creatinine, GFR, fasting lipid panel (cholesterol, triglycerides), fasting blood glucose, HbA1c, and liver enzymes (ALT, AST, bilirubin).
    *   **Complete Blood Count:** Baseline hemoglobin, platelets, and white blood cell count.
    *   **Infection Screening:** Screen for latent Tuberculosis (IGRA or TST), Hepatitis B (HBsAg, anti-HBc), Hepatitis C, HIV, and baseline EBV/CMV serologies.
    *   **Pregnancy Verification:** Mandatory negative pregnancy test for female patients of childbearing potential.
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    *   Known immediate hypersensitivity to everolimus, sirolimus, other rapalogs, or any components of the formulation.
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:**
    *   **Pregnancy Category:** Category C. Everolimus is embryotoxic and teratogenic in animal models, causing skeletal malformations and fetal resorption. It is contraindicated or highly restricted during pregnancy.
    *   **Contraceptive Mandates:** Female patients of childbearing potential must use highly effective contraception during everolimus therapy and for at least 8 weeks after stopping treatment.
    *   **Lactation:** Breastfeeding is contraindicated during everolimus therapy due to the risk of serious adverse reactions (e.g., growth suppression, myelosuppression) in the nursing infant.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    *   **"Rapalog":** Term used to denote derivatives or chemical structural analogs of the parent macrolide rapamycin (sirolimus).
    *   **"mTORC1 Allosteric Inhibitor":** Highlights its unique mechanical binding to the FRB domain of mTORC1 via FKBP12, rather than acting as a competitive ATP-catalytic site inhibitor.
    *   **"Sirolimus 40-O-(2-hydroxyethyl) derivative":** Denotes its exact chemical modification, which alters its physical properties and half-life.
*   **Historical Milestones:**
    *   **The Development of Everolimus:** Developed by Novartis under the development code RAD001 in the late 1990s as a second-generation rapalog to improve upon the unfavorable pharmacokinetics (notably the long, variable half-life of ~60 hours) of sirolimus. By adding a 2-hydroxyethyl group at the 40 position, everolimus achieved a shorter half-life (~30 hours), allowing patients to reach steady-state much faster and enabling clinicians to adjust doses more rapidly in response to toxicity.
    *   **FDA Approval Milestones:** First approved in Europe (Certican) in 2003 for transplantation, and later by the US FDA in 2009 (as Afinitor) for renal cell carcinoma, in 2010 (as Zortress) for kidney transplantation, and in 2013 for liver transplantation.
*   **Naming Conventions:**
    *   **RAD001:** The original investigational pre-clinical code name used by Novartis during its development.
    *   **Zortress vs. Afinitor vs. Certican vs. Votubia:** A classic clinical naming convention. Zortress (USA) and Certican (Europe/international) are marketed in low-dose strengths (0.25 mg, 0.5 mg, 0.75 mg) for transplant immunosuppression. Afinitor (USA/international) is marketed in high-dose strengths (2.5 mg, 5 mg, 10 mg) for oncology, while Votubia is used in Europe specifically for SEGA and TSC-related indications.
