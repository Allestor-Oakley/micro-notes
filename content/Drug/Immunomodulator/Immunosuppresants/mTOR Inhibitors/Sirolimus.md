### Sirolimus
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class:**
    *   Mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitor; macrolide lactone; immunosuppressive and antiproliferative agent.
*   **Target Cytokine, Receptor, or Immune Cell:**
    *   Intracellular target: FK506-binding protein 12 (FKBP12, a 12 kDa immunophilin) and the serine/threonine protein kinase mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1).
    *   Target cell populations: CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes, B lymphocytes, dendritic cells, hepatocytes, and neoplastic/smooth muscle cells.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    *   **Passive Cellular Entry & FKBP12 Binding:** Sirolimus is a lipophilic macrolide lactone that passively diffuses across the cell membrane into the cytoplasm. Once inside, it binds with high affinity to the cytosolic receptor immunophilin FKBP12, forming a stable, active sirolimus-FKBP12 complex.
    *   **Allosteric Inhibition of mTORC1:** The sirolimus-FKBP12 complex binds specifically and allosterically to the FKBP12-rapamycin-binding (FRB) domain of the serine/threonine protein kinase mTOR within the mTOR Complex 1 (mTORC1). This binding does not directly block ATP binding but physically restricts access to the kinase active site of mTORC1. The sirolimus-FKBP12 complex has no inhibitory effect on calcineurin activity, distinguishing its pathway from cyclosporine and tacrolimus.
    *   **Blockade of Downstream Translation Regulators:** By inhibiting mTORC1 kinase activity, sirolimus halts the phosphorylation of two key downstream substrates:
        1.  **p70S6 Kinase 1 (S6K1):** Prevents S6K1 phosphorylation and activation, which blocks the downstream phosphorylation of ribosomal protein S6, thereby shutting down the translation of ribosomal proteins and elongation factors encoded by mRNAs containing a 5'-terminal oligopyrimidine (5'TOP) tract.
        2.  **Eukaryotic Translation Initiation Factor 4E-Binding Protein 1 (4E-BP1):** Inhibits 4E-BP1 phosphorylation, keeping it tightly bound to eukaryotic initiation factor 4E (eIF4E). This prevents the assembly of the eIF4F cap-binding complex, selectively suppressing the translation of critical mRNAs required for cell cycle progression (such as Cyclin D1, c-Myc, proliferating cell nuclear antigen [PCNA]) and angiogenesis (such as Hypoxia-Inducible Factor 1-alpha [HIF-1α]).
    *   **Cell Cycle Arrest:** Unlike calcineurin inhibitors, sirolimus does not prevent the initial transcription of cytokines like IL-2. Instead, it blocks the intracellular signaling response downstream of the IL-2 receptor (and other cytokine receptors like IL-4 and IL-15). This blocks cytokine-driven cell cycle progression, arresting T and B cells at the junction of the G1 to S phase.
*   **Downstream Immunological Effects:**
    *   **Inhibition of Lymphocyte Proliferation:** Potently suppresses cytokine-driven (IL-2, IL-4, IL-15) proliferation and clonal expansion of activated CD4+ and CD8+ T cells and B cells.
    *   **Suppressed Humoral Immunity:** Blocks B-cell differentiation and maturation into antibody-producing plasma cells, leading to a significant decrease in antibody production.
    *   **Impaired Antigen Presentation:** Reduces the maturation and endocytic capacity of dendritic cells, downregulating co-stimulatory molecules (CD80, CD86) and IL-12 secretion, thereby suppressing antigen-specific T-cell priming.
    *   **T Regulatory Cell (Treg) Sparing/Promotion:** Unlike calcineurin inhibitors, mTOR inhibitors like sirolimus selectively spare or even promote the survival, expansion, and functional stability of CD4+CD25+FoxP3+ regulatory T cells, which favors the induction and maintenance of immunological tolerance.
    *   **Inhibition of Smooth Muscle Proliferation:** Suppresses growth-factor-driven proliferation of vascular smooth muscle cells and fibroblasts, reducing intimal hyperplasia.

#### Pharmacokinetics & Administration
*   **Route of Administration:**
    *   Oral: Administered as tablets (0.5 mg, 1 mg, 2 mg) or oral solution (1 mg/mL). It must be taken consistently either with or without food.
    *   Local: Formulated as a drug-eluting stent coating (slow release to prevent coronary restenosis) and investigational systemic nanoparticle formulations.
*   **Metabolism & Clearance Pathway:**
    *   **Intestinal and Hepatic Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized in the intestinal wall and liver, primarily by the Cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4) and 3A5 enzymes. It undergoes O-demethylation and hydroxylation, producing several metabolites (including 41-O-demethyl, 7-O-demethyl, hydroxy, dihydroxy, and didemethyl sirolimus). These metabolites have more than 100-fold less immunosuppressive activity than the parent drug.
    *   **Transporter Efflux:** It is a substrate for the active efflux transporter P-glycoprotein (P-gp/ABCB1) in the small intestine, which limits its systemic absorption.
    *   **Excretion:** Primarily eliminated via biliary/fecal excretion. Following a single oral dose, approximately 91% of the drug-derived radioactivity is recovered in the feces, and only 2.2% is excreted in the urine.
*   **Onset of Action & Half-Life:**
    *   **Absorption and Peak (Tmax):** Rapidly but poorly absorbed. Oral bioavailability is approximately 14% for the oral solution and 18% for the tablet. Peak blood concentrations (Cmax) are reached within 1 to 2 hours for the solution and 1 to 3 hours for the tablet in the fasting state. Co-administration with a high-fat meal reduces Cmax but increases overall exposure (AUC) by up to 35%, requiring consistent dietary administration.
    *   **Erythrocyte Sequestration:** Extensively partitions into formed blood elements, resulting in a mean whole blood-to-plasma concentration ratio of approximately 36:1 (up to 142:1 in some assays).
    *   **Elimination Half-Life:** Extremely long, averaging 62 hours (ranging from 46 to 78 hours) in healthy volunteers, and approximately 30 hours in maintenance renal transplant patients. Steady-state concentrations are achieved after 7 to 14 days of daily dosing.
*   **Therapeutic Drug Monitoring Required:**
    *   Mandatory for all solid organ transplant recipients due to a narrow therapeutic index and highly variable pharmacokinetics.
    *   **Whole Blood Testing:** Must be performed using EDTA whole blood (not plasma or serum) due to high erythrocyte partition, measured via liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) or immunoassay.
    *   **Target Trough Concentrations:** Trough levels (C0) are drawn immediately before the next scheduled dose.
        - In combination with cyclosporine and corticosteroids: Target trough levels are typically 4 to 12 mcg/L (or ng/mL).
        - Following cyclosporine withdrawal: Target trough levels are typically 12 to 20 mcg/L.
    *   **Dose Adjustments:** Due to the long half-life, dose adjustments should not be made more frequently than every 7 to 14 days to allow the drug to reach steady state.

#### Clinical Indications
*   **Primary Autoimmune / Inflammatory Indications:**
    *   **Primary Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP) (Off-Label):** Used as salvage therapy for chronic refractory ITP to increase platelet counts by restoring Treg cells and suppressing autoreactive B cells.
    *   **Autoimmune Lymphoproliferative Syndrome (ALPS) (Off-Label):** First-line therapy to control lymphoproliferation and autoimmune cytopenias.
    *   **Psoriasis (Off-Label):** Evaluated systemically or topically due to its ability to block keratinocyte stem cell hyperproliferation and T-cell activation.
*   **Oncology Indications:**
    *   **Perivascular Epithelioid Cell Tumor (PEComa):** Albumin-bound sirolimus nanoparticles are FDA-approved for adult patients with locally advanced unresectable or metastatic malignant PEComa.
    *   **Various Cancers (Off-Label / Investigational):** Evaluated in clinical trials (alone or with other agents like sunitinib in gastrointestinal stromal tumors, GIST) to target tumor cell division, metabolism, and angiogenesis.
*   **Transplant Rejection / Prophylaxis:**
    *   **Renal Transplantation:** FDA-approved for the prophylaxis of organ rejection in patients aged 13 years and older receiving allogeneic kidney transplants.
        - Low-to-moderate immunologic risk: Initially used in combination with cyclosporine and corticosteroids; cyclosporine is recommended to be withdrawn 2–4 months post-transplant.
        - High immunologic risk: Used in combination with cyclosporine and corticosteroids for the first 12 months.
    *   **Graft-versus-Host Disease (GvHD) (Off-Label):** Prophylaxis and treatment of acute and chronic GvHD following allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation.
*   **Specialized Pulmonary Indications:**
    *   **Lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM):** FDA-approved for the treatment of patients with LAM, a rare cystic lung disease affecting predominantly women of childbearing age, by stabilizing lung function and reducing the size of chylous effusions and lymphangioleiomyomas.

#### Adverse Effects & Toxicity
*   **Infection Risks & Opportunistic Pathogens:**
    *   **Opportunistic Pathogens:** Systemic immunosuppression increases the risk of serious bacterial, viral, fungal, and protozoal infections. Notable pathogens include Pneumocystis jirovecii (PCP), Cytomegalovirus (CMV), Aspergillus, and Candida.
    *   **BK Polyomavirus Nephropathy:** BK virus reactivation can occur, leading to polyomavirus-associated nephropathy (PVAN), which can cause interstitial nephritis and progressive graft failure.
    *   **Viral Reactivation:** Highly increased risk of herpes zoster (shingles) reactivation and Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV) flares.
*   **Malignancy Risks:**
    *   **Lymphoma & Lymphoproliferative Disorders:** Increased risk of developing lymphoma, post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD), particularly driven by Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV).
    *   **Skin Malignancies:** Elevated risk of developing squamous cell and basal cell carcinomas of the skin (patients must avoid excessive UV light exposure).
*   **Organ-Specific Toxicity:**
    *   **Hematologic:** Reversible, dose-dependent bone marrow suppression presenting as thrombocytopenia, anemia, and leukopenia/neutropenia.
    *   **Metabolic / Hyperlipidemia:** Extremely common. Causes significant elevations in serum triglycerides and cholesterol (hypercholesterolemia, hypertriglyceridemia), necessitating baseline and periodic lipid monitoring and often requiring treatment with lipid-lowering agents. It can also cause hyperglycemia and insulin resistance.
    *   **Wound Healing & Fluid Accumulation:** Impairs wound healing, leading to increased risk of surgical wound dehiscence, incisional hernia, fascial separation, lymphocele, and peripheral or facial edema.
    *   **Pulmonary:** Interstitial lung disease (ILD) / non-infectious pneumonitis, presenting as cough, dyspnea, pleural effusion, and pulmonary infiltrates, which can progress to severe pulmonary fibrosis and be fatal.
    *   **Renal:** Proteinuria and a decline in renal function. Although less nephrotoxic than calcineurin inhibitors, it can exacerbate calcineurin-inhibitor-induced nephrotoxicity and increase the risk of hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS), thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), and thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA).
    *   **Oral / Gastrointestinal:** Stomatitis/mucositis (painful mouth ulcers), diarrhea, abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting.
    *   **Dermatologic:** Acne, rash, and dry skin.
    *   **Reproductive (Male Infertility):** Azoospermia or oligospermia, which is usually reversible upon drug discontinuation.
*   **Black Box Warnings:**
    *   **Malignancies and Serious Infections:** Increased susceptibility to infection and the possible development of lymphoma and other malignancies. Only physicians experienced in immunosuppressive therapy and renal transplant management should prescribe the drug.
    *   **Liver Transplantation (Not Recommended):** Safety and efficacy have not been established in liver transplant patients. Early de novo use in liver transplantation is associated with excess mortality, graft loss, and hepatic artery thrombosis (HAT).
    *   **Lung Transplantation (Not Recommended):** Safety and efficacy have not been established in lung transplant patients. Its use in de novo lung transplantation has been associated with cases of severe, sometimes fatal, bronchial anastomotic dehiscence.

#### Pre-Screening & Contraindications
*   **Required Pre-Treatment Screening:**
    *   **Infection Screening:** Screen for active or latent systemic infections, including latent Tuberculosis (TST or IGRA), chronic Hepatitis B (HBsAg, anti-HBc), Hepatitis C, HIV, and baseline EBV/CMV serologies.
    *   **Metabolic & Lipid Panels:** Establish baseline serum cholesterol and triglyceride levels, fasting blood glucose, and HbA1c.
    *   **Renal and Hepatic Function:** Baseline GFR, serum creatinine, BUN, liver transaminases (ALT, AST), and bilirubin.
    *   **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** Establish baseline white blood cell, absolute neutrophil, hemoglobin, and platelet counts to monitor for cytopenias.
    *   **Pregnancy Screening:** Mandatory negative pregnancy test for female patients of childbearing potential.
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    *   Known immediate hypersensitivity to sirolimus, everolimus, other rapalogs, or any component of the formulation.
    *   De novo liver or lung transplantation (due to FDA Boxed Warnings).
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:**
    *   **Pregnancy Category:** Category C. Animal studies show embryofetal toxicity, including increased mortality and skeletal anomalies. Can cause fetal harm in humans.
    *   **Contraceptive Mandates:** Females of reproductive potential must use highly effective, dual-method contraception starting prior to initiation, during the entire duration of therapy, during any dose interruptions, and for at least 12 weeks after the final dose of sirolimus.
    *   **Lactation:** Strictly contraindicated. Excreted in breast milk with potential for serious adverse reactions (such as growth suppression, myelosuppression, and nephrotoxicity) in the nursing infant.
*   **Major Drug Interactions:**
    *   **CYP3A4 and P-gp Inhibitors (Dramatically elevate sirolimus levels, increasing toxicity risk):**
        - Azole antifungals: Ketoconazole, itraconazole, posaconazole (which increases sirolimus AUC by 70%), and voriconazole (co-administration is contraindicated as voriconazole increases sirolimus AUC 11-fold).
        - Macrolides: Erythromycin and clarithromycin.
        - Calcium channel blockers: Diltiazem and verapamil.
        - Antiretrovirals/Booster: Ritonavir and cobicistat.
        - Echinocandins: Micafungin is a mild CYP3A4 inhibitor that increases sirolimus AUC by 21%.
        - Other: Grapefruit and grapefruit juice (which blocks intestinal CYP3A4, causing toxic elevations).
    *   **CYP3A4 and P-gp Inducers (Dramatically decrease sirolimus levels, risking acute allograft rejection):**
        - Antimycobacterials: Rifampin and rifabutin.
        - Anticonvulsants: Phenytoin, carbamazepine, and phenobarbital.
        - Herbal supplements: St. John's Wort.
    *   **Cyclosporine Interaction:** Cyclosporine increases the absorption and exposure of sirolimus. To minimize this interaction, sirolimus must be administered 4 hours after the cyclosporine dose.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    *   **Rapamycin:** The original name of the natural product, widely used in research and longevity communities.
    *   **Easter Island / Rapa Nui:** The famous, remote island in the southeastern Pacific Ocean where the soil sample containing the producing organism was discovered.
    *   **G1-to-S Phase Arrest:** The classic pharmacological hallmark of mTOR inhibition, stopping T-cell division without inhibiting calcineurin or cytokine transcription.
    *   **Non-nephrotoxic sparing alternative:** Historically marketed as an alternative to calcineurin inhibitors to avoid kidney damage, though in practice it is often used with them or has other renal liabilities.
*   **Naming Conventions:**
    *   **Sirolimus (INN / USAN):** Generic name representing the compound.
    *   **Rapamycin:** Derived from Rapa Nui (Easter Island) + "mycin" (indicating its origin from a soil-dwelling actinomycete).
    *   **Rapamune:** The primary trade name used by Pfizer (originally Wyeth) for transplant immunosuppression.
    *   **AY-22989 and NSC-226080:** Historical pre-clinical investigational code numbers.
