### Cyclosporine
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class:**
    *   First-in-class calcineurin inhibitor (CNI); lipophilic cyclic polypeptide immunomodulatory agent.
*   **Target Cytokine, Receptor, or Immune Cell:**
    *   Intracellular target: Cyclophilin A (CyP-A) and the serine/threonine phosphatase Calcineurin.
    *   Target cell populations: Helper CD4+ and cytotoxic CD8+ T lymphocytes.
    *   Target cytokine pathways: Interleukin-2 (IL-2) transcription.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    *   **Lipophilic Passive Diffusion:** Cyclosporine is a neutral, highly lipophilic cyclic undecapeptide. Due to its hydrophobic structure, it easily diffuses across the host cell membrane of T lymphocytes into the cytoplasm.
    *   **Binding to Cyclophilin A:** Once inside the cytoplasm, cyclosporine binds with high affinity to its specific immunophilin receptor protein, Cyclophilin A (CyP-A), forming a stable, active drug-receptor complex (Cyclosporine-Cyclophilin complex).
    *   **Calcineurin Inhibition:** Under normal physiological conditions of T-cell activation, the engagement of the T-cell receptor (TCR) by antigen-presenting cells triggers a signal transduction cascade that elevates intracellular calcium levels. This calcium binds to calmodulin, forming a calcium-calmodulin complex that binds to and activates the heterodimeric serine/threonine phosphatase Calcineurin. Activated calcineurin then dephosphorylates the cytoplasmic transcription factor NFAT (Nuclear Factor of Activated T cells), exposing its nuclear localization signals.
    *   **Blockade of NFAT Nuclear Translocation:** The Cyclosporine-Cyclophilin complex binds directly and competitively to Calcineurin, completely inhibiting its phosphatase activity. As a result, cytoplasmic NFAT cannot be dephosphorylated and remains in its phosphorylated, inactive state, preventing its translocation into the nucleus.
    *   **Arrest of Cytokine Gene Transcription:** In the absence of nuclear NFAT, the transcription of Interleukin-2 (IL-2)—the primary autocrine and paracrine growth factor for T cells—is completely arrested. Transcription of other critical T-cell activation cytokines, including Interleukin-3 (IL-3), Interleukin-4 (IL-4), Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha (TNF-α), Interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), and Granulocyte-Macrophage Colony-Stimulating Factor (GM-CSF), is also severely suppressed.
*   **Downstream Immunological Effects:**
    *   **Arrest of T-Cell Proliferation:** The profound blockade of IL-2 expression prevents the G1-to-S phase cell cycle transition of antigen-activated CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, halting their clonal expansion and downstream adaptive immune cascade.
    *   **Inhibition of Cytotoxic T Lymphocyte (CTL) Generation:** Prevents the maturation, activation, and cytolytic function of CD8+ CTLs, blocking cellular cytotoxicity against allografts.
    *   **Suppression of T-Dependent B-Cell Activation:** While cyclosporine does not directly inhibit B lymphocytes at therapeutic doses, the suppression of helper T-cell-derived lymphokines (such as IL-4 and IL-5) severely impairs T-dependent B-cell activation, clonal expansion, and antibody synthesis.
    *   **Reduction of Macrophage Activation:** Lower levels of secreted IFN-γ lead to reduced recruitment, chemotaxis, and microbiocidal/antigen-presenting activation of tissue macrophages and monocytes.
    *   **Selective Sparing of T Regulatory Cells (Tregs):** At standard therapeutic concentrations, cyclosporine may preserve or selectively spare the homeostatic function of CD4+CD25+FoxP3+ regulatory T cells, supporting the maintenance of immunological tolerance.

#### Pharmacokinetics & Administration
*   **Route of Administration:**
    *   **Oral:** Available as capsules and oral solutions. Formulated in two distinct types: standard oil-based preparations (Sandimmune) and microemulsion preparations (Neoral, Gengraf).
    *   **Intravenous:** Administered as a slow IV infusion over 2 to 6 hours. The IV formulation is dissolved in a vehicle consisting of ethanol and polyoxyethylated castor oil (Cremophor EL), which is highly immunogenic and can cause severe anaphylactoid hypersensitivity reactions.
    *   **Ophthalmic:** Available as a 0.05% topical emulsion (Restasis) used to treat severe dry eye (keratoconjunctivitis sicca) by reducing local lymphocyte-mediated inflammation.
*   **Metabolism & Clearance Pathway:**
    *   **Hepatic Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized in the liver by Cytochrome P450 CYP3A4 and CYP3A5 enzymes. It undergoes biotransformation via mono- and di-hydroxylation, demethylation, and cyclization, producing more than 30 metabolites with minimal immunosuppressive activity.
    *   **Biliary Excretion:** Excreted primarily in the bile into the feces, which accounts for over 90% of drug elimination.
    *   **Renal Clearance:** Plays a minor role in drug elimination. Less than 6% of the administered dose is excreted in the urine, with less than 1% excreted as unchanged parent drug. It is not significantly cleared by hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis.
*   **Onset of Action & Half-Life:**
    *   **Absorption and Peak (Tmax):** Sandimmune exhibits erratic, slow, and bile-dependent absorption (bioavailability ~30%), with a Tmax of 3 to 4 hours. Neoral (the microemulsion) spontaneously forms a microemulsion in gastric fluids, resulting in rapid, consistent, bile-independent absorption with a Tmax of 1.5 to 2 hours, higher Cmax, and improved bioavailability (~45%).
    *   **Plasma Protein Binding:** Highly bound (~90%) to plasma proteins, primarily to low-density and high-density lipoproteins and erythrocytes.
    *   **Volume of Distribution:** Large, typically ranging from 3.5 to 5 L/kg, reflecting extensive uptake and sequestration in tissues (such as liver, fat, and kidney).
    *   **Elimination Half-Life:** Biphasic, with a terminal elimination half-life ranging from 8 to 20 hours (average of approximately 19 hours).
*   **Therapeutic Drug Monitoring Required:**
    *   **Narrow Therapeutic Index:** Highly variable pharmacokinetics and narrow therapeutic window make routine TDM mandatory.
    *   **Whole Blood Assays:** Must be performed using whole blood (by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry, LC-MS/MS, or monoclonal antibody immunoassay), NOT plasma or serum, because cyclosporine highly partitions into erythrocytes in a temperature-dependent manner.
    *   **Monitoring Parameters:**
        - **Trough Levels (C0):** Drawn immediately prior to the next scheduled dose. Target range is typically 100 to 400 ng/mL, depending on the transplant type, time post-transplantation, and concomitant immunosuppression.
        - **2-Hour Post-Dose Levels (C2):** Strongly correlates with the area under the concentration-time curve (AUC), providing a superior estimate of systemic exposure. It is highly predictive of acute rejection risk and nephrotoxicity. Target C2 levels are typically 800 to 1500 ng/mL in the early post-transplant period.

#### Clinical Indications
*   **Primary Autoimmune / Inflammatory Indications:**
    *   **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Severe, active, erosive rheumatoid arthritis in adults who have had an inadequate response to methotrexate or other conventional DMARDs.
    *   **Psoriasis:** Treatment of severe, recalcitrant, plaque psoriasis in immunocompetent adults who are unresponsive to systemic therapy (methotrexate, retinoids, PUVA).
    *   **Nephrotic Syndrome:** Treatment of steroid-resistant or steroid-dependent nephrotic syndrome (such as minimal change disease, focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, or membranous glomerulonephritis) in pediatric and adult patients.
    *   **Severe Acute Ulcerative Colitis (Off-Label):** Used as salvage "rescue" therapy (via continuous IV infusion) in patients with severe acute colitis who are refractory to high-dose intravenous corticosteroids, to avoid emergency colectomy.
    *   **Uveitis:** Treatment of severe, non-infectious posterior or pan-uveitis, particularly in patients at risk of blindness.
*   **Oncology Indications:**
    *   N/A
*   **Transplant Rejection / Prophylaxis:**
    *   **Solid Organ Transplantation:** Prophylaxis of organ rejection in patients receiving allogeneic kidney, liver, heart, and pancreas transplants. It is typically used as baseline maintenance therapy in combination with corticosteroids and an antimetabolite (e.g., mycophenolate mofetil).
    *   **Graft-versus-Host Disease (GvHD):** Prophylaxis and treatment of acute and chronic GvHD in patients undergoing allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT), often paired with methotrexate.

#### Adverse Effects & Toxicity
*   **Infection Risks & Opportunistic Pathogens:**
    *   **Opportunistic Pathogens:** High risk of developing Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP), cytomegalovirus (CMV) tissue-invasive disease, invasive aspergillosis, and cryptococcal meningitis.
    *   **Viral Reactivations:** Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) reactivation, Polyomavirus (BK virus) nephropathy (which can lead to renal allograft failure), and Varicella Zoster Virus (shingles) reactivation.
*   **Malignancy Risks:**
    *   **Post-Transplant Lymphoproliferative Disorder (PTLD):** An EBV-driven B-cell lymphoproliferative disease that can progress to aggressive B-cell lymphoma.
    *   **Non-Melanoma Skin Cancers:** Increased risk of squamous cell carcinoma and basal cell carcinoma of the skin.
*   **Organ-Specific Toxicity:**
    *   **Nephrotoxicity:**
        - *Acute (Reversible):* Dose-dependent afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction, leading to increased renal vascular resistance, decreased renal blood flow, and a reduction in GFR. It presents as an acute, reversible rise in serum creatinine and BUN, which resolves upon dose reduction.
        - *Chronic (Irreversible):* Progressive obliterative arteriolopathy (arteriolar hyalinosis), striped interstitial fibrosis, and tubular atrophy. This leads to permanent, irreversible renal impairment and can progress to end-stage renal disease (ESRD).
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Severe, dose-related Hypertension develops in up to 50% of transplant patients due to renal vasoconstriction, increased vascular tone, and sodium retention. Hyperlipidemia and fluid retention are also common.
    *   **Neurotoxicity:** Tremor (very common, up to 50% of patients), burning paresthesias (particularly in the hands and feet), headache, sleep disturbances, confusion, visual disturbances, seizures, and rarely, posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES).
    *   **Dermatologic / Mucosal:** Gingival Hyperplasia (severe, painful overgrowth of gum tissue, exacerbated by poor oral hygiene) and Hirsutism (excessive male-pattern hair growth on the face and body).
    *   **Metabolic / Electrolyte:** Hyperkalemia (due to suppression of aldosterone secretion in the distal tubule), hypomagnesemia (due to renal wasting), hyperuricemia (which can precipitate acute gouty arthritis), and mild metabolic acidosis.
    *   **Hepatotoxicity:** Reversible, dose-dependent elevations in serum transaminases (ALT, AST) and bilirubin.
*   **Black Box Warnings:**
    *   **Severe Nephrotoxicity / Renal Impairment:** Risk of acute and chronic, progressive renal injury.
    *   **Increased Risk of Serious Infections:** Susceptibility to opportunistic pathogens and fatal sepsis.
    *   **Increased Risk of Malignancies:** Elevated risk of lymphomas and skin cancers.
    *   **Hypertension:** Risk of severe, uncontrolled systemic hypertension.
    *   **Formulation Bioequivalence Warning:** Sandimmune and Neoral/Gengraf are not bioequivalent and cannot be interchanged on a 1:1 basis without close clinical supervision and therapeutic drug monitoring.

#### Pre-Screening & Contraindications
*   **Required Pre-Treatment Screening:**
    *   **Renal Function:** At least two baseline serum creatinine and BUN measurements to establish a true baseline GFR.
    *   **Blood Pressure:** Multiple baseline readings.
    *   **Lipid Panel:** Fasting cholesterol and triglycerides.
    *   **Liver Function Panel:** Baseline AST, ALT, alkaline phosphatase, and bilirubin.
    *   **Electrolytes:** Baseline potassium, magnesium, and uric acid.
    *   **Infectious Screening:** Serological screening for Hepatitis B (HBsAg, anti-HBc), Hepatitis C, HIV, latent Tuberculosis (IGRA or TST), EBV, and CMV.
    *   **Pregnancy Screening:** Mandatory negative pregnancy test for female patients of childbearing potential.
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    *   Known immediate hypersensitivity to cyclosporine or polyoxyethylated castor oil (Cremophor EL, present in the IV formulation).
    *   Uncontrolled, severe systemic hypertension.
    *   Active, uncontrolled severe infections.
    *   Pre-existing severe renal impairment (except in patients being treated for steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome).
    *   Active malignancy (except non-melanoma skin cancer undergoing curative treatment).
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:**
    *   **Pregnancy Category:** Category C. It crosses the placental barrier. Human exposure is associated with an increased risk of low birth weight, premature delivery, and neonatal thrombocytopenia/leukopenia. Use in pregnant women only if the clinical benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risks to the fetus.
    *   **Contraceptive Requirements:** Female patients of childbearing potential must use highly effective, double-method contraception during therapy and for at least 3 months following drug discontinuation.
    *   **Lactation:** Strictly contraindicated. Cyclosporine is excreted in breast milk and poses a significant risk of severe adverse events (such as myelosuppression and nephrotoxicity) in the nursing infant.
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:**
    *   **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (Dramatically elevate cyclosporine levels, increasing the risk of severe nephrotoxicity):**
        - Azole antifungals: ketoconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, posaconazole.
        - Macrolide antibiotics: erythromycin, clarithromycin (but NOT azithromycin).
        - Calcium channel blockers: diltiazem, verapamil.
        - Antiretroviral protease inhibitors: ritonavir, cobicistat.
        - Other agents: amiodarone, quinidine, chloroquine, and grapefruit juice.
        - Streptogramins: quinupristin-dalfopristin (significantly inhibits CYP3A4, requiring cyclosporine dosage reduction).
    *   **CYP3A4 Inducers (Accelerate cyclosporine clearance, dropping serum levels below therapeutic range and risking acute transplant rejection):**
        - Anticonvulsants: phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbital.
        - Antimycobacterials: rifampin, rifabutin.
        - Herbal supplements: St. John's Wort.
    *   **Synergistic Nephrotoxicity (Concomitant use with other nephrotoxic drugs exponentially increases the risk of acute kidney injury):**
        - Aminoglycosides (gentamicin, tobramycin, kanamycin, neomycin, amikacin).
        - Amphotericin B (C-AMB and lipid formulations, though lipid forms are less nephrotoxic, synergism still occurs).
        - Vancomycin.
        - NSAIDS (ibuprofen, naproxen, celecoxib).
        - Cisplatin.
        - ACE Inhibitors (e.g., lisinopril).
    *   **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors (Statins):** Cyclosporine potently inhibits the hepatic uptake transporter OATP1B1 and CYP3A4, leading to massive increases in the plasma concentrations of statins (especially simvastatin, lovastatin, and atorvastatin). This dramatically elevates the risk of severe rhabdomyolysis, myopathy, and acute tubular necrosis.
    *   **Echinocandins (Caspofungin):** Caspofungin slightly increases cyclosporine levels, and concurrent administration has been associated with significant elevations in liver transaminases. This combination should be avoided if possible; if necessary, close monitoring of liver function is mandatory. Micafungin has also been shown to increase cyclosporine levels.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    *   **"Calcineurin Inhibitor (CNI) Prototype":** Cyclosporine is the historic, first-in-class prototype of calcineurin inhibitors that revolutionized transplant medicine.
    *   **"Gingival Hyperplasia & Hirsutism":** The classic, pathognomonic clinical pair of cosmetic side effects that distinguishes cyclosporine from tacrolimus (which does not cause gingival hyperplasia or hirsutism, but instead causes alopecia).
    *   **"Cremophor EL Hypersensitivity":** Refers to the polyoxyethylated castor oil vehicle in the IV formulation that can trigger life-threatening anaphylactoid reactions.
*   **Historical Milestones:**
    *   **Tolypocladium inflatum Soil Discovery:** Discovered in 1969 by Sandoz scientists from a soil sample collected on the Hardangervidda mountain plateau in Norway. It was isolated from the fungus Tolypocladium inflatum (initially misidentified as Trichoderma polysporum).
    *   **Sir Roy Calne & Modern Transplantation:** Sir Roy Calne performed the first successful clinical renal and hepatic transplants using cyclosporine in the late 1970s. Its introduction dramatically increased 1-year graft survival rates from ~50% to over 80%, transforming clinical transplantation from an experimental, high-mortality procedure into a highly successful, routine life-saving therapy.
*   **Naming Conventions:**
    *   **Chemical Structure:** It is a lipophilic cyclic polypeptide consisting of 11 amino acids (undecapeptide). It contains several N-methylated peptide bonds and a unique, unusual 9-carbon amino acid called Bmt ((4R)-4-\[(E)-2-butenyl\]-4-methyl-L-threonine) at position 1, which is absolutely essential for binding to cyclophilin and inhibiting calcineurin.
    *   **Sandimmune vs. Neoral Formulation Shift:** Sandimmune is the original formulation, which is a standard oil-based solution that requires bile salts for emulsification and absorption in the GI tract, resulting in extremely erratic, unpredictable bioavailability. Neoral (and Gengraf) is a microemulsion formulation that spontaneously emulsifies into microscopic droplets upon contact with gastric fluids. This provides rapid, consistent, bile-independent absorption, significantly higher peak blood levels, shorter Tmax, and vastly reduced inter- and intra-patient pharmacokinetic variability.
