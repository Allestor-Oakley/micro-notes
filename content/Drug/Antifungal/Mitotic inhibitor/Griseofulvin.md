### Griseofulvin
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:**
    - Penicillium-derived antifungal agent; a highly insoluble fungistatic drug derived from a species of Penicillium (specifically Penicillium griseofulvum).
*   **Target Organisms:**
    - Various species of dermatophytes, specifically Microsporum, Epidermophyton, and Trichophyton. It has no effect on bacteria or on other fungi.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    - Griseofulvin enters susceptible fungal cells and inhibits microtubule function, thereby disrupting the assembly of the mitotic spindle. This blocks fungal mitosis, leading to a prominent morphological manifestation of producing multinucleate cells. Although its effects are similar to those of colchicine and the vinca alkaloids, griseofulvin's binding sites on the microtubular protein are distinct. In addition to binding to tubulin, it interacts with microtubule-associated proteins.
    - Systemically, the absorbed drug is keratophilic; it is deposited in newly forming skin where it binds to keratin, protecting the skin, hair, and nails from new infection. Because its action is to prevent infection of these new structures, it must be administered for a prolonged duration to allow the replacement of infected keratin by these resistant structures.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:**
    - Fungal microtubules, tubulin, and microtubule-associated proteins.
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):**
    - Fungistatic in vitro.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Gene Mutations:**
    - Although clinical failure of ringworm lesions to improve is not rare, isolates from these patients usually are still susceptible to griseofulvin in vitro. Fungi do not transfer resistance genes, but resistance can occur through protective mutations or biofilms.

#### Pharmacokinetics (ADME)
*   **Absorption & Bioavailability:**
    - Practically insoluble in water. Oral absorption is poor and highly variable, limited primarily by the rates of dissolution and disaggregation.
    - Oral absorption is significantly improved when the drug is taken with a fatty meal.
    - Due to bioavailability limitations, micro-sized (e.g., Grifulvin V) and ultra-micro-sized (e.g., Gris-Peg) powders are used in commercial preparations. The bioavailability of the ultra-microcrystalline preparation is said to be 50% greater than that of the conventional micro-sized powder, though this is not always true.
    - Barbiturates decrease griseofulvin absorption from the gastrointestinal tract.
*   **Distribution & Tissue Penetration:**
    - Widely distributed systemically and selectively taken up by newly formed skin, hair, and nails, where it binds to and concentrates in keratin. It is retained in the skin for much longer than in plasma.
*   **Metabolism & CYP450 Dynamics:**
    - Extensively metabolized in the liver, with the primary metabolite being 6-methylgriseofulvin.
    - Potently induces hepatic Cytochrome P450 enzymes, leading to clinically important drug interactions by accelerating the metabolism of other co-administered agents.
*   **Excretion & Half-Life:**
    - Plasma elimination half-life is approximately 1 day (24 hours).
    - Approximately 50% of the oral dose is excreted in the urine within 5 days, mostly in the form of metabolites (primarily 6-methylgriseofulvin).

#### Clinical Indications & Spectrum
*   **Primary Clinical Indications:**
    - Mycotic diseases of the skin, hair, and nails caused by Microsporum, Trichophyton, or Epidermophyton (dermatophytosis/ringworm).
    - Tinea capitis in children: Remains the drug of choice for efficacy, safety, and availability as an oral suspension. Efficacy is best for tinea capitis caused by Microsporum canis, Microsporum audouinii, Trichophyton schoenleinii, and Trichophyton verrucosum.
    - Ringworm of the glabrous skin, tinea cruris, and tinea corporis caused by Microsporum canis, Trichophyton rubrum, Trichophyton verrucosum, and Epidermophyton floccosum.
    - Tinea of the hands (tinea manuum) caused by Trichophyton rubrum and Trichophyton mentagrophytes.
    - Tinea of the beard (tinea barbae) caused by Trichophyton species.
    - Tinea pedis (athlete's foot), particularly the vesicular form (most commonly due to Trichophyton mentagrophytes) and the hyperkeratotic type (due to Trichophyton rubrum), when local topical therapy is ineffective (topical therapy is sufficient for most cases of tinea pedis).
*   **Specific Pathogen Spectrum:**
    - Microsporum canis
    - Microsporum audouinii
    - Trichophyton rubrum
    - Trichophyton mentagrophytes
    - Trichophyton verrucosum
    - Trichophyton schoenleinii
    - Epidermophyton floccosum
    - (Completely inactive against bacteria and other non-dermatophyte fungi).

#### Adverse Effects & Boxed Warnings
*   **Common Side Effects:**
    - Gastrointestinal tract upsets, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal discomfort.
    - Headache.
    - Photosensitivity skin reactions.
*   **Severe / Life-Threatening Adverse Effects:**
    - Allergic syndrome resembling serum sickness.
    - Serious skin reactions (such as severe rashes, fever).
    - Lupus-like syndrome.
    - Hepatotoxicity.
*   **Pathognomonic / Highly Specific Side Effects:**
    - Severe photosensitivity skin reactions (exaggerated sunburn-like reactions upon exposure to sunlight).
    - Allergic syndrome resembling serum sickness.
    - Lupus-like syndrome.
*   **Black Box Warnings:**
    - N/A

#### Contraindications & Drug Interactions
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    - Pregnancy: The drug must not be given to pregnant women.
*   **Relative Contraindications & Cautions:**
    - Prolonged therapy requirement: Griseofulvin must be administered for 2 to 6 weeks for skin and hair infections to allow the replacement of infected keratin, and for several months for nail infections to allow regrowth of the protected nail structure. Nail infections are frequently followed by relapse.
    - Non-dermatophyte infections: Should not be used for infections not caused by Microsporum, Trichophyton, or Epidermophyton, as it has no activity against other fungi.
    - Dose escalation caution: Trichophyton rubrum and Trichophyton mentagrophytes infections may require higher-than-conventional doses of griseofulvin, increasing the risk of adverse effects.
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:**
    - Barbiturates (e.g., phenobarbital): Decrease griseofulvin absorption from the gastrointestinal tract.
    - Warfarin: Griseofulvin potently induces Cytochrome P450 enzymes, which accelerates the hepatic clearance of warfarin, reducing its anticoagulant efficacy and requiring dose adjustment.
    - Phenobarbital: Interacts with griseofulvin (decreased absorption and altered clearance).
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:**
    - Strictly contraindicated during pregnancy. The sources explicitly state that the drug should not be given to pregnant women.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "Keratophilic drug," highlighting its selective deposition and concentration in newly formed keratin structures of the skin, hair, and nails.
    - "Mitotic spindle disruption," describing its core cellular mechanism.
    - "Production of multinucleate cells," the pathognomonic cell morphology seen under microtubule and mitosis inhibition.
    - "Derived from Penicillium," indicating its microbiological source.
    - "Fatty meal absorption," the standard clinical counseling point to enhance bioavailability.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - While most systemic antifungals disrupt fungal membranes or cell wall synthesis (such as azoles, polyenes, and echinocandins), griseofulvin is a unique exception that enters the fungal cell to target tubulin and disrupt mitosis.
    - Although administered systemically (orally), it has absolutely no efficacy against deeply invasive or systemic mycoses (such as systemic candidiasis or cryptococcosis); its activity is strictly limited to superficial dermatophyte infections.
    - Unlike most drugs which are cleared rapidly from systemic sites, griseofulvin remains concentrated and bound within keratin structures of the skin, hair, and nails for months after systemic clearance from the blood.
