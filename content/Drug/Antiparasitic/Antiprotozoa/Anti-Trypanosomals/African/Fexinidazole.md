### Fexinidazole
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:** Biopharmaceutical antiparasitic; 5-nitroimidazole derivative.
*   **Target Organisms:** [[Trypanosoma brucei|Trypanosoma brucei gambiense]], [[Trypanosoma brucei|Trypanosoma brucei rhodesiense]], [[Trypanosoma cruzi]], [[Leishmania donovani]], Leishmania major.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:** Fexinidazole is a prodrug that requires bioreductive activation within the target parasite to exert its antimicrobial effects. It is activated by a parasite-encoded, oxygen-insensitive, bacteria-like Type I nitroreductase (NTR) enzyme. This bioreductive pathway reduces the nitro group of fexinidazole and its active oxidative metabolites—fexinidazole sulfoxide (M1) and fexinidazole sulfone (M2)—to produce highly reactive, toxic amine/radical intermediates (including reactive oxygen and nitrogen species). These reactive intermediates bind to and cause extensive damage to the parasite's genomic DNA, proteins, and other crucial macromolecules. This halts replication by inhibiting DNA, RNA, and protein synthesis, ultimately leading to parasite cell death.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:** Parasite-encoded oxygen-insensitive Type I nitroreductase (NTR); genomic DNA, proteins, and cellular macromolecules of the parasite.
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):** Parasiticidal (trypanocidal) against replicating and non-replicating forms.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Gene Mutations:** Resistance to fexinidazole is primarily mediated by loss-of-function mutations or down-regulation of the gene encoding the Type I nitroreductase (NTR) enzyme (specifically the NTR gene in Trypanosoma brucei, and potentially equivalent genes in T. cruzi or Leishmania). Parasites lacking or possessing mutated NTR are unable to perform the bioreductive activation of fexinidazole and its metabolites, rendering them highly resistant. Cross-resistance has been observed in vitro between fexinidazole and other nitroheterocyclic drugs (e.g., nifurtimox, benznidazole) that share this activation pathway, but no cross-resistance is expected with pentamidine, eflornithine, or suramin due to different targets.

#### Pharmacokinetics (ADME)
*   **Absorption & Bioavailability:** Well absorbed after oral administration. Bioavailability is significantly influenced by food; administration under fasted conditions results in low absorption, whereas taking fexinidazole with food (such as a high-fat meal or standard field-adapted meal) increases drug absorption and plasma concentrations of the parent drug and its active metabolites by approximately 2-fold (200%). Thus, it must always be taken with food. Tmax of fexinidazole is approximately 2 to 5 hours, while the active metabolites reach Tmax at 2 to 5 hours (sulfoxide M1) and 18 to 24 hours (sulfone M2) post-dose.
*   **Distribution & Tissue Penetration:** Highly distributed into tissues. The apparent volume of distribution (Vd) of fexinidazole is extremely large (approximately 3222 ± 1199 L), indicating extensive tissue uptake. It exhibits excellent penetration across the blood-brain barrier (BBB) into the central nervous system (CNS), achieving therapeutic concentrations in both the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and brain parenchyma (including cortex and hippocampus), which allows it to be effective in treating the late (meningoencephalitic) stage of human African trypanosomiasis (HAT). Fexinidazole is highly bound to plasma proteins (98%), whereas its metabolites M1 (sulfoxide) and M2 (sulfone) are moderately bound at 41% and 57%, respectively.
*   **Metabolism & CYP450 Dynamics:** Fexinidazole is a prodrug that undergoes rapid and extensive first-pass hepatic metabolism via oxidative pathways to form two major active metabolites: fexinidazole sulfoxide (M1) and fexinidazole sulfone (M2). The conversion is mediated by multiple enzymes, including Cytochrome P450 isoforms CYP1A2, CYP2D6, CYP2C19, CYP3A4, CYP3A5, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, and flavin monooxygenase-3 (FMO-3). In vivo, M2 (sulfone) is the predominant active moiety responsible for the majority of the trypanocidal activity due to its high and prolonged exposure (relative in vivo exposure ratio of fexinidazole:M1:M2 is 1:100:700). Fexinidazole is an inhibitor of CYP3A4, CYP1A2, CYP2C19, and CYP2B6, and a weak in vitro inducer of CYP3A4/5. However, in vivo, fexinidazole acts as a significant inducer of CYP3A4 (decreases midazolam exposure by 57% AUC).
*   **Excretion & Half-Life:** Elimination is almost completely extra-renal. Only a very small fraction (<3.15%) of the administered dose is excreted in the urine, primarily as M1 and M2, with only traces of the parent drug. The major route of excretion is fecal/biliary, with M1 and M2 representing the main excreted forms. In healthy subjects, following the full 10-day regimen, the mean terminal elimination half-lives are approximately 14 to 15 hours for the parent fexinidazole, 15 to 16 hours for M1 (sulfoxide), and 23 hours for M2 (sulfone).
*   **Dosage Adjustments:**
    *   **Renal Impairment:** Since renal excretion is a minor pathway (<3.15%), no dosage adjustment is required in patients with mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment. Fexinidazole is unlikely to be removed by hemodialysis.
    *   **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment. No specific dosing recommendations are available for mild-to-moderate hepatic impairment, and caution is advised.
    *   **Pediatric population:** Safety and efficacy are established in pediatric patients aged 6 years and older weighing at least 20 kg. Dosage is weight-based. Not recommended for children under 6 years or weighing less than 20 kg due to a lack of data.

#### Clinical Indications & Spectrum
*   **Primary Clinical Indications:**
    *   **Human African Trypanosomiasis (HAT):** Treatment of both the first-stage (hemolymphatic) and second-stage (meningoencephalitic) Human African Trypanosomiasis (HAT) caused by Trypanosoma brucei gambiense in adults and pediatric patients aged 6 years and older weighing at least 20 kg.
    *   **Limitations of Use:** Due to decreased efficacy observed in patients with severe second-stage HAT (defined by CSF white blood cell count >100 cells/mm3 at baseline), fexinidazole should only be used in these patients if there are no other treatment options (such as Nifurtimox-Eflornithine Combination Therapy, NECT) available.
*   **Specific Pathogen Spectrum:** Trypanosoma brucei gambiense, Trypanosoma brucei rhodesiense, Trypanosoma cruzi, Leishmania donovani.
*   **Off-Label / Niche Uses:**
    *   **Trypanosoma brucei rhodesiense HAT:** Investigational use for East African sleeping sickness.
    *   **Chagas Disease (Trypanosoma cruzi):** Under investigation for chronic indeterminate Chagas disease (high efficacy observed but limited by cumulative dose-dependent hepatotoxicity and neutropenia if used for more than 14 days).
    *   **Visceral Leishmaniasis (Leishmania donovani):** Investigational use as oral therapy.

#### Adverse Effects & Boxed Warnings
*   **Common Side Effects:** Nausea, vomiting, headache, insomnia, asthenia, dizziness, decreased appetite, dyspepsia, tremor, abdominal pain, diarrhea, and metallic taste. Vomiting is reported to occur at higher frequencies in pediatric patients.
*   **Severe / Life-Threatening Adverse Effects:**
    *   **Neuropsychiatric Events:** Insomnia, anxiety, personality changes, depression, hallucinations, panic attacks, and suicidal ideation / thoughts of self-harm.
    *   **Hepatotoxicity:** Drug-induced liver injury, marked by significant elevations of transaminases (ALT/AST), which can progress to acute liver failure (especially in Cockayne syndrome patients).
    *   **QT Prolongation:** Clinically significant prolongation of the QT interval, predisposing patients to ventricular arrhythmias (such as torsades de pointes).
    *   **Neutropenia / Bone Marrow Suppression:** Decreases in white blood cell count (neutropenia), which are cumulative and duration-dependent.
*   **Pathognomonic / Highly Specific Side Effects:** Disulfiram-like reaction when co-administered with alcohol. Severe, irreversible, and potentially fatal acute liver failure specifically in patients with Cockayne syndrome.
*   **Black Box Warnings:** N/A (None officially designated by the FDA, but prominent warnings exist for severe second-stage HAT efficacy and psychiatric reactions).

#### Contraindications & Drug Interactions
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    *   Known hypersensitivity to fexinidazole or any nitroimidazole-class drugs (e.g., metronidazole, tinidazole).
    *   Patients with Cockayne syndrome (risk of irreversible, fatal hepatotoxicity).
    *   Severe hepatic impairment.
*   **Relative Contraindications & Cautions:**
    *   **History of Psychiatric Disorders:** Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of depression, anxiety, psychosis, or other psychiatric illnesses, as fexinidazole can precipitate or worsen severe neuropsychiatric symptoms.
    *   **Hepatic or Renal Disease:** Caution in patients with underlying hepatic or renal impairment.
    *   **Cardiac Risk Factors:** Avoid in patients with congenital long QT syndrome, history of symptomatic cardiac arrhythmias (e.g., torsades de pointes, bradyarrhythmias), uncompensated heart failure, or uncorrected hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia due to the risk of QT prolongation.
    *   **Pregnancy (First Trimester):** Avoid use during the first trimester of pregnancy due to risks of embryo-fetal toxicity.
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:**
    *   **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Co-administration is contraindicated or strongly avoided with other drugs known to prolong the QT interval or induce bradycardia (e.g., antiarrhythmics like amiodarone, sotalol; neuroleptics/phenothiazines; fluoroquinolones; imidazole/triazole antifungals; pentamidine; IV erythromycin; tricyclic antidepressants; terfenadine; astemizole; and beta-blockers).
    *   **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Fexinidazole inhibits and in vivo induces CYP3A4, altering the clearance of CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., midazolam, lovastatin). Avoid concomitant use or monitor closely.
    *   **CYP2B6 Substrates:** Fexinidazole inhibits CYP2B6, potentially increasing the exposure of substrates like bupropion and efavirenz.
    *   **CYP1A2 and CYP2C19 Substrates:** Use caution as fexinidazole inhibits these enzymes, potentially increasing concentrations of duloxetine (CYP1A2) or lansoprazole (CYP2C19).
    *   **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors or Inducers:** Avoid co-administration with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., voriconazole, ketoconazole) as they can increase fexinidazole levels, or strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St. John's wort) as they can drastically reduce fexinidazole concentrations and efficacy.
    *   **Alcohol:** Avoid alcohol during treatment and for at least 48 hours after the last dose due to the risk of a disulfiram-like reaction (nausea, vomiting, flushing, tachycardia).
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:** Category C. Available data are insufficient to establish a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, but animal studies have shown embryo-fetal toxicity (increased abortions and skeletal malformations in rabbits and rats). Pregnancy testing is recommended for females of reproductive potential before initiating therapy. Advise patients to use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 2 weeks after the last dose. Avoid use in the first trimester. Breastfeeding is not recommended during therapy.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** First all-oral treatment for sleeping sickness, DNDi (Drugs for Neglected Diseases initiative) success, 10-day oral regimen, Type I nitroreductase activation, blood-brain barrier penetrant, Cockayne syndrome hazard, disulfiram-like reaction.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   Unlike eflornithine or NECT (nifurtimox-eflornithine combination therapy), which are highly effective but require complex intravenous infusions, systematic hospitalization, and skilled nursing staff in specialized units, fexinidazole is a completely oral 10-day monotherapy that can be administered at the primary healthcare level in remote, resource-limited settings.
    *   Unlike other nitroimidazoles like metronidazole, which have poor activity in the brain parenchyma at standard doses, fexinidazole has exceptional blood-brain barrier penetration and maintains highly therapeutic levels of its active sulfone (M2) metabolite in the CNS, allowing oral treatment of late (stage 2, meningoencephalitic) human African trypanosomiasis.
    *   Fexinidazole is one of the very few drugs that was rediscovered through "compound mining" (original development by Hoechst was stopped in the 1980s) and brought to clinical success entirely through a public-private partnership (DNDi and Sanofi) specifically for a neglected tropical disease.
    *   While in vitro assays predicted that fexinidazole would act as a CYP3A4 inhibitor and increase midazolam exposure, human in vivo phase I studies unexpectedly demonstrated the opposite: fexinidazole acts as a potent CYP3A4 inducer in vivo, significantly reducing midazolam exposure (AUC reduced by 57%).
