### Niclosamide
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:**
    - Salicylamide derivative; specifically a halogenated salicylanilide derivative introduced in the 1960s for human use as a taeniacide.
*   **Target Organisms:**
    - Cestodes (Tapeworms): Taenia saginata, Taenia solium, Diphyllobothrium latum, Hymenolepis nana, Hymenolepis diminuta, and Dipylidium caninum.
    - Trematodes (Intestinal Flukes): Fasciolopsis buski, Heterophyes heterophyes, and Metagonimus yokogawai.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    - Niclosamide rapidly kills adult worms but is not active against ova. The mechanism of action is presumably due to the inhibition of oxidative phosphorylation or the stimulation of ATPase activity in the parasite. The drug causes irreversible damage to the scolex (the head of the worm that attaches to the host intestine) and a proximal segment, causing the worm to separate from the intestinal wall and undergo expulsion from the gastrointestinal tract.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:**
    - Parasite mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation pathways and ATPase enzyme activity.
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):**
    - Rapidly vermicidal (taeniacidal) against adult worms; non-ovicidal (has no effect on tapeworm eggs/ova).
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Gene Mutations:**
    - N/A

#### Pharmacokinetics (ADME)
*   **Absorption & Bioavailability:**
    - Minimally absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract; systemic absorption is negligible.
*   **Distribution & Tissue Penetration:**
    - Actions are restricted locally to the gut lumen due to minimal gastrointestinal tract absorption. Neither the drug nor its metabolites have been recovered from the blood or urine. It does not penetrate tissues and is completely ineffective against tissue-dwelling larval infections such as cysticercosis or hydatid disease.
*   **Metabolism & CYP450 Dynamics:**
    - N/A
*   **Excretion & Half-Life:**
    - Eliminated and excreted unchanged in the feces. Systemic half-life is not applicable due to the lack of systemic circulation.
*   **Dosage Adjustments:**
    - Renal Impairment: N/A
    - Hepatic Impairment: N/A

#### Clinical Indications & Spectrum
*   **Primary Clinical Indications:**
    - Intestinal Taenia saginata (beef tapeworm) infection: Administered as a single oral dose of 2 g, yielding cure rates of approximately 95%.
    - Intestinal Diphyllobothrium latum (fish tapeworm) infection: Administered as a single oral dose of 2 g, yielding cure rates of over 85%.
    - Intestinal Taenia solium (pork tapeworm) infection: Administered as a single oral dose of 2 g.
    - Intestinal Hymenolepis diminuta and Dipylidium caninum infections: Treated with a 7-day course of therapy; some patients may require a second course.
    - Intestinal Hymenolepis nana (dwarf tapeworm) infection: Used as a second-line or alternative treatment (praziquantel is the superior drug of choice).
    - Intestinal fluke infections (Fasciolopsis buski, Heterophyes heterophyes, and Metagonimus yokogawai): Used as an alternative drug. The standard dose is administered every other day for a total of three doses.
    - Preferred choice for tapeworm infections when the parasitological diagnosis is uncertain between T. saginata and T. solium to avoid the danger of an inadvertent secondary inflammatory response to occult cysticercosis that other systemic drugs might trigger.
*   **Specific Pathogen Spectrum:**
    - Taenia saginata, Taenia solium, Diphyllobothrium latum, Hymenolepis nana, Hymenolepis diminuta, Dipylidium caninum, Fasciolopsis buski, Heterophyes heterophyes, and Metagonimus yokogawai.
*   **Off-Label / Niche Uses:**
    - N/A

#### Adverse Effects & Boxed Warnings
*   **Common Side Effects:**
    - Infrequent, mild, and transitory gastrointestinal events including nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal discomfort.
*   **Severe / Life-Threatening Adverse Effects:**
    - Secondary Cysticercosis: Treatment of Taenia solium poses a risk because the drug-induced destruction and subsequent digestion of gravid tapeworm segments can release viable ova into the gut lumen. These eggs can hatch, penetrate the intestinal wall, and develop into larvae that cause cysticercosis, a dangerous infection that responds poorly to chemotherapy.
*   **Pathognomonic / Highly Specific Side Effects:**
    - Release of viable ova from drug-damaged gravid segments of Taenia solium, which carries a risk of inducing secondary cysticercosis.
*   **Black Box Warnings:**
    - N/A

#### Contraindications & Drug Interactions
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    - Known hypersensitivity or severe allergic reactions to niclosamide or any component of the formulation.
*   **Relative Contraindications & Cautions:**
    - Taenia solium (pork tapeworm) infection: Requires extreme caution. To minimize the risk of cysticercosis from released ova, the single oral dose of niclosamide is usually followed by a purgative 2 hours later to rapidly expel the damaged worm segments and eggs.
    - Pregnancy: Safety has not been established in pregnant patients.
    - Children: Safety has not been established for children younger than 2 years of age.
    - Systemic/Tissue Larval Infections: Highly contraindicated for the treatment of cysticercosis or hydatid disease as it is not effective against tissue-dwelling larval stages.
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:**
    - Alcohol: Patients must not consume alcohol during therapy or for 1 day after treatment.
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:**
    - Safety during pregnancy has not been established.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "Chewed thoroughly on an empty stomach," referring to the mandated administration protocol where the 2-g adult dose is taken in the morning and must be chewed completely before swallowing with water.
    - "Negligible systemic absorption," highlighting why neither the drug nor its metabolites appear in blood or urine.
    - "Non-ovicidal cestocide," emphasizing that the drug kills the adult worm but leaves tapeworm eggs viable.
    - "Not available in the USA," indicating that the drug is no longer approved for use in the United States.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - Gut-Restricted Luminal Exception: Although niclosamide is a highly effective cestocide and trematocide, it is completely inactive against systemic larval stages of the same parasites (such as Echinococcus hydatid disease or Taenia solium cysticercosis) because it lacks gastrointestinal absorption and cannot reach therapeutic levels in systemic tissues.
    - Active Segment Digestion Hazard: Unlike most antiparasitic therapies where killing the organism resolves the pathology, killing Taenia solium with niclosamide introduces a unique danger. The subsequent digestion of the dead tapeworm segments releases viable, infective eggs into the bowel lumen, which can hatch and penetrate the tissues to cause a dangerous, secondary cysticercosis.
