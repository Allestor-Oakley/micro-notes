### Triclabendazole
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:**
    - Benzimidazole class of anthelmintics (specifically a halogenated benzimidazole derivative).
*   **Target Organisms:**
    - Fasciola hepatica and Paragonimus skrjabini.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    - Belongs to the benzimidazole class, which generally acts by inhibiting parasite microtubule synthesis. However, the exact molecular mechanism of action of triclabendazole specifically is not fully detailed in the provided sources.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:**

*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):**

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - Accidental watercress infection: Humans are only accidentally infected with Fasciola hepatica by eating contaminated freshwater plants such as watercress.
    - Praziquantel resistance exception: Unlike other flukes, Fasciola species do not respond to praziquantel, making triclabendazole the indispensable first-line therapy.
    - Novartis: In the United States, this agent is not widely commercially available and is only obtainable directly from the manufacturer, Novartis.
    - Turkish Cohort Trial: A clinical study by Saba and colleagues (2004) demonstrated a 78% cure rate with a single dose, which increased to 90% with additional courses, with no significant side effects reported.
    - Bithionol replacement: Triclabendazole has largely replaced bithionol, which was previously the recommended drug of choice for fascioliasis.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - Praziquantel Failure Exception: Although praziquantel is a highly effective, broad-spectrum trematocide that kills almost all flukes, Fasciola species are completely resistant to it, requiring treatment with triclabendazole or bithionol instead.
