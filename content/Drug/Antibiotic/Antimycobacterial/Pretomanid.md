### Pretomanid
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:** Nitroimidazopyran antimycobacterial agent.
*   **Target Organisms:** Mycobacterium tuberculosis.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:** It is a pro-drug with a dual mechanism of action:
    1. It inhibits mycolic acid and protein synthesis at the step between hydroxymycolate and ketomycolate.
    2. Similar to the structurally related metronidazole, it is activated within the bacillus via a nitro-reduction step. This activation generates reactive nitrogen species, such as nitric oxide (NO) via its des-nitro metabolite, which subsequently augment the kill of intracellular nonreplicating persistent bacilli by the innate immune system.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:** Inhibits mycolic acid synthesis at the step between hydroxymycolate and ketomycolate. Its activation within the bacillus requires a specific glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (FGD1) and the reduced deazaflavin co-factor F420.
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):** Bactericidal (exhibits enhanced bactericidal activity against Mycobacterium tuberculosis when combined with rifampin and/or pyrazinamide in murine models, and is active against both replicating and intracellular nonreplicating persistent bacilli).

*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike typical cell-wall inhibitors that only target actively replicating bacilli, pretomanid possesses a dual-action mechanism that allows it to inhibit cell wall synthesis in replicating bacilli and simultaneously act as a respiratory poison (releasing nitric oxide) to kill dormant, nonreplicating persistent bacilli.
