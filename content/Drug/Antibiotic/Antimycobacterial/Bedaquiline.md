### Bedaquiline
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:** Diarylquinoline antimycobacterial agent.
*   **Target Organisms:** Mycobacterium tuberculosis and other nontuberculous mycobacteria.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the proton pump activity of the mycobacterial ATP synthase, thereby targeting and disrupting bacillary energy metabolism.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:** Targets subunit c of the ATP synthase of Mycobacterium tuberculosis, specifically binding to the membrane-spanning domain of the ATP synthase c subunit.
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):** Bactericidal and sterilizing activity against both replicating and nonreplicating bacilli.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Gene Mutations:** Resistance is associated with two point mutations, D32V and A63P, in the region of the gene encoding the membrane-spanning domain of the ATP synthase c subunit. The proportion of Mycobacterium tuberculosis mutants resistant to four times the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) is 5 x 10^-7 to 2 x 10^-8. Cross-resistance has been reported between bedaquiline and clofazimine, likely via upregulation of the MmpL5 multisubstrate efflux pump.

#### Pharmacokinetics (ADME)
*   **Absorption & Bioavailability:** Peak plasma concentration (Cmax) and plasma exposure (AUC) to bedaquiline increase approximately twofold when administered with high-fat food. For oral ingestion of a 400 mg dose, the time to reach maximum concentration (tmax) is 4 hours, the Cmax is 5.5 mg/L after a 400 mg/day regimen, and the AUC0-24 is 65 mg·h/L. It must be taken with food to maximize absorption.
*   **Distribution & Tissue Penetration:** Highly protein-bound (>99%). It features excellent tissue and intracellular penetration, resulting in a large volume of distribution and low peak serum concentrations. Slow release of bedaquiline and its major metabolite (M2) from peripheral tissues results in a very long elimination phase.
*   **Metabolism & CYP450 Dynamics:** Metabolized chiefly through the cytochrome P450 system. CYP3A4 is the major isoenzyme involved in the metabolism of bedaquiline. It is metabolized to its major active metabolite, M2, which is four to six times less active in terms of antimycobacterial potency. The systemic clearance (CL) is approximately 6.2 L/h, although systemic clearance reportedly is triexponential. Population pharmacokinetic studies have not been published.
*   **Excretion & Half-Life:** Excreted primarily via the feces. The mean terminal half-life of bedaquiline and its major metabolite (M2) is approximately 5.5 months.
*   **Dosage Adjustments:** N/A

#### Clinical Indications & Spectrum
*   **Primary Clinical Indications:** Indicated for 24 weeks of treatment in adults with laboratory-confirmed pulmonary tuberculosis if the isolate is multidrug-resistant, specifically resistant to both isoniazid and rifampin. It must be used in combination with at least three other active medications.
*   **Specific Pathogen Spectrum:** Highly active against Mycobacterium tuberculosis, with an MIC of 0.03-0.12 mg/L. It also has good in vitro activity against other mycobacteria, including Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC), Mycobacterium leprae, Mycobacterium bovis, Mycobacterium marinum, Mycobacterium kansasii, Mycobacterium ulcerans, Mycobacterium fortuitum, Mycobacterium szulgai, and Mycobacterium abscessus.
*   **Off-Label / Niche Uses:** N/A

#### Adverse Effects & Boxed Warnings
*   **Common Side Effects:** The most common adverse effects (occurring at rates of 25% or more) are nausea, arthralgia, and headache. Diarrhea (occurring in approximately 13% of patients), pain in extremities, and hyperuricemia have also been reported.
*   **Severe / Life-Threatening Adverse Effects:** Associated with both hepatotoxicity and cardiac toxicity, including QTc prolongation and associated mortality.
*   **Pathognomonic / Highly Specific Side Effects:** N/A
*   **Black Box Warnings:** The FDA has issued a black-box warning related to the risk of QTc prolongation and associated mortality.

#### Contraindications & Drug Interactions
*   **Absolute Contraindications:** N/A
*   **Relative Contraindications & Cautions:** Should be reserved for patients who do not have other treatment options and used with caution in patients with other risk factors for cardiac conduction abnormalities.
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:** Potent inhibitors or inducers of CYP3A4 cause clinically significant drug interactions.
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:** N/A
