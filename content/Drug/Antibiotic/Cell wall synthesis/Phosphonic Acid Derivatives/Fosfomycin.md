### Fosfomycin
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:**
    - Phosphonic acid class of antibacterial agents.
    - It is a small organic molecule originally found in Streptomyces.
    - Formulated as fosfomycin trometamol, which is a stable salt of fosfomycin.
*   **Target Organisms:**
    - Broad spectrum active against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    - It is structurally unrelated to any other antimicrobial agent and acts as an analog of phosphoenolpyruvate.
    - It inhibits a very early stage of bacterial cell wall synthesis by blocking the cytoplasmic enzyme enolpyruvate transferase (MurA).
    - Specifically, it covalently binds to the cysteine residue of the active site of enolpyruvate transferase, blocking the addition of phosphoenolpyruvate to UDP-N-acetylglucosamine. This reaction is the first step in the formation of UDP-N-acetylmuramic acid, the precursor of N-acetylmuramic acid (which is found exclusively in bacterial cell walls).
    - The drug is actively transported into the bacterial cell by glycerophosphate or glucose 6-phosphate transport systems.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:**
    - Cytoplasmic enzyme enolpyruvate transferase (MurA).
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):**
    - Bactericidal (exhibits rapid bactericidal activity against susceptible bacteria).
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Gene Mutations:**
    - Resistance is due to inadequate transport of the drug into the bacterial cell (impaired glycerophosphate or glucose 6-phosphate transport systems).
#### Pharmacokinetics (ADME)
*   **Absorption & Bioavailability:**
    - Oral bioavailability is approximately 40%.
    - Peak serum concentrations are 10 mcg/mL and 30 mcg/mL following a 2-g or 4-g oral dose, respectively.
*   **Distribution & Tissue Penetration:**
    - Active drug is widely distributed, concentrating exceptionally well in the kidneys.
    - Urinary concentrations exceed the MICs for most urinary tract pathogens.
    - Case reports suggest clinical efficacy and tissue penetration in the prostate.
*   **Metabolism & CYP450 Dynamics:**
    - N/A
*   **Excretion & Half-Life:**
    - Excreted in active form by the kidneys.
    - The plasma elimination half-life is approximately 4 hours.
*   **Dosage Adjustments:**
    - N/A
#### Clinical Indications & Spectrum
*   **Primary Clinical Indications:**
    - Approved for use as a single 3-g oral dose for the treatment of uncomplicated lower urinary tract infections (UTIs) in women.
*   **Specific Pathogen Spectrum:**
    - Active against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms at concentrations >= 125 mcg/mL, particularly common lower urinary tract pathogens.
*   **Off-Label / Niche Uses:**
    - Treatment of lower urinary tract infections in males (given as a 3-g oral dose every 3 days for 9 days).
    - Treatment of prostatitis in males (given as a 3-g oral dose every 3 days for 21 days).

#### Contraindications & Drug Interactions
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    - N/A
*   **Relative Contraindications & Cautions:**
    - There are no supportive data for using fosfomycin to treat pyelonephritis (upper urinary tract infection), and its use is not recommended for this indication.
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:**
    - In vitro synergism occurs when fosfomycin is combined with beta-lactam antibiotics, aminoglycosides, or fluoroquinolones.
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:**
    - The drug appears to be safe for use in pregnancy.
#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "Analog of phosphoenolpyruvate," highlighting its unique chemical structure.
    - "Covalent binding to cysteine residue of enolpyruvate transferase (MurA)," identifying its exact molecular target.
    - "Single 3-g dose for uncomplicated lower UTI."
    - "Glucose 6-phosphate supplemented medium," describing the requirement for in vitro susceptibility tests to minimize false-positive indications of resistance.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - While most cell-wall active agents (such as beta-lactams and glycopeptides) target late-stage extracellular peptidoglycan polymerization and cross-linking, fosfomycin is unique because it blocks the first cytoplasmic step of peptidoglycan precursor synthesis.
    - Although formulated in both oral and parenteral preparations, only the oral preparation is approved for use in the USA.
    - Despite achieving extremely high urinary concentrations that make it highly effective for lower UTIs, it lacks supportive data and is not indicated for treating upper urinary tract infections such as pyelonephritis.
