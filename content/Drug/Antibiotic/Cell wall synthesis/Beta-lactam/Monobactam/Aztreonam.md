### Aztreonam
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:** Monocyclic beta-lactam compound (monobactam) isolated from Chromobacterium violaceum.
*   **Target Organisms:** Aerobic Gram-negative bacilli, including Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Enterobacteriaceae.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis. It interacts selectively with penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) of susceptible Gram-negative microorganisms, which halts peptidoglycan synthesis, prevents cell wall cross-linking, induces the formation of long filamentous bacterial structures, and eventually leads to osmotic instability, cell lysis, and bacterial cell death.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:** Penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) / cell wall transpeptidases.
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):** Rapidly bactericidal.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Gene Mutations:** Resistant to and stable against hydrolysis by many of the beta-lactamases elaborated by Gram-negative bacteria, including metallo-beta-lactamases. However, it is not stable against and is hydrolyzed by AmpC beta-lactamases, extended-spectrum beta-lactamases (ESBLs), and KPC beta-lactamases.

#### Pharmacokinetics (ADME)
*   **Absorption & Bioavailability:** Poorly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract, necessitating parenteral administration (intravenous or intramuscular) or inhalation (e.g., Cayston). Following a 1-g intramuscular dose, peak plasma concentrations average nearly 50 mcg/mL. Following a 1-g to 2-g intravenous dose, peak serum levels reach approximately 100 mcg/mL.
*   **Distribution & Tissue Penetration:** Widely distributed in tissues and body fluids. It penetrates well into the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) in the presence of inflamed meninges.
*   **Metabolism & CYP450 Dynamics:** N/A
*   **Excretion & Half-Life:** Eliminated primarily unchanged in the urine via renal excretion. The elimination half-life is 1 to 2 hours (or approximately 1.7 hours) in patients with normal renal function, but is prolonged to approximately 6 hours in anephric patients or those with severe renal failure.
*   **Dosage Adjustments:** Clearance is renal and directly proportional to renal function; the dosage must be adjusted and reduced in patients with renal insufficiency or renal impairment.

#### Clinical Indications & Spectrum
*   **Primary Clinical Indications:** Indicated for the treatment of serious infections such as pneumonia, meningitis, urinary tract infections, and sepsis caused by susceptible aerobic Gram-negative pathogens, especially as an alternative in patients who have a history of immediate hypersensitivity reactions (such as anaphylaxis) to penicillins or cephalosporins.
*   **Specific Pathogen Spectrum:** Excellent activity against Enterobacteriaceae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. It is also highly active in vitro against Haemophilus influenzae and Neisseria gonorrhoeae. It completely lacks activity against Gram-positive bacteria and anaerobic organisms.
*   **Off-Label / Niche Uses:** Used in combination with linezolid (such as linezolid 600 mg twice daily with or without aztreonam) as an effective alternative to ampicillin/sulbactam for the management of complicated diabetic foot infections. It can also be added to antipseudomonal regimens in combination with aminoglycosides or other agents.

#### Adverse Effects & Boxed Warnings
*   **Common Side Effects:** Generally well tolerated, with occasional skin rashes and transient elevations of serum aminotransferases (transaminases).
*   **Severe / Life-Threatening Adverse Effects:** Major toxicity is uncommon, though severe hypersensitivity reactions may occur in rare instances.
*   **Pathognomonic / Highly Specific Side Effects:** N/A
*   **Black Box Warnings:** N/A

#### Contraindications & Drug Interactions
*   **Absolute Contraindications:** Prior history of immediate, severe hypersensitivity reactions to aztreonam.
*   **Relative Contraindications & Cautions:** Must be avoided or used with caution in patients with a history of documented severe immediate hypersensitivity or allergy to ceftazidime, due to their considerable structural similarity and potential for in vivo cross-reactivity. Use with caution and adjusted dosage in patients with renal insufficiency.
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:** Safely administered to patients with a history of penicillin-induced anaphylaxis because it does not cross-react with penicillins or other beta-lactam classes (with the unique exception of ceftazidime).
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:** N/A

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Isolated from Chromobacterium violaceum; monocyclic ring; Gram-negative only spectrum; resembles aminoglycoside spectrum; ceftazidime structural relative; no cross-allergenicity with penicillins; induction of long filamentous structures.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    *   Unlike typical beta-lactam antibiotics (such as penicillins, cephalosporins, and carbapenems), which possess a bicyclic ring structure and cover a broad range of Gram-positive, Gram-negative, and/or anaerobic bacteria, monobactams are characterized by a monocyclic beta-lactam ring and possess a highly restricted spectrum active exclusively against aerobic Gram-negative bacilli.
    *   While other beta-lactam classes show significant rates of cross-allergenicity in patients with a history of penicillin or cephalosporin allergy, aztreonam exhibits virtually no cross-reactivity and is safe to administer to patients with a history of penicillin-induced anaphylaxis, with the highly specific exception of ceftazidime due to their near-identical side-chain structures.
