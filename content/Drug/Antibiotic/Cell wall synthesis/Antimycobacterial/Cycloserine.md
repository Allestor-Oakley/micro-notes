### Cycloserine
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:** Synthetic broad-spectrum antibiotic produced by Streptococcus orchidaceous; classified as a second-line antimycobacterial drug.
*   **Target Organisms:** Mycobacterium tuberculosis and a variety of Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:** Cycloserine is a structural analog of D-alanine. It enters bacterial cells and competitively inhibits two critical enzymes involved in early peptidoglycan monomer synthesis: alanine racemase and D-alanyl-D-alanine ligase (also known as D-alanine:D-alanine synthase). By blocking these enzymes, cycloserine prevents the conversion of L-alanine to D-alanine and stops the synthesis of the D-Ala-D-Ala dipeptide. Consequently, the addition of the two terminal alanine residues to the initial tripeptide side-chain on N-acetylmuramic acid is halted, preventing the completion of the major lipid-linked peptidoglycan building block and disrupting cell-wall synthesis.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:** Cytoplasmic enzymes alanine racemase and D-alanyl-D-alanine ligase (D-alanine:D-alanine synthase).
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):** Bacteriostatic.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Gene Mutations:** Specific mutations involved in cycloserine resistance in pathogenic mycobacteria are currently unknown. However, resistance in clinical isolates of Mycobacterium tuberculosis has been documented at rates ranging from 10% to 82%. There is no cross-resistance with isoniazid or other first-line antimycobacterial drugs.

#### Pharmacokinetics (ADME)
*   **Absorption & Bioavailability:** Oral cycloserine is almost completely absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract. Following oral ingestion of a 0.25 g dose, peak blood levels reach 20 to 30 mcg/mL. The maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) is reached within 45 minutes in fasting subjects, but absorption is delayed for up to 3.5 hours when the drug is administered with a high-fat meal. Its population pharmacokinetics are best described by a one-compartment model with first-order absorption and elimination.
*   **Distribution & Tissue Penetration:** Widely and extensively distributed throughout body tissues and fluids. There is no appreciable barrier to central nervous system entry; cycloserine penetrates extremely well into the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), yielding concentrations approximately equal to those in plasma.
*   **Metabolism & CYP450 Dynamics:** N/A (The sources do not detail significant hepatic metabolism or CYP450 interactions, noting instead that the majority of the drug is excreted unchanged in the urine).
*   **Excretion & Half-Life:** Cleared primarily via renal excretion, with the active form of the drug eliminated in the urine. Approximately 50% of an oral dose is excreted unchanged within the first 12 hours, and a total of 70% is recoverable in its active form over 24 hours. The terminal plasma elimination half-life of cycloserine is approximately 9 hours. Approximately 60% of the drug is cleared from the systemic circulation by hemodialysis.
*   **Dosage Adjustments:** Because cycloserine is renally cleared, it can accumulate to dangerous, toxic concentrations in patients with renal insufficiency. In patients with severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance less than 50 mL/min), the standard dose must be reduced by half, or alternatively reduced to 500 mg administered three times weekly. Additionally, because 60% of the drug is cleared during hemodialysis, it must be re-dosed after each hemodialysis session.

#### Clinical Indications & Spectrum
*   **Primary Clinical Indications:** Used almost exclusively as a second-line agent for the treatment of active tuberculosis caused by strains of Mycobacterium tuberculosis resistant to first-line drugs. It is also indicated as an alternative agent in combination regimens for treating Mycobacterium kansasii infections.
*   **Specific Pathogen Spectrum:** Highly active against Mycobacterium tuberculosis, inhibiting most strains at concentrations of 5 to 20 mg/L. It also has good activity against Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC). Beyond mycobacteria, it displays good activity against enterococci, Escherichia coli, Staphylococcus aureus, Nocardia species, and Chlamydia.
*   **Off-Label / Niche Uses:** N/A

#### Adverse Effects & Boxed Warnings
*   **Common Side Effects:** Headaches, tremors, and gastrointestinal discomfort.
*   **Severe / Life-Threatening Adverse Effects:** Serious, dose-related central nervous system toxicity, including acute psychosis, convulsions (seizures), memory loss, somnolence, and ataxia. Accumulation to toxic levels can occur in patients with renal failure.
*   **Pathognomonic / Highly Specific Side Effects:** Serious, dose-related neurotoxicity manifesting as headaches, tremors, acute psychosis, and convulsions.
*   **Black Box Warnings:** N/A

#### Contraindications & Drug Interactions
*   **Absolute Contraindications:** N/A
*   **Relative Contraindications & Cautions:** Must be used with extreme caution in patients with renal insufficiency, renal failure, pre-existing seizure disorders, or psychiatric illnesses. To minimize the risk of severe CNS toxicities (such as psychosis and convulsions), oral dosages must be carefully managed and generally maintained below 0.75 g/day (750 mg/day).
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:** Concomitant administration of pyridoxine (vitamin B6) may be utilized to help alleviate or prevent the neurologic symptoms and peripheral neuropathy associated with cycloserine therapy.
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:** N/A

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** D-4-amino-3-isoxazolidone; Seromycin; structural analog of D-alanine; alanine racemase inhibitor; D-alanyl-D-alanine ligase inhibitor; second-line tuberculosis drug; complete CSF penetration; dose-related neurotoxicity.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike many hydrophilic antimicrobials that struggle to cross the blood-brain barrier, cycloserine penetrates the central nervous system without restriction, yielding CSF levels that directly equal those in the plasma. Furthermore, while most cell wall-active agents (such as penicillins or glycopeptides) act on extracellular steps of peptidoglycan synthesis, cycloserine is unique in acting within the cytoplasm to block the very early synthesis of peptidoglycan monomers. It is also highly water-soluble but extremely unstable in acidic environments, being rapidly destroyed at low pH.
