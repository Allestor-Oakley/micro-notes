### Fidaxomicin
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:**
    - Narrow-spectrum macrocyclic antibiotic (also described in some sources as a minimally absorbed macrolide) originally discovered in actinomycetes.
*   **Target Organisms:**
    - Gram-positive aerobes and anaerobes, specifically Clostridium difficile. It lacks activity against Gram-negative bacteria.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    - Inhibits bacterial RNA polymerase (transcription) by binding to the sigma subunit of the enzyme, thereby preventing bacterial protein synthesis.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:**
    - Sigma subunit of bacterial RNA polymerase.
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):**
    - Bactericidal in Gram-positive bacteria (specifically Clostridium difficile).
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Gene Mutations:**
    - N/A

#### Pharmacokinetics (ADME)
*   **Absorption & Bioavailability:**
    - Systemic absorption is negligible following oral administration.
*   **Distribution & Tissue Penetration:**
    - Poorly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract, resulting in negligible blood levels but extremely high fecal concentrations. It is not used to treat systemic infections.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - Minimally absorbed macrolide / macrocyclic antibiotic.
    - Sigma subunit of RNA polymerase binder.
    - Discovered in actinomycetes.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - While most macrolides bind to the 50S subunit of the ribosome to inhibit translation, fidaxomicin is unique because it inhibits transcription by binding to the sigma subunit of RNA polymerase.
    - Although it is an antibiotic, it has zero utility in treating systemic infections because it is poorly absorbed from the gut, making its clinical use strictly localized to the gastrointestinal lumen for treating Clostridium difficile infections.
    - It is as effective as oral vancomycin for treating Clostridium difficile infection but may be associated with lower rates of relapsing disease.
