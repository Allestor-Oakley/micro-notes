### Nitrofurantoin
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:**
    - Synthetic nitrofuran class of urinary tract antiseptics (urinary antiseptics).
*   **Target Organisms:**
    - Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, specifically Escherichia coli and enterococci. It is not active against Pseudomonas aeruginosa, most species of Proteus, and many strains of Enterobacter and Klebsiella.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    - Nitrofurantoin is a prodrug that requires rapid intracellular conversion and activation by bacterial reductases.
    - Highly reactive reduced intermediates are formed, which react nonspecifically with many ribosomal proteins and disrupt vital metabolic processes, as well as the synthesis of proteins, RNA, and DNA.
    - These reactive intermediates also directly damage bacterial DNA.
    - Selective toxicity is achieved because bacterial cells reduce nitrofurantoin much more rapidly than do mammalian cells.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:**
    - Bacterial DNA, ribosomal proteins, and multiple bacterial enzyme systems involved in protein synthesis and metabolic processes. Activated by bacterial reductases.
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):**
    - Bacteriostatic for most susceptible microorganisms at concentrations of 32 mcg/mL or less, and bactericidal at therapeutic doses or concentrations of 100 mcg/mL or greater.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Gene Mutations:**
    - Bacteria that are susceptible to the drug rarely become resistant during therapy, and resistance emerges slowly.
    - There is no cross-resistance between nitrofurantoin and other classes of antimicrobial agents.
    - Inherent/intrinsic resistance is present in Pseudomonas aeruginosa, most species of Proteus, and many strains of Enterobacter and Klebsiella due to lack of susceptibility.

#### Pharmacokinetics (ADME)
*   **Absorption & Bioavailability:**
    - Absorbed rapidly and completely from the gastrointestinal (GI) tract following oral administration.
    - The macrocrystalline form of the drug is absorbed and excreted more slowly.
*   **Distribution & Tissue Penetration:**
    - Extensively and rapidly cleared from the blood; consequently, therapeutic antibacterial concentrations are not achieved in the plasma or systemic tissues following ingestion of recommended doses.
    - It is concentrated in the renal tubules, and the average dose yields a very high concentration in the urine of approximately 200 mcg/mL.
    - Nitrofurantoin colors the urine brown.
*   **Metabolism & CYP450 Dynamics:**
    - N/A
*   **Excretion & Half-Life:**
    - Rapidly cleared from the body with a short plasma elimination half-life of 0.3 to 1 hour (or approximately 0.5 hour).
    - Approximately 40% of the dose is excreted unchanged into the urine.
    - The rate of renal excretion is linearly related to the creatinine clearance.
*   **Dosage Adjustments:**
    - Contraindicated in patients with renal failure or impaired renal function (specifically, creatinine clearance less than 40 mL/minute), because decreased glomerular filtration decreases the efficacy of the drug in the urine and increases systemic drug accumulation and toxicity.
    - No dosage adjustments are listed for hepatic impairment.

#### Clinical Indications & Spectrum
*   **Primary Clinical Indications:**
    - Prevention (prophylaxis) and treatment of uncomplicated lower urinary tract infections (UTIs).
    - Long-term prophylaxis of recurrent urinary tract infections (e.g., using a single daily dose of 50 to 100 mg at bedtime to prevent recurrences in women).
*   **Specific Pathogen Spectrum:**
    - Susceptible pathogens: Many strains of Escherichia coli and enterococci.
    - Resistant pathogens: Pseudomonas aeruginosa, most species of Proteus, and many strains of Enterobacter and Klebsiella.
*   **Off-Label / Niche Uses:**
    - N/A

#### Adverse Effects & Boxed Warnings
*   **Common Side Effects:**
    - Anorexia, nausea, and vomiting (the principal side effects, which can be lessened by taking the drug with meals and at bedtime).
    - Gastrointestinal upset and distress.
*   **Severe / Life-Threatening Adverse Effects:**
    - Neuropathies (peripheral neuropathy), particularly with prolonged use or in patients with renal impairment.
    - Pulmonary toxicities, including acute hypersensitivity pneumonitis, pulmonary infiltration, and pulmonary fibrosis.
    - Hemolytic anemia, which can occur in patients with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency.
    - Dermatological rashes and other hypersensitivity reactions.
*   **Pathognomonic / Highly Specific Side Effects:**
    - Turning the urine a brown color.
    - Gastrointestinal upset (anorexia, nausea, vomiting) which are the principal dose-limiting side effects.
*   **Black Box Warnings:**
    - N/A

#### Contraindications & Drug Interactions
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    - Individuals with impaired renal function or renal failure (creatinine clearance less than 40 mL/minute).
    - Pregnant women.
    - Children less than 1 month of age.
*   **Relative Contraindications & Cautions:**
    - Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency (due to risk of provoking hemolytic anemia).
    - Use should not exceed a single course of 14 days, and repeated courses should be separated by rest periods.
    - Not recommended for the treatment of upper urinary tract infections (such as pyelonephritis) or prostatitis because therapeutic concentrations are not achieved in systemic tissues or prostate tissues.
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:**
    - Antagonizes the action of nalidixic acid.
    - Urine Alkyalinizers: The urine should not be alkalinized because alkalinization reduces the antimicrobial activity of nitrofurantoin (the antibacterial activity of the drug is higher in acidic urine, and keeping the urinary pH below 5.5 greatly enhances drug activity).
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:**
    - Contraindicated in pregnant women. No specific details on teratogenicity in humans are documented in the texts.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "Urinary antiseptic" that has little or no systemic antibacterial effect.
    - "Highly reactive intermediates" generated by bacterial reductases.
    - "Colors the urine brown."
    - "Urine acidification" (keeping pH below 5.5) to enhance drug activity.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - Although most systemically administered antibiotics are intended to achieve therapeutic plasma levels, nitrofurantoin achieves negligible blood levels and is effective only because it concentrates in the urine.
    - It is not recommended for treatment of pyelonephritis or prostatitis, despite being a urinary tract agent, because it does not achieve therapeutic tissue concentrations in the kidney parenchyma or prostate.
    - While resistance to other agents (like trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole and fluoroquinolones) has risen rapidly in E. coli, resistance to nitrofurantoin emerges very slowly and remains rare, making it a valuable alternative for uncomplicated UTIs.
