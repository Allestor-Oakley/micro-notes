### Daptomycin
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:** Cyclic lipopeptide antibiotic (fermentation product derived from Streptomyces roseosporus).
*   **Target Organisms:** Aerobic, facultative, and anaerobic Gram-positive bacteria.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:** Binds to the bacterial cell membrane via calcium-dependent insertion of its lipophilic decanoic acid tail. This insertion results in rapid depolarization of the bacterial cell membrane, potassium efflux, loss of membrane potential, and rapid bacterial cell death.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:** Bacterial cell membrane.
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):** Rapidly bactericidal (exhibits more rapid bactericidal activity than vancomycin in vitro); exhibits concentration-dependent bactericidal activity.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Gene Mutations:** Resistance is rare but can emerge while on therapy, typically during the treatment of deep-seated infections (such as osteomyelitis, endocarditis, or infections of prosthetic material). Associated with genetic changes in the mprF gene, which regulates bacterial cell membrane charge. Staphylococci with decreased susceptibility to vancomycin also exhibit higher daptomycin MICs than fully susceptible strains.

#### Pharmacokinetics (ADME)
*   **Absorption & Bioavailability:** N/A (Requires intravenous administration; systemic absorption of oral formulations is not clinically utilized).
*   **Distribution & Tissue Penetration:** N/A
*   **Metabolism & CYP450 Dynamics:** N/A (Does not undergo significant hepatic metabolism; is neither an inducer nor an inhibitor of cytochrome P450 enzymes).
*   **Excretion & Half-Life:** Cleared renally; has a terminal elimination half-life of approximately 8 hours.
*   **Dosage Adjustments:** Standard dosing is once daily (typically 4 mg/kg per day for complicated skin and soft tissue infections, and 6 mg/kg per day for bacteremia and right-sided endocarditis). Dosing frequency must be adjusted in patients with severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance of less than 30 mL/min) to once every other day (or a 50% dose reduction). No dosage adjustments are needed for mild-to-moderate hepatic impairment.

#### Clinical Indications & Spectrum
*   **Primary Clinical Indications:** Complicated skin and skin-structure infections; complicated bacteremia; right-sided endocarditis caused by Staphylococcus aureus.
*   **Specific Pathogen Spectrum:** Highly active against Gram-positive pathogens, including:
    *   Staphylococcus aureus (including methicillin-susceptible [MSSA], methicillin-resistant [MRSA], vancomycin-intermediate, and vancomycin-resistant strains).
    *   Coagulase-negative staphylococci.
    *   Streptococci (including Streptococcus pyogenes, Streptococcus pneumoniae [penicillin-susceptible and resistant strains], Streptococcus agalactiae, and viridans streptococci).
    *   Enterococci (including vancomycin-resistant enterococci [VRE] such as Enterococcus faecalis and Enterococcus faecium).
    *   Corynebacterium species, Peptostreptococcus, propionibacteria, and Clostridium perfringens (generally inhibited by less than or equal to 0.5-1 mcg/mL).
    *   Actinomyces species (inhibited over the range of 4-32 mcg/mL).
    Gram-negative aerobic, facultative, and anaerobic organisms are intrinsically resistant due to the lack of penetration of the high-molecular-weight drug through the Gram-negative outer membrane.
*   **Off-Label / Niche Uses:** For serious infections, many experts recommend using higher doses of 8-10 mg/kg/dose (with some clinical programs evaluating doses up to 10-12 mg/kg/dose). Active in salvage situations against vancomycin-insensitive strains of Staphylococcus aureus.

#### Adverse Effects & Boxed Warnings
*   **Common Side Effects:** N/A
*   **Severe / Life-Threatening Adverse Effects:** Myopathy (muscle toxicity), allergic pneumonitis (typically in patients receiving prolonged therapy of greater than 2 weeks).
*   **Pathognomonic / Highly Specific Side Effects:** Myopathy (correlated with elevations in creatine phosphokinase; weekly monitoring of creatine phosphokinase levels is recommended) and allergic pneumonitis.
*   **Black Box Warnings:** N/A

#### Contraindications & Drug Interactions
*   **Absolute Contraindications:** Contraindicated for the treatment of pneumonia, as pulmonary surfactant binds to and inactivates daptomycin.
*   **Relative Contraindications & Cautions:** Use with caution in patients with pre-existing renal impairment (creatinine clearance of less than 30 mL/min), requiring adjustments in dose frequency. Weekly monitoring of creatine phosphokinase (CPK) levels is recommended to watch for myopathy. Avoid use in patients with a history of daptomycin-induced myopathy or allergic pneumonitis.
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:** Antagonized and inactivated by pulmonary surfactant.
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:** N/A

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Derived from Streptomyces roseosporus; calcium-dependent insertion; membrane depolarization; CPK monitoring; myopathy; surfactant inactivation; inactive in pneumonia.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike most other systemic anti-Gram-positive bactericidal agents, daptomycin is completely ineffective for treating pneumonia because it is bound and inactivated by pulmonary surfactant.
