### Polymyxin
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:** Basic peptide antibiotics; cationic detergents. Includes polymyxin B (a mixture of polymyxins B1 and B2) and polymyxin E (colistin).
*   **Target Organisms:** Aerobic Gram-negative bacilli, including Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Acinetobacter baumannii, Stenotrophomonas, and various Enterobacteriaceae.
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:** Polymyxins are surface-active amphipathic agents that act as cationic detergents. They attach to and interact strongly with lipopolysaccharide and phospholipid components of the bacterial outer and cytoplasmic cell membranes, disrupting membrane structure. This physical interaction immediately changes membrane permeability on contact with the drug, leading to rapid cell death. Additionally, polymyxins bind to the lipid A portion of endotoxin (the lipopolysaccharide of the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria) and chemically inactivate this molecule, neutralizing its toxic pathophysiological effects.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:** Fungal/bacterial cell wall-membrane complex phospholipids, specifically targeting lipid A and lipopolysaccharide.
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):** Rapidly bactericidal.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Gene Mutations:** Intrinsic resistance is present in all Gram-positive organisms, Proteus species, and Neisseria species. Sensitivity to polymyxins is related to the phospholipid content of the cell wall-membrane complex; the cell walls of certain resistant bacteria may physically prevent access of the drug to the cell membrane.

#### Pharmacokinetics (ADME)
*   **Absorption & Bioavailability:** Polymyxins are not significantly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract following oral administration, nor are they absorbed to a significant degree from the skin or mucous membranes. Systemic therapy requires parenteral (intravenous) administration.
*   **Distribution & Tissue Penetration:** Tissue distribution is systemically wide when administered intravenously, but penetration into cerebrospinal fluid and the central nervous system is poor.
*   **Metabolism & CYP450 Dynamics:** N/A
*   **Excretion & Half-Life:** Eliminated primarily via renal clearance.
*   **Dosage Adjustments:** Dosage must be adjusted in patients with renal impairment due to the risk of drug accumulation and enhanced systemic toxicity.

#### Clinical Indications & Spectrum
*   **Primary Clinical Indications:**
    *   Topical use: Applied as ointments or creams (often combined with bacitracin, neomycin, or both) for the treatment of superficial skin infections, external ear infections, and eye infections due to mixed bacterial flora in surface lesions.
    *   Systemic use: Reserved as salvage therapy or an agent of last resort for serious systemic infections (such as sepsis or pneumonia) caused by multi-drug resistant Gram-negative bacteria (including Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Acinetobacter baumannii, and Enterobacteriaceae) that are resistant to all other available agents.
    *   Oral use: Confined to localized gastrointestinal decontamination or gut sterilization.
*   **Specific Pathogen Spectrum:** Highly active against Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Acinetobacter baumannii, Stenotrophomonas maltophilia, and Enterobacteriaceae. It lacks useful activity against Gram-positive bacteria, Proteus species, and Neisseria species, which are intrinsically resistant.
*   **Off-Label / Niche Uses:** N/A

#### Adverse Effects & Boxed Warnings
*   **Common Side Effects:** Local irritation and hypersensitivity reactions following topical application.
*   **Severe / Life-Threatening Adverse Effects:** Significant systemic toxicity when administered parenterally, specifically severe nephrotoxicity (kidney damage) and neurotoxicity.
*   **Pathognomonic / Highly Specific Side Effects:** N/A
*   **Black Box Warnings:** N/A

#### Contraindications & Drug Interactions
*   **Absolute Contraindications:** N/A
*   **Relative Contraindications & Cautions:** Parenteral use must be avoided or managed with extreme caution in patients with pre-existing renal impairment or renal insufficiency. It must be treated strictly as an agent of last resort.
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:** Concomitant use with other nephrotoxic or neurotoxic medications (such as aminoglycosides) can result in additive, severe toxicities and must be avoided.
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:** N/A

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:** Cationic detergents; lipid A binder; endotoxin inactivator; agent of last resort; colistin; salvage therapy for multidrug-resistant Gram-negatives.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:** Unlike typical cell wall-active antibiotics that target enzymatic pathways, polymyxins act as physical surfactants that directly disintegrate the bacterial outer cell membrane bilayer immediately upon contact.
