### HIV Entry Inhibitors
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:**
    - Entry Inhibitors / Viral Attachment and Entry Blockers
    - Maraviroc: CCR5 Co-receptor Antagonist (Host-directed entry inhibitor)
    - Enfuvirtide (T-20): Fusion Inhibitor (Synthetic 36-amino-acid peptide)
*   **Target Organisms:**
    - Human Immunodeficiency Virus Type 1 (HIV-1)
    - Maraviroc is selectively active only against CCR5-tropic HIV-1 strains
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    - Entry Process: Viral attachment to the host cell entails binding of the viral envelope glycoprotein complex gp160 (consisting of gp120 and gp41) to its cellular receptor CD4. This binding induces conformational changes in gp120 that enable access to the chemokine receptors CCR5 or CXCR4. Chemokine receptor binding induces further conformational changes in gp120, allowing exposure to gp41 and leading to fusion of the viral envelope with the host cell membrane and subsequent entry of the viral core into the cellular cytoplasm.
    - Maraviroc: Binds specifically and selectively to the host transmembrane chemokine co-receptor CCR5. This binding blocks the viral envelope glycoprotein gp120 from interacting with the co-receptor, thereby preventing the subsequent conformational changes in gp120 necessary for membrane fusion and host cell entry.
    - Enfuvirtide: Binds specifically to the gp41 subunit of the viral envelope glycoprotein gp160, physically preventing the conformational changes required for the fusion of the viral and host cellular membranes, thereby halting entry of the viral core into the cytoplasm.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:**
    - Maraviroc: Host chemokine co-receptor CCR5 protein
    - Enfuvirtide: Viral gp41 subunit of the envelope glycoprotein complex (gp160)
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):**
    - Virustatic (like all available antiretrovirals, they prevent the infection of susceptible cells but do not eradicate the virus from cells that already harbor integrated proviral DNA or affect latent virus)
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Gene Mutations:**
    - Maraviroc: Associated with one or more mutations in the V3 loop of gp120. However, the emergence of CXCR4-using virus (either previously undetected minority quasi-species or newly selected/developed CXCR4-tropic strains) is a more common cause of clinical virologic failure than the selection of specific maraviroc-binding site mutations.
    - Enfuvirtide: Can result from specific mutations in the gp41 subunit of the viral envelope glycoprotein. Lacks cross-resistance with other currently approved antiretroviral drug classes.

#### Pharmacokinetics (ADME)
*   **Absorption & Bioavailability:**
    - Maraviroc: Rapid but variable oral absorption, with the time to maximum absorption (tmax) generally occurring 1 to 4 hours after oral ingestion.
    - Enfuvirtide: Formulated strictly for parenteral subcutaneous injection. It is the only parenterally administered antiretroviral agent. Oral administration is impossible due to lack of bioavailability.
*   **Distribution & Tissue Penetration:**
    - Maraviroc: Excellent penetration into the cervicovaginal fluid, with levels almost four times higher than corresponding concentrations in blood plasma. It is a substrate for the efflux transporter P-glycoprotein, which limits its intracellular concentrations.
    - Enfuvirtide: N/A
*   **Metabolism & CYP450 Dynamics:**
    - Maraviroc: Extensively metabolized by the liver microsomal system; it is a major substrate of Cytochrome P450 CYP3A4. It is also a substrate for the P-glycoprotein transporter.
    - Enfuvirtide: Metabolism appears to be by proteolytic hydrolysis into individual amino acids, without any involvement of the hepatic Cytochrome P450 enzyme system.
*   **Excretion & Half-Life:**
    - Maraviroc: Most of the drug (greater than or equal to 75%) is excreted in the feces, whereas approximately 20% is excreted in the urine. Intracellular half-life is not detailed, but dosage frequencies are adjusted based on CYP3A inhibitors/inducers.
    - Enfuvirtide: Elimination half-life is approximately 3.8 hours. Excreted as peptide metabolites.
*   **Dosage Adjustments:**
    - Maraviroc: Recommended dose varies according to renal function and the concomitant use of Cytochrome P450 CYP3A inhibitors or inducers:
      - Standard dose: 300 mg orally twice daily.
      - Decreased dose (150 mg twice daily): When co-administered with strong CYP3A inhibitors (such as delavirdine, ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin, or any protease inhibitor other than tipranavir).
      - Increased dose (600 mg twice daily): When co-administered with strong CYP3A inducers (such as efavirenz, etravirine, carbamazepine, phenytoin, or St. John's wort).
      - Renal Impairment: Contraindicated in patients with severe or end-stage renal impairment who are concurrently taking strong CYP3A inhibitors or inducers.
      - Hepatic Impairment: Caution is advised in patients with preexisting hepatic impairment, and in those coinfected with HBV or HCV.
    - Enfuvirtide: Administered as 90 mg subcutaneously twice daily. No routine dosage adjustments are required for renal or hepatic insufficiency. It does not exhibit significant Cytochrome P450-mediated drug-drug interactions.

#### Clinical Indications & Spectrum
*   **Primary Clinical Indications:**
    - Combined suppressive antiretroviral therapy (HAART) of patients infected with Human Immunodeficiency Virus Type 1 (HIV-1).
    - Maraviroc: Indicated in combination with other antiretroviral agents in adult patients infected exclusively with CCR5-tropic HIV-1 (as determined by tropism testing).
    - Enfuvirtide: Typically indicated in combination with other antiretrovirals for treatment-experienced patients harboring multi-drug resistant strains of HIV-1.
*   **Specific Pathogen Spectrum:**
    - Human Immunodeficiency Virus Type 1 (HIV-1).
    - Maraviroc lacks activity against HIV-1 strains utilizing the CXCR4 co-receptor, or those with dual/mixed tropism.
*   **Off-Label / Niche Uses:**
    - N/A

#### Adverse Effects & Boxed Warnings
*   **Common Side Effects:**
    - Maraviroc: Cough, muscle pain (myalgia), diarrhea, sleep disturbances (insomnia), and mild elevations in liver enzymes.
    - Enfuvirtide: Nearly universal local injection site reactions (consisting of painful erythematous nodules, induration, pruritus, and ecchymosis), insomnia, headache, dizziness, and nausea.
*   **Severe / Life-Threatening Adverse Effects:**
    - Maraviroc: Severe hepatotoxicity and liver injury, and possible increased risk of myocardial infarction.
    - Enfuvirtide: Severe systemic hypersensitivity reactions (which may rarely occur, are of varying severity, and can recur on drug challenge), and a significantly higher clinical rate of bacterial pneumonia.
*   **Pathognomonic / Highly Specific Side Effects:**
    - Enfuvirtide-induced local subcutaneous injection site reactions consisting of painful, indurated erythematous nodules.
    - Enfuvirtide-induced absolute eosinophilia as the primary laboratory abnormality.
    - Maraviroc-induced clinical failure due to phenotypic "coreceptor switch" or emergence of CXCR4-utilizing virus.
*   **Black Box Warnings:**
    - N/A

#### Contraindications & Drug Interactions
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    - Known history of severe hypersensitivity, immediate allergic reaction, or serious systemic reaction to maraviroc, enfuvirtide, or any component of the formulations.
    - Co-administration of maraviroc with the strong CYP3A inducer rifampin.
    - Maraviroc is contraindicated in patients with severe or end-stage renal impairment who are concurrently taking strong CYP3A inhibitors or inducers.
    - Patients harboring HIV-1 strains that are not exclusively CCR5-tropic (as determined by tropism testing prior to therapy).
*   **Relative Contraindications & Cautions:**
    - Maraviroc: Preexisting hepatic impairment, or coinfection with Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) or Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) (increases the risk of hepatotoxicity).
    - Enfuvirtide: Preexisting high risk factors for developing bacterial pneumonia.
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:**
    - Maraviroc + CYP3A Inhibitors: Co-administration of maraviroc with strong CYP3A inhibitors (such as delavirdine, ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin, or any protease inhibitor other than tipranavir) significantly increases maraviroc plasma concentrations, requiring a maraviroc dose reduction to 150 mg twice daily.
    - Maraviroc + CYP3A Inducers: Co-administration of maraviroc with strong CYP3A inducers (such as efavirenz, etravirine, carbamazepine, phenytoin, or St. John's wort) significantly decreases maraviroc plasma concentrations, requiring a maraviroc dose increase to 600 mg twice daily.
    - Rifampin: Strong inducer of CYP3A and P-glycoprotein; co-administration is contraindicated due to severe decreases in maraviroc levels.
    - Enfuvirtide: No major drug-drug interactions have been identified that require dosage alterations of concomitant antiretroviral or other medications.
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:**
    - N/A

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - "gp41 fusion inhibitor," characterizing the molecular target of enfuvirtide.
    - "CCR5 co-receptor antagonist," highlighting the host-directed entry-blocking mechanism of maraviroc.
    - "Coreceptor tropism testing," the mandatory diagnostic pre-screening test required prior to starting maraviroc.
    - "Painful erythematous nodules," describing the near-universal local injection site reaction of enfuvirtide.
    - "Phenotypic coreceptor switch," describing the clinical escape mechanism of HIV-1 from maraviroc blockade.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - The Host-Targeting Exception: While virtually all other approved classes of antiretrovirals are designed to target viral-encoded catalytic enzymes (such as reverse transcriptase, protease, or integrase), maraviroc exhibits a major exception in that it selectively targets and binds to a normal host cellular protein (the CCR5 receptor) rather than a viral target.
    - The Parenteral Route Exception: While almost all antiretroviral agents are formulated as oral tablets or solutions to facilitate long-term compliance, enfuvirtide exhibits an absolute exception as the only approved antiretroviral agent that must be administered parenterally via twice-daily subcutaneous injections.
    - The CYP450 Metabo-Exemption of Enfuvirtide: While non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors and protease inhibitors are heavily metabolized by and interact with hepatic Cytochrome P450 enzymes, the entry inhibitor enfuvirtide exhibits an exception in being metabolized entirely via proteolytic hydrolysis into individual amino acids, with zero CYP450 involvement.
    - The Non-Viral Failure Exception: In patients experiencing virologic failure on maraviroc therapy, the most common clinical cause is not the development of drug-resistant viral mutations in the target binding site, but rather a phenotypic "switch" or expansion of pre-existing, CXCR4-using viral variants that bypass the blocked CCR5 receptor entirely.
