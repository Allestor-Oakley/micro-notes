### HIV Integrase Inhibitors
#### Classification & Mechanism of Action
*   **Drug Class & Subclass:**
    - Class: Direct-acting antiretroviral agents.
    - Subclass: Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors (INSTIs).
    - Specific agents: Raltegravir (RAL), Dolutegravir, and Elvitegravir.
*   **Target Organisms:**
    - Human Immunodeficiency Virus Type 1 (HIV-1).
    - Human Immunodeficiency Virus Type 2 (HIV-2).
*   **Exact Mechanism of Action:**
    - Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors (INSTIs) bind specifically to the viral enzyme integrase, which is essential to the replication of both HIV-1 and HIV-2.
    - By binding to the integrase enzyme, these agents selectively inhibit strand transfer, which is the third and final step of provirus integration.
    - This blocks the integration of reverse-transcribed viral complementary DNA (cDNA) into the chromosomes of host cells, thereby preventing the formation of a provirus and halting the replication cycle.
*   **Target Structure / Enzyme:**
    - HIV-encoded integrase enzyme.
    - Reverse-transcribed viral complementary DNA (cDNA) strands.
*   **Activity Type (-cidal vs. -static):**
    - Virustatic. Like all available antiretrovirals, they prevent the infection of susceptible host cells but do not eradicate the virus from cells that already harbor integrated proviral DNA.
*   **Resistance Mechanisms & Gene Mutations:**
    - Resistance can emerge if these agents are not used as part of a fully suppressive, multi-drug combination regimen.
    - Specific mutational patterns and genetic pathways associated with INSTI resistance can compromise drug susceptibility and lead to treatment failure or viral rebound.
    - Certain specific integrase mutations can cause cross-resistance within the INSTI class (e.g., dolutegravir requires twice-daily dosing rather than once-daily dosing in the presence of certain specific INSTI mutations).

#### Pharmacokinetics (ADME)
*   **Absorption & Bioavailability:**
    - Raltegravir: Administered orally. Highly bioavailable.
    - Dolutegravir: Administered orally. Absorption is excellent.
    - Elvitegravir: Administered orally. Its absorption and systemic exposure are significantly enhanced by food, and it must be taken with a meal. Co-formulated with the pharmacokinetic enhancer (booster) cobicistat, which increases its bioavailability.
    - Absorption of INSTIs is impaired by chelation when co-administered with polyvalent cations or metal-containing antacids.
*   **Distribution & Tissue Penetration:**
    - INSTIs distribute widely throughout systemic compartments.
    - Raltegravir: Moderately bound to plasma proteins.
    - Dolutegravir: Highly bound to plasma proteins.
    - Elvitegravir: Highly protein-bound.
*   **Metabolism & CYP450 Dynamics:**
    - Raltegravir: Eliminated primarily via non-CYP pathways, specifically glucuronidation by the UGT1A1 enzyme.
    - Dolutegravir: Metabolism involves UGT1A1 with minor CYP3A pathways. It is a substrate of UGT1A1.
    - Elvitegravir: Extensively metabolized by the Cytochrome P450 3A (CYP3A) system. Because of its rapid clearance, it is co-formulated with cobicistat, a potent selective CYP3A inhibitor with no antiviral activity, which acts as a pharmacokinetic booster to increase and prolong elvitegravir plasma concentrations.
*   **Excretion & Half-Life:**
    - Raltegravir: Elimination half-life allows twice-daily dosing (400 mg bid).
    - Dolutegravir: Has a prolonged terminal elimination half-life that permits once-daily dosing (50 mg once daily) in INSTI-naïve patients.
    - Elvitegravir: Co-formulated with cobicistat, the prolonged half-life of boosted elvitegravir allows for once-daily dosing (150 mg once daily).
    - Primary routes of excretion are renal and fecal (biliary).
*   **Dosage Adjustments:**
    - Renal Impairment: No routine dosage adjustments are required for raltegravir or dolutegravir in patients with renal insufficiency. However, the elvitegravir/cobicistat/emtricitabine/tenofovir co-formulation must be avoided in severe renal insufficiency.
    - Hepatic Impairment: Dolutegravir must be avoided in patients with severe hepatic insufficiency.
    - Drug-Inducer Adjustments: For dolutegravir, if co-administered with CYP/UGT inducers (such as efavirenz, fosamprenavir/ritonavir, tipranavir/ritonavir, or rifampin) or in patients with certain integrase-mutant strains, the dosage must be escalated from 50 mg once daily to 50 mg twice daily.

#### Clinical Indications & Spectrum
*   **Primary Clinical Indications:**
    - Suppressive combination antiretroviral therapy (Highly Active Antiretroviral Therapy, HAART) for patients infected with Human Immunodeficiency Virus Type 1 (HIV-1). Standard-of-care regimens require at least three active drugs, typically combining two NRTIs with one INSTI (e.g., raltegravir or dolutegravir).
    - Treatment of Human Immunodeficiency Virus Type 2 (HIV-2) infection (INSTIs are highly active against both HIV-1 and HIV-2).
    - Raltegravir is reserved in some settings as a preferred or alternative agent for treatment-experienced patients harboring multi-drug resistant viral strains.
    - Post-exposure Prophylaxis (PEP): Tenofovir/emtricitabine combined with raltegravir is a standard first-line 28-day regimen for occupational and non-occupational exposures to blood or body fluids containing HIV, initiated within 72 hours of exposure.
*   **Specific Pathogen Spectrum:**
    - Human Immunodeficiency Virus Type 1 (HIV-1).
    - Human Immunodeficiency Virus Type 2 (HIV-2).
    - No activity against non-retroviral DNA or RNA viruses.
*   **Off-Label / Niche Uses:**
    - N/A

#### Adverse Effects & Boxed Warnings
*   **Common Side Effects:**
    - Headache, insomnia, fatigue, dizziness, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, flatulence, and muscle aches.
    - Mild-to-moderate elevations in liver enzymes (aminotransferases).
*   **Severe / Life-Threatening Adverse Effects:**
    - Severe, potentially life-threatening or fatal cutaneous hypersensitivity reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), generalized hypersensitivity reactions, and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) (reported primarily with raltegravir).
    - Rhabdomyolysis and severe myopathy (associated as a rare class-wide risk for INSTIs, manifesting with severe muscle pain, weakness, and marked elevations in creatine kinase levels).
    - Severe systemic hypersensitivity reactions presenting with fever, rash, constitutional symptoms, and organ dysfunction (including clinical hepatitis and liver injury).
*   **Pathognomonic / Highly Specific Side Effects:**
    - Raltegravir-induced severe, life-threatening Stevens-Johnson syndrome or toxic epidermal necrolysis.
    - Severe rhabdomyolysis and muscle toxicity with marked elevations in serum creatine kinase levels.
    - Mild-to-moderate elevations in serum amylase and aminotransferase (liver enzyme) levels associated with raltegravir.
    - Reversible elevations in serum creatinine without affecting the true glomerular filtration rate (GFR), caused by cobicistat-mediated inhibition of renal tubular creatinine secretion in elvitegravir regimens.
*   **Black Box Warnings:**
    - N/A

#### Contraindications & Drug Interactions
*   **Absolute Contraindications:**
    - Known history of severe hypersensitivity, immediate allergic reaction, or serious cutaneous reaction (such as Stevens-Johnson syndrome) to raltegravir, dolutegravir, elvitegravir, or any component of their formulations.
    - Elvitegravir/cobicistat regimens are contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic insufficiency.
*   **Relative Contraindications & Cautions:**
    - The chewable formulation of raltegravir contains phenylalanine and must be avoided or used with caution in patients with phenylketonuria.
    - Preexisting severe renal insufficiency or end-stage renal disease (requires caution, particularly for co-formulations containing tenofovir).
    - Preexisting liver disease or chronic co-infection with HBV or HCV (increases the risk of drug-induced liver enzyme elevations or clinical hepatotoxicity).
*   **Major Drug-Drug Interactions:**
    - Polyvalent Cation-Containing Antacids & Supplements: Co-administration of INSTIs (dolutegravir, raltegravir, elvitegravir) with antacids, iron, calcium, or other supplements containing divalent or trivalent cations (magnesium, aluminum, zinc) must be separated by at least 2 hours because they chelate the drug, severely reducing absorption and systemic exposure, which can cause virologic failure.
    - CYP/UGT Inducers: Co-administration with strong CYP3A4/UGT1A1 inducers (such as rifampin, phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, or St. John's Wort) significantly decreases dolutegravir or raltegravir plasma concentrations. For dolutegravir, co-administration with rifampin, efavirenz, fosamprenavir/ritonavir, or tipranavir/ritonavir requires the dolutegravir dose to be doubled to 50 mg twice daily.
    - CYP3A4 Inhibitors & Boosted Regimens: Cobicistat or ritonavir, co-administered as boosters, inhibit CYP3A4 to dramatically increase elvitegravir levels but introduce numerous severe drug interactions with other CYP3A substrates (such as statins, oral contraceptives, benzodiazapines, or inhaled steroids).
    - Dofetilide: Dolutegravir can increase dofetilide plasma concentrations, and concurrent use must be avoided due to the risk of life-threatening arrhythmias.
    - Nevirapine & Efavirenz: Concomitant use with elvitegravir/cobicistat or dolutegravir must be avoided due to significant interactions that compromise INSTI levels.
*   **Pregnancy & Teratogenicity:**
    - Raltegravir is a recommended antiretroviral agent for use in pregnant women (it is highly effective, has a reassuring safety profile, and is recommended for pregnant women who are at greater than or equal to 14 weeks of gestation).
    - Dolutegravir and Elvitegravir: Safety and pharmacokinetics during pregnancy are less fully established compared to raltegravir, requiring careful risk-benefit evaluation.

#### Trivia & Edge Cases
*   **Buzzwords & Descriptors:**
    - Strand transfer inhibition, describing the final step of provirus integration blocked by this class.
    - Integrase strand transfer inhibitors, the formal class name.
    - Booster-dependent elvitegravir, referencing elvitegravir's strict requirement for co-formulated cobicistat.
    - Cobicistat-mediated creatinine rise, describing the benign elevation of serum creatinine due to tubular secretion blockade.
    - Phenylalanine chewable warning, highlighting the formulation hazard of raltegravir chewable tablets for phenylketonurics.
    - Post-exposure raltegravir backbone, denoting raltegravir's role as the standard third agent in PEP regimens.
*   **Exceptions to the Rule:**
    - The Benign Creatinine Exception: While elevations in serum creatinine typically signal acute kidney injury or worsening renal disease, elvitegravir/cobicistat regimens exhibit a benign exception in that cobicistat causes a mild, reversible rise in serum creatinine by blocking active tubular secretion without altering the true glomerular filtration rate (GFR).
    - The Phenylalanine Chewable Trap: While standard oral tablet formulations of antiretrovirals are safe for all metabolic phenotypes, the chewable tablet formulation of raltegravir represents a highly specific exception as it contains phenylalanine, making it potentially hazardous for patients with phenylketonuria.
    - The Booster-Free NRTI Exemption: Unlike other classes of antiretroviral drugs that are highly dependent on pharmacological boosting (such as the protease inhibitor class or the integrase inhibitor elvitegravir, which requires cobicistat), the integrase inhibitors raltegravir and dolutegravir exhibit a major exception in being highly potent, once- or twice-daily agents that require absolutely zero pharmacological boosting.
    - The Twice-Daily Inducer Exception: Although dolutegravir is highly favored as a convenient once-daily integrase inhibitor for INSTI-naïve patients, it exhibits a major dosing exception in that its administration must be escalated to a twice-daily regimen (50 mg twice daily) if co-administered with strong enzyme inducers (such as rifampin, efavirenz, or boosted tipranavir) or in the presence of certain INSTI-associated resistance mutations.
